\section{Complete classical prices for the four retained cases}
\label{sec:four-complete-classical-proof}

I use the exact stipulated synthetic risk-neutral models, rather than any
observed or licensed market data.  The models satisfy
\(\mu_i+\tfrac12(\lambda^2+\sum_j R_{ij}^2)=r-q\), with
\(r=0.03\), \(q=0.02\), unit maturity and spot and strike equal to
\(100\).  Under the stipulated pricing measure, the discounted total-return
asset prices are martingales.  The capped arithmetic-basket payoff has cap
\(H=1\), positive weights summing to one and values in \([0,1]\).
Integrating the independent common Gaussian factor gives
\[
 f(y)=P_\lambda(m_A(y)),\qquad
 m_A(y)=\log\!\left(\sum_i w_i e^{\mu_i+R_i\cdot y}\right).
\]
Consequently \(0\leq f\leq1\), and the undiscounted continuous price is
\(V=\mathbb E f(Y)\) for independent standard Gaussian coordinates \(Y_j\).
The conditional formula used for production is
\[
 P_\lambda(m)=100e^{m+\lambda^2/2}
   \left[\Phi(m/\lambda+\lambda)
   -\Phi((m-\ell)/\lambda+\lambda)\right]
 -100\left[\Phi(m/\lambda)-\Phi((m-\ell)/\lambda)\right]
 +\Phi((m-\ell)/\lambda),\quad \ell=\log(101/100).
\]
The replay evaluates the two conditional vanilla calls separately and takes
their difference.  Both formulae describe the same bounded payoff.

\subsection{A global derivative envelope for the full conditional price}

Let \(\pi_i(y)=w_i e^{\mu_i+R_i\cdot y}/\sum_k w_k
e^{\mu_k+R_k\cdot y}\).  Then \(\pi_i>0\), \(\sum_i\pi_i=1\), and direct
differentiation gives
\[
 (m_A)_j=\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij},\qquad
 (m_A)_{jj}=\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}^2-\left(\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}\right)^2.
\]
Write \(C_2=P_\lambda''-P_\lambda'\), and use the previously certified
global bounds \(M_1\geq\|P_\lambda'\|_\infty\) and
\(M_2\geq\|C_2\|_\infty\).  The chain rule has the useful exact form
\[
 f_{jj}=P_\lambda'(m_A)\sum_i\pi_i R_{ij}^2
              +C_2(m_A)\left(\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}\right)^2.
\]
For \(A_j=\max_i|R_{ij}|\), this proves, on the entire real coordinate
space,
\[
 |f_j|\leq D_{1j}:=M_1A_j,\qquad
 |f_{jj}|\leq D_{2j}:=(M_1+M_2)A_j^2.
\]
These use the full asset loadings \(R_{ij}\), including the geometric
loading.  Replacing them by centred residual loadings would be invalid.

\subsection{Gaussian transfer and full tails}

Let \(L=6\), \(N=1024\), \(a=\Phi(L)-\Phi(-L)\).  Each Gaussian CDF cell
has mass \(a/N\), and its implemented node \(c_k\) is the midpoint
quantile rounded to \(2^{-28}\).  I certify that every rounded node lies
inside its cell.  For cell \(I_k\), define
\[
 \mu_{1k}=\int_{I_k}(y-c_k)\phi(y)\,dy,\qquad
 \mu_{2k}=\int_{I_k}(y-c_k)^2\phi(y)\,dy.
\]
Taylor's identity with integral remainder gives, for any twice continuously
differentiable coordinate section,
\[
 \left|\int_{I_k}g(y)\phi(y)\,dy-\frac aN g(c_k)\right|
 \leq |\mu_{1k}|\,\|g'\|_\infty+
                       \frac{\mu_{2k}}2\,\|g''\|_\infty.
\]
The first term follows by integrating the exact linear Taylor term.  The
second follows because the integral remainder along the segment from
\(c_k\) to \(y\) has absolute value at most
\(\|g''\|_\infty(y-c_k)^2/2\).  No normalisation or sign decision for a
tiny first moment is needed.  I use certified rational upper bounds on
\(|\mu_{1k}|\).

For \(p\) coordinates, telescope the product retained Gaussian measure
\(\nu^{\otimes p}\) against the product unnormalised grid measure
\(\tau^{\otimes p}\), where \(\tau=(a/N)\sum_k\delta_{c_k}\).
Every other-coordinate factor has mass \(a\).  The preceding identity and
the global derivative bounds therefore give
\[
 \left|\int f\,d\nu^{\otimes p}-a^p\mu\right|\leq\beta,
 \qquad
 \beta=a^{p-1}\sum_{j=1}^p\left(D_{1j}b_1+D_{2j}b_2\right),
\]
where \(\mu=N^{-p}\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_p}f(c_{k_1},\ldots,c_{k_p})\),
\(b_1=\sum_k|\mu_{1k}|\), and \(b_2=\frac12\sum_k\mu_{2k}\).
The construction retains the original subprobability masses.
Since \(0\leq f\leq1\), the omitted contribution satisfies
\[
 0\leq V-\int f\,d\nu^{\otimes p}\leq1-a^p.
\]
All cell boundaries, rounded nodes, moment intervals and mass factors are
retained.  Production uses 512-bit interval arithmetic; the audit
reconstructs all cells and the transfer at 1024 bits.

\subsection{Fixed sampling, numerical score error and confidence}

I freeze \(n=100000\) samples per case and \(\varepsilon=1/150\) before
requesting fresh random words.  Each coordinate label is the low ten bits
of a captured little-endian unsigned 16-bit word.  The confidence statement
assumes those words are independent and uniform.  Retaining a realised
trace enables arithmetic replay; it does not prove this stochastic premise.
Under the premise, each label is independent and uniform on
\(\{0,\ldots,1023\}\).

For completeness, the bounded-score exponential inequality can be derived
without a distributional approximation.  If \(X\in[0,1]\), let
\(\psi(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{t(X-\mathbb E X)}\).  Under the exponentially
tilted distribution, \(\psi''(t)=\operatorname{Var}_t(X)\leq1/4\), because
\(\operatorname{Var}_t(X)\leq\mathbb E_t(X-1/2)^2\leq1/4\).
Together with \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\), twice integrating gives
\(\psi(t)\leq t^2/8\) for either sign of \(t\).  Independence and Markov's
inequality now give
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\frac1n\sum_{h=1}^nX_h-\mathbb E X\geq\varepsilon\right\}
 \leq\inf_{t>0}\exp\!\left(-tn\varepsilon+nt^2/8\right)
 =e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Applying the same reasoning to \(-X_h\), and adding the two tail
probabilities, yields the established bounded-sample inequality
\(\Pr(|\overline X-\mu|>\varepsilon)\leq2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}\).
At the frozen values, interval arithmetic verifies
\(2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}\leq1/3600\).

Every score is evaluated at 128 bits, intersected with the proved range
\([0,1]\), then rounded outwards to endpoints
\(l_h,u_h\in2^{-40}\mathbb Z\).  Their integer arrays and every raw random
word are retained.  With exact rational means
\(\overline l=n^{-1}\sum l_h\) and
\(\overline u=n^{-1}\sum u_h\), set
\[
 L_\mu=\max(0,\overline l-\varepsilon),\qquad
 U_\mu=\min(1,\overline u+\varepsilon).
\]
The realised exact score mean lies between \(\overline l\) and
\(\overline u\), so the same two-sided bound proves that
\(\mu\in[L_\mu,U_\mu]\) with failure probability at most \(1/3600\).
The audit replays all scores at 256 bits using the separately evaluated
conditional call spread.  No normal approximation, adaptive stopping or
estimated variance enters this certificate.

If \(F_-\leq a^p\leq F_+\), the complete undiscounted interval is
\[
 \left[\max(0,F_-L_\mu-\beta),\
       \min(1,F_+U_\mu+\beta+1-F_-)\right].
\]
The endpoint multiplications are valid because the mean bounds are
nonnegative.  The upper endpoint includes the full bounded tail.
Multiplication by a separately certified positive interval enclosing
\(e^{-0.03}\) gives the discounted price interval.  A union bound over the
four retained sampling experiments gives family failure probability at
most \(4/3600=1/900\).

For the one-dimensional case, I additionally sum all 1024 score intervals
before drawing any samples.  This gives a deterministic finite-grid mean
interval and therefore a deterministic complete continuous-price interval
using the same transfer and tail formula.  It is the primary reported
method for that case; the fresh sampling trace remains as an independently
replayable comparison.  These complete prices test classical sufficiency
for the four cases.  They establish neither theoretical novelty nor a
physical quantum advantage.
