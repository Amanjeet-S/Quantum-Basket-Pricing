# 1. Finding

I find no new mathematical principle in the positive-measure moment closure. Once the residual derivatives have the displayed finite exponential-polynomial envelopes, Cauchy's inequality removes the two absolute factors and independence factorises the remaining finite moments automatically. Degree four follows from the maximum polynomial degree after two residual differentiations and the chosen Cauchy splits. Calling this a new generic transfer theory would overstate the mathematics.

The residual-specific derivative derivation is nevertheless necessary. An abstract Peano identity does not identify the bounded combinations C2, C3, C4, C2', C2'' and C3', prove that the log interpolation has t at least one, derive the exact coefficients, retain arithmetic-to-geometric dispersion, or certify actual dyadic nodes and full monetary tails. The completed construction is a useful financial numerical result. Its literature priority and a material advantage over established conditional basket, polynomial-control and Gaussian-integration techniques are separate questions, still unresolved by this mathematical reduction.

# 3. Automatic implications and extra assumptions

The Taylor cell identity and its positive second kernel are established quadrature tools. Testing that identity on an exponential yields the transport transform directly. Product-measure telescoping is algebraic. Cauchy applies to positive finite measures without dividing by their mass. A finite polynomial in asset exponentials integrates as a finite signed sum of products of scalar transforms. Gaussian tilting gives the explicit outside-box expression in this model. None of those implications distinguishes a new quantum method.

The added project-specific argument is the differentiation of the conditioned Taylor residual. Positive basket weights and weighted centring imply U at least zero and t at least one. Positive weights also imply Tjj at least zero. The resulting derivative envelopes have positive coefficients multiplying pointwise nonnegative geometry terms or the two absolute products. Their Cauchy closure uses exactly U2, Tj2, U Tjj, U Tj2, U2 Tjj, U2 Tj2 and U4. Signed coefficients in their exponential-polynomial expansions are preserved; pointwise positivity is established by their geometric form rather than by the signs of expansion coefficients.

Gaussianity is not a minimal assumption for the finite-transform closure. Compact product measures and certified scalar transforms suffice on the retained box. A full pricing certificate additionally needs a finite outside allowance. Gaussianity and a positive smoothing scale are practical sufficient conditions for the present boundary norms and exact tilted tails. Independence is sufficient for scalar factorisation, and can be supplied by a justified Gaussian latent representation. Marginals alone are insufficient for correlated non-product laws.

The transfer theorem certifies an integral under the previously specified risk-neutral law. It does not itself prove that law is a martingale pricing measure, create new asset dynamics, or supply a new no-arbitrage model. Financial validity must be established where the model and discounting are specified.

# 4. Limits demonstrated by explicit counterexamples

Signed basket weights invalidate the displayed geometry even when they sum to one and the loadings are weighted-centred. Take w=(2,-1), d=(0,0), s=(1,2). Then sum wi=1 and sum wi si=0, but T(y)=2 exp(y)-exp(2y). At y=log 3, T=-3 and U=-4; t=1+zU vanishes at z=1/4. The logarithmic interpolation is undefined and the claimed nonnegative terms Uv and Uu2 need not be nonnegative. This is a counterexample to extending the current corollary to arbitrary spread baskets, not a criticism of its stated positive-weight domain. A lower positive bound on T could permit a different local retained-box argument, with new denominator constants, but it would not be the current theorem.

Unbounded falling boundary derivatives invalidate the stated constant-norm corollary. For P(m)=exp(2m), C2=2 exp(2m), so no finite global M2 exists. An exponential envelope might give a different valid closure using shifted rates and extra integrability assumptions; replacing an infinite norm by a finite number would not. At zero Gaussian smoothing scale, an ordinary call has a kink, so the C2 function and C2 derivative assumptions fail. As the scale tends to zero, the Gaussian boundary norm contains inverse powers of the scale. There is therefore no uniform zero-scale certificate.

Non-Gaussian laws need not possess the required full moments. A one-sided exponential latent variable with rate one has an infinite transform at v at least one; a symmetric Cauchy variable has infinite transforms for every nonzero v. Compact retained transforms remain finite, but the full outside allowance may be infinite. The abstract proposition deliberately requires this extra allowance. These examples do not establish arbitrage-free financial models; they demonstrate why the mathematical integrability premise is essential.

Correlated coordinates do not satisfy the scalar product identity. If Y1=Y2 is standard Gaussian, then E exp(Y1+Y2)=exp(2), whereas the product of marginal transforms is exp(1). A Gaussian factorisation can repair this by using one independent latent coordinate with combined loading; silently multiplying the two original marginal transforms cannot.

Signed measures do not support Cauchy's nonnegative square-root closure. Let eta=delta0+delta1-delta2, A(y)=1 and B(y)=(3y2-7y+2)/2. Then B is respectively 1, -1 and 0 at the three atoms. Both square moments are positive: eta(A2)=1 and eta(B2)=2. However eta(|AB|)=2 exceeds sqrt(2). Positive numerical square moments alone therefore cannot justify an L2 argument under a signed measure. The correct first-moment dominating atoms and positive Peano measures avoid this failure.

Signed polynomial coefficients are legitimate, but signed-coefficient termwise absolute values needlessly weaken the certificate. For X=1 pointwise, U2=(X-1)2 has exact moment zero; taking absolute values of the coefficients gives 1+2+1=4. The correct cancellation is algebraic, not permission to set a genuinely negative certified upper endpoint to zero. Finite precision can make cancellation difficult, and the necessary bit count depends on the conditioning of the sums.

There is no uniform ordering of first- and second-order positive transport bounds, even for Gaussian cells. Start with the probability measure uniform on [-1,1], one node zero and f(y)=sin(2 pi y). The centred first moment is zero. Exact derivative-integral FTC and Peano allowances are respectively 2 and 3 pi/2-2/pi, with the latter approximately 4.07577, so second order is worse. For an actual retained standard Gaussian cell [-h,h], node zero and f_h(y)=sin(2 pi y/h), its density satisfies phi(h) at most phi(y) at most phi(0). Positivity of both kernels therefore gives FTC at most 4 h phi(0) and Peano at least 2 h phi(h)(3 pi/2-2/pi). At h=1/10, the ratio lower bound is exp(-1/200)(3 pi/4-1/pi), which exceeds two. This is a fully explicit counterexample for the general smooth-function transport claim. It does not assert that one of the project's particular basket residuals exhibits this ordering.

Transported-coordinate refinement cannot promise a substantial tightening. If A and B are proportional almost everywhere under every block measure, Cauchy is already exact and every block level has the same value. Moreover, refining only coordinate j retains Cauchy loss caused by variation in the other coordinates. The finest transported-coordinate level is a strong factorised comparator, not the exact absolute derivative integral or a universally sharp classical baseline.

# 5. Computational scope

For fixed derivative order and degree D=4, M at most binomial(n+4,4) is polynomial in the asset count. The current grouped producer spends O(p2 M) other-coordinate products followed by O(B p M) block dot products for a fixed number of geometry polynomials. Scalar queries, rate construction, exact coefficients, Gaussian inversion, N actual nodes, factorial-series prefixes, memory and certified precision are additional work. A product prefix/suffix implementation could in principle reduce the O(p2 M) products to O(p M), but that change is absent from the audited producer and cannot be credited as an achieved gain.

Arithmetic counts do not by themselves give polynomial bit complexity uniformly over financial input scales. Large rates and retained radii affect transcendental evaluation and series order; nearly cancelling signed moments can require additional bits. The degree-four count also ceases to be polynomial of fixed degree if the Taylor order is an input allowed to grow. None of the closure arguments removes quantum preparation, coherent arithmetic or estimation cost.

# 6. Audit recommendations

I recommend testing the generic reduction on unequal retained masses and non-equal cell weights, in addition to the current Gaussian CDF cohort, to expose accidental normalisation. Each zero-rate scalar factor must equal its actual raw measure mass. Negative, zero and very small rational rates should be checked independently in the MGF quotient and its support fallback. Required derivative envelopes should be tested with nonzero geometric loadings, since omitting the r and r2 terms would create a materially incorrect certificate. Existing audit results already cover many of these points.

The signed expansion should be reconstructed independently for all seven geometry polynomials, with at least one nearly degenerate dispersion example, and compared against direct positive integral calculations on a small non-Cartesian audit fixture. The current producer is consistent with the checked coefficients: D31=min(M4+3 M3,D2+2 D1) follows from C3'=C4+3 C3 and C3'=C2''-2 C2'. The maximum residual degree after the Cauchy splits is four. No flaw in this algebra was identified.

A future advantage claim requires a stronger result than a shared application of these identities. A lower required precision, materially reduced complete construction cost, sharper guaranteed admissible domain, or a new coherent residual representation would need a precise opponent, shared information and reproducible evidence. The present first-order comparison shares the same closure, so it cannot show that the closure is exclusively quantum or exclusively second order. The frozen numerical reductions support utility, not priority or practical quantum advantage.

It reconstructs both uniform-cell analytic integrals through their split primitives and checks them against independent outward midpoint quadrature with explicit Lipschitz remainders. The retained Gaussian-cell example with h=1/10 gives a certified Peano-to-FTC lower ratio of approximately 2.027720612. Independent outward Gaussian quadrature verifies the strict reverse ordering. These are smooth generic test functions, not additional financial validation cases.

The separately staged classical degree-three pricing test is audited by audit_continuous_degree3.py. I expand (T-1)k by direct exact rational polynomial multiplication, rather than using the producer's binomial differences of raw Tk moments. I independently integrate its signed monomials with full Gaussian transforms and independently evaluate Gaussian-convolved call boundary kernels at 1,024 bits. The audit covers the same nine risk-neutral models and 36 payoff cases. It checks 180 continuous moment enclosures, 180 coefficient enclosures, 108 exact remainder calculations, 108 constructed interval halfwidths, 108 undiscounted interval containments, 108 discounted containments and all 36 admission decisions. All pass. Thirty-three prices meet the undiscounted radius target of 0.01; the three retained n2/lambda0.05 failures are the same cap10, cap25 and vanilla cases. This confirms that the residual geometry and shared conditional norm information also supports a competitive ordinary classical pricing construction; it does not show that a transported moment and a continuous price coefficient are literally the same input.
