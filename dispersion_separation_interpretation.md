# 1. What a separator would establish

I define a separator as a contract for which the certified second-residual continuous-to-rounded-grid allowance meets its fixed transfer allocation, while the retained classical Taylor price intervals through the declared degree cap fail the complete monetary radius. This disproves equality of those two admission sets outside the original cohort. It establishes a distinction between two certificate constructions. Their quantities have different meanings: transport bounds the error of integrating the exact correction on a numerical grid; Taylor bounds the error of omitting a higher-order correction from a price approximation. A separator therefore does not establish a quantum speed-up, a new pricing capability, or a failure of classical pricing.

Increasing a grid's resolution can reduce its transfer error even when a fixed-degree approximation has a poor remainder bound. That is the expected distinction between refining a quadrature target and retaining a fixed approximation order. A numerical instance is useful for identifying the certificate's role, but the observation alone is not a new convergence principle. Conversely, failure of the Taylor upper allowance does not show that its approximate price is inaccurate. It may show that a global derivative norm is conservative.
