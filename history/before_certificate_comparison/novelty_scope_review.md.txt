# 1. Verdict and claim boundary

I identify a substantive project-specific contribution candidate: a computable financial-grid error certificate for a conditioned basket Taylor residual which retains dispersion, uses actual rounded equal-CDF Gaussian nodes, and avoids both a continuous price input and Cartesian enumeration. The displayed argument is a complete mathematical construction, rather than an aspiration for later proof. I do not yet identify it as a proved first contribution to the literature. Literature originality requires a comparison of the resulting theorem and its assumptions with close antecedents, including those whose full text remains unavailable.

The quantum development is a coherent evaluator of an established classical pricing residual, with a sparse payoff-boundary kernel and positive encoding when the payoff is convex. It develops a quantum pricing method within an existing amplitude-estimation framework. It does not invent a new quantum-mechanical asset model, a new generic amplitude-estimation primitive, or a new no-arbitrage theorem. The complete resource findings are original experimental results for the declared compiler and models; they do not themselves demonstrate algorithmic priority or practical quantum advantage.

The precise mathematical value is the closure of the certificate: after the residual derivative envelopes are derived, every quantity needed in the financial transfer bound can be constructed from finitely many scalar transforms and monomial moments, without obtaining the unknown price and without visiting the full tensor grid. Its fixed-degree term count is polynomial in the number of exponentials. This is not a dimension-free error bound or a proof that total bit complexity is uniformly polynomial in arbitrary problem parameters. The current theorem requires a positive-weight basket, independent latent Gaussian coordinates after conditioning, suitable bounded boundary derivatives, finite exponential-polynomial envelopes and certified retained cells. It is not a theorem for arbitrary market dynamics or general multivariate piecewise-affine payoffs.

# 5. Publishability and the next decisive comparison

I regard the work as more than a reproduction because it supplies a proved, usable project-specific certificate and reproducible complete implementation experiments. I do not yet regard it as an established original theoretical contribution at the level required for a top journal. Independent mathematical checks establish correctness; they do not establish novelty. A new proof of an existing conclusion would be a contribution only if its different mechanism, stronger scope, sharper guarantee or reduced construction cost were material and demonstrated.

The decisive next step is a proposition-level comparison of the exact tensor certificate with Blair's final transform-kernel theorem and the GBM discretisation theorem of Herman et al., followed by an executed common-grid comparison of the resulting certified allowances and construction costs. The paper should identify the precise extra conclusion that cannot be recovered at comparable cost from those antecedents. The unread Ju, Curran and Dingeç–Hörmann final bodies and compatible QSP/modern estimation comparison remain explicit gaps. The absence of practical quantum advantage does not logically preclude a useful numerical-finance certificate paper, but it makes a quantum-breakthrough positioning unsupported.
