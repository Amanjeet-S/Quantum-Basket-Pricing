# 2. Pricing antecedents

Ju, N. (2002). Pricing Asian and basket options via Taylor expansion. *Journal of Computational Finance*, 5(3), 79–103. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2002.088). I read the [publisher abstract](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-computational-finance/2160548/pricing-asian-and-basket-options-via-taylor-expansion) and verified the issue, pages, DOI and first publication date, 1 April 2002, in Safari. The final full article was gated. Its abstract specifies moment-matched lognormal approximation and a Taylor expansion of the characteristic-function ratio around zero volatility through sixth order. This distinguishes the stated expansion variable from the present ratio-one payoff expansion, but cannot exclude overlapping coefficient identities or remainder arguments in the unread full article. Secondary citations incorrectly assigning volume 6 or pages 1–33 must not be used.

Korn, R. and Zeytun, S. (2013). Efficient basket Monte Carlo option pricing via a simple analytical approximation. *Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics*, 243(1), 48–59. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cam.2012.10.035). The [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042712004827) and [author's institutional record](https://open.metu.edu.tr/handle/11511/64531) establish the arithmetic-to-geometric limiting approximation and its use as a control variate. The earlier retained audit read indexed publisher Sections 1–4, including the text surrounding Theorem 1, with some formulae missing. Direct retrieval again returned 403. That audit identifies the additive shift tending to infinity. It establishes a close geometric approximation antecedent, but does not establish equivalence to a finite Taylor certificate in the unshifted ratio.

Curran, M. (1994). Valuing Asian and portfolio options by conditioning on the geometric mean price. *Management Science*, 40(12), 1705–1711. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.40.12.1705). I checked the [final publisher metadata and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.40.12.1705). The final full text was not read. The abstract establishes geometric-mean conditioning as an existing Asian and basket option technique; conditioning itself cannot carry a novelty claim here.

Jipreze, K. and Date, P. (2025). New control variates for pricing basket options. *IMA Journal of Management Mathematics*, 36(1), 111–133. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/imaman/dpae023). I read the [final published HTML](https://academic.oup.com/imaman/article/36/1/111/7816702), Introduction and Section 2.1, equations (5)–(29), Proposition 1 and its proof; the [institutional typeset PDF](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/29848/5/FullText.pdf) was also accessible. The method projects a logarithmic basket function onto first-order Hermite polynomials, then prices a lognormal proxy. Third-order Taylor expansion approximates projection coefficients. The consulted section does not supply the present uniform finite-order remainder certificate. Its additional logarithm-of-an-affine-Gaussian approximation requires domain scrutiny; I do not adopt its mathematical assertions solely because the paper is published. Hermite functions or Taylor coefficients used to construct analytic control variates are already precedented.

Caldana, R., Fusai, G., Gnoatto, A. and Grasselli, M. (2016). General closed-form basket option pricing bounds. *Quantitative Finance*, 16(4), 535–554. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2015.1073854). I verified publication metadata in the [author's institutional record](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/15729/). I read the [author-hosted manuscript](https://cms-cdn.lmu.de/media/16-finmath/publikation/basket_option_v2.pdf), Introduction, Section 2, Proposition 2, equations (13)–(16), and the corresponding proof in Appendix 2. This 56-page manuscript is not the final journal layout. The bounds use the arithmetic–geometric inequality and joint characteristic functions, with one-dimensional Fourier inversion. In the positive-weight case, a geometric call and the expected arithmetic–geometric gap bound the arithmetic call. Their conditioning, bounds and dimensional reduction preclude claiming that high-dimensional computable basket bounds are new. The present certificate identifies its Taylor centre, derivative majorant and finite-order truncation explicitly, which differs from the consulted construction.

Dingeç, K.D. and Hörmann, W. (2013). Control variates and conditional Monte Carlo for basket and Asian options. *Insurance: Mathematics and Economics*, 52(3), 421–434. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.insmatheco.2013.03.002). I read the [publisher abstract and Introduction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167668713000309). Its combination of a geometric-conditioning control variate, conditional Monte Carlo and quadratic control variates is a direct classical comparator antecedent. I did not read final Sections 3–4 or attribute their detailed formulae. This source reinforces the need to compare complete classical algorithms, rather than plain Monte Carlo alone.

Bouaziz, L., Briys, E. and Crouhy, M. (1994). The pricing of forward-starting Asian options. *Journal of Banking & Finance*, 18(5), 823–839. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-4266(94)00031-X). # 3. Randomised quadrature and uncertainty antecedents

Jain, A., Hickernell, F.J., Owen, A.B. and Sorokin, A.G. (2026). Empirical Bernstein and betting confidence intervals for randomized quasi-Monte Carlo. *Information and Inference: A Journal of the IMA*, 15(1), iaag003. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/imaiai/iaag003). I verified final publication, 6 March 2026, and read the publisher abstract in Safari. Existing institutional access subsequently authenticated, but I could not verify the full article body. I read [arXiv version 2, 2 February 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18677v2), Sections 2.1–2.4 and 3, Theorems 1–2. This is a published paper whose consulted detailed source is the preceding author version. It treats independent bounded RQMC replicates, empirical-Bernstein and betting intervals, and the trade-off between replicate count and points per replicate. Rigorous bounded-replicate RQMC confidence is therefore already published. The present independent-pair/Chernoff implementation is a conservative self-contained construction, not a new generic statistical method. Its hypotheses must apply to whole independent replicate estimates, not dependent points within one randomisation.

Waudby-Smith, I. and Ramdas, A. (2024). Estimating means of bounded random variables by betting. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology*, 86(1), 1–27. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkad009). I checked the [publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/86/1/1/7043257); detailed reading used the [author manuscript hosted by the Royal Statistical Society](https://rss.org.uk/RSS/media/File-library/Events/Discussion%20meetings/Estimating-means-of-bounded-random-variables-by-betting.pdf), Sections 2–4 and Theorems 1–3, rather than claiming consultation of final typeset text. The paper provides nonasymptotic bounded-mean confidence sequences and empirical-Bernstein/betting procedures. Finite predeclared checkpoint error spending is an elementary conservative alternative to this established framework. Adaptive stopping does not validate a fixed-time Student interval or a data-chosen bound without simultaneous coverage.

Maurer, A. and Pontil, M. (2009). Empirical Bernstein bounds and sample variance penalization. *Proceedings of the 22nd Annual Conference on Learning Theory*. [Final proceedings PDF](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~colt2009/papers/012.pdf). This is a peer-reviewed conference paper, retained as a methodological supplement to the journal sources. I read Section 1.1, Theorem 4, the pairwise representation of unbiased sample variance and the Section 2 concentration argument. The paper establishes finite-sample observable variance-sensitive mean bounds; a variance estimate substituted directly into Bennett's inequality is not automatically valid. The project uses disjoint independent pairs rather than the all-pairs sample variance and derives its own conservative inversion.

Hoeffding, W. (1963). Probability inequalities for sums of bounded random variables. *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 58(301), 13–30. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830). I verified the [publisher metadata and abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830); the final full text was not read. This establishes the classical bounded-sum concentration antecedent. The project's lower-tail moment-generating-function and checkpoint-union arguments are supplied in full rather than represented as new concentration theory.

L'Ecuyer, P., Nakayama, M.K., Owen, A.B. and Tuffin, B. (2023). Confidence intervals for randomized quasi-Monte Carlo estimators. *Proceedings of the 2023 Winter Simulation Conference*, 445–456. [Published proceedings PDF](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc23papers/037.pdf). I read Section 1, equations (1)–(2), and its independent-replicate discussion. The paper explains why observations within one randomisation cannot supply an ordinary independent-sample standard error and why replicate Student intervals are generally asymptotic. Its empirical interval comparisons are not nonasymptotic coverage theorems. It is supplementary to the published 2026 journal paper.

# 4. Permissible contribution statement

I describe the result as a checked, project-specific certificate for the specified capped Gaussian basket: a finite Taylor expansion in the arithmetic–geometric ratio, exact evaluation of finite coefficients by Gaussian tilting, and an explicit retained remainder. The numerical implementation can establish whether that certificate is useful at the predeclared tolerance and whether it changes the quantum comparison after a stronger classical benchmark is charged. I do not claim that Taylor pricing, geometric conditioning, Hermite coefficients, basket price bounds, RQMC or variance-sensitive confidence procedures are original.

The outstanding comparison is the complete Ju article and the wider published expansion/bounding family. Search failure or inaccessible text is not evidence that a result is absent. Any claim of a new theory must await that comparison. The current supported positive result can instead be a reproducible joint error-and-cost finding, including adverse quantum comparisons, if the certificates and numerical record pass independent checks.
