\begin{abstract}
I investigate when a financial error certificate resolves a pricing task
before quantum mean estimation is required. For positive Gaussian baskets
with a common Gaussian factor, I expand the smoothed capped payoff in the
arithmetic--geometric ratio. Gaussian exponential tilting evaluates every
finite coefficient while preserving correlation with the geometric control;
a uniform derivative bound encloses the finite remainder. The order-zero-to-eight
study certifies 25 of 27 model prices within an undiscounted radius of $0.01$,
including all 18 four- and eight-asset contracts. Existing validated
one-dimensional quadrature resolves the two remaining controls. I also execute
independently shifted finite digital nets, with a derived variance-sensitive
confidence rule valid over prescribed stopping checkpoints. All 50,331,648
financial outputs are replayed independently. That conservative randomised
implementation requires more evaluator work than matched fixed-IID plans
on every contract. These classical comparisons supplement 819 fully charged
quantum profiles: range and clipping reduce selected logical T upper budgets
in 17 contracts, while the implemented dyadic family loses every comparison
with the strongest single flag. The deterministic price certificate supplies
a sufficient criterion for meeting a financial tolerance without a quantum
mean-estimation stage, and identifies a substantive classical comparator for
research on quantum pricing.
\end{abstract}

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} financial mathematics; capped basket option; conditional expectation;
Gaussian smoothing; joint moment transforms; weak dispersion; fixed-point error;
reversible arithmetic; variance-aware estimation; quantum finance.

\section{Research question and results}

The research question is whether a computable financial error certificate
can resolve the required pricing accuracy before coherent mean estimation,
and how complete classical and quantum implementations compare when it
cannot. The target is a risk-neutral capped arithmetic basket price with
specified positive weights and a stated monetary tolerance. It is a problem
in financial mathematics and computational finance; no observed exotic price
or empirical return prediction is used as the accuracy target.

The principal mathematical result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-gaussian-price-certificate}. I derive a finite
hierarchy of continuous-model price enclosures. Its coefficient formula
retains the covariance between the arithmetic--geometric ratio and the
geometric log price through an explicit Gaussian tilt. Its remainder uses
positive-weight ordering and a uniform bound on the smoothed payoff's
derivatives. The result applies beyond the frozen numerical grid, including
unequal initial prices and monitored averages satisfying the common-factor
hypothesis. An enclosure with midpoint radius at most the financial
tolerance supplies a pricing answer without sampling that mean.

I test that argument against the full frozen family. The finite Taylor
certificate resolves all eighteen higher-dimensional contracts and seven
two-asset controls; earlier validated quadrature covers the two remaining
controls. A separate randomised digital-net experiment retains exact finite
financial evaluations and confidence intervals under independent shift
randomisations. It is more expensive in evaluator work than matched fixed-IID
plans in this study. The quantum resource experiment remains constructive:
its gates, coherent inverses, confidence allocation, numerical representation
and reusable-shot resets are fully charged. Its 82.1 per cent maximum T-cap
reduction is an encoding comparison, and is not evidence of practical
advantage once these deterministic price certificates are available.

Geometric conditioning, Taylor basket approximations and computable basket
bounds have published predecessors
\citep{curran,ju,kornzeytun,caldana,jiprezedate}.
The candidate literature contribution concerns the explicit finite-order
certificate and its effect on the choice of pricing method, rather than
invention of those ingredients. The source comparison identifies distinct
expansion variables and error treatments; inaccessible full texts, especially
Ju's article, prevent a proof-level priority conclusion at present.
Published quantum and randomised-quadrature mechanisms are attributed where
used. Consultation versions and sections are retained in \texttt{SOURCES.md}
and \texttt{higherdim\_literature\_comparison.md}.
The current comparison does not cover every modern quantum encoding,
in particular the complete QSP alternative, and does not compare physical
quantum hardware times with CPU seconds. These limits define the remaining
research work rather than reversing the checked mathematical or numerical
results.

\section{Financial model and the controlled target}
I fix a one-year maturity, initial prices and strike $K=100$, risk-free rate
$r=0.03$ and dividend yield $d=0.02$. Let $Z,Y_1,\ldots,Y_p$ be independent
standard normal variables under the stipulated risk-neutral probability
measure. For strictly positive weights $w_i$ summing to one, residual loading
rows $R_i\in\R^p$ and common loading $\lambda>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
 S_i=K\exp(\mu_i+R_i\cdot Y+\lambda Z),\qquad
 \mu_i=r-d-\tfrac12(\|R_i\|^2+\lambda^2).
\end{equation}
These terminal variables are obtained from the risk-neutral GBM system
$dS_i(t)/S_i(t)=(r-d)dt+R_i\cdot dW(t)+\lambda dW_0(t)$ with independent
Brownian motions. Applying It\^o's formula to $\log S_i(t)$ gives drift
$r-d-(\|R_i\|^2+\lambda^2)/2$ and diffusion $R_i\cdot dW+\lambda dW_0$,
which proves~\eqref{eq:model} at maturity. In particular
$\E S_i=K\exp(\mu_i+(\|R_i\|^2+\lambda^2)/2)=K e^{r-d}$.

I set $A=\sum_iw_iS_i$, $G=\prod_iS_i^{w_i}$ and
$F_H(x)=\min\{(x-K)_+,H\}$, with $H>0$ and $h=H/K$.
Concavity of the logarithm gives
$\log A\geq\sum_iw_i\log S_i=\log G$, so $A\geq G$ pointwise. Since
$F_H$ is increasing and lies in $[0,H]$, the residual
$F_H(A)-F_H(G)$ lies in $[0,H]$. The undiscounted target and price are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 V=\E F_H(G)+\E[F_H(A)-F_H(G)],\qquad \Pi=e^{-r}V.
\end{equation}
This is an exact control identity, without a fitted control coefficient.
No variance reduction follows solely from the identity.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bases}
 B_A(Y)=\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i+R_i\cdot Y},\quad
 m_A(Y)=\log B_A(Y),\quad
 m_G(Y)=\sum_iw_i\mu_i+\overline R\cdot Y,\quad
 \overline R=\sum_iw_i R_i.
\end{equation}
Then $A=K e^{m_A+\lambda Z}$ and $G=K e^{m_G+\lambda Z}$, with
$m_A\geq m_G$. If $\overline\mu=\sum_iw_i\mu_i$ and
$v_G=\lambda^2+\|\overline R\|^2$, the geometric correction is a capped
lognormal expectation with log mean $\overline\mu$ and variance $v_G$.
The same call-spread identity derived below evaluates it analytically. It is
an offline scalar constant, not a table of joint financial-input payoffs.

\section{The frozen covariance family}
For two assets I use $w=(1/2,1/2)$ and $R=(-0.1,0.2)^{\mathsf T}$.
For $n\in\{4,8\}$, let the $j$th column of the $n\times(n-1)$ Helmert
matrix $H_n$ have its first $j$ entries equal to
$1/\sqrt{j(j+1)}$, its next entry equal to $-j/\sqrt{j(j+1)}$, and all
remaining entries zero, for $1\leq j\leq n-1$.
Its column sums vanish. Each column has squared norm
$(j+j^2)/(j(j+1))=1$. For $j<k$, the inner product is a multiple of
$j-j=0$, since all $j+1$ nonzero entries of column $j$ lie among the equal
entries of column $k$. Thus $H_n^{\mathsf T}H_n=I$ and the columns form an
orthonormal basis of $\ind^\perp$. Consequently
$H_nH_n^{\mathsf T}=I-\ind\ind^{\mathsf T}/n$.

I choose $R=cH_n$, with
$c^2=0.0225/(1-\sum_iw_i^2)$. The weights are
$(0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1)$ and $(0.3,0.2,0.15,0.1,0.08,0.07,0.06,0.04)$.
The covariance $\Sigma=\lambda^2\ind\ind^{\mathsf T}+RR^{\mathsf T}$ has
eigenvalue $n\lambda^2$ on the common direction and $c^2$ on its orthogonal
complement. Both are positive. Also
\begin{align}
 \sum_iw_i\|R_i\|^2-\|\overline R\|^2
 &=c^2(1-1/n)-c^2(\sum_iw_i^2-1/n)\\
 &=c^2(1-\sum_iw_i^2)=0.0225.
\end{align}
The two-asset anchor has the same dispersion:
$0.5(0.1^2+0.2^2)-0.05^2=0.0225$.

The ideal Helmert algebra does not silently turn rounded irrational
coefficients into exact values. The protocol freezes 70-digit decimal
loadings as exact rational model coefficients. Drifts and covariances are
computed at 200 digits and independently checked with exact fractions:
$\Sigma_{ij}=\lambda^2+R_i\cdot R_j$ and
$\mu_i=0.01-(\lambda^2+\|R_i\|^2)/2$. Positive definiteness of each actual
finite-decimal matrix is checked by exact rational $LDL^{\mathsf T}$
elimination with strictly positive pivots. That check concerns all nine
retained models and does not require an approximate eigenvalue test.
The nonzero ideal-to-frozen dispersion discrepancy is below
$1.8\times10^{-71}$. Matching this dispersion does not match
$\|\overline R\|^2$ or the complete payoff distribution. I make no causal
claim about pure dimension scaling or classical difficulty.

\section{Conditional and random-strike identities}
Let $\ph(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ and
$\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x\ph(t)dt$. The layer-cake identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layer}
 \min\{(x-1)_+,h\}=\int_1^{1+h}\ind\{x>k\}\,dk.
\end{equation}
It follows by considering $x\leq1$, $1<x<1+h$ and $x\geq1+h$ separately.
Nonnegative integrands permit Tonelli's theorem. For $x=e^{m+\lambda Z}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 P_{h,\lambda}(m):=\frac1h\E\min\{(e^{m+\lambda Z}-1)_+,h\}
 =\frac1h\int_1^{1+h}\Phi\left(\frac{m-\log k}{\lambda}\right)dk.
\end{equation}
Thus $P$ is increasing, belongs to $[0,1]$, and satisfies
$P(m)\leq\Psi_\lambda(m):=\Phi(m/\lambda)$.
The continuously conditioned residual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional}
 g(Y)=H\{P_{h,\lambda}(m_A(Y))-P_{h,\lambda}(m_G(Y))\}.
\end{equation}
For an independent uniform $U\in(0,1)$, the integrand difference
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interval}
 r(Y,U)=\Phi\left(\frac{m_A(Y)-\log(1+hU)}\lambda\right)
       -\Phi\left(\frac{m_G(Y)-\log(1+hU)}\lambda\right)
\end{equation}
is nonnegative and at most one, and $H\E_U r(Y,U)=g(Y)$.
These identities compare the same target, rather than different derivatives.

For completeness, let $a=\log(1+h)$, $d_0=m/\lambda$ and
$d_1=(m-a)/\lambda$. Splitting the expectation between
$Z\in(-m/\lambda,(a-m)/\lambda)$ and the upper tail, and using
$e^{\lambda z}\ph(z)=e^{\lambda^2/2}\ph(z-\lambda)$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourcdf}
 P(m)=\frac{ e^{m+\lambda^2/2}\{\Phi(d_0+\lambda)-\Phi(d_1+\lambda)\}
            -\{\Phi(d_0)-\Phi(d_1)\}+h\Phi(d_1)}h.
\end{equation}
The middle-interval probability is $\Phi(d_0)-\Phi(d_1)$ by symmetry of
$\Phi$; its exponential moment is the first term in the numerator. The
remaining upper tail is $h\Phi(d_1)$. This establishes every sign and scale
in~\eqref{eq:fourcdf}. The coherent full-conditioning constructor evaluates
the scalar function $P$ with a costed lookup and interpolation block. It
does not coherently evaluate the four-CDF formula as a free oracle.

\section{Global cap-dependent derivative certificates}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:derivative}
For every $h,\lambda>0$ and every $m\in\R$,
\begin{align}
0\leq P'(m)&\leq L_{h,\lambda}
 :=\min\{\ph(0)/\lambda,(1+h)/h\},\label{eq:L}\\
|P''(m)|&\leq C_{h,\lambda}
 :=\min\{\ph(1)/\lambda^2,(1+h)\ph(0)/(h\lambda)\}.\label{eq:C}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $f_\lambda(x)=\ph(x/\lambda)/\lambda$ and $a=\log(1+h)$.
The first two derivatives of the CDF integrand are bounded, respectively,
by $\ph(0)/\lambda$ and $\ph(1)/\lambda^2$, independently of $m$ and $k$.
The finite integration interval and these bounds justify dominated
differentiation twice. Changing variables to $s=\log k$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pprime}
 P'(m)=\frac1h\int_0^a e^s f_\lambda(m-s)ds.
\end{equation}
Its positive weights integrate to $(e^a-1)/h=1$, proving the first
Gaussian slope bound. Since $e^s/h\leq(1+h)/h$ and an integral of the
normal density over any interval is at most one, the second slope bound
follows. Directly averaging $f_\lambda'$ proves the generic curvature
bound: $f_\lambda'(x)=-x\ph(x/\lambda)/\lambda^3$ and
$\sup_t|t|\ph(t)=\ph(1)$, because $t e^{-t^2/2}$ increases to $t=1$
and decreases thereafter on $t\geq0$.

For the cap-dependent curvature, $\partial_m f_\lambda(m-s)
=-\partial_s f_\lambda(m-s)$. Integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ibp}
 P''(m)=P'(m)+\{f_\lambda(m)-(1+h)f_\lambda(m-a)\}/h.
\end{equation}
Dropping nonnegative terms yields
$P''(m)\geq-(1+h)\ph(0)/(h\lambda)$.
Dropping the negative term and using
$P'(m)\leq\ph(0)/\lambda$ gives
$P''(m)\leq\ph(0)/\lambda+\ph(0)/(h\lambda)$, the same magnitude.
Taking the minimum of the two valid curvature bounds proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

The cap-dependent slope is strictly smaller precisely when
$\lambda<\ph(0)h/(1+h)$; the cap-dependent curvature is strictly smaller
precisely when $\lambda<e^{-1/2}h/(1+h)$. These are comparisons of sufficient
global bounds, not claims about the exact supremum of each derivative.
For the nine prescribed $(\lambda,H)$ pairs, curvature improves only at
$(0.05,10)$ and $(0.05,25)$, and slope improves only at $(0.05,25)$.
At the latter pair, $L=5$ and $C=39.894228040\ldots$, compared with generic
$L=7.978845608\ldots$ and $C=96.788289808\ldots$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sharp}
For each fixed $h>0$, the cap-dependent bounds have sharp weak-loading
leading constants:
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}\|P'_{h,\lambda}\|_\infty=\frac{1+h}{h},
 \qquad
 \lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}\lambda\|P''_{h,\lambda}\|_\infty
 =\frac{(1+h)\ph(0)}{h}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $M=(1+h)/h$ and $a=\log(1+h)>0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:derivative}
gives the respective upper limits $M$ and $M\ph(0)$.
For the slope, choose $m_\lambda=a-\sqrt\lambda$ and substitute
$s=m_\lambda+\lambda u$ in~\eqref{eq:Pprime}. This gives
\[
 P'(m_\lambda)=\frac1h\int_{-m_\lambda/\lambda}^{(a-m_\lambda)/\lambda}
 e^{m_\lambda+\lambda u}\ph(u)du.
\]
The lower endpoint tends to $-\infty$ and the upper endpoint to $+\infty$.
Extend the integrand by zero outside this interval. On its support,
$e^{m_\lambda+\lambda u}/h\leq e^a/h=M$, so $M\ph(u)$ is an
integrable dominator. Pointwise the extended integrand tends to
$M\ph(u)$. Dominated convergence gives $P'(m_\lambda)\to M$, proving
the matching lower limit for the supremum.
For the curvature, evaluate~\eqref{eq:ibp} at $m=a$ and apply
$|x-y|\geq |y|-|x|$ with $P'(a)\leq M$:
\[
 \lambda\|P''\|_\infty\geq
 M\ph(0)-\lambda M-\ph(a/\lambda)/h.
\]
The last two terms tend to zero. The matching upper bound proves the
second limit. These are fixed-cap limits; simultaneous vanishing of
$h$ and $\lambda$ requires another argument.
\end{proof}

This derivation explains why a $1/\lambda$ curvature bound is informative
in the weak-loading regime compared with the generic $1/\lambda^2$
bound. 

\section{A shared coordinate sensitivity bound}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:outer}
The continuously inner-averaged currency residual $g$ in
\eqref{eq:conditional} is coordinatewise Lipschitz with constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outerlip}
 L_j=\min\{K+H,HL_{h,\lambda}\}
       \{\max_i|R_{ij}|+|\overline R_j|\}.
\end{equation}
These constants may be used for the outer distribution transfer of all
three evaluator families, including direct evaluation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All $B_A$ terms are positive. Differentiation gives
$\partial_jm_A=\sum_i\alpha_i R_{ij}$, where
$\alpha_i=w_i e^{\mu_i+R_i\cdot Y}/B_A$ are positive and sum to one.
Hence $|\partial_jm_A|\leq\max_i|R_{ij}|$.
Also $\partial_jm_G=\overline R_j$. The chain rule and
Theorem~\ref{thm:derivative} prove~\eqref{eq:outerlip} with factor $HL$.

Independently, $x\mapsto F_H(K e^x)$ is globally $(K+H)$-Lipschitz.
It is absolutely continuous; its derivative is $K e^x$ when
$1<e^x<1+h$, and zero elsewhere almost everywhere, so its derivative
magnitude is at most $K(1+h)=K+H$. Integrating that derivative between
any two arguments proves the global claim, including both kink points.
Taking expectation over $Z$ retains this Lipschitz constant. Applying the
chain rule to the resulting smooth conditional function gives the second
factor in~\eqref{eq:outerlip}. The minimum of two valid constants is valid.

For direct evaluation I first transfer the outer law while the inner
$Z$ remains continuous, and then replace the inner law. The first target
is exactly $g$, so its outer constant is the same. Withholding this
bound from direct evaluation would introduce an artificial comparator
disadvantage. The argument uses independence of the coordinates and inner
factor, rather than any empirical sample sensitivity.
\end{proof}

\section{Gaussian midpoint-grid transfer with tails}
For $N=2^b$, define the exact midpoint grid
$z_k=\Phi^{-1}((k+1/2)/N)$, $0\leq k<N$. Couple a continuous normal
$Z=\Phi^{-1}(U)$ to $Z_N=z_{\lfloor NU\rfloor}$ using uniform $U$.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:coupling}
For $b\leq18$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupling}
 \E|Z-Z_N|\leq D_5(N):=5/N+2\ph(5)/26.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $Q(u)=\Phi^{-1}(u)$ and $Q_5(u)=\min\{\max\{Q(u),-5\},5\}$.
The midpoint quantiles lie strictly within $[-4.75,4.75]$ for $b\leq18$;
the retained independent CDF enclosure verifies
$\Phi(-4.75)<1/(2\,2^{18})$. Thus $Q_5$ and $Q$ agree at each midpoint.
On one cell $[v,v+1/N]$, an increasing function $q$ has midpoint-step
$L^1$ error at most
$(q(v+1/N)-q(v))/(2N)$: on each half-cell bound the deviation by its
corresponding endpoint-to-midpoint difference and multiply by $1/(2N)$.
Summing telescopes. Since the range of $Q_5$ is ten, this gives $5/N$.
Furthermore
$\E|Q(U)-Q_5(U)|=2\int_5^\infty(z-5)\ph(z)dz
=2\{\ph(5)-5\overline\Phi(5)\}$.

To bound this tail without an unproved approximation, integration by
parts gives, for $R>0$,
$\overline\Phi(R)=\ph(R)/R-\int_R^\infty\ph(z)/z^2\,dz
\geq\ph(R)/R-\overline\Phi(R)/R^2$.
Therefore $\overline\Phi(R)\geq R\ph(R)/(R^2+1)$ and the positive-part
tail is at most $2\ph(R)/(R^2+1)$. With $R=5$, the triangle inequality
proves~\eqref{eq:coupling}.
\end{proof}

Successively replacing independent outer coordinates gives currency error
at most $\sum_j L_jD_5(2^{b_j})$: at each replacement the pointwise
Lipschitz inequality bounds the coupled change, and expectation preserves
it. The finite fixed-point quantile register is different from this exact
grid. Its exhaustive inverse-CDF enclosure permits coordinate error
$\delta=2^{-q}$, which is charged to arithmetic propagation once.
I do not add that rounding error again to the exact-grid transfer.

\section{Bounded-variation alternatives and the error split}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bv}
If a function $f$ has total variation $V_f$ on $[0,1]$, midpoint quadrature
with $N$ equal cells satisfies
$|\int_0^1 f(u)du-N^{-1}\sum_k f((k+1/2)/N)|\leq V_f/(2N)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On either half of a cell, $|f(u)-f(c)|$ is bounded by the variation of
$f$ on that half, where $c$ is the midpoint. Integration over a half-cell
multiplies this bound by $1/(2N)$. Summing half-cell variations is at most
the total variation. This proves the claimed inequality, without requiring
smoothness or monotonicity of $f$.
\end{proof}

Fix all but one outer coordinate. The arithmetic basket as a function of
that coordinate is a positive sum of exponentials, and is convex. Its
derivative is increasing, so it decreases and then increases, allowing a
one-sided or empty branch. An increasing capped function of this basket
therefore has variation at most $2H$. The geometric basket is an exponential
of an affine function, so its capped payoff is monotone or constant and has
variation at most $H$. Their difference has variation at most $3H$.
Taking the inner expectation cannot increase this variation: for any
partition the triangle inequality bounds the sum of averaged increments
by the average of the sum. Monotone quantile composition cannot increase
variation either. Lemma~\ref{lem:bv} therefore gives $3H/(2N)$ per outer
coordinate for every method. I use the minimum of this bound and
\eqref{eq:outerlip}--\eqref{eq:coupling} coordinate by coordinate.
Each quantile-composed bounded-variation function is initially defined on
$(0,1)$. Its finite one-sided limits exist because its total variation is
finite. I extend it by those limits at zero and one before applying the
lemma. Endpoint values do not change the integral or midpoint sum.

For the direct inner factor, both capped payoffs are increasing in $Z$,
and each has variation at most $H$. The residual has variation at most
$2H$, giving inner midpoint error $H/N_Z$; alternatively its Lipschitz
constant is $2\lambda(K+H)$, giving
$2\lambda(K+H)D_5(N_Z)$. For the strike coordinate, each CDF in
\eqref{eq:interval} is decreasing in $U$ and has variation at most one.
The currency integrand has variation at most $2H$, giving $H/N_U$.
Full conditioning has no inner quadrature error.

The frozen study reserves $0.4\epsilon-10^{-12}$ for distribution transfer,
$0.1\epsilon$ for arithmetic and $10^{-12}$ for the geometric correction,
with $\epsilon=0.01$. Hence deterministic undiscounted bias is at most
$0.5\epsilon$. Discounting cannot increase it because $r>0$.
In the original 108-row evaluator-only study, the remaining
$0.5\epsilon$ was reserved for an unimplemented statistical estimator.
Section~\ref{sec:estimator-coverage} now supplies the confidence proof
and Section~\ref{sec:estimator-budget} its finite resource cap.
No observed standard deviation is used to supply that guarantee.

\section{Linear interpolation and complete rounding rules}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:interpolation}
Let a twice continuously differentiable function $f$ on a cell of width
$s$ satisfy $|f''|\leq C$, and let its computed endpoint centres have
absolute errors at most $\eta$. Nearest fixed-point rounding with step
$\delta$ and integer linear interpolation with downward product truncation
gives error at the realised argument at most
$Cs^2/8+\eta+3\delta/2$. If the input argument has error $e$ and
$|f'|\leq L$ on the interval containing both arguments, total error is at
most $Le+Cs^2/8+\eta+3\delta/2$.
For the unsigned emitter I additionally require increasing nonnegative
rounded endpoints, an exact nonnegative stored endpoint difference, and
safe product and output ranges. These are verified premises of the
retained tables, not consequences of twice differentiability alone.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\ell$ be the exact endpoint chord on $[a,b]$. For an interior $x$,
subtract the suitable multiple of $(t-a)(t-b)$ from $f(t)-\ell(t)$ so
that it also vanishes at $x$. Two applications of Rolle's theorem give
$f(x)-\ell(x)=f''(\xi)(x-a)(x-b)/2$ for some $\xi\in(a,b)$.
Since $(x-a)(b-x)\leq(b-a)^2/4$, the chord error is at most $Cs^2/8$.
Each rounded ordinate differs from the true ordinate by at most
$\eta+\delta/2$. The interpolated chord is a convex combination of the
two endpoints, so this is also its perturbation bound; there is no need
to charge the slope difference independently. The integer product
truncation introduces an additional error strictly below $\delta$.
Finally the mean-value theorem bounds the exact-function argument
perturbation by $Le$. Combining these inequalities proves the result.
\end{proof}

The implemented unsigned arithmetic is analysed before modular reduction.
For nonnegative ideal operands $a,b$ with errors $e_a,e_b$, truncating a
product to $q$ fractional bits gives error at most
$|a|e_b+|b|e_a+e_ae_b+\delta$. The cross term is retained. A constant
multiplier $k$ rounded to error $e_k\leq\delta/2$ gives
$|k|e_a+|a|e_k+e_ae_k+\delta$; for an integer multiplier, $e_k=0$ and
there is no fractional truncation. Addition and subtraction introduce no
rounding at a common scale and add operand error bounds. Shifting down by
$t$ gives error at most $2^{-t}e_a+\delta$.

Projection $C_c(x)=\min\{\max\{x,0\},c\}$ is 1-Lipschitz in $x$ and
changes by at most $|c-c'|$ when its cap changes. Thus the error of a
clamp with rounded cap is at most the input error plus $\delta/2$.
If the cap is exactly representable, this extra allowance is conservative.
Endpoint range enclosures and these rules are propagated through every
graph node. At each function call, the perturbed input must lie strictly
inside its declared address domain; the derivative interval includes both
arguments and the complete interpolation cell. Every signed mathematical
intermediate and its error enclosure must have magnitude below
$2^{w-q-1}=64$, since $w=q+7$. Unsigned multiplicands must represent
nonnegative values. These checks prevent a modular wrap from being
misread as a signed value or lookup index. The physically clipped output
and its subsequent classical scale have separate range checks.
Ideal nonnegativity alone is insufficient for an unsigned multiplication:
I require either a nonnegative lower enclosure for the implemented operand
or structural nonnegativity, such as a physically clamped register.
In the selected-premise audit the only lower enclosures below zero occur
at the 27 direct residual clamps, whose implemented outputs are
nonnegative by construction.

I use 70-digit decimal centres with explicit numerical envelopes and one
outward binary rounding when recording final bounds. These are finite
numerical certificates for the declared domains, not a general interval
arithmetic library or a proof of a transcendental library implementation.
The ordinary binary-float values in \texttt{domain\_checks} are diagnostic
summaries, rather than outward-rounded interval endpoints. The independent
100-digit recurrence checks the domains and budgets separately.
Python Decimal's correctly rounded exponential, logarithm and square-root

check the retained generated endpoints and quantiles. No sampled error

\section{Tail clipping and constrained table generation}
The probability table domain is $m\in[-2,2]$, with
$0.05\leq\lambda\leq0.4$ and $0.01\leq h\leq0.25$.
The four-CDF formula can have arguments outside $[-8,8]$. I use a
70-digit CDF series inside that interval, set the centre to zero for
arguments at most $-8$, and to one for arguments at least $8$.
Mills' upper inequality follows from
$\int_8^\infty\ph(t)dt\leq\int_8^\infty(t/8)\ph(t)dt=\ph(8)/8$;
the clipping error is below $6.4\times10^{-16}$.

The series used inside the interval is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(x)=\tfrac12+\ph(0)\sum_{n=0}^\infty
 \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{2^n n!(2n+1)}.
\end{equation}
It follows by uniformly integrating the exponential power series on the
compact interval between zero and $x$. The absolute term ratio is
$x^2(2n+1)/(2(n+1)(2n+3))$, eventually below one. The retained generator
stops after this decreasing regime with last term below $10^{-50}$, so
the first omitted term bounds the alternating remainder. For $|x|\leq8$,
the absolute sum is at most $8e^{32}<10^{15}$ and the 250th term has
magnitude below $10^{-118}$; fewer than 250 recurrences suffice. Machin's
identity $\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$, with alternating remainders,
supplies the normalising constant. Under 70 significant decimal digits,
each rounded arithmetic operation has relative error at most
$u=5\times10^{-70}$. A product of at most 4,000 such rounding factors
has relative error at most $\gamma_{4000}=4000u/(1-4000u)
<2.01\times10^{-66}$; applying this bound termwise
to the recurrence and sum, together with the absolute-sum bound and the
Machin remainder, gives recurrence and summation error below
$1.3\times10^{-51}$. The Machin-derived $\ph(0)$ error is below
$10^{-64}$; multiplying it by the absolute-term sum and adding the
alternating remainder gives total interior CDF error below $10^{-48}$.
The generator uses this
deliberately enlarged $10^{-38}$ interior CDF envelope. The independent
100-digit calculation uses a different recurrence and stopping rule.

In~\eqref{eq:fourcdf}, $B=e^{m+\lambda^2/2}\leq e^{2.08}$.
If each CDF error is at most $\eta_\Phi$, its combined contribution to
the normalised kernel error is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen}
 \frac{(2B+2+h)\eta_\Phi}{h}.
\end{equation}
This follows by taking absolute values of the four coefficients; the
coefficient of the second unshifted CDF is $1+h$, rather than discarding
its $h$ term. With $\eta_\Phi=6.4\times10^{-16}$, replacing $B$ by
$e^{2.08}$, $h$ in the numerator by $0.25$ and the denominator by $0.01$
gives less than $1.17\times10^{-12}$.
For the remaining decimal computations, all log arguments are in
$[1.01,1.25]$, divisions have denominator at least $0.05$ or $0.01$,
and $|d_0|,|d_1|<45$. Correctly rounded logarithms, exponentials and
the finite rational arithmetic give argument perturbations below
$10^{-60}$ and normalised value perturbations below $10^{-55}$ under
the same numerical assumptions. The generous
$\eta_P=2\times10^{-12}$ covers these terms and~\eqref{eq:gen}.
I do not reuse a $10^{-34}$ allowance after tail clipping.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:repair}
Let exact increasing functions $p,s$ satisfy $0\leq p\leq s\leq1$ at
ordered endpoints $x_j$. Suppose centres $\widehat p_j,\widehat s_j$
have uniform errors at most $\eta_p,\eta_s$, with $\eta_s\leq\eta_p$.
Clip their centres to $[0,1]$, then form
$v_j=\max_{i\leq j}\min\{\widehat p_i,\widehat s_i\}$ and
$t_j=\max_{i\leq j}\widehat s_i$.
Then $v_j\leq t_j$, both sequences are increasing,
$|v_j-p(x_j)|\leq\eta_p$ and $|t_j-s(x_j)|\leq\eta_s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Projection to $[0,1]$ cannot increase error because both exact values lie
there. For $i\leq j$, $\widehat p_i\leq p(x_i)+\eta_p
\leq p(x_j)+\eta_p$, proving the upper bound on $v_j$.
The current endpoint has
$\widehat p_j\geq p(x_j)-\eta_p$ and
$\widehat s_j\geq s(x_j)-\eta_s\geq p(x_j)-\eta_p$.
Their minimum supplies the lower bound. The same prefix-maximum argument
applied to $s$ proves its enclosure. Order follows because each minimum
is no greater than its corresponding $\widehat s_i$ and prefix maxima
are increasing. Nearest rounding is an increasing map, so it retains
both integer order relations and adds at most $\delta/2$ to each endpoint
enclosure.
\end{proof}

The actual CDF generator needs no extra projection to $[0,1]$. Inside
$|x|<8$, its exact value lies between $\Phi(-8)$ and $1-\Phi(-8)$.
The previously proved Mills lower bound gives
$\Phi(-8)\geq8\ph(8)/65>6.2\times10^{-16}$, which exceeds the
$10^{-38}$ interior envelope. Thus its centre already lies in $[0,1]$;
outside this interval it returns exactly zero or one.

I apply the lemma with $p=P$ and $s=\Psi$. It remains valid if the upper
sequence is prefix-maximised before the pointwise minimum. The actual
generator projects each cap centre below its same-endpoint CDF centre
and takes a prefix maximum. Its table rows store the rounded left endpoint
and nonnegative rounded endpoint difference. An independently enclosed
exact endpoint need not have a uniquely determined nearest integer if its
enclosure crosses a half-ulp. The certificate requires only absolute error
at most $\eta_P+\delta/2$, and does not assert unique exact rounding.

\section{Coherent evaluation and Born probabilities}
Every X, CNOT and exact Toffoli is a permutation of computational-basis
states, hence a unitary with no input-dependent phase. Clean arithmetic
computes a result, copies it into a previously zero output register, and
reverses its complete body. Copying a computational-basis result is CNOT,
so on a superposition the procedure produces the coherent correlated
output by linearity. It does not clone arbitrary quantum states.

Let the total uniformly prepared financial input bits be $B$, and let
$a(x)\in\{0,\ldots,2^q\}$ be the implemented normalised output integer.
Prepare an independent $q$-bit comparison integer $J$ by Hadamards. The
clean comparator flips a reserved flag when $J<a(x)$. It preserves its
inputs and clears its own work. Reversing the financial body then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary}
 |x\rangle|J\rangle|0_{\rm work}\rangle|0\rangle
 \longmapsto
 |x\rangle|J\rangle|0_{\rm work}\rangle|\ind\{J<a(x)\}\rangle.
\end{equation}
There are exactly $a(x)$ good comparison integers, including the boundary
cases $a(x)=0$ and $a(x)=2^q$. Orthogonality of distinct $(x,J)$ basis
states and the Born rule therefore give
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(\text{flag}=1)=2^{-B}\sum_x\frac{a(x)}{2^q}.
\end{equation}
This is an exact finite quantum probability. It is distinct from the
continuous price, whose deterministic bias is bounded separately.
Small complete gate tapes verify basis mappings and zero work; they do not
constitute exhaustive production-scale circuit simulation.
The counted Grover iterate uses two clean evaluators, uniform preparation
and inverse, the good-flag phase and an explicit zero reflection on the
invariant input/comparison/flag subspace. These are the historical
108-row evaluator-only counts. Sections~\ref{sec:estimator-coverage}
and~\ref{sec:estimator-budget} subsequently supply the repetition
schedule and confidence proof. The historical seven-T, three-layer
assignment is retained as a declared profile \citep{selinger};
Section~\ref{sec:estimator-resources} separately emits and proves the
actual seven-T expansion and retains its conservative five-layer bound.
No optimal fault-tolerant resource claim follows.

\section{Exact interpolation cost and a coarsening criterion}\label{sec:counts}
I now analyse the precise primitive emitter. Inputs and generated carry
wires are distinct, constants are represented as registers, and there is
no constant folding of zero data bits into gates. Non-identical intended
constants do not alias after rounded deduplication in the audited family.
These assumptions matter for exact count equalities.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:block}
Let $w$ be the endpoint register width, $b$ the table address width,
$S$ the power-of-two input span and $f=q+\log_2 S-b>0$ the remainder
width. Let $I_b$ be the number of nonempty internal selector nodes visited
by the emitter for the actual packed table. One forward interpolation
block emits exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 T_{\rm ff}(b)=2I_b+2f(4w+3f)+6w
\end{equation}
Toffoli gates. For the full residual's two probability calls and complete
body inverse, holding all other nodes and $q,w$ fixed, changing $b$ to
$b-1$ saves exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saving}
 4\{2(I_b-I_{b-1})-(8w+12f+6)\}
\end{equation}
Toffolis. The assigned T saving is seven times this quantity and is
strictly positive precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
I_b-I_{b-1}>4w+6f+3.
\end{equation}
The widths are integers with $w\geq1$, $b\geq1$, $f\geq1$, $f+b\leq w$,
and every packed word fits in $2w$ bits. For coarsening I require $b\geq2$
and the corresponding valid premises at both widths.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The selector emitter visits each nonempty internal binary-tree node once,
computes its conjunction flag with one Toffoli and clears it with one
Toffoli. Leaf ordinate copying uses CNOT gates only. Thus the lookup
uses exactly $2I_b$ Toffolis.

The ripple construction uses three Toffolis per output carry: for bits
$a,b,c$, their majority is $ab\oplus ac\oplus bc$. If zero or one bit
is one, this expression is zero; if two are one, exactly one product is
one; if three are one, three products have odd parity. Hence it is the
carry bit and emits exactly three Toffolis. A length-$L$ ripple has $3L$
Toffolis. Clean out-of-place addition computes that ripple, copies with
CNOTs, and reverses it, giving $6w$ Toffolis at width $w$.

For a variable multiply with a $w$-bit endpoint difference and $f$-bit
remainder, the constructor has $f$ rows. Each row creates $w$ partial
product bits, using $w$ Toffolis, then adds its shifted row to a
$(w+f)$-bit accumulator with a raw ripple of $3(w+f)$ Toffolis.
The raw multiply therefore uses $f\{w+3(w+f)\}=f(4w+3f)$.
Output truncation copies selected product bits and does not remove raw
rows. The clean inverse doubles the count to $2f(4w+3f)$.
Adding the lookup and final clean addition proves~\eqref{eq:F}.

Coarsening by one address bit increases $f$ by one. The multiplier
difference is
\[
 2(f+1)\{4w+3(f+1)\}-2f(4w+3f)=8w+12f+6.
\]
All other forward nodes and the comparator are unchanged. There are two
probability calls in the body and two in its inverse, so multiplying the
single-block difference by four gives~\eqref{eq:saving}.
Rearranging its strict positivity gives~\eqref{eq:criterion}.
\end{proof}

For a dense selector tree $I_b=2^b-1$ and
$I_b-I_{b-1}=2^{b-1}$. Actual probability tails can prune rows, so I use
the emitted tree, not this dense approximation. A tighter curvature bound
can make $b-1$ feasible while violating~\eqref{eq:criterion}; then its
coarser table increases complete flag cost. At $q=18,w=25,b=6$, three
retained two-asset checks have increased flag counts of 1,352, 1,304 and
1,248 Toffolis upon coarsening. These examples are cost counterexamples,
not monetary-target configurations.

For an unchanged full-conditioning prefix with argument errors $e_A,e_G$,
the scalar propagation certificate has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta_b=H\left[L(e_A+e_G)+\frac{CS^2}{4\,4^b}
            +\frac72\delta+2\eta_P+\eta_{\rm rat}\right],
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\rm rat}$ encloses the remaining explicitly charged decimal
bound-operation rounding. The two calls each contribute
$Cs^2/8+3\delta/2+\eta_P$; exact subtraction adds operand errors and a
small envelope, and the final rounded-cap clamp is charged $\delta/2$.
This derives every term of~\eqref{eq:beta} rather than fitting it to a
reported cost. For arithmetic budget $\epsilon_A$, define
$D=\epsilon_A/H-L(e_A+e_G)-7\delta/2-2\eta_P-\eta_{\rm rat}$.
If $D\leq0$, no finite table width can satisfy this certificate when
$C>0$. If $D>0$, feasibility is precisely
$4^b\geq CS^2/(4D)$ for this bound. Integer widths and declared domain
checks remain compulsory; a real-valued logarithm estimate is insufficient.
Replacing generic $(L,C)$ by cap-aware constants can cross this dyadic
threshold. A certified cost saving additionally requires
\eqref{eq:criterion}. These are distinct mathematical conditions.

\section{Matched interval and full selector counts}
For identical $q$, prefix and probability address width, the repaired
integer full-kernel endpoint is no greater than the CDF endpoint. A packed
monotone row is zero exactly when both its left endpoint and its endpoint
difference are zero, equivalently when its right endpoint is zero.
If a CDF row is zero, its right endpoint is zero and the ordered full
endpoint is also zero; monotonicity then makes the full row zero.
Thus full nonzero-row support is contained in CDF nonzero-row support,
and every visited full internal selector node is visited by the CDF tree.
Hence $I_P\leq I_\Psi$. This support argument does not compare CNOT
popcounts or other lookup architectures.

The current interval graph has one extra strike-log interpolation block,
two extra probability-argument subtractions and the same final residual
subtraction. A clean subtraction at width $w$ has $6w$ Toffolis, since
its borrow-majority raw ripple also has $3w$ and is reversed. If
$T_{\rm strike}$ is the forward strike-log block count and $F_\Psi,F_P$
the respective forward probability block counts, complete flag counts obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervalgap}
 T^{\rm interval}_{\rm ff}-T^{\rm full}_{\rm ff}
 =2T_{\rm strike}+24w+4(F_\Psi-F_P).
\end{equation}
The first two terms account for the body and its inverse. The probability
block arithmetic is identical at matched widths and spans, so
$F_\Psi-F_P=2(I_\Psi-I_P)\geq0$. The uniform strike input uses Clifford
preparation and no Toffolis; its reflection cost is counted separately.
Equation~\eqref{eq:intervalgap} proves a matched-constructor count statement,
not a universal ranking after independently choosing different precisions.
The historical shortlist in fact retains an interval winner in one case;
the subsequently inherited opponent pool tests that search limitation.

\section{Retained numerical findings and interpretation}
The immutable financial protocol contains nine frozen models, three caps
and four methods. I retain every case. A shared outer sensitivity certificate
was added after the preliminary smoke run and before the complete study;
the financial cases were unchanged and the improvement was applied to all
opponents. This is a transparent algorithm amendment, not a claim that every
proof and implementation detail was frozen before any diagnostic.
For each method, I greedily refine table widths at each $q\in\{18,\ldots,30\}$,
exactly count the two proxy-shortlisted feasible candidates, and retain the
cheapest emitted candidate. The cap-aware search also retains the global
winner as a feasible candidate. This search is not an exhaustive circuit
optimisation. Inheritance of the global winner ensures weak noninferiority
within the retained candidates; it does not prove strict improvement or
global optimality.

The cap-aware full evaluator has lower T count in exactly three of 27 cases,
all with $\lambda=0.05,H=25$. The reductions relative to the independently
selected generic full evaluator are 13.108, 3.870 and 1.033 per cent for
two, four and eight assets. At fixed generic prefix, the minimum feasible
probability address width falls from 13 to 12 in these same cases; emitted
T savings are 116,816, 116,424 and 116,032. All other 24 cases retain zero
saving. Direct evaluation is cheapest in three cases and interval
conditioning in one. Full cap-aware conditioning is cheapest in the other
23, including ties with its generic counterpart. In the eight-asset weak
loading, largest-cap case, the cap-aware saving does not beat direct
evaluation. I cannot infer universal conditioning efficiency from these data.

Independent exact rational model checks, endpoint and quantile enclosures,
production-grid replay diagnostics, small complete gate tapes and classical
positive-integral references support the retained evidence. Shifted-Sobol
replicate dispersion and quadrature refinements are numerical diagnostics,
not rigorous confidence or integration-error guarantees. The proof establishes
a conditional constructor-specific precision consequence; the experiments
establish its observed extent in this synthetic family. QSP, piecewise QSVT and
arithmetic-free preparation remain stronger unimplemented architecture
comparisons \citep{stamatopoulos,obrien,mcardle}. The later sections
implement a pruned unary traversal following \citet{babbush}, with a
four-T logical AND and corrected measurement erasure following
\citet{gidney}.
The original positive literature contribution is therefore still a candidate,
requiring a tighter source-level distinction and a complete comparison
with stronger classical and quantum estimators before an algorithm-level
superiority claim is justified.
In a separately labelled post-study diagnostic, replacing each of the
27 interval prefixes by a full kernel at identical $q$, Gaussian width
and function-address widths remains certified and is strictly cheaper.
For the apparent eight-asset interval winner at $\lambda=0.4,H=25$, the
matched full evaluator uses 17,363,612 T gates against 17,547,124 for
interval conditioning. Its count is below the original retained full

\section{An a priori financial certificate for the implemented amplitude}
The geometric-control context has established average-option antecedents
\citep{kemna}; variance-aware quantum financial estimation is established
\citep{rebentrost}. The following inequalities provide the finite-input certificate required by the implementation.

\label{sec:prior}
I now use a model-derived residual-mean certificate to parameterise statistical
estimation. This uses established positive-control, lognormal-moment and
concentration arguments. I give its derivation for the frozen model rather
than claiming a new quantum theorem from those ingredients.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:meanprior}
Let $D_R=\sum_i w_i\|R_i\|^2-\|\sum_iw_iR_i\|^2$ for the model in
\eqref{eq:model}. Then $D_R\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meanprior}
 0\leq\E[F_H(A)-F_H(G)]\leq
 \min\{H,B_R\},\qquad
 B_R=K e^{r-d}\{1-e^{-D_R/2}\}.
\end{equation}
Define additionally
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J0}
 J_0=\log\sum_i w_i\exp\{\mu_i-\overline\mu+
                  \tfrac12\|R_i-\overline R\|^2\}
 =\tfrac12 D_R+\log\sum_iw_i e^{-(R_i-\overline R)\cdot\overline R}.
\end{equation}
Then the stronger cap-dependent mean bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:combinedmeanprior}
 \E[F_H(A)-F_H(G)]\leq B_*:=\min\{H,B_R,HL_{h,\lambda}J_0\}.
\end{equation}
If a retained implemented normalised residual has mean $p$ and its
undiscounted residual bias certificate is $\beta_R$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteprior}
 0\leq p\leq u:=\min\{1,(B_*+\beta_R)/H\}.
\end{equation}
For its finite fractional output $X\in[0,1]$, and also for a Bernoulli good
flag with the same mean, a variance certificate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:varprior}
 v_u=\begin{cases}u(1-u),&u\leq1/2,\\1/4,&u>1/2.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expansion gives
$D_R=\sum_iw_i\|R_i-\overline R\|^2\geq0$, where
$\overline R=\sum_iw_iR_i$. The function $F_H$ is increasing and
1-Lipschitz, and $A\geq G$. Therefore
$0\leq F_H(A)-F_H(G)\leq\min\{H,A-G\}$ pointwise.
The risk-neutral drift identity gives $\E A=K e^{r-d}$.
The geometric log mean is
$\overline\mu=r-d-(\lambda^2+\sum_iw_i\|R_i\|^2)/2$,
and its log variance is $v_G=\lambda^2+\|\overline R\|^2$.
Consequently $\E G=K e^{\overline\mu+v_G/2}
=K e^{r-d-D_R/2}$. Taking expectations of the pointwise bounds proves
\eqref{eq:meanprior}. This cancellation explains why the mean bound
does not depend on the common loading $\lambda$ within this stipulated
model, although the payoff distribution does.

For the second bound let $J(Y)=m_A(Y)-m_G(Y)\geq0$.
Since $P'\leq L_{h,\lambda}$, the conditional residual is at most
$HL_{h,\lambda}J(Y)$ pointwise. Write
$J(Y)=\log\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i-\overline\mu+(R_i-\overline R)\cdot Y}$.
The sum inside the logarithm has finite expectation by Gaussian moments.
Also $0\leq J(Y)$, and Jensen's inequality for the concave logarithm
gives $\E J(Y)\leq J_0$. More formally, applying Jensen first to a
truncation and using integrability follows from the upper bound
$J(Y)\leq\max_i\{|\mu_i-\overline\mu|+
\|R_i-\overline R\|\|Y\|\}$ and the finite normal first moment.
The drift identity simplifies the exponent in the Gaussian expectation
to $D_R/2-(R_i-\overline R)\cdot\overline R$, proving the equality
in~\eqref{eq:J0}. Taking expectations and combining the three valid
mean bounds proves~\eqref{eq:combinedmeanprior}. The positivity of
$J_0$ also follows because $\sum_i w_i(R_i-\overline R)=0$ and convexity
of the exponential gives $\sum_iw_i e^{-(R_i-\overline R)\cdot\overline R}
\geq1$.

Write $\mu_R=\E[F_H(A)-F_H(G)]$. The definition of residual bias gives
$|Hp-\mu_R|\leq\beta_R$, so $Hp\leq B_*+\beta_R$.
The implemented clamp supplies $p\in[0,1]$, proving
\eqref{eq:finiteprior}. The residual bias contains distribution and
arithmetic errors, but not the offline geometric correction, because the
correction does not enter $p$.
For $0\leq X\leq1$, $X^2\leq X$, hence
$\operatorname{Var}(X)\leq p-p^2=p(1-p)$.
The function $p(1-p)$ increases on $[0,1/2]$ and is globally at most
$1/4$, which gives~\eqref{eq:varprior}. Equality of the Bernoulli
variance with $p(1-p)$ follows by $X^2=X$ for a good-flag measurement.
\end{proof}

I compute $D_R$ from the exact fractions represented by the frozen decimal
loadings and verify the stored drift identities with fractions. The
220-digit correctly rounded exponential calculation is charged an absolute
currency envelope of $10^{-100}$, much larger than the operation error
under the declared bounded operands and numerical assumptions. For the
matched family $B_R=1.129938615958719\ldots$ currency units, with the tiny
frozen-dispersion discrepancy retained. I also retain $J_0$ and
$HL_{h,\lambda}J_0$ and use their cap-dependent minimum with $H$ and $B_R$.
The density constant is computed at 220 digits with Machin's alternating
arctangent series, charged within the same numerical envelope. I add the exact binary values of the
outward-recorded arithmetic and distribution bounds before dividing by $H$.
The resulting finite-amplitude prior is not an observed pilot mean, an
empirical variance estimate or a prior belief about a market probability.
It is a deterministic consequence of this stipulated model and its retained
bias certificates.

\section{A rigorous classical comparator at the same statistical target}
\label{sec:classicalconfidence}
I retain both a variance-sensitive concentration bound and a
distribution-free bounded-output bound, using the smaller certified sample
budget. The classical samples are independent evaluations of the same
finite fractional output $X$, with the same deterministic bias. This
comparison counts function evaluations; a classical function evaluation
is not assigned the physical cost of a logical T gate.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bernstein}
For independent copies $X_1,\ldots,X_N\in[0,1]$ with mean $p$ and variance
at most $v$, and for $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernstein}
 \Pr\{|N^{-1}\sum_iX_i-p|\geq t\}
 \leq 2\exp\left\{-\frac{Nt^2}{2v+2t/3}\right\}.
\end{equation}
If $v=0$, the sample mean equals $p$ almost surely and no positive-error
failure is possible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $Y=X-p$. Then $\E Y=0$, $|Y|\leq1$ and
$\E|Y|^k\leq\E Y^2\leq v$ for every integer $k\geq2$.
Induction gives $k!\geq2\,3^{k-2}$: it is equality at $k=2,3$, and
multiplication by $k+1\geq3$ preserves the inequality. For $0<s<3$,
absolute convergence of the exponential series and boundedness of $Y$
justify expectation term by term. Thus
\[
 \E e^{sY}\leq1+v\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{s^k}{k!}
 \leq1+\frac{vs^2}{2(1-s/3)}
 \leq\exp\left\{\frac{vs^2}{2(1-s/3)}\right\}.
\]
Independence multiplies the moment-generating functions. Markov's
inequality then gives upper-tail probability at most
$\exp\{-Nst+Nvs^2/[2(1-s/3)]\}$.
For $v>0$, choose $s=t/(v+t/3)<3$. Substitution gives exponent
$-Nt^2/[2(v+t/3)]$. Apply the same argument to $-Y$ for the lower tail
and add the two probabilities. If $v=0$, $\E Y^2=0$ implies $Y=0$
almost surely, proving the separate statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hoeffding}
Under the same bounded-output and independence assumptions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hoeffding}
 \Pr\{|N^{-1}\sum_iX_i-p|\geq t\}\leq2e^{-2Nt^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one centred variable $Y=X-p$, let $\psi(s)=\log\E e^{sY}$.
Its first two derivatives exist for all real $s$ because $Y$ is bounded.
Exponential tilting preserves the support $X\in[0,1]$, and
$\psi''(s)$ is the variance under the tilted probability measure.
Under any such measure, $\operatorname{Var}(X)\leq\E X(1-\E X)\leq1/4$.
Since $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$, integrating twice gives
$\psi(s)\leq s^2/8$ for every real $s$.
For $s>0$, independence and Markov's inequality therefore give the
upper tail at most $e^{-Nst+Ns^2/8}$. Choosing $s=4t$ gives
$e^{-2Nt^2}$. Applying the same proof to $-Y$ and adding tails proves
the result.
\end{proof}

For failure probability $\alpha=0.01$ and exact statistical currency
allowance $\epsilon_S=0.005$, I set $t=\epsilon_S/H$ and retain
\begin{align}
N_B&=\left\lceil\frac{(2v_u+2t/3)\log(2/\alpha)}{t^2}\right\rceil,\\
N_H&=\left\lceil\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{2t^2}\right\rceil,\qquad
N_C=\min\{N_B,N_H\}.
\end{align}
I use the inequality corresponding to the chosen integer budget; taking
the minimum does not combine two failure events. If the deterministic
price-bias bound is $\beta\leq0.005$, then with probability at least
$1-\alpha$, the estimate obtained by adding the retained geometric
correction to $H\overline X$ has undiscounted error at most
$\beta+\epsilon_S\leq0.01$. Multiplication by $e^{-0.03}\leq1$ preserves
that upper bound. These are mathematical confidence statements under
independent exact draws, rather than confidence inferred from a realised
pilot standard deviation. The quantum estimator is required to meet the
same $\epsilon_S$ and $\alpha$.


\section{A phase-correct logical lookup instrument}
\label{sec:lookupinstrument}
I use an explicit clean-target logical-AND isometry. In chronological
order its gates on controls $a,b$ and initially zero target $t$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:andsequence}
 H_t,\ T_t,\ \operatorname{CX}_{b,t},\ T_t^\dagger,
 \operatorname{CX}_{a,t},\ T_t,\ \operatorname{CX}_{b,t},
 T_t^\dagger,\ H_t,\ S_t^\dagger.
\end{equation}
There are four T or adjoint-T gates. The final $S^\dagger$ is essential
to the clean-target phase. This is not a four-T full Toffoli on an
arbitrary dirty target. Let $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$. For control values
$(a,b)=(0,0)$ or $(0,1)$, the middle target action between the Hadamards
is identity; for $(1,0)$ it is X. Therefore the full sequence maps
$|0\rangle_t$ to $|0\rangle_t$ without phase in all three cases, since
$H I H=I$ and $H X H=Z$. For $(1,1)$, the successive target vectors,
omitting their common $1/\sqrt2$ factor after the first Hadamard, are
\[
 (1,1),\ (1,\omega),\ (\omega,1),\ (\omega,\omega^{-1}),\
 (\omega^{-1},\omega),\ (\omega^{-1},i),\ (i,\omega^{-1}),\ (i,-i).
\]
The last Hadamard produces $i|1\rangle$, and $S^\dagger$ removes that
phase. Thus for all computational control pairs
$|a,b,0\rangle\mapsto|a,b,ab\rangle$ with identical phase. Linearity
proves the isometry on coherent and externally entangled control states.
Its literal inverse after unchanged controls and target $ab$ costs four
additional T gates and resets the target exactly.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:instrument}
Suppose a computed target $t=ab$ is erased after intervening operations
that preserve $a,b$ and restore $t$ to $ab$. Those operations may act on
arbitrary dirty data registers coherently. Apply $H_t$, measure t in the
computational basis with outcome $z$, apply $\operatorname{CZ}_{a,b}$
if $z=1$, then apply $X_t$ if $z=1$. Each corrected Kraus branch is
$2^{-1/2}$ times the same logical data operation with clean t. Its
outcome probability is $1/2$, independent of the logical input, including
inputs entangled with an external reference.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Immediately before erasure, write the general state as
$\sum_{a,b}|a,b,ab\rangle_t|\chi_{ab}\rangle$, where the unnormalised
vectors $|\chi_{ab}\rangle$ may include arbitrary data and a reference.
Hadamard measurement with outcome z multiplies the $(a,b)$ component by
$2^{-1/2}(-1)^{zab}$ and sets t to $|z\rangle$.
The conditional CZ supplies $(-1)^{zab}$ again, cancelling the phase
exactly. The conditional X resets t to zero. The branch state is therefore
$2^{-1/2}\sum_{a,b}|a,b,0\rangle|\chi_{ab}\rangle$.
The preserved computational controls are orthogonal, so the branch norm
squared is one half of the premeasurement norm squared. The same formula
holds with any reference, establishing equality of the logical channel
rather than mere equality of classical output distributions.
\end{proof}

In the emitted selector tree, the child initially stores the conjunction
of its incoming prefix and address bit. The positive subtree reads that
child, then a CNOT from the prefix changes it to the negative-branch
prefix. After the negative subtree a second CNOT restores the original
conjunction. Addresses and incoming prefixes are preserved. Induction
from leaf CNOT word fanout to the root therefore supplies the premises
of Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument}. A coherent enable control can serve
as the root without altering the argument. Every lookup branch has the
same input-independent scalar $2^{-I_b/2}$ and the same logical XOR
lookup on an arbitrary dirty output. The classical record is independent
of the logical input and leaks no address information. The primitive
conditional Clifford operations and reset remain counted.

The XOR lookup's logical inverse is the same XOR map with fresh clean
selectors. In the measurement-assisted mode I invoke a fresh corrected
instrument; I do not reverse measurements or their recorded outcomes.
Composing branchwise scalar-times-unitary instruments gives the exact
logical unitary channel on the retained clean-work subspace, including
its action on entangled states. This is the premise needed to substitute
the lookup into a Grover preparation and its logical adjoint. It is an
ideal logical statement under exact gates, measurement and feedback;
physical error and feedback latency require a separate implementation
model. The published temporary-AND and QROM methods supply the antecedents
\citep{gidney,babbush}; I claim no new four-T AND or universal lookup scheme.

\section{Precision consequences under stronger lookup architectures}
\label{sec:architectureprecision}
The exact-seven-T selector pair in Section~\ref{sec:counts} is not the
strongest published lookup implementation. Unary-iteration QROM and temporary
logical ANDs provide the relevant antecedents \citep{babbush,gidney}.
I keep three separately instantiated modes: the exact-Toffoli pair, a
clean-target four-T AND followed by its literal four-T unitary inverse, and
a clean-target four-T AND erased by an X-basis measurement and conditional
Clifford correction. The last mode is a quantum instrument. It cannot be
obtained by assigning four T gates to every Toffoli in the old Boolean tape.
The arithmetic multipliers and adders still use the declared exact
seven-T Toffoli construction.

Let $\kappa$ be the lookup T count per visited internal selector node:
$\kappa=14$ for an exact-seven-T compute/inverse pair, $\kappa=8$ for
the clean-target unitary alternative, and $\kappa=4$ for the corrected
measurement-assisted alternative. These coefficients apply only after
the primitive gate sequence and its clean-target or corrected-channel
premises have been checked. The same table support, word fanout and visited
node count $I_b$ are retained, not replaced by an assumed smaller sparse
index domain. Conditional Clifford operations, measurements and resets
are separately charged.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:architectureprecision}
For the specified emitted interpolation block under such a proved lookup
mode, the forward T count is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:architectureblock}
 C_b=\kappa I_b+7\{2f(4w+3f)+6w\}.
\end{equation}
At fixed full-conditioning prefix and $q$, valid one-bit coarsening saves
complete flag T gates exactly by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:architecturesaving}
 4\{\kappa(I_b-I_{b-1})-7(8w+12f+6)\}.
\end{equation}
Thus strict T saving requires
$\kappa(I_b-I_{b-1})>7(8w+12f+6)$, in addition to the monetary
certificate and domain feasibility.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lookup's gate plan supplies $\kappa I_b$ T gates. Its fanout is
Clifford, independent of whether the output target is clean or dirty.
The multiply and addition counts are exactly those proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:block}, multiplied by seven for their exact-Toffoli
decomposition. The complete flag has two probability blocks and their
logical inverses. The inverse of a measurement-assisted XOR lookup is a
fresh corrected instrument realising the same logical XOR map; it has
the same gate count, rather than a reversed measurement tape. The clean
target unitary implementation also supplies its literal inverse on its
declared subspace. Subtracting the two width counts and multiplying by
four proves~\eqref{eq:architecturesaving}. The sign condition is its
algebraic rearrangement.
\end{proof}

Cheaper lookup therefore raises the selector-node saving required to
compensate for the wider remainder multiplier. The old exact-Toffoli
criterion is recovered at $\kappa=14$. At $\kappa=4$, it becomes
$I_b-I_{b-1}>14w+21f+10.5$; because the left side is an integer, this
strict inequality is interpreted with its actual integer threshold.
An error-bound improvement that saved T gates under the older constructor
can fail this stronger architecture's condition. This is a concrete
architecture-dependent inference, not an assertion that fewer table
entries are intrinsically worse.

At identical prefix and table widths, the corresponding interval/full
complete T gap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:architectureintervalgap}
 C^{\rm interval}-C^{\rm full}
 =2C_{\rm strike}+168w+4\kappa(I_\Psi-I_P).
\end{equation}
The two removed argument subtractions each cost $42w$ forward T gates,
and are also present in the financial inverse. The strike block and two
probability blocks follow the same accounting. The support inclusion
proved earlier gives $I_\Psi\geq I_P$. Independently selected precisions
need not be equal, so fair candidate pools inherit matched opponent
prefixes and then check each method's own monetary certificate. Neither
this identity nor candidate inheritance proves global circuit optimality.

\section{Scope and statistical target}
\label{sec:estimator-scope}

I estimate the existing finite normalised residual probability, rather than repeat its continuous-model or arithmetic approximation. Let the already constructed clean evaluator and uniform Hadamards define a unitary \(A\) with \(A|0\rangle=\sqrt{1-p}\,|b\rangle+\sqrt p\,|g\rangle\), where the flag is zero on the normalised bad state \(|b\rangle\) and one on the normalised good state \(|g\rangle\). They are orthogonal because their flag values differ. The cases \(p=0\) and \(p=1\) are included by continuity, without defining an unused normalised component. The finite Born probability is exactly the uniformly averaged implemented output divided by \(2^q\). A retained deterministic bound \(B_{\rm det}\leq0.005\) already covers distribution transfer, arithmetic and the geometric correction in undiscounted currency.

I choose statistical currency tolerance \(\epsilon_M=0.005\), so \(\epsilon_p=0.005/H=1/(200H)\). My default failure probability is \(\alpha=0.01\) per price. On the coverage event, the completed price estimate differs from the continuous price by at most \(e^{-r}(B_{\rm det}+H\epsilon_p)\leq0.01\), because the stipulated rate is positive. The statement is a 99 per cent confidence guarantee for one price. A simultaneous 99 per cent guarantee for 27 selected prices uses \(\alpha=0.01/27\) for each run, followed by a union bound. A simultaneous guarantee for all 108 method-contract outputs would instead use \(0.01/108\). I record these different scopes explicitly.

Published QFT-free counting is a relevant estimation antecedent \citep{aaronson};
I consulted its publication record and abstract, rather than its full proof.
The algorithm below is a conservative IQAE-style construction using factors three and five. The closest published mechanism is \citet{grinko}. I consulted the final publisher PDF, equations 4 and 5 and Algorithms 1 and 2,
and separately consulted the publisher HTML theorem and algorithms. The
source ledger distinguishes those readings. The amplitude-amplification identity has its established foundation in \citet{brassard}. The published IQAE query coefficient is not applied to this modified policy. The explicit constants and proof below are rederived. \section{Exact amplification and controlled powers}
\label{sec:estimator-amplification}

Write \(p=\sin^2\theta\), \(0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2\). Let \(S_g=I-2\Pi_g\) be Z on the good flag, and \(S_0=I-2|0\rangle\langle0|\) on the invariant financial-input, comparison and flag subspace. Work is zero throughout that invariant subspace. Define

\begin{equation}
Q=-A S_0A^\dagger S_g.
 \label{eq:est-Q}
\end{equation}

On the ordered basis \((|b\rangle,|g\rangle)\), \(A S_0A^\dagger=I-2|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\), where \(|\psi\rangle=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\). Direct multiplication gives

\begin{equation}
Q=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\theta&-\sin2\theta\\
                      \sin2\theta&\cos2\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q^kA|0\rangle=\cos((2k+1)\theta)|b\rangle+
                   \sin((2k+1)\theta)|g\rangle.
 \label{eq:est-amplified-probability}
\end{equation}

Thus a fresh measurement of the flag after that circuit is Bernoulli with probability \(p_k=\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)\). This is the exact quantum mechanism. Repeated independent runs supply the statistical samples. The code emits \(A S_0A^\dagger S_g=-Q\) when no external control is present; the overall sign \((-1)^k\) has no effect on any measurement probability.

No controlled arithmetic is necessary even if a controlled Q primitive is required. In chronological gate order I apply controlled \(S_g\), then unconditional \(A^\dagger\), then controlled \(S_0\), then unconditional \(A\), then Z on the control. In the control-zero branch the middle pair cancels to identity. In the control-one branch the final Z supplies precisely the global-minus convention in \eqref{eq:est-Q}. Controlled \(S_g\) is CZ on control and flag. Controlled \(S_0\) is an explicit multiple-controlled phase with negative data controls, obtained by X on the zero-tested data before and after a clean positive-conjunction ladder. It uses \(2(n-1)\) exact Toffolis and one CZ for \(n\) zero-tested data wires, compared with \(2(n-2)\) for the uncontrolled reflection. The extra controlled primitive costs two Toffolis, or fourteen assigned T gates, plus the control wire and one additional conjunction-work wire. It uses no arbitrary rotation.

Each fresh instrument-based architecture must realise the same logical A and logical adjoint on inputs entangled with the flag or an external control. It is insufficient that its classical output distribution agrees. A corrected measurement branch proportional to the same logical map, with an input-independent proportionality and record probability, supplies that channel-level premise. Its inverse invokes a fresh corrected instrument for the adjoint logical map; measurements are not reversed. The present state-vector audit uses the original exact seven-T Toffoli unitary constructor. Improved four-T lookup profiles can substitute their separately checked logical costs once that premise is proved.

\section{Conditional Hoeffding intervals and adaptive coverage}
\label{sec:estimator-coverage}

I use a fixed probability radius \(r_0=1/25\). Let \(R\) be the deterministic maximum number of rounds defined in Section~\ref{sec:estimator-budget}. Each round takes

\begin{equation}
N=\left\lceil\frac{\log(2R/\alpha)}{2r_0^2}\right\rceil
 \label{eq:est-shots}
\end{equation}

fresh independent shots at its current Grover power. If \(C\) flags are one, I form the exact rational interval

\begin{equation}
I=[\max(0,C/N-r_0),\min(1,C/N+r_0)].
 \label{eq:est-measured-interval}
\end{equation}

For completeness, the Bernoulli Hoeffding inequality follows from an elementary moment-generating-function argument. For a Bernoulli variable \(X\) with mean \(a\), let \(g(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{t(X-a)}\). Then \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\), while \(g''(t)\) is the variance under the exponentially tilted Bernoulli law and is at most \(1/4\). Integrating twice gives \(g(t)\leq t^2/8\), for either sign of \(t\). For N independent observations, Chernoff's inequality and minimisation at \(t=4r_0\) give \(\Pr(\overline X-a\geq r_0)\leq e^{-2Nr_0^2}\), and the same bound for the lower tail. Hence \(\Pr(a\notin I)\leq2e^{-2Nr_0^2}\leq\alpha/R\).

The current power may depend on the entire previous measurement history. Conditional on that history, its fresh shots have a fixed Bernoulli parameter, so the same bound holds conditionally. Averaging over histories preserves it. A union bound over at most \(R\) updates proves that all measured probabilities lie in their corresponding intervals with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). I do not pool adaptively stopped batches at a fixed nominal confidence, use empirical variance for coverage or infer coverage from simulation success rates.

I initialise the angle with any proved upper probability \(p_{\max}\): \(p\in[0,p_{\max}]\). The financial Jensen/moment certificate supplied separately applies equally to all estimator opponents. The interval controller retains an outward inverse of that initial probability range and the prior-dependent plan in Section~\ref{sec:estimator-budget}. On the joint confidence event, every update retains the true angle. Empty intersections occur only off that event; the implementation returns estimate zero with an explicit confidence-failure status. That outcome is included in the allowed failure probability and is not silently repaired into a narrow interval.

\section{Single-quadrant inversion and finite classical computation}
\label{sec:estimator-inversion}

I represent the unknown angle by \(t=2\theta/\pi\in[0,1]\) and maintain rational bounds \([l,u]\). The odd multiplier is \(m=2k+1\). Its admissibility condition is that \([ml,mu]\) lies in one closed unit cell \([j,j+1]\). This is tested with exact rational arithmetic, using \(j=\lfloor ml\rfloor\) and \(mu\leq j+1\). Endpoints at a stationary value are allowed; a strict crossing is not. On that cell let \(v=mt-j\in[0,1]\), and set \(f(v)=\sin^2(\pi v/2)\). The measured probability is \(f(v)\) for even j and \(1-f(v)\) for odd j. Equation \eqref{eq:est-measured-interval} therefore has a unique interval preimage in the selected cell, using the inverse of increasing f and reversing its interval for odd j. The preimage is intersected with the existing angle interval.

All branch decisions are rational. For inverse postprocessing I use outward bisection with tolerance \(\eta=2^{-70}\). The scalar probability is evaluated as \(f(v)=(1-\cos(\pi v))/2\). Pi has an exact rational enclosure from Machin's identity, with eighty alternating terms for each arctangent and its explicit next-term remainder. The cosine Taylor polynomial is evaluated with directed Decimal interval arithmetic at precision eighty; terms with indices zero through forty-four are retained and the remaining decreasing alternating tail is bounded by the next term. Every multiply, divide and addition is directed, including conversion of rational endpoints. This is an interval calculation under Decimal's specified correctly rounded elementary arithmetic, with no appeal to a double-precision arcsine.

The relevant argument lies in \([0,\pi]\subset[0,4]\). The sum of absolute Taylor terms is at most \(\cosh4<28\), and the next-term bound is \(4^{90}/90!<10^{-83}\). I make the rounding allowance explicit. Every retained term and partial sum has magnitude below 28, and every predivision product has magnitude below 256. At precision eighty, each directed arithmetic rounding therefore has absolute error below \(10^{-76}\). Propagation of an additive term-recurrence error through all later summed terms has factor at most \(\cosh4<28\): its successive positive-series denominators are no smaller than those in the series beginning at index zero. Conversion and squaring enclose the input \(x^2\) with width below \(10^{-76}\); its effect on the cosine polynomial is at most four times that width, since

\begin{equation}
\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{j x^{2j-2}}{(2j)!}
 =\frac{\sinh x}{2x}\leq\frac{\sinh4}{8}<4,
\end{equation}

with the continuous value at zero. Fewer than one thousand elementary operations thus give the deliberately coarse total width envelope \(1000\times28\times10^{-76}+4\times10^{-76}+2\times10^{-83}<10^{-70}\), including the much smaller exact Machin truncation. The generated bisection and controller endpoints stay within Decimal's default exponent range, so this construction does not underflow or overflow. Final logarithm rounding in \eqref{eq:est-shots} is covered separately: its correctly rounded eighty-digit centre receives an additive \(10^{-60}\) envelope before the exact integer ceiling. The code validates \(0<\log(2R/\alpha)<100\), where this envelope is larger than the argument-conversion and logarithm rounding errors. This states the numerical domain rather than claiming an unrestricted confidence-parameter API.

If a midpoint probability enclosure contains an inverse target, I avoid slope division near 0 or 1. For \(0\leq x<y\leq1\), the trigonometric identity gives

\begin{equation}
f(y)-f(x)=\sin(\pi(x+y)/2)\sin(\pi(y-x)/2)
 \geq\sin^2(\pi(y-x)/2)\geq(y-x)^2.
 \label{eq:est-inverse-modulus}
\end{equation}

The first inequality uses \(x+y\in[y-x,2-(y-x)]\); the second uses concavity of sine on \([0,\pi/2]\), giving \(\sin(\pi z/2)\geq z\). Therefore a probability enclosure of width at most \(\eta^2\) permits an inverse interval within \(\eta\) of its midpoint. This is the code's ambiguity branch. Ordinary bisection returns width at most \(\eta\); the ambiguity branch returns width at most \(2\eta\). The initial exact values 0, 1 and 1/2 are handled directly. At most 71 bisections are needed. Thus each selected inverse endpoint differs from its exact inverse by at most \(2\eta\), and no branch is decided by an unbounded floating-point rounding error.

\section{The three-or-five quadrant cover}
\label{sec:estimator-cover}

After one update, the exact probability interval in the current cell has width at most \(2r_0=2/25=0.08\). Outward inversion extends its two v endpoints by at most \(2\eta\) each. Because \(f'\leq\pi/2<2\), its realised probability span is at most \(0.08+8\eta<0.0808\). This bound is uniform in the quadrant parity.

Within any current quadrant, multiplying by an odd factor s creates internal stationary boundaries at \(v=a/s\), \(a=1,\ldots,s-1\). In probability coordinates their locations for s=3 are

\begin{equation}
\{1/4,3/4\},
\end{equation}

and for s=5 they are

\begin{equation}
\{(3-\sqrt5)/8,(5-\sqrt5)/8,(3+\sqrt5)/8,(5+\sqrt5)/8\}.
 \label{eq:est-critical-values}
\end{equation}

I derive these values explicitly. With $z=e^{2\pi i/5}$, the identity
$1+z+z^2+z^3+z^4=0$ gives
$1+2c+2(2c^2-1)=0$, where $c=\cos(2\pi/5)>0$.
Hence $4c^2+2c-1=0$ and $c=(\sqrt5-1)/4$.
The positive half-angle root is
$\cos(\pi/5)=\sqrt{(1+c)/2}=(1+\sqrt5)/4$.
Applying $\sin^2 x=(1-\cos 2x)/2$ gives the first two
values in~\eqref{eq:est-critical-values}; replacing $x$ by $\pi/2-x$
gives the other two. For thirds, $\cos(\pi/3)=1/2$ gives
$\sin^2(\pi/6)=1/4$ and its complementary value $3/4$.
Writing $s=\sqrt5$, the four distances from $1/4$ to the fifth values
are $(s-1)/8$, $(3-s)/8$, $(1+s)/8$ and $(3+s)/8$.
The distances from $3/4$ form the same set by symmetry. Since
$2<s<9/4$, their minimum is $(3-s)/8$. Thus
$d_*=(3-\sqrt5)/8>3/32=0.09375>0.0808$.

If both multiplier three and multiplier five strictly crossed an internal stationary boundary, the retained probability interval would contain one element of each set in \eqref{eq:est-critical-values}, and would have width at least \(d_*\). This contradicts the span bound. Therefore at least one multiplier leaves the angle interval wholly inside a single monotone quadrant. Common endpoint boundaries 0 and 1 cause no exception: the existing interval already lies inside their cell, so neither can be crossed by the subinterval. A boundary coinciding with an endpoint is admissible. If the quadrant is decreasing, probability coordinates are reflected as \(1-f(v)\), which preserves every cross-distance. The implementation tries factor three first and otherwise factor five, checking their cells exactly.

This is a checked sufficient cover for the stated radius and outward padding. It does not establish that this policy is better than the published IQAE optimisation, nor that its number-theoretic idea is absent from prior work.

\section{Financial-prior bootstrap, deterministic termination and query bounds}
\label{sec:estimator-budget}

For an inverse cell interval with probability width d, the identity in \eqref{eq:est-inverse-modulus}, now expressed in angle rather than normalised coordinates, gives angle width at most \(\arcsin\sqrt d\). In particular exact inversion of a width-0.08 interval has angle width below 0.3: \(\sin(0.3)\geq0.3-0.3^3/6=0.2955\), whose square exceeds 0.08. Outward endpoint padding adds at most \(2\pi\eta<8\eta\). Hence the realised interval for \(m\theta\) has width strictly below \(1/3\). After updating at multiplier m, the retained original angle interval therefore has width below \(1/(3m)\).

Write \(u=p_{\max}\), \(u_+=\min(1,u+10^{-18})\). The outward initial inverse endpoint differs from its exact normalised angle by at most \(2\eta\), so its exact amplitude is at most \(u+4\eta\leq u_+\). Every subsequent angle interval is an intersection with this initial interval, so its exact amplitudes remain in \([0,u_+]\). Define

\begin{equation}
v_+=\begin{cases}u_+(1-u_+),&u_+\leq1/2,\\1/4,&u_+>1/2,\end{cases}
 \qquad \gamma=2\overline{\sqrt{v_+}},
\end{equation}

where the square-root is rounded upward by exact rational arithmetic. I compute an integer ceiling of \(v_+2^{200}\), take its integer square-root ceiling and divide by \(2^{100}\). This supplies a proved upper bound without a transcendental-library assumption. Since \(dp/d\theta=2\sqrt{p(1-p)}\), the amplitude function is \(\gamma\)-Lipschitz on every retained angle interval. At \(u_+>1/2\), \(\gamma=1\). The amplitude interval from the two outward endpoint evaluations therefore has width at most \(\gamma/(3m)+2\times10^{-70}\).

If \(u\leq2\epsilon_p\), the initial proved interval already has sufficient width and I return its midpoint without quantum shots. Otherwise let \(t_u\) be the outward initial normalised-angle upper endpoint. I choose \(m_0\) as the largest positive odd integer satisfying \(m_0t_u\leq1\). This initial amplification is in the first closed quadrant and requires no pilot. The plan uses

\begin{equation}
T_* =\max\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{\gamma}{5\epsilon_p}\right\rceil\right),
 \qquad R=1+\left\lceil\log_3\max(1,T_*/m_0)\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:est-rounds}
\end{equation}

If \(m\geq T_*\), the amplitude enclosure width is at most \(5\epsilon_p/3+2\times10^{-70}<2\epsilon_p\), within the declared implementation range \(\epsilon_p\geq10^{-6}\). The unused \(\epsilon_p/3\) is explicit numerical slack. Thus it must stop at that update. The multipliers begin at \(m_0\) and grow by at least three, so at most \(R\) rounds are needed. The code computes R with an exact integer loop, rather than rounding a logarithm. This computational termination does not require the confidence event: nonempty intervals obey the same width argument; empty intervals return a flagged failure immediately.

For a nonzero-shot plan, I retain a tighter executable cap than the convenient coarse bound \(\max(m_0,5T_*)\). The code uses

\begin{equation}
M=\begin{cases}
 m_0,&m_0\geq T_*,\\
 \min\{5T_*,m_0 5^{R-1}\},&m_0<T_*.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

In the first case the first update must terminate. In the second case each multiplier before the final update is less than \(T_*\), so the next multiplier is less than \(5T_*\); also the round-\(i\) multiplier is at most \(m_0 5^{i-1}\), with at most \(R\) rounds. Both bounds hold, so their minimum holds. The largest Grover power is at most \(\lfloor(M-1)/2\rfloor\).

For \(R=1\), the exact cap on \(\sum_i k_i\) is \((m_0-1)/2\). For \(R>1\), reversing the at-least-three geometric growth gives \(\sum_i m_i\leq(3/2)m_{\rm last}\leq3M/2\), hence \(\sum_i k_i\leq\lfloor3M/4\rfloor\). Independently, if a run uses \(r\) rounds, then

\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^{r} k_i\leq\frac12\left\{m_0\frac{5^r-1}{4}-r\right\}.
\end{equation}

The right-hand side is nondecreasing in the integer \(r\) because its increment is \((m_0 5^r-1)/2\geq0\). Thus replacing \(r\) by \(R\) is valid. Since \(\sum_i k_i\) is an integer, I take the floor and then the smaller of the two independent bounds. Define

\begin{equation}
K_* = \begin{cases}
 (m_0-1)/2,&R=1,\\
 \min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{3M}{4}\right\rfloor,
 \left\lfloor\frac{m_0(5^R-1)/4-R}{2}\right\rfloor\right\},&R>1.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad N_{\rm shots}\leq RN,\quad N_Q\leq N K_*.
 \label{eq:est-query-budget}
\end{equation}

Every floor is implemented on exact integers. Zero-shot plans separately have \(R=N=M=K_*=0\). These are the tightened caps actually retained in the resource tables.

Each shot uses one preparation A and each Q call uses one A and one logical adjoint, so clean evaluator invocations are at most \(RN+2N_Q\). Equation \eqref{eq:est-query-budget}, not a likelihood fit or an observed stopping count, is the worst-case resource guarantee. Combining \eqref{eq:est-shots}, \eqref{eq:est-rounds} and \eqref{eq:est-query-budget} yields \(O(\epsilon_p^{-1}\log(\log(1/\epsilon_p)/\alpha))\) logical Grover calls with explicit, deliberately conservative constants. The published coefficient fifty is not used for this policy. Large constants or expensive coherent evaluation can remove any practical advantage over classical sampling.

On the joint confidence event, the final amplitude interval contains p and has width at most \(2\epsilon_p\). Its midpoint is therefore within \(\epsilon_p\) of p. Section~\ref{sec:estimator-coverage} proves that event has probability at least \(1-\alpha\). Sections~\ref{sec:estimator-scope} and~\ref{sec:estimator-budget} consequently give both an absolute monetary confidence guarantee and a finite resource cap. The proved financial prior improves the worst-case caps through both \(\gamma\) and \(m_0\), in addition to any observed early stopping. Setting \(u=1\) provides the separately retained full-range comparison. This prior-dependent substitution is applied fairly to every financial evaluator and does not rely on observed variance.

\section{Fully charged quantum resources}
\label{sec:estimator-resources}

For each selected financial constructor I retain the existing emitted clean-evaluator T count \(C_A\), its uniform preparation Hadamards, serial logical depth \(D_A\), work qubits and reflection profile. Let \(C_Q=2C_A+C_{S_0}\), where the good-flag phase, uniform preparation and inverse use Clifford gates; \(C_{S_0}=14(n-2)\) for the original seven-T clean-conjunction reflection. The total T upper bound is

\begin{equation}
C_T\leq RN C_A+N_Q C_Q.
 \label{eq:est-total-T}
\end{equation}

Uniform-preparation Hadamards number at most \((RN+2N_Q)B\), where B is the number of financial and comparison input bits. Flag measurements and fresh-shot resets are at most RN. Writing \(D_A,D_Q\) in this sentence for the assigned T depths, the serial sum of assigned T depths is \(RN D_A+N_QD_Q\); the maximum individual coherent shot depth is \(D_A+\lfloor(M-1)/2\rfloor D_Q\). All-gate dependency depth is charged separately from T depth. A fully serial expanded upper bound is the total emitted Clifford-plus-T primitive count, including all uniform Hadamards and flag/reflection phases. I do not multiply qubits by shot count: sequential shots reuse registers. Peak logical qubits are the maximum of the clean evaluator and reflection scratch demand. No extra estimation register is required by this schedule. Controlled Q, supplied as a separate reusable primitive, adds one control and the reflection overhead described in Section~\ref{sec:estimator-amplification}; the estimator itself does not call it.

The exact primitive gate lists use X, H, CNOT, exact Toffoli, Z and CZ. I retain the constructor's assigned three-T-layer Toffoli profile explicitly as an assigned profile. I separately emit and phase-check a concrete exact decomposition with seven T or inverse-T gates, six CNOTs and two Hadamards. Its small gate dependency audit has four T layers; a five-layer serial upper bound is retained separately. The fully serial Clifford-plus-T gate count charges fifteen primitive gates per Toffoli. I also retain dependency-depth upper bounds by adding two times the total exact-Toffoli count to the assigned three-layer T-depth sum, and fourteen times that count to the logical all-gate dependency-depth sum. Substitution of a gate with a fifteen-primitive circuit increases any critical path by at most fourteen per substituted Toffoli; summing over all substitutions is conservative. The maximum-shot bounds use the Toffoli count of one A plus the largest allowed power times the Q count, with the same additions. Thus the assigned profile is not confused with the particular decomposition replayed, and both bounds preserve the exact seven-T count. There is no arbitrary-angle quantum gate, QFT or rotation-synthesis approximation, so this logical construction has zero rotation-synthesis bias. That statement is about its declared ideal logical gates, rather than a claim that a physical machine has zero noise. Any physical implementation requires a separately allocated fault budget or a proved bound on the realised shot probabilities. Iteration-dependent physical inaccuracies are not treated as one fixed oracle bias without evidence.

For completeness, the exact seven-T expansion on controls a,b and target c has chronological sequence

\begin{equation}
H_c,\ C_{b,c},\ T_c^\dagger,\ C_{a,c},\ T_c,\ C_{b,c},\ T_c^\dagger,
 C_{a,c},\ T_b,\ T_c,\ H_c,\ C_{a,b},\ T_a,\ T_b^\dagger,\ C_{a,b},
\end{equation}

where C denotes CNOT. Between the target Hadamards, the total computational-basis phase exponent is

\begin{equation}
a+b+c-(a\mathbin\oplus b)-(a\mathbin\oplus c)
 -(b\mathbin\oplus c)+(a\mathbin\oplus b\mathbin\oplus c)=4abc
 \pmod 8.
\end{equation}

The bitwise identity follows by expanding \(a\oplus b=a+b-2ab\) and its three-bit counterpart. Hence its phase is \(e^{i\pi abc}=(-1)^{abc}\), an exact CCZ; conjugating the target by H gives an exact Toffoli with no input-dependent leftover phase. The six CNOTs restore all linear labels. There are four T gates and three inverse-T gates. The dependency calculation permits the last control phases to run alongside the fourth target layer, giving four T layers for this particular sequence; the retained five-layer bound remains conservative. The identity proves exact action, while the eight-basis replay independently checks the emitted ordering.

An improved lookup architecture may replace \(C_A,C_Q,D_A,D_Q\) with its proved instrument-based profiles. Its per-AND measurement, feed-forward and reset overhead must be multiplied by the total A and adjoint invocations, not omitted. Address-independent corrected records make the logical amplification probability \eqref{eq:est-amplified-probability} unchanged. T-depth alone does not include measurement latency or a physical surface-code schedule. Classical table setup remains a separate retained cost and is not paid once per measurement shot.

\section{Exact classical evaluation of the finite quantum target}
\label{sec:exactclassical}

I construct an exact classical comparator for the retained finite graph. I use
unsigned 64-bit NumPy arrays to execute integer operations and retain Python
integers for sample moments. This comparator uses the same financial graph
and the same one-dimensional tables as the coherent evaluator. It does not
establish that these tables are the strongest classical discretisation of the
continuous financial model. I make no novelty claim for the following
elementary integer identities.

\begin{lemma}[Safe product after the financial truncation]
Let $18\leq q\leq30$, $w\leq q+7$, and $0\leq a,b<2^w$. Define
$a_H=\lfloor a/2^q\rfloor$, $a_L=a\bmod2^q$ and similarly $b_H,b_L$.
Then
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{ab}{2^q}\right\rfloor
 =2^q a_Hb_H+a_Hb_L+a_Lb_H
   +\left\lfloor\frac{a_Lb_L}{2^q}\right\rfloor .
\]
Every product, shift and addition on the right-hand side fits in an unsigned
64-bit word. Its final reduction modulo $2^w$ is the retained unsigned
fixed-point multiplication.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $(2^q a_H+a_L)(2^q b_H+b_L)$ gives the identity, since the first
three terms after division by $2^q$ are integers. The high limbs are smaller
than $2^7$ and the low limbs are smaller than $2^q$. Hence the unshifted
low-limb product is smaller than $2^{60}$, each cross product is smaller than
$2^{37}$ and the shifted high-limb product is smaller than $2^{44}$.
The sum is exactly $\lfloor ab/2^q\rfloor<2^{2w-q}\leq2^{44}$.
Every partial sum is nonnegative and bounded by this sum. All operations
therefore fit in 64 bits. The output bit mask is exactly reduction modulo
$2^w$. For a known $k<2^q$, the high limb of $k$ vanishes and the formula
reduces to $a_Hk+\lfloor a_Lk/2^q\rfloor$. A binary-power multiplier reduces
to a shift. A native unsigned product is used for a positive drop only when
$(2^w-1)k<2^{64}$ has been checked. With zero drop, native wrap followed by
the $w$-bit mask remains exact because $2^w$ divides $2^{64}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact unsigned product windows]
Put $B=2^{32}$ and write unsigned 64-bit inputs as $a=a_0+Ba_1$ and
$b=b_0+Bb_1$, with all limbs smaller than $B$. Let $p_{ij}=a_i b_j$ and
\[
 m=\lfloor p_{00}/B\rfloor+(p_{01}\bmod B)+(p_{10}\bmod B),
\]
\[
 L=(p_{00}\bmod B)+B(m\bmod B),\qquad
 U=p_{11}+\lfloor p_{01}/B\rfloor+\lfloor p_{10}/B\rfloor
       +\lfloor m/B\rfloor .
\]
Then $ab=L+2^{64}U$. For $0\leq d<128$ and $1\leq v\leq64$, the
implementation in \texttt{multiply\_shift\_u64} returns
$\lfloor ab/2^d\rfloor\bmod2^v$ exactly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $ab=p_{00}+B(p_{01}+p_{10})+B^2p_{11}$ and carry its central limb.
This gives the asserted decomposition. Each $p_{ij}<2^{64}$ and $m<3B$.
Moreover $U<2^{64}$ because $ab<2^{128}$, so every nonnegative partial sum
forming $U$ fits in 64 bits. If $0<d<64$, the low 64-bit window is the bitwise
OR of $L\gg d$ and $U\ll(64-d)$, where the latter is reduced modulo
$2^{64}$. The first term occupies only the low $64-d$ bits and the second
only the remaining bits. Their OR is therefore their sum in that window.
If $64\leq d<128$, the low window is $U\gg(d-64)$. If $d=0$, it is the
native low product word. Masking the final $v$ bits gives the required result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Equality of the finite classical and quantum targets]
For a retained graph with valid function addresses and the same stored
tables, \texttt{FiniteEvaluator.evaluate} equals the scalar integer graph
output for every valid input tuple. Under independent uniform finite input
addresses, let $a$ be that output and $X=a/2^q$. Then the coherent comparator
flag has success probability $\E X$, and an independent classical sample
mean of $X$ estimates the same finite target without bias.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Addition and subtraction followed by the register mask give their specified
modular results, since $2^w$ divides $2^{64}$. The preceding lemmas prove the
unsigned multiplication and constant-product branches. A quantile operation
gathers its stored integer word. A midpoint uniform operation returns
$(2i+1)2^{q-b-1}$, as specified. For an interpolation fractional width $f$,
the gathered cell is $j=\lfloor x/2^f\rfloor$, the remainder is
$r=x\bmod2^f$, and the returned value is
\[
 \left(b_j+\left\lfloor\delta_jr/2^f\right\rfloor\right)\bmod2^w.
\]
The native product branch is used only after checking
$\max_j\delta_j(2^f-1)<2^{64}$; otherwise the product-window lemma applies.
If a register's sign bit is set, its signed interpretation is $a-2^w<0$
and the lower clipping branch returns zero. Otherwise its signed value is
$a\geq0$ and the unsigned minimum with the cap is exactly the upper clip.
Every node consequently agrees with its scalar rule. Induction in the
topological graph order proves equality of outputs. Releasing a last-used
array changes no value consumed later.

Conditioned on an address tuple, there are exactly $a$ good comparison
integers among the $2^q$ equally likely comparison integers. The flag's
conditional success probability is therefore $a/2^q$. Averaging over the
uniform address law gives $\E X$. Linearity of expectation gives the same
expectation for an independent classical sample mean. Existing distribution,
arithmetic and geometric-correction bounds are still required to transfer
this finite target to the continuous financial price. A seeded NumPy
pseudorandom run is an implementation diagnostic; its seed is not a proof of
the independent random-bit premise of a confidence theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact accumulated sample moments]
Let $0\leq a_i\leq2^q$ with $q\leq30$. The class
\texttt{ExactMoments} retains $N$, $S=\sum_i a_i$ and $Q=\sum_i a_i^2$
exactly, including when $Q>2^{64}$. For $N>1$, its normalised sample mean
and unbiased sample variance are the exact fractions
\[
 \overline X=\frac{S}{N2^q},\qquad
 s_X^2=\frac{NQ-S^2}{N(N-1)2^{2q}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $a_i=\ell_i+2^{16}h_i$, with $\ell_i<2^{16}$ and
$h_i\leq2^{14}$. Each reduction chunk contains at most $2^{20}$ outputs.
The low-square, high-square and cross-product sums are respectively smaller
than $2^{52}$, at most $2^{48}$ and smaller than $2^{50}$. The first-moment
sum is at most $2^{50}$. They therefore fit in unsigned 64-bit words. Their
assembly is performed with arbitrary-precision Python integers using
\[
 Q=\sum_i\ell_i^2+2^{17}\sum_i\ell_i h_i+2^{32}\sum_i h_i^2.
\]
Summing chunks and merging accumulators preserve these integer identities.
The mean formula follows directly. Expanding
$\sum_i(a_i-S/N)^2=Q-S^2/N$ and dividing by $(N-1)2^{2q}$ gives the variance
formula. A floating rendering of either exact fraction is a diagnostic. The
sample variance is not substituted for a proved variance upper bound in a
fixed confidence certificate.
\end{proof}

The operation profile reports conservative scalar-lane counts for this
particular NumPy implementation, table bytes and separate setup timing. It
also charges scalar broadcasts to every lane. A complete classical workflow
must additionally charge table generation, random generation, input validation,
allocation and exact-moment reduction. These counts do not describe CPU
instructions or an optimised classical lower bound. Comparing them directly
with logical T gates cannot establish physical quantum speed-up.


\section{Second moments of the actual finite financial output}
\label{sec:finite-second-moment}

I strengthen the classical comparator by sampling its fractional financial
output directly. The variance of that output differs from the variance of
the Bernoulli flag in Section~\ref{sec:estimator-coverage}. I do not substitute
the former into the present quantum confidence controller. A quantum mean
estimator that exploits a second-moment promise would need its own complete
construction, confidence proof and cost.

For any real $y\in\R^p$, put
\begin{equation}
 d_i(y)=\mu_i-\overline\mu+(R_i-\overline R)\cdot y,
 \qquad T(y)=\sum_iw_i e^{d_i(y)},\qquad J(y)=\log T(y).
\end{equation}
The weights are positive and sum to one. Hence
$\sum_iw_i d_i(y)=0$ for every $y$, not merely almost surely under a
Gaussian law. Convexity of the exponential gives $T(y)\geq1$. Therefore
$0\leq J(y)\leq T(y)-1$: integrating $1/t\leq1$ from $1$ to $T(y)$
proves the last inequality.

Let $\widetilde Y$ and, for direct evaluation, $\widetilde Z$, have the
independent uniform laws of the actual rounded quantile registers. All
moments in this section first concern these finite laws. They need not
be Gaussian. Let $x$ denote the exact mathematical normalised output of
the chosen financial formula at those register values, before the body
of the financial graph is rounded. For full conditioning,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-full}
 0\leq x=P(m_A)-P(m_G)\leq
 \min\{1,L_{h,\lambda}[T(\widetilde Y)-1]\}.
\end{equation}
This follows from $m_A-m_G=J$, the global kernel slope theorem and
$J\leq T-1$. For the random-strike integrand the same proof uses
$L_{\rm CDF}=\varphi(0)/\lambda$, since translation of the CDF argument
does not change its global slope. It holds for every strike-register
value, so averaging over that register preserves the second-moment bound.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-gap}
 \E x^2\leq L^2\{\E T^2-2\E T+1\},
\end{equation}
with $L=L_{h,\lambda}$ for full conditioning and $L=L_{\rm CDF}$ for
random-strike conditioning. It is essential to retain the negative
$-2\E T$ term with a lower, rather than an upper, enclosure of $\E T$.

I evaluate these moments without a joint payoff table. Define the scalar
moment-generating function $M_{b,q}(t)=\E e^{t\widetilde Y_j}$ for the
common marginal with latent width $b$ and fractional width $q$. Independence
and multiplication of finite sums give, for any real $c$ and vector $v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exponential}
 \E e^{c+v\cdot\widetilde Y}
 =e^c\prod_{j=1}^pM_{b,q}(v_j).
\end{equation}
Writing $s_i=R_i-\overline R$ and $a_i=\mu_i-\overline\mu$ therefore yields
\begin{align}\label{eq:finite-t-moments}
 \E T&=\sum_iw_i e^{a_i}\prod_jM_{b,q}(s_{ij}),\\
 \E T^2&=\sum_{i,k}w_iw_k e^{a_i+a_k}
                  \prod_jM_{b,q}(s_{ij}+s_{kj}).
\end{align}
These identities are exact for the stipulated finite law. Replacing
$M_{b,q}(t)$ by $e^{t^2/2}$ gives the separate continuous Gaussian
comparison. That substitution is not made in the finite certificate.

An additional bound follows from the positive arithmetic--geometric gap.
Set $B=\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i+R_i\cdot\widetilde Y}$ and
$C=e^{\overline\mu+\overline R\cdot\widetilde Y}$, so $B\geq C$.
The unit Lipschitz payoff gives $F_H(A)-F_H(G)\leq A-G$.
For direct evaluation this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-direct}
 \E x^2\leq \frac{K^2}{H^2}M_{b_Z,q}(2\lambda)
                       \E(B-C)^2.
\end{equation}
For full conditioning, taking the continuous inner Gaussian expectation
first gives $Hx\leq K e^{\lambda^2/2}(B-C)$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-condition-AG}
 \E x^2\leq\frac{K^2e^{\lambda^2}}{H^2}\E(B-C)^2.
\end{equation}
The two inner factors are different: the square of
$\E e^{\lambda Z}$ equals $e^{\lambda^2}$, whereas
$\E e^{2\lambda Z}=e^{2\lambda^2}$. For a finite direct register the
last factor is its actual $M_{b_Z,q}(2\lambda)$.
Expanding the square gives
\begin{align}
 \E(B-C)^2={}&
 \sum_{i,k}w_iw_k e^{\mu_i+\mu_k}
      \prod_jM_{b,q}(R_{ij}+R_{kj})\\
 &-2\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i+\overline\mu}
      \prod_jM_{b,q}(R_{ij}+\overline R_j)
 +e^{2\overline\mu}\prod_jM_{b,q}(2\overline R_j).
\end{align}
The cross term again requires a lower enclosure. I use the minimum of the
applicable proved bounds and $\E x^2\leq1$.

\subsection{A scalar certificate for the dyadic marginal}

The installed quantile constructor rounds the same floating seed
$s_k=\operatorname{NormalDist.inv\_cdf}((k+1/2)/2^b)+5$ to each chosen
fractional width and imposes exact mirror symmetry. This description
defines its actual integer law; it does not assert that the floating seed
is the exact Gaussian quantile. I retain a reference table at width
$q_*=48$. The largest scaled seed is below $2^{53}$, and scaling a finite
binary floating number by the powers of two used here is exact. Nearest
integer rounding consequently gives the pointwise displacement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-marginal}
 |\widetilde y_{k,q}-\widetilde y_{k,q_*}|
 \leq \delta_q:=\tfrac12(2^{-q}+2^{-q_*}).
\end{equation}
The reference and production mirror operations preserve this bound on the
negative half. Reproduction compares their integer maps with the installed
constructor, rather than relying only on a floating-distribution name.

Symmetry gives $M_{b,q_*}(t)=\E\cosh(t\widetilde Y_*)$. Exact integer
power sums supply every even moment of this dyadic table. For $m=64$,
\begin{equation}
 P_m(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{t^{2k}\E\widetilde Y_*^{2k}}{(2k)!}
 \leq M_{b,q_*}(t).
\end{equation}
If $a=|t|\max_k|\widetilde y_{k,q_*}|$, the first omitted term is at
most $a^{2m+2}/(2m+2)!$. Every subsequent term ratio is at most
$\rho=a^2/[(2m+3)(2m+4)]<1$. Summing a geometric majorant proves
\begin{equation}
 0\leq M_{b,q_*}(t)-P_m(t)
 \leq\frac{a^{2m+2}}{(2m+2)!(1-\rho)}.
\end{equation}
The retained coefficient and support checks make this last bound smaller
than $10^{-120}$. Since
$e^{-|t|\delta_q}e^{t\widetilde Y_*}\leq
 e^{t\widetilde Y_q}\leq
 e^{|t|\delta_q}e^{t\widetilde Y_*}$ pointwise, taking expectations
encloses the production MGF by multiplying the reference lower and upper
bounds by the respective exponential factors. All power sums and
coefficient inputs are exact integers or rational numbers. The Taylor sum
uses correctly rounded decimal operations at 220 significant digits and an
aggregate $10^{-100}$ outward envelope. Its at most 65 moment terms have
magnitudes below $5^{128}<10^{90}$; the coefficient magnitude is at most one,
and the remaining moment, density, exponential and budget intermediates
have magnitude below $10^{100}$. Fewer than $10^6$ correctly rounded
operations contribute to any retained scalar bound, so their conservative
accumulated absolute roundoff is below $10^{-113}$, before the larger
$10^{-100}$ envelope. I also apply that envelope at subsequent product and
moment boundaries. The finite set of operations, their magnitudes and the
Taylor remainder premises are checked independently. No mean-error certificate is treated
as a second-moment or pointwise distribution certificate.

\subsection{Transfer to the implemented output and confidence budget}

Let $X$ be the actual implemented fractional output. The graph recurrence
has a pointwise currency error bound $a_{\rm body}$ that includes a
conservative allowance for quantile-register error. I can set the ideal
quantile node equal to its actual rounded value. Its initial error then
equals zero, which is no larger than the retained allowance, and its value
lies in the certified quantile range. Every subsequent propagation rule is
monotone in the incoming nonnegative error allowances. The original domain
and overflow enclosures continue to contain the resulting subsets.
Consequently the same retained bound proves
\begin{equation}
 |X-x|\leq a_{\rm body}/H
\end{equation}
at every finite address. Generator enclosures, interpolation, constant
rounding, clamps and product truncation remain included in that bound.

If $M_2$ is a proved bound for $\E x^2$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\E[x|X-x|]\leq\sqrt{M_2}\,a_{\rm body}/H$. Expanding
$X^2\leq(x+|X-x|)^2$ therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-second-transfer}
 \E X^2\leq\min\{1,(\sqrt{M_2}+a_{\rm body}/H)^2\}=:M_{2,+}.
\end{equation}
This is the $L^2$ triangle inequality written out for the present finite
law. The moment transfer does not use the distribution bias budget. That
budget still enters the final target-price guarantee through the unchanged
deterministic mean-bias certificate.

Since $0\leq X\leq1$, its variance is at most $1/4$. Also
$\operatorname{Var}(X)=\E X^2-(\E X)^2\leq M_{2,+}$.
Combining this with the already proved mean-prior variance bound
$v_{\rm mean}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 v_+=\min\{1/4,M_{2,+},v_{\rm mean}\}.
\end{equation}
For independent exact draws of $X$, the previously derived Bernstein
inequality consequently gives the sufficient fixed sample count
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-variance-samples}
 N_B=\left\lceil
 \frac{(2v_++2t/3)\log(2/\alpha)}{t^2}\right\rceil,
 \qquad t=0.005/H.
\end{equation}
I also retain the smaller applicable Hoeffding count. The count is fixed
before sampling; choosing the certified variance or prefix through
deterministic model calculations does not invalidate coverage. At
$\alpha=0.01$, the finite mean has error at most $0.005$ in undiscounted
currency with probability at least $0.99$. The retained deterministic bias
of at most $0.005$ and $e^{-r}\leq1$ give absolute price error at most
$0.01$. For one preselected price per case, allocating
$\alpha=0.01/27$ and applying the union bound gives simultaneous
$99$ per cent coverage over the 27 prices. This does not certify every
retained candidate output simultaneously.

The financial inequalities, exponential moment factorisation, exact dyadic
sums, Taylor majorant, $L^2$ transfer and concentration inequality are
established mathematics. Their combined parameter certificate is a
project-specific argument. I have not established theoretical originality
or a new quantum primitive from this calculation.


\section{A moment theorem beyond the stipulated Gaussian grid}
\label{sec:general-moment}

I state the argument separately from the retained GBM and circuit
specialisation. Let $w_i>0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$, and let
$\ell_i(y)=a_i+b_i\cdot y$ be arbitrary affine log-price functions.
Set $\overline a=\sum_iw_i a_i$,
$\overline b=\sum_iw_i b_i$, $m_G=\overline a+\overline b\cdot Y$ and
$m_A=\log\sum_iw_i e^{\ell_i(Y)}$. The latent vector $Y$ can have any
joint law for which the moments specified below exist. Define its joint
moment-generating function by
$\Lambda(v)=\E e^{v\cdot Y}$ at the required arguments. Independence
is not assumed. Let $P:\R\to[0,1]$ be increasing and globally
$L$-Lipschitz, with $L<\infty$, and put
$X=P(m_A)-P(m_G)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general-moment}
Define $d_i=a_i-\overline a$ and $s_i=b_i-\overline b$. If
$\Lambda(s_i)$ and $\Lambda(s_i+s_k)$ are finite for every $i,k$, then
$0\leq X\leq1$ and
\begin{align}
 \E X^2&\leq\min\{1,L^2M\},\\
 M&=\sum_{i,k}w_iw_k e^{d_i+d_k}\Lambda(s_i+s_k)
       -2\sum_iw_i e^{d_i}\Lambda(s_i)+1.
\end{align}
In particular $M\geq0$. For an implemented output $\widehat X\in[0,1]$
with a proved pointwise error $|\widehat X-X|\leq\eta$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat X)
 \leq\min\{1/4,(\sqrt{\min\{1,L^2M\}}+\eta)^2\}.
\end{equation}
Any separately proved mean-based variance bound can also enter this
minimum. The finite-law factorisation in the preceding section is the
special case $\Lambda(v)=\prod_jM_j(v_j)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $y$, $\sum_iw_i(d_i+s_i\cdot y)=0$. Jensen's inequality therefore
gives $T(y)=\sum_iw_i e^{d_i+s_i\cdot y}\geq1$.
Moreover $m_A-m_G=\log T\leq T-1$, as proved above. Monotonicity,
boundedness and the Lipschitz condition imply
$0\leq X\leq\min\{1,L(T-1)\}$. Squaring and taking expectations proves
the first assertion once $M=\E(T-1)^2$ is evaluated. The assumed
finite moment-generating functions make all sums integrable. Expanding
the finite sums for $T$ and $T^2$ gives the displayed expression for $M$,
which is nonnegative because it is the expectation of a square.
For the implemented output,
$\E\widehat X^2\leq\E(X+\eta)^2
\leq(\sqrt{\E X^2}+\eta)^2$, using
$\E X\leq\sqrt{\E X^2}$. Subtracting its nonnegative squared mean
bounds its variance by that second moment. Finally any variable in
$[0,1]$ has variance at most $1/4$, because
$\E \widehat X^2\leq\E\widehat X$ and
$p-p^2\leq1/4$ for $p\in[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

This result can certify conditional basket-payoff sampling under Gaussian
mixtures, finite scenario laws or other latent models with the stated joint
moments. A suitable kernel and joint moment certificate must be supplied
for each application. It does not justify treating an arbitrary financial
model as Gaussian, and it does not cover models with infinite required
exponential moments. The theorem is an original derivation in this
project; its theoretical novelty remains subject to comparison with
published moment and variance-reduction results.

\section{Weak dispersion and the variance lost in a canonical flag}
\label{sec:weak-dispersion}

I first state a distribution-general limit. The argument concerns
financial random variables before any fixed grid or arithmetic error.
It supplies an independent limit experiment, rather than extrapolating
the original 27 finite configurations.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general-weak-dispersion}
Let $V$ be an arbitrary $\R^p$-valued latent random vector, let
$w_i>0$ sum to one, and fix $b_i\in\R^p$ with
$\sum_iw_i b_i=0$. Write $B=\max_i\|b_i\|$ and
$\Sigma_b=\sum_iw_i b_i b_i^{\mathsf T}$.
For $0<|\varepsilon|\leq\varepsilon_0$, suppose deterministic coefficients
$a_i(\varepsilon)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \sum_iw_i a_i(\varepsilon)=0,\qquad
 |a_i(\varepsilon)|\leq C\varepsilon^2
\end{equation}
for a finite common $C$. Define
\begin{equation}
 T_\varepsilon=\sum_iw_i
        e^{\varepsilon b_i\cdot V+a_i(\varepsilon)},\qquad
 J_\varepsilon=\log T_\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Let the finite random base $m_\varepsilon$ converge almost surely to
a finite $m_0$, and let $P:\R\to[0,1]$ be increasing and continuously
differentiable, with $|P'|\leq L<\infty$. Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-dispersion-integrability}
 \E\{(1+\|V\|^4)
                 e^{2\varepsilon_0B\|V\|}\}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-weak-L2}
 \frac{P(m_\varepsilon+J_\varepsilon)-P(m_\varepsilon)}
      {\varepsilon^2}
 \longrightarrow
 \tfrac12P'(m_0)V^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_b V=:H_0
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2.
\end{equation}
The mean is $\varepsilon^2\E H_0+o(\varepsilon^2)$ and the variance is
$\varepsilon^4\operatorname{Var}(H_0)+o(\varepsilon^4)$.
The respective $\Theta$ orders require
$\E H_0>0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(H_0)>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $d_{i,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon b_i\cdot V+a_i(\varepsilon)$.
The assumptions imply $\sum_iw_i d_{i,\varepsilon}=0$ pointwise.
Jensen's inequality gives $T_\varepsilon\geq1$.
For fixed $V$, $d_{i,\varepsilon}/\varepsilon\to b_i\cdot V$, because
$|a_i(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon|\leq C|\varepsilon|\to0$.
Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{T_\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon^2}
 =\sum_iw_i\frac{d_{i,\varepsilon}^2}{\varepsilon^2}
                 \int_0^1(1-t)e^{t d_{i,\varepsilon}}\,dt
 \longrightarrow\tfrac12V^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_b V.
\end{equation}
The centring hypothesis cancels the complete linear term, including
the order-$\varepsilon^2$ intercepts; no individual limit of
$a_i(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^2$ is required.
Moreover
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\frac{T_\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon^2}
 \leq\tfrac12(B\|V\|+C\varepsilon_0)^2
           e^{\varepsilon_0B\|V\|+C\varepsilon_0^2}
 =:D(V).
\end{equation}
The square of $D(V)$ is at most a finite constant times
$(1+\|V\|^4)e^{2\varepsilon_0B\|V\|}$ and is therefore integrable
by~\eqref{eq:general-dispersion-integrability}.
Since $0\leq\log T_\varepsilon\leq T_\varepsilon-1$ and
$\log(1+z)/z\to1$ at zero, $J_\varepsilon/\varepsilon^2$ has the
same pointwise limit and $L^2$ dominator.
The fundamental theorem of calculus writes the left-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:general-weak-L2} as
\begin{equation}
 \frac{J_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2}
       \int_0^1P'(m_\varepsilon+tJ_\varepsilon)\,dt.
\end{equation}
The integral converges almost surely to $P'(m_0)$ by continuity
and the base convergence, and its magnitude is at most $L$.
Dominated convergence with the integrable square $L^2D(V)^2$ proves
the $L^2$ limit. Cauchy--Schwarz proves convergence of first moments.
For the second moments I use
$|\E U^2-\E W^2|\leq\|U-W\|_2(\|U\|_2+\|W\|_2)$.
Subtracting the convergent squared means proves the variance expansion.
\end{proof}

Bounded latent scenario laws, centred Gaussian laws and suitable
exponentially integrable mixture laws can satisfy these hypotheses.
A moment-generating function finite at isolated arguments does not by
itself prove~\eqref{eq:general-dispersion-integrability}; the requisite
norm-exponential and polynomial moment must be checked. Degenerate
boundaries also matter. A symmetric one-dimensional two-point latent law
can make $V^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_bV$ constant. If its limiting base $m_0$
also has constant $P'(m_0)$, then $H_0$ is constant and the leading
fractional variance is zero. Constant quadratic form alone is
insufficient when $P'(m_0)$ remains random. The theorem does not replace
that boundary by a positive variance coefficient.

I next vary cross-sectional dispersion while retaining Gaussian
risk-neutral drifts. This is a financial specialisation of the general
theorem, and I retain its exact drift algebra and explicit constants.
Let $Y\sim N(0,I_p)$, fix positive weights, vectors
$b_i\in\R^p$ with $\sum_iw_i b_i=0$, a common residual loading
$R_0\in\R^p$, $\lambda>0$, and $c=r-d$. Set
\begin{align}
 R_i(\varepsilon)&=R_0+\varepsilon b_i,\qquad
 D_b=\sum_iw_i\|b_i\|^2,\\
 \mu_i(\varepsilon)&=c-\tfrac12\{
           \|R_i(\varepsilon)\|^2+\lambda^2\},\\
 \Sigma_b&=\sum_iw_i b_i b_i^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 Q(v)=v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_b v.
\end{align}
The normalised increasing conditional payoff kernel $P:\R\to[0,1]$ is
continuously differentiable with globally bounded derivative.
The capped-lognormal kernel satisfies these conditions at every
$\lambda>0$. Define the arithmetic and geometric log-baskets
$m_{A,\varepsilon}$ and $m_{G,\varepsilon}$ as above, and
$X_\varepsilon=P(m_{A,\varepsilon})-P(m_{G,\varepsilon})$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:weak-dispersion}
As $\varepsilon\to0$ through nonzero real values,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-L2}
 \frac{X_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2}
 \longrightarrow
 H_0(Y):=\tfrac12P'(m_0(Y))Q(Y-R_0)
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2,
\end{equation}
where
$m_0(Y)=c-\tfrac12(\|R_0\|^2+\lambda^2)+R_0\cdot Y$.
Consequently, with $p_\varepsilon=\E X_\varepsilon$,
\begin{align}
 p_\varepsilon&=\varepsilon^2\E H_0+o(\varepsilon^2),\\
 \operatorname{Var}(X_\varepsilon)
   &=\varepsilon^4\operatorname{Var}(H_0)+o(\varepsilon^4).
\end{align}
If $\E H_0>0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(H_0)>0$, these orders are
respectively $\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\Theta(\varepsilon^4)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Weighted centring of the loading and the drift gives the exact identities
\begin{align}
 m_{G,\varepsilon}
 &=m_0(Y)-\tfrac12\varepsilon^2D_b,\\
 d_{i,\varepsilon}(Y)
 &:=\mu_i(\varepsilon)-\sum_kw_k\mu_k(\varepsilon)
              +(R_i(\varepsilon)-R_0)\cdot Y\\
 &=\varepsilon u_i(Y)+\varepsilon^2v_i,\\
 u_i(Y)&=b_i\cdot(Y-R_0),\qquad
 v_i=-\tfrac12(\|b_i\|^2-D_b).
\end{align}
Both $\sum_iw_i u_i$ and $\sum_iw_i v_i$ are zero. Thus
$T_\varepsilon=\sum_iw_i e^{d_{i,\varepsilon}}\geq1$ and
$J_\varepsilon=m_{A,\varepsilon}-m_{G,\varepsilon}
=\log T_\varepsilon\geq0$.
At each fixed $Y$, Taylor's formula gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{T_\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon^2}
 \longrightarrow \tfrac12\sum_iw_i u_i(Y)^2
 =\tfrac12 Q(Y-R_0).
\end{equation}
For a detailed domination argument, the integral Taylor remainder
$e^z-1-z=z^2\int_0^1(1-t)e^{tz}\,dt$ implies
$0\leq e^z-1-z\leq z^2e^{|z|}/2$ for real $z$.
Since the weighted linear terms vanish,
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\frac{T_\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon^2}
 \leq \tfrac12\sum_iw_i
       (|u_i|+|v_i|)^2e^{|u_i|+|v_i|}
 =:D(Y),\qquad0<|\varepsilon|\leq1.
\end{equation}
Each $u_i$ is affine in $Y$. Hence
$D(Y)\leq C(1+\|Y\|^2)e^{C(1+\|Y\|)}$ for a finite constant $C$
depending only on the fixed coefficients. Its square is integrable under
the Gaussian law: completing the square in the radial Gaussian exponent
dominates any fixed linear exponential times a polynomial by an integrable
Gaussian with a smaller negative quadratic coefficient.
Since $0\leq\log T_\varepsilon\leq T_\varepsilon-1$ and
$\log(1+z)/z\to1$ as $z\downarrow0$, the same pointwise limit and
$L^2$ domination hold for $J_\varepsilon/\varepsilon^2$.

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{X_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2}
 =\frac{J_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2}
   \int_0^1P'(m_{G,\varepsilon}+tJ_\varepsilon)\,dt.
\end{equation}
At each $Y$, the integral converges to $P'(m_0(Y))$ by continuity.
Its absolute value is at most the global derivative bound $L$.
The product is dominated in square by $L^2D(Y)^2$.
Dominated convergence proves~\eqref{eq:weak-L2}.
An $L^2$ limit gives convergence of first moments by
Cauchy--Schwarz and convergence of second moments by
$|\E U^2-\E V^2|\leq\|U-V\|_2(\|U\|_2+\|V\|_2)$.
Subtracting the convergent squared means proves the variance expansion.
The asserted $\Theta$ orders follow when their leading coefficients are
strictly positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:weak-centred}
If $R_0=0$, $\Sigma_b\ne0$ and
$a=P'(c-\lambda^2/2)>0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \E H_0=\tfrac a2\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(H_0)=\tfrac{a^2}{2}
                         \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_b^2)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $m_0$ is constant and $H_0=aY^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_bY/2$.
The real symmetric positive semidefinite matrix $\Sigma_b$ has an
orthogonal diagonalisation with eigenvalues $\rho_j\geq0$.
The Gaussian density is invariant under an orthogonal transformation,
so $Y^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_bY$ has the law of
$\sum_j\rho_j Z_j^2$ with independent standard normals $Z_j$.
Integration by parts using $\varphi'(z)=-z\varphi(z)$ gives
$\E Z_j^2=1$ and $\E Z_j^4=3$; all boundary terms vanish because
a polynomial times the Gaussian density vanishes at infinity.
Thus $\operatorname{Var}(Z_j^2)=2$. Independence now gives
$\E Q=\sum_j\rho_j$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(Q)=2\sum_j\rho_j^2$, which proves the formulas.
At least one $\rho_j$ is positive when $\Sigma_b\ne0$.
\end{proof}

Let a canonical flag be
$B_\varepsilon=\mathbf1\{U<X_\varepsilon\}$ with an independent
uniform $U$ on $(0,1)$. The flag has exactly the Bernoulli law with
parameter $p_\varepsilon$. Conditioning on $Y$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-variance-loss}
 \operatorname{Var}(B_\varepsilon)
 =\operatorname{Var}(X_\varepsilon)+
                        \E[X_\varepsilon(1-X_\varepsilon)].
\end{equation}
It follows either by expanding $\E B_\varepsilon^2=p_\varepsilon$,
or by the conditional-variance formula since
$\E(B_\varepsilon\mid Y)=X_\varepsilon$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(B_\varepsilon\mid Y)
=X_\varepsilon(1-X_\varepsilon)$.
Under the nondegeneracy conditions above,
$\operatorname{Var}(B_\varepsilon)
 =\varepsilon^2\E H_0+o(\varepsilon^2)$.
The fractional output has variance of order $\varepsilon^4$, whereas
the canonical single-shot flag has variance of order $\varepsilon^2$.

The implication concerns a defined estimation experiment. For the
arithmetic sample mean of $N$ independent draws,
the relative mean-square errors are exactly
$\operatorname{Var}(X_\varepsilon)/(Np_\varepsilon^2)$ and
$p_\varepsilon(1-p_\varepsilon)/(Np_\varepsilon^2)$.
At a fixed relative error target these scale respectively as a constant
divided by $N$, and as a positive constant divided by
$N\varepsilon^2$. Chebyshev's inequality turns a proved variance bound
and a proved positive lower bound for $p_\varepsilon$ into a sufficient
relative-error confidence count. The theorem is not a lower bound for
all quantum estimators, and an amplitude-estimation algorithm exploits
coherent powers rather than independent unamplified flag averages.
Nor do I claim that the present bounded-range Bernstein formula yields
a dispersion-independent relative-error count: its linear range term
can dominate. At a fixed absolute monetary tolerance, the residual
eventually becomes smaller than that tolerance, so omitting its
estimation can become valid. I therefore retain the relative-residual
experiment when interpreting these weak-dispersion orders.

This identifies a loss of fractional variance information when only the
canonical flag is observed. It is not fundamental information loss in the
full coherent state or a universal quantum disadvantage.
At fixed numerical precision a grid or arithmetic error floor can dominate
the limit. Transferring the continuous result to a sequence of implemented
outputs requires a separately proved total $L^2$ implementation and
latent-law error $o(\varepsilon^2)$. A pointwise arithmetic error
$\eta_\varepsilon=o(\varepsilon^2)$ supplies its arithmetic component,
but a mean-bias bound alone does not supply the required latent-law
$L^2$ component. The original finite confidence certificates remain
separate from this asymptotic statement.
The mechanism is a project-specific derivation from established
Rao--Blackwell conditioning and small-dispersion analysis. A defensible
claim of a new contribution requires the literature comparison and
finite-parameter error evidence retained elsewhere in this project.


\section{Risk-neutral applications beyond the original terminal basket}
\label{sec:moment-applications}

The preceding moment theorem concerns a mathematical class, rather than an
extension obtained by substituting new data into the original GBM. Transform
controls and conditional Monte Carlo across general financial models already
have published antecedents \citep{dingecsakhormann}. I distinguish those established
methods from the finite-moment and encoding consequences investigated here.

\begin{theorem}[A certificate without an affine or Gaussian return model]
\label{thm:positive-asset-moment}
Let $S_1,\ldots,S_n$ be strictly positive random terminal asset values
under a stipulated pricing law, with $\E S_i^2<\infty$. Let positive
weights sum to one and set $A=\sum_iw_iS_i$, $G=\prod_iS_i^{w_i}$.
Let $F:\mathbb R_+\to[0,H]$ be increasing and $c_F$-Lipschitz,
and let $\mathcal H$ be any sub-$\sigma$-algebra. Define
\begin{equation}
 X_0=\{F(A)-F(G)\}/H,\qquad
 X=\E[X_0\mid\mathcal H],\qquad
 \Delta=\E[A-G\mid\mathcal H].
\end{equation}
Then $X\in[0,1]$ and
\begin{equation}
 \E X^2\leq\min\{1,c_F^2\E\Delta^2/H^2\}
 \leq\min\{1,c_F^2\E(A-G)^2/H^2\}.
\end{equation}
For a finite-law implementation $\widehat X\in[0,1]$ with a
proved pointwise error $|\widehat X-X|\leq\eta$, its variance is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat X)
 \leq\min\{1/4,(\sqrt{\min\{1,c_F^2\E\Delta^2/H^2\}}+\eta)^2\},
\end{equation}
provided the expectation in this last expression concerns the same
ideal finite law. A continuous-law expectation cannot be substituted
without a separate distribution-transfer argument.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Weighted arithmetic--geometric ordering gives $A\geq G>0$.
The payoff conditions yield
$0\leq X_0\leq\min\{1,c_F(A-G)/H\}$.
Conditional expectation preserves these inequalities, so
$0\leq X\leq\min\{1,c_F\Delta/H\}$.
The square-moment assertions follow by squaring and then by conditional
Jensen's inequality, $\Delta^2\leq\E[(A-G)^2\mid\mathcal H]$.
All expectations exist: convexity gives
$A^2\leq\sum_iw_iS_i^2$, while $0\leq A-G\leq A$.
The pointwise implementation assumption gives
$\|\widehat X\|_2\leq\|X\|_2+\eta$ by the triangle inequality
in $L^2$. Subtracting the squared mean and using the bounded-range
variance bound $1/4$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For the capped call spread $c_F=1$. The unconditioned case takes
$\mathcal H$ to contain all terminal asset information; a tractable
conditioning factor can reduce the square moment further. This statement
does not require a log-affine model, independence or a moment-generating
function. Its practical use still requires a computable conservative
moment certificate and a checked numerical law. The subsequent transform
formula is one such route, rather than an assertion that all financial
models satisfy its moment assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[The correct general risk-neutral shift]
\label{prop:mgf-risk-neutral}
Let $\psi(v)=\log\Lambda(v)$ be finite and twice continuously differentiable
on a neighbourhood of every $R_0+\varepsilon b_i$ for
$|\varepsilon|\leq\varepsilon_0$, with its Hessian bounded on those
compact line segments. Let $Z\sim N(0,1)$ be independent of $Y$ and define
the rebased terminal assets
\begin{equation}
 S_{i,\varepsilon}=K\exp\{\mu_i(\varepsilon)
                  +(R_0+\varepsilon b_i)\cdot Y+\lambda Z\},\qquad
 \mu_i(\varepsilon)=r-d-\lambda^2/2-\psi(R_0+\varepsilon b_i).
\end{equation}
Then $\E S_{i,\varepsilon}=Ke^{r-d}$.
Suppose $w_i>0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$, $\sum_iw_i b_i=0$, and the
integrability hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-weak-dispersion} holds for
$V=Y-\nabla\psi(R_0)$. Let $P:\R\to[0,1]$ be increasing,
continuously differentiable and have a globally bounded derivative. The
leading fractional residual is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{X_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2}
 \longrightarrow
 \tfrac12P'(m_0(Y))\sum_iw_i
       \{b_i\cdot(Y-\nabla\psi(R_0))\}^2
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2,
\end{equation}
where $m_0(Y)=r-d-\lambda^2/2-\psi(R_0)+R_0\cdot Y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Independence and the normal exponential moment give
$\E S_{i,\varepsilon}
=Ke^{\mu_i(\varepsilon)}\Lambda(R_0+\varepsilon b_i)
e^{\lambda^2/2}=Ke^{r-d}$.
Taylor's integral formula writes
\begin{equation}
 \psi(R_0+\varepsilon b_i)
 =\psi(R_0)+\varepsilon\nabla\psi(R_0)\cdot b_i
       +\varepsilon^2 e_i(\varepsilon),\qquad
 e_i(\varepsilon)=\int_0^1(1-t)
 b_i^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2\psi(R_0+t\varepsilon b_i)b_i\,dt.
\end{equation}
The Hessian hypothesis bounds every $e_i$ uniformly. Subtracting the
weighted geometric log gives the exact relative logs
\begin{equation}
 D_{i,\varepsilon}
 =\varepsilon b_i\cdot(Y-\nabla\psi(R_0))
 -\varepsilon^2\{e_i(\varepsilon)-\sum_kw_ke_k(\varepsilon)\}.
\end{equation}
Their intercepts have weighted mean zero and are uniformly
$O(\varepsilon^2)$. The geometric base converges almost surely to
$m_0(Y)$ because the weighted linear Taylor term is zero.
The hypotheses therefore match
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-weak-dispersion}; its $L^2$ conclusion proves
the assertion. No Gaussian drift is reused after changing the latent law.
\end{proof}

This drift calculation establishes consistency of the stipulated pricing
measure. It does not calibrate that measure or validate an observed exotic
price. Rebased components allow the moment theorem to concern portfolio
returns, while its unrestricted affine constants also accommodate unequal
initial asset levels in the non-asymptotic result. The weak-dispersion
specialisation requires the stated common limiting base.

\subsection{A variance-gamma financial example and its moment domain}

The symmetric variance-gamma construction is established financial
modelling \citep{madan}. I use $Y=\sqrt G\,N$, where $N\sim N(0,1)$,
$G$ is independent with gamma shape $1/\nu$ and scale $\nu$, and
$\nu=0.4$. I derive the required terminal moments, rather than transfer
an empirical claim from that source. With $k=1/\nu$, the gamma density is
$g^{k-1}e^{-g/\nu}/\{\Gamma(k)\nu^k\}$ for $g>0$. Conditional on $G$,
$\E(e^{tY}\mid G)=e^{t^2G/2}$. Its integral is
\begin{equation}
 M_Y(t)=\frac1{\Gamma(k)\nu^k}
 \int_0^\infty g^{k-1}e^{-(1/\nu-t^2/2)g}\,dg
 =(1-\nu t^2/2)^{-1/\nu},\qquad |t|<\sqrt{2/\nu}.
\end{equation}
The equality follows by changing variables in the defining gamma
integral; outside the stated open domain the necessary integral is
not finite. Gamma recursion gives $\E G=1$ and
$\E G^2=1+\nu$. Conditioning on $G$ therefore gives
$\E Y^2=1$ and $\E Y^4=3(1+\nu)=4.2$.

For equal weights and $b_1=-0.2,b_2=0.2$, $R_0=0$,
$\varepsilon_0=1$, the norm-exponential constant is
$2\varepsilon_0B=0.4$. For any $\delta>0$ with
$0.4+\delta<\sqrt5$,
$\E e^{(0.4+\delta)|Y|}\leq
M_Y(0.4+\delta)+M_Y(-0.4-\delta)<\infty$.
The exponential series gives
$|Y|^4\leq4!\delta^{-4}e^{\delta|Y|}$, so the exact
polynomial-times-exponential hypothesis is satisfied.
The drift is $r-d-\lambda^2/2-\log M_Y(0.2\varepsilon)$.
In this particular centred symmetric example the geometric base is
constant before the common normal shock, so its correction remains
lognormal. For a general non-Gaussian $R_0\cdot Y$, the geometric
correction requires its own law-specific pricing certificate; the
original Gaussian correction cannot simply be reused.
This is a correctly normalised non-Gaussian terminal pricing family;
the original fixed Gaussian drifts would give a different model.

In this symmetric family the relative log ratio is
$J_\varepsilon=\log\cosh(0.2\varepsilon Y)$ and the geometric base
is deterministic before integrating the independent common normal shock.
Writing $a=P'(r-d-\lambda^2/2)>0$, the exact limit coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 \E H_0=0.02a,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(H_0)=0.0004a^2\operatorname{Var}(Y^2).
\end{equation}
The variance factors are $2$ for a standard normal, $0.8$ for a
standardised uniform law, $3.2$ for this gamma-normal mixture, and zero
for a symmetric two-point law. In the last case $Y^2=1$ and the
entire conditional fractional payoff is deterministic, not just its
leading coefficient. A flag drawn at that constant probability can still
have positive variance. This is a boundary of the estimator comparison;
it is not a lower bound on a quantum method that uses the full value oracle.

\subsection{Monitored Asian baskets and a common Gaussian factor}

The general affine-log-price theorem can also cover a finite collection
of monitored GBM prices with positive averaging weights. Geometric
average controls and quantum Asian valuation are established
\citep{kemna,rebentrost}. Let their non-singular joint Gaussian
log-covariance be $\mathcal C$. For a common load $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-factor-domain}
 \mathcal C-\lambda^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\succeq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lambda^2\leq(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathcal C^{-1}\mathbf1)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x)^2\leq
(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathcal C^{-1}\mathbf1)(x^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Cx)$,
proving sufficiency. Necessity follows by substituting
$x=\mathcal C^{-1}\mathbf1$ into the quadratic-form inequality.
A square root of the residual covariance then constructs independent
$Y,Z$ with log prices $a_i+B_i\cdot Y+\lambda Z$.
Thus the moment theorem applies to the resulting arithmetic/geometric
monitored average once its kernel and moments are supplied.
Singular monitoring covariances require a separate range condition;
I do not assert~\eqref{eq:common-factor-domain} using an inverse where
none exists. This application states a mathematical scope beyond the
original terminal experiments, rather than claiming to have executed
new Asian circuits or demonstrated scalability on market data.

\label{sec:finite-range}

I now certify the support of the finite numerical oracle rather than

\subsection{Exact latent support and the vertex maximum}

Let the emitted rounded marginal table, after removing its offset, consist
of integer values \(z_0,\ldots,z_{2^b-1}\), representing
\(2^{-q}z_j\). Its exact constructor is symmetric and increasing.
The independently checked maximum is therefore
\(v=2^{-q}z_{2^b-1}\), and every outer latent coordinate belongs to
\([-v,v]\). This is a statement about the actual integer table, whose
hash is retained. It is not a statement about the largest value of an
unbounded Gaussian random variable.

Write the frozen weights as \(w_i>0\), \(\sum_iw_i=1\), the outer
loadings as \(r_i\), and the log drifts as \(\mu_i\). Define
\(r_G=\sum_iw_i r_i\), \(\mu_G=\sum_iw_i\mu_i\),
\(s_i=r_i-r_G\), and \(d_i=\mu_i-\mu_G\). The ideal arithmetic to
geometric basket ratio at an exact rounded latent input \(y\) is
\[
 J(y)=\log\frac{A(y)}{G(y)}
     =\log\left(\sum_i w_i\exp(d_i+s_i\cdot y)\right).
                                                               \tag{R1}
\]
The shared inner factor cancels from this ratio. Since
\(\sum_iw_i d_i=0\) and \(\sum_iw_i s_i=0\), Jensen's inequality
for the exponential gives \(J(y)\geq0\).

For a direct convexity proof, put
\(\pi_i(y)=w_i\exp(d_i+s_i\cdot y)/\sum_jw_j\exp(d_j+s_j\cdot y)\).
Then
\[
 \nabla J=\sum_i\pi_i s_i,\qquad
 \nabla^2J=\sum_i\pi_i s_i s_i^{\mathsf T}
             -\left(\sum_i\pi_i s_i\right)
              \left(\sum_i\pi_i s_i\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For every vector \(h\), the corresponding quadratic form is
\(\sum_i\pi_i(h\cdot s_i)^2-(\sum_i\pi_i h\cdot s_i)^2\),
a nonnegative variance. Thus \(J\) is convex. For \(v>0\), every
\(y\in[-v,v]^p\) is the convex combination of the vertices
\(v\sigma\), \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^p\), with coefficients
\[
 \lambda_\sigma(y)=\prod_{j=1}^p\frac{1+\sigma_j y_j/v}{2}.
\]
These coefficients are nonnegative, sum to one, and have weighted vertex
mean \(y\). Convexity therefore proves
\[
 0\leq J(y)\leq
 J_*:=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^p}J(v\sigma).               \tag{R2}
\]
If \(v=0\), the box has one point and the same statement uses \(J(0)\).
The vertex search is exhaustive in the finite family \(p\leq7\);
it is not a numerical optimiser whose termination is assumed to find a
global maximum. The geometric outer log price has the independent bound
\[
 m_G(y)=\mu_G+r_G\cdot y
       \leq m_G^*:=\mu_G+v\sum_j\lvert r_{G,j}\rvert.        \tag{R3}
\]
The maxima in (R2) and (R3) need not occur at the same vertex. Multiplying
their separate nonnegative bounds below remains conservative.

\subsection{Method-specific ideal residual bounds}

Let \(K\) be the strike, \(H>0\) the payoff cap, \(h=H/K\), and
\(F_H(s)=\min((s-K)_+,H)\). The normalised capped payoff is one-Lipschitz
after scaling prices by \(H\). More precisely,
\[
 0\leq\frac{F_H(s)-F_H(t)}{H}\leq\frac{s-t}{H}
 \quad(s\geq t>0).
\]
As a function of log price, \(F_H(S_0e^m)/H\) has derivative
\(S_0e^m/H\) only while \(K<S_0e^m<K+H\), and derivative zero
outside that interval. Its global log-price Lipschitz constant is therefore
\(L_H=(K+H)/H=(1+h)/h\). This conclusion also follows by integrating
the bounded derivative across the two kinks.

For the direct method, the rounded inner table has exact support
\([-v_Z,v_Z]\), and the declared shared loading satisfies \(\lambda>0\).
The ideal rounded-grid normalised residual is bounded uniformly by
\[
 U_{\mathrm{direct}}=
 \min\left\{1,\ L_HJ_*,\
  \frac{S_0}{H}\exp(m_G^*+\lambda v_Z)(e^{J_*}-1)\right\}.   \tag{R4}
\]
Indeed, the arithmetic price equals the geometric price times \(e^{J(y)}\),
so its positive gap is at most the last expression. The other two terms
follow from the payoff cap and the log-price Lipschitz bound.

For a fixed strike sampled in the interval method, the residual is the
difference of two Gaussian distribution functions whose arguments differ
by \(J(y)/\lambda\). The Gaussian density is at most
\(\varphi(0)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\). Hence
\[
 U_{\mathrm{interval}}=
 \min\{1,\ \varphi(0)J_*/\lambda\}.                       \tag{R5}
\]
This bound is uniform in the strike uniform, so the vertex search does not
need to enumerate that additional input.

For either fully conditioned method, averaging over the inner Gaussian
and over the capped payoff preserves the log-price slope bound \(L_H\)
and also gives the slope bound \(\varphi(0)/\lambda\).
The latter follows by writing the cap payoff as an integral of strike
indicators over \([K,K+H]\), dividing by \(H\), and differentiating
the bounded Gaussian distribution function. The positive price-gap bound
uses \(\mathbb E e^{\lambda Z}=e^{\lambda^2/2}\). Consequently,
\[
 U_{\mathrm{full}}=
 \min\left\{1,
   \min\{\varphi(0)/\lambda,L_H\}J_*,
   \frac{S_0}{H}\exp(m_G^*+\lambda^2/2)(e^{J_*}-1)\right\}.  \tag{R6}
\]
Equations (R4)--(R6) bound the ideal functions evaluated at the actual
rounded outer latent coordinates. They do not remove function-table
approximation, arithmetic truncation, or the continuous distributional
transfer allowance.

the eight weighted terms and their additions together give a conservative
absolute exponential-sum centre error below \(10^{-206}\).
The exact sum is at least one, so a perturbation of that size remains above
one half and the logarithm amplifies it by at most two. The subsequent
positive-gap candidate involves two exponentials with arguments of magnitude
at most 20 in the independently checked family and a coefficient
\(S_0/H\leq100\). Allowing all their conversions, products and
subtractions, the deliberately loose centre-error bound \(10^{-190}\)
still dominates those errors. The density slope divides only by
\(\lambda\geq0.05\), and its other denominator has \(h\geq0.01\),
so it does not invalidate that bound. Each added \(10^{-100}\)
allowance dominates this finite computation bound by ninety decimal orders.
Maximisation and minimisation select stored real enclosures and add no
new rounding operation. Weighted
arithmetic--geometric order keeps the exact exponential sum at least one,
so the logarithm does not amplify a small denominator. The independent
audit evaluates scaled log-sum-exp at 450 digits, uses a distinct positive
series for \(\pi\), and checks the retained upper enclosures against
all reference vertices with an explicit \(10^{-350}\) reference envelope.
That high-precision calculation supplements the numerical premise and
the uniform analytical proof; it is not a claim of a formally verified
implementation of Decimal itself.

To specify the density calculation's numerical premise in detail, the
generator evaluates Machin's identity
\(\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)\) by the alternating series
\(\arctan x=\sum_{n\geq0}(-1)^n x^{2n+1}/(2n+1)\).
Its loop includes the first power no greater than \(10^{-230}\).
For \(x=1/5\), it takes at most 166 updates; for \(x=1/239\),
at most 49. The margins at those powers are much larger than their
relative rounding errors, so these bounds also hold for the emitted
recurrences. For \(k\) successive rounded operations, the standard
product bound follows directly by expanding the product of their
\((1+\delta_j)\) factors:
\[
 \left\lvert\prod_{j=1}^k(1+\delta_j)-1\right\rvert
 \leq\frac{ku}{1-ku}=: \gamma_k\quad
 (\lvert\delta_j\rvert\leq u,\ ku<1).
\]
Each power update has at most two rounded multiplications. Since
\(\sum_{n\geq0}x^{2n+1}\leq x/(1-x^2)<0.21\) for both
arguments, the power, term-division and summation errors together are
bounded conservatively by
\(0.21(\gamma_{332}+\gamma_{167}+3u)<230u\).
The second argument's initial reciprocal error is at most \(u/239\);
the derivative of arctangent has magnitude at most one, so adding \(u\)
covers this perturbation. The alternating remainder is at most
\(10^{-230}\). Multiplying by the Machin coefficients and charging
their products and subtraction gives a pi error less than
\(20(231u+10^{-230})+100u<10^{-210}\).
The exact and computed pi therefore remain above three minus
\(10^{-210}\). The derivative of \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\) there is less
than 0.08 in magnitude. The correctly rounded reciprocal and square root
add less than \(10u\) absolute error. Thus the computed
\(\varphi(0)\) has absolute error below \(10^{-210}\).
Division by \(\lambda\geq0.05\) amplifies it by at most twenty,
which remains covered by the stated \(10^{-190}\) aggregate centre
bound before the \(10^{-100}\) outward pad.

For the other centre-error claims, exact rational exponent conversion has
absolute error at most \(20u\). The mean-value theorem and correct
rounding bound each exponential's error by
\(e^{20+20u}(20u)+u e^{20+20u}<6\times10^9u\).
Converting a weight in \([0,1]\), multiplying it by that exponential,
and summing at most eight positive terms contributes less than
\(10^{-206}\) in total. Dividing the logarithmic perturbation by a sum
at least one half and charging the rounded logarithm gives less than
\(3\times10^{-206}\). Every vertex obeys that bound, so taking the
maximum does too. The frozen weights have minimum at least 0.04. At least
one centred affine exponent is nonpositive because their weighted mean
is zero; consequently,
\(J_*\leq\log(0.96e^{20}+0.04)<19.96\).
Thus even its padded centre remains below 20. The independent audit also
checks the geometric-price exponential arguments in (R4) and (R6) against
the bound 20. Their conversions and products, two exponentials no greater
than \(5\times10^8\), and the coefficient at most 100 give an absolute
candidate centre error below \(10^{-190}\), including cancellation in
\(e^{\overline J_*}-1\): an absolute-error estimate is used, so a small
gap does not invalidate it. The slope product, final sum and bounded
normalisations satisfy the same loose bound. Every such candidate receives
an outward \(10^{-100}\) addition before the minimum is taken.
These estimates prove the numerical enclosure on the stated finite
parameter domain conditional on correct rounding of the declared Decimal
primitives; they do not extrapolate that envelope to arbitrary parameters.

\subsection{Exact transfer to mean and second-moment priors}

Let \(R=M2^{-q}\), and suppose previous certificates give
\(m\geq\mathbb E X\) and \(s\geq\mathbb E[X^2]\).
The support inequality \(X^2\leq RX\) gives the valid initial
tightenings
\[
 m_0=\min(m,R),\qquad s_0=\min(s,Rm_0,R^2).                \tag{R8}
\]
If \(M=0\), the finite output is identically zero and both bounds are
zero. Otherwise, write \(\delta=2^{-q}\). For every integer
\(a\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\) and every integer \(j\geq0\),
\((a-j)(a-j-1)\geq0\). For adjacent grid points
\(x=j\delta\), \(y=(j+1)\delta\), this proves the exact pointwise
linear moment certificate
\[
 X^2\geq(x+y)X-xy,
 \qquad \mathbb E X\leq\frac{s_0+xy}{x+y}.                \tag{R9}
\]
When \(s_0<R^2\), choose the unique \(j\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\)
with \(j^2\delta^2\leq s_0<(j+1)^2\delta^2\). The right side
of (R9) is the inverse of the lower convex hull of the finite squared-value
graph at \(s_0\). It is attained in the unrestricted lattice support
class by a mixture of \(x\) and \(y\) with second moment \(s_0\).
For \(j=0\), that mixture uses zero and \(\delta\), and (R9) reads
\(\mathbb E X\leq s_0/\delta\). If \(s_0\geq R^2\), the support
bound \(R\) is the corresponding hull bound. Denoting this inverse by
\(H_q(s_0,M)\), I retain
\[
 m_1=\min(m_0,H_q(s_0,M)),\qquad
 s_1=\min(s_0,Rm_1).                                      \tag{R10}
\]
Every step applies a pointwise inequality or a previously valid moment
bound, so it remains an upper bound for the actual finite financial law.
Sharpness is asserted only over all probability laws on the permitted
lattice, not over the narrower values attained by a particular financial
circuit. The circuit need not realise the extremising mixture. These
fair mean and second-moment priors are supplied to every eligible encoding,
including the single-flag range and clipping opponents.

The retained production-point checks evaluate the exact integer graph at
latent vertices, central and boundary addresses and reproducible random
inputs, then independently cross-check selected outputs using unbounded
Python integer replay. They can reveal a construction or coding failure,
but their maxima are not substituted for the uniform proof in (R7).

\section{Exact numerical values and coherent dyadic buckets}

I distinguish a numerical-value oracle from an oracle that prepares only a
Bernoulli flag. This distinction is necessary because a variance-sensitive
mean-estimation result formulated in terms of numerical values does not
automatically apply to an already Bernoulli-encoded payoff. Throughout this
section, the previously certified finite graph determines an integer function
\(a:\mathcal L\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,2^q\}\), where
\(\mathcal L\) is the finite uniform latent grid and \(q\geq1\).
The arithmetic, function tables and distributional approximation are fixed.
I set \(X(x)=2^{-q}a(x)\). The dyadic decomposition below is an exact
identity for this finite random variable. I do not identify that elementary
identity, the compute--copy--uncompute construction, or the established
amplitude-amplification geometry \citep{brassard} as a new theoretical
contribution.

\subsection{Clean numerical-value oracle}

Let \(B\) be the emitted financial-body computation. Its computational-basis
action, on initially clean private work, has the form
\[
 B\lvert x\rangle\lvert0\rangle_W
 =\lvert x\rangle\lvert h_x\rangle_W,
\]
where a declared \((q+1)\)-bit subregister of \(h_x\) contains \(a(x)\).
The remaining body history is retained until it is uncomputed. Every
arithmetic atom is an exact Boolean permutation. Every lookup macro has the
phase-correct logical XOR action proved in the instrument section, whose
published lookup and temporary-AND antecedents are
\citet{babbush} and \citet{gidney}, including
arbitrary coherent dirty output registers. Its private selector work is
reset by the corrected erasure. Each measurement-record Kraus operator is a
scalar, independent of its logical input, times that XOR action. These facts
also hold when the logical input is entangled with an external reference.
Consequently, composing the body implements the corresponding coherent
logical map without revealing \(x\) or any numerical intermediate.

Allocate a distinct \((q+1)\)-bit target \(Z\) before allocating the body
work. Copy the value subregister to \(Z\) using one CNOT per value bit, then
apply the logical inverse of \(B\). In the measured architecture, a lookup
inverse is a fresh corrected XOR instrument. It is not a reversal of a
measurement record. On basis states the resulting value oracle is
\[
 V\lvert x\rangle\lvert z\rangle_Z\lvert0\rangle_W
 =\lvert x\rangle\lvert z\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}a(x)\rangle_Z
   \lvert0\rangle_W.                                      \tag{V1}
\]
The body is independent of \(z\), and the copy leaves the body value
unchanged. The inverse therefore clears exactly the same history that the
forward body produced. This proves (V1) for every dirty target \(z\), not
merely \(z=0\). By linearity and the input-independent Kraus factors,
(V1) holds on arbitrary coherent superpositions with an external reference.
The logical action is an XOR involution on the clean-work subspace. A fresh
instance implements \(V^\dagger\) there. These claims do not assert that
the operational measurement tape is unitary on arbitrary dirty private work.

\subsection{A fully specified bucket predicate}

For \(b\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\), define
\[
 D_b(a)=\boldsymbol1\{2^b\leq a<2^{b+1}\},\qquad
 g_b(a,u)=D_b(a)\boldsymbol1\{u<a\},
 \quad 0\leq u<2^{b+1}.
\]
I use a distinct \((b+1)\)-bit comparison register for \(u\). Write
\(a=\sum_{j=0}^q2^j a_j\). Membership is equivalent to
\(a_b=1\) and \(a_{b+1}=\cdots=a_q=0\). I temporarily negate the
\(q-b\) higher bits and compute the conjunction of
\(a_b,\ldots,a_q\) into a clean ladder of \(q-b\) targets.
This requires \(q-b\) exact Toffoli gates. The lowest \((b+1)\)
bits of \(a\) are unaffected by those negations. I compute the borrow bit
of the unsigned subtraction
\(u-(a\bmod2^{b+1})\) using the existing explicitly emitted ripple
construction. A Toffoli controlled by membership and borrow XORs
\(g_b\) into the good flag. I then reverse the complete predicate history,
excluding that final flag operation.

The unsigned borrow is one precisely when
\(u<a\bmod2^{b+1}\). On the membership branch, \(a<2^{b+1}\),
so this is \(u<a\). On every other branch, the membership control is
zero. Thus the flag is correct even when the truncated comparison alone
has no financial meaning. The inverse clears every predicate carry,
conjunction target and temporary negation, while preserving \(a\), \(u\)
and the marked flag. The predicate therefore has the exact logical action
\[
 P_b\lvert a,u,f\rangle\lvert0\rangle_J
 =\lvert a,u,f\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}g_b(a,u)\rangle
   \lvert0\rangle_J.                                      \tag{V2}
\]
For the top bucket, set \(D_q(a)=\boldsymbol1\{a=2^q\}\).
The certified range \(0\leq a\leq2^q\) implies \(D_q(a)=a_q\).
I therefore use a single CNOT from \(a_q\) to the flag, with no comparison
register and no conjunction ladder. On basis states outside the certified
range, this circuit remains a Boolean permutation, but its financial
top-bucket interpretation is not asserted.

In the lower buckets, the ripple construction uses three Toffoli gates,
four CNOT gates and one X gate per bit in its forward history. That history
and the membership ladder are reversed after marking the flag. Hence the
exact predicate counts are
\[
 N_{\mathrm{CCX}}(P_b)=2(q-b)+6(b+1)+1=2q+4b+7,
 \quad N_{\mathrm{CX}}(P_b)=8(b+1),
 \quad N_{\mathrm X}(P_b)=2(q+1).                          \tag{V3}
\]
The predicate uses \(q+2b+4\) clean temporary wires beyond its value,
comparison and flag registers under the declared ripple construction.
At the top bucket, the counts are one CNOT and no Toffoli or X gates.
These are construction-specific counts, not lower bounds or optimality claims.

\subsection{Exact Born probabilities and price transfer}

Apply Hadamards to all latent input bits and, for \(b<q\), to all
\((b+1)\) comparison bits. Compute the financial body, apply (V2),
and uncompute the financial body. All body and predicate work returns to
zero. Denote the resulting preparation by \(A_b\). For a fixed latent
state \(x\) in bucket \(b\), exactly \(a(x)\) of the
\(2^{b+1}\) equally weighted comparison integers satisfy \(u<a(x)\).
The Born probability of the good flag is therefore exactly
\[
 p_b=\mathbb E\!\left[
    \frac{a}{2^{b+1}}D_b(a)\right],\quad 0\leq b<q,
 \qquad p_q=\mathbb P(a=2^q).                             \tag{V4}
\]
No arbitrary rotation, continuous probability oracle or free QRAM is used.
Each nonzero integer below \(2^q\) has a unique highest set bit.
Zero belongs to none of the buckets and contributes zero. Consequently,
with \(r_b=2^{b+1-q}\) for \(b<q\) and \(r_q=1\),
\[
 \mathbb E X=\sum_{b=0}^{q}r_b p_b.                       \tag{V5}
\]
This is a finite exact decomposition, including the mass at one. Any
statistical intervals for the \(p_b\) can be propagated through this
nonnegative linear identity. The already proved deterministic currency
error between the continuous financial expectation and \(\mathbb E X\)
is unchanged by (V5); partitioning the same finite values adds no bias.

\subsection{Coherent amplification and the two oracle cost models}

After \(A_b\), the financial and predicate work is clean. The invariant
subspace consists of the latent, comparison and flag registers with that
work fixed to zero. Let \(S_0=I-2\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert\)
on those active logical registers and let \(S_g\) negate the good flag.
Then \(Q_b=-A_b S_0 A_b^\dagger S_g\) has the usual two-dimensional
amplification action. To make the probability identity explicit, for
\(0<p_b<1\) define the normalised bad and good projections
\(\lvert B\rangle\) and \(\lvert G\rangle\) of
\(A_b\lvert0\rangle\), and set
\(\theta=\arcsin\sqrt{p_b}\). In the ordered basis
\((\lvert B\rangle,\lvert G\rangle)\),
\[
 A_b\lvert0\rangle=
 \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta\\\sin\theta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Q_b=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\theta&-\sin2\theta\\
                    \sin2\theta& \cos2\theta\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Multiplying this rotation \(k\) times proves that the flag probability
after \(Q_b^kA_b\) is \(\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)\).
The endpoint cases follow directly or by continuity. A controlled iterate
uses the chronological gates
\(\mathrm{CZ}_{c,f}\), \(A_b^\dagger\), controlled \(S_0\),
\(A_b\), and \(Z_c\). At control zero the two preparations cancel.
At control one, \(Z_c\) supplies the conditional global minus. Only the
reflections need added controls; the financial arithmetic is not promoted
to multiply controlled gates. Corrected lookup instruments are valid in
these coherent compositions because every measurement-record branch is
proportional to the same logical map, including on entangled inputs.

Let \(T_B\) be the fully charged T count of one financial body, including
its instantiated lookup architecture. The separately available value oracle
has \(T_V=2T_B\) and \((q+1)\) additional value-copy CNOT gates.
The fused known-body preparation computes the body once, marks the bucket,
then uncomputes that body. Under the declared seven-T exact Toffoli
decomposition, (V3) gives
\[
 T_{A_b}=2T_B+7(2q+4b+7)\quad(b<q),\qquad T_{A_q}=2T_B.     \tag{V6}
\]
In contrast, treating \(V\) as a numerical-value black box and emitting
\(V;P_b;V^\dagger\) gives
\[
 T_{A_b}^{\mathrm{blackbox}}=4T_B+7(2q+4b+7)\quad(b<q),
 \qquad T_{A_q}^{\mathrm{blackbox}}=4T_B,                    \tag{V7}
\]
with two value-copy sets of CNOT gates and a held value register.
I retain both cost models. A numerical-value query theorem cannot silently
receive the lower fused cost without assuming access to the financial body.

If \(L\) is the number of latent input bits, the lower-bucket reflection
acts on \(L+b+2\) logical qubits. Its clean conjunction ladder uses
\(2(L+b)\) exact Toffoli gates. Thus
\[
 T_{Q_b}=2T_{A_b}+14(L+b)\quad(b<q),\qquad
 T_{Q_q}=2T_{A_q}+14(L-1).                                 \tag{V8}
\]
The uniform Hadamard counts per preparation are \(L+b+1\) and \(L\),
respectively. These formulas retain all body, predicate and reflection
inverse gates. In the measured lookup mode, each selector erasure also
contributes a measurement, conditional CZ and conditional reset X; all
are multiplied by the actual number of forward and inverse body uses.
The final flag measurement and whole-shot reinitialisation remain separate
from the oracle-internal counts. Physical measurement latency, error
correction overhead and hardware execution time are not inferred from
logical T counts.

The retained resource audit expands each exact CCX into its actual seven-T
gate sequence. It reports the literal dependency schedule, a conservative
five-layer-per-CCX serial bound, and the historical assigned three-layer
profile separately. It independently emits both directions of each cached
financial block, so equality of forward and inverse depth is not assumed.
For production graphs, the counts compose fully emitted blocks and the
fully emitted predicate; they do not require retaining a full production
gate tape. Complete small-graph tapes are compared against those block
counts, and their Boolean and state-vector semantics are retained.

\subsection{Fair single-flag range and clipping opponents}

The dyadic construction must be compared with a competent single-flag
construction supplied with the same certified information. Suppose first
that a separate deterministic certificate proves \(a\leq2^c\) on every
latent grid state, with \(0\leq c\leq q\). For \(c>0\), I prepare
\(c\) uniform comparison bits and append a clean zero high bit. I compare
this \((c+1)\)-bit integer with the lowest \((c+1)\) value bits.
The zero extension is essential at the possible endpoint \(a=2^c\).
The resulting Born probability is \(p_c=\mathbb E[a]/2^c\), and
\(\mathbb E X=2^{c-q}p_c\). At \(c=0\), the range promise is
\(a\in\{0,1\}\); a single CNOT from the value bit gives this
probability without comparison work. Under the declared ripple construction,
the predicate uses \(6(c+1)\) exact Toffoli gates for \(c>0\),
and none for \(c=0\). It has no approximation or clipping bias when its
range certificate is valid. That certificate is a support bound, not a
mean or variance estimate, and is retained separately from the circuit counter.

A second opponent does not require a smaller support promise. Prepare
\(c\) uniform comparison bits and pad them with \(q+1-c\) clean zero
bits. Compare this full \((q+1)\)-bit integer with all the value bits.
For every \(a\in\{0,\ldots,2^q\}\), including \(c=0\),
the number of comparison integers below \(a\) is
\(\min(a,2^c)\). Setting \(\rho=2^{c-q}\) therefore gives
\[
 p_c^{\mathrm{clip}}=\frac{\mathbb E[\min(X,\rho)]}{\rho}.
                                                               \tag{V9}
\]
The numerical payoff register is not explicitly clamped. The finite
comparison creates the clipping exactly. Its predicate uses
\(6(q+1)\) Toffoli gates, \(8(q+1)+1\) CNOT gates and
\(2(q+1)\) X gates for every \(c\). The clean padding is private
work and is excluded from the logical all-zero reflection. The uniform
Hadamard and reflection costs decrease with \(c\), while the financial
body and full comparison counts remain charged. This opponent is valid
on the entire certified original value range. Its omitted excess-tail mean
must be included in the error allowance; it cannot be declared an unbiased
estimator of the unclipped payoff.

For completeness, an elementary sharp pointwise second-moment bound supplies
one such allowance. Let \(0\leq X\leq R\), \(R>0\), and assume
that a proved bound \(s_2\geq\mathbb E[X^2]\) is available. For
\(0<\rho<R\), the largest ratio
\((x-\rho)_+/x^2\) on \(0\leq x\leq R\) is
\[
 \gamma_R(\rho)=
 \begin{cases}
   (4\rho)^{-1},&2\rho\leq R,\\
   (R-\rho)/R^2,&\rho<R<2\rho.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{V10}
\]
Indeed, the ratio is zero up to \(\rho\), and its derivative above
\(\rho\) is \((2\rho-x)/x^3\). It increases up to \(2\rho\)
and then decreases, proving both cases by maximisation. Equivalently, the
first case follows from \((x-2\rho)^2\geq0\). If
\(\rho\geq R\), the excess tail is identically zero. Thus
\(\tau=\mathbb E[(X-\rho)_+]\leq\gamma_R(\rho)s_2\)
when \(\rho<R\). For a certified mean bound
\(m\geq\mathbb E X\), the pointwise inequality
\((x-\rho)_+\leq(1-\rho/R)x\) also gives
\(\tau\leq(1-\rho/R)m\). The minimum of these valid bounds may be
used. Here \(s_2\) is a second-moment bound, not a variance bound;
substituting \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\) for \(\mathbb E[X^2]\)
would be incorrect. 

If \(0\leq\tau\leq U_\tau\) is certified and a statistical event gives
\(\lvert\widehat p-p_c^{\mathrm{clip}}\rvert\leq\varepsilon_c\),
then
\[
 \left\lvert\rho\widehat p+\frac{U_\tau}{2}-\mathbb E X\right\rvert
 \leq\rho\varepsilon_c+\frac{U_\tau}{2}.                  \tag{V11}
\]
To prove this, write \(\mathbb E X=\rho p_c^{\mathrm{clip}}+\tau\),
subtract, and use
\(\lvert U_\tau/2-\tau\rvert\leq U_\tau/2\) with the triangle
inequality. Multiplying by the residual currency scale and adding the
previous deterministic numerical allowance gives a complete price-error
bound on the same statistical event. The midpoint tail correction is
therefore explicit; it neither estimates the tail nor silently drops its
uncertainty. Any claimed dyadic advantage must survive comparison with
these range and clipping opponents using the same proved moments.

\subsection{Reusing previously emitted financial bodies}

For the production study, I recover the primitive multiset of a forward
financial body by summing the retained node-block multisets of the earlier
resource profiles. Before reusing it, I independently emit the original
\((q+1)\)-bit comparison. Doubling the recovered body multiset and adding
that comparison must reproduce the complete retained original flag profile,
gate by gate and in its T count. The source profile hash is retained.
Only after this equality passes do I attach a freshly emitted dyadic,
range or clipped-range predicate and its reflection. Every arithmetic gate
is self-inverse, and a fresh phase-correct logical XOR lookup has exactly
the same primitive multiset as its forward lookup. Hence doubling the
body multiset is an exact count for its forward and logical inverse,
without assuming any symmetry of optimised dependency depths.

I use a deliberately conservative depth bound for this reuse path. Each
body T or T-dagger contributes one serial T layer, and each CCX contributes
the four layers of its verified literal seven-T sequence, or five layers
in the separately retained conservative schedule. Summing these quantities
is valid for either direction and for every ordering of the body atoms.
For all-gate depth, placing all expanded primitive operations in serial
order is a valid upper bound. Measured lookup erasures, feedback and resets
remain in those primitive multisets. The reusable body-space bound removes
only the original comparison and flag wires, which were reserved throughout
the original body, then adds the new predicate's reserved and private work.
It is a conservative workspace bound. Fresh complete coarse financial
circuits and small value circuits are checked against this adapter for
primitive counts, phases, clean work and quantum amplification. This
reuse method changes the resource-accounting workflow, not the quantum
oracle or the retained financial values.

\section{A finite-value dyadic quantum mean construction}
\label{sec:dyadic-construction}

I now give a quantum comparator that uses the second moment of the numerical output, rather than assigning that moment to the variance of a single comparison flag. The dyadic reduction belongs to the established bounded-variance quantum mean framework of \citet{montanaro}, Sections 2(c)--2(d). The original mean and variance bounds in this project supply a checkable finite-oracle input to that framework. The allocation construction below is a finite implementation choice. General cost-dependent quantum error allocation is already developed by \citet{an}; I make no theoretical novelty claim for allocating error among summands.

Let the actual finite numerical value be
\[
 X= a2^{-q},\qquad a\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^q\},\qquad q\geq1,
 \qquad \mu=\mathbb E X.
\]
The expectation refers to the exact finite input law used by the emitted value circuit. I require proved rational bounds
\[
 \mathbb E X\leq u,\qquad \mathbb E X^2\leq v,
 \qquad 0\leq u,v\leq1.
\]
The square-root inequality \(\mu\leq\sqrt v\) follows from
\(\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]\geq0\). I therefore replace \(u\) by
\(u_* = \min(u,\overline{\sqrt v})\), where the bar denotes the exact upward rational square-root enclosure used by the scalar certificate. The same strengthened mean prior must be supplied to the single-flag competitors. This is not a statement that their Bernoulli variance equals the variance of \(X\).

For \(b=0,\ldots,q-1\), define the disjoint events and weights
\[
 B_b=\{2^b\leq a<2^{b+1}\},\qquad r_b=2^{b+1-q},
 \qquad Z_b={X\over r_b}\mathbf 1_{B_b},\qquad p_b=\mathbb E Z_b.
\]
The top atom is \(B_q=\{a=2^q\}\), with \(r_q=1\), \(Z_q=\mathbf1_{B_q}\) and \(p_q=\Pr(B_q)\). The event \(a=0\) contributes zero. Every positive integer \(a<2^q\) belongs to exactly one ordinary bucket. Consequently the pointwise identity and its expectation are exact:
\[
 X=\sum_{b=0}^q r_bZ_b,
 \qquad \mu=\sum_{b=0}^q r_bp_b.
 \tag{D1}\label{eq:dyadic-identity}
\]
In particular the ordinary bucket with \(b=q-1\) and the separate top atom both have weight one. The top atom cannot be omitted merely because the ordinary dyadic weights end at one.

On an ordinary bucket, \(r_b/2\leq X<r_b\). Thus
\[
 {X\over r_b}\mathbf1_{B_b}\leq {2X^2\over r_b^2}\mathbf1_{B_b},
 \qquad
 {X\over r_b}\mathbf1_{B_b}\leq {X\over r_b},
 \qquad
 Z_b\leq1-2^{-(b+1)}.
\]
Taking expectations proves the bucket prior
\[
 0\leq p_b\leq U_b=
 \min\left(1-2^{-(b+1)},\,{2v\over r_b^2},\,{u_*\over r_b}\right).
 \tag{D2}\label{eq:dyadic-prior}
\]
For the top atom \(X=X^2=1\) on \(B_q\), so
\[
 0\leq p_q\leq U_q=\min(v,u_*).
 \tag{D3}\label{eq:dyadic-top-prior}
\]
These are deterministic moment implications. An observed bucket frequency or observed sample mean is not substituted for a proved prior.

To construct its Born flag, I prepare the finite input state, compute \(a\) reversibly, and introduce a uniform \((b+1)\)-bit comparison register \(J_b\). I compute
\[
 F_b=\mathbf1_{B_b}\mathbf1_{\{J_b<a\}}.
\]
Conditional on the value \(a\), exactly \(a\) of the \(2^{b+1}\) comparison integers satisfy the inequality when \(B_b\) holds. The success probability is therefore \(a/2^{b+1}\), and its unconditional probability is precisely \(p_b\). The top atom uses the exact equality flag \(\mathbf1_{\{a=2^q\}}\) without a comparison register. Bucket detection, arithmetic comparisons and their uncomputation are charged to the emitted preparation \(A_b\). The value computation is also charged to every invocation of \(A_b\); its cost is not a free shared resource across independent Grover experiments.

The logical circuit must implement \(A_b\) and its logical adjoint on the stipulated clean-work subspace, including arbitrary entanglement with an external reference. A lookup instrument is admissible only when every corrected branch is a scalar multiple of the same logical map and the branch record is independent of the logical state. The previously proved phase-correct instrument satisfies that premise. Reversing a measurement tape is not an adjoint. The Grover circuit is
\[
 Q_b=-A_bS_{0,b}A_b^\dagger S_{F_b}.
\]
Its good probability after \(k\) applications is
\(\sin^2((2k+1)\arcsin\sqrt{p_b})\), by the separately derived two-dimensional rotation. All zero-state reflection inputs, comparison bits, flag bits and reflection scratch are charged in \(Q_b\). The confidence statement assumes ideal fresh Born measurements and includes no physical noise assertion.

\section{Omitted prefixes and a deterministic aggregate error budget}
\label{sec:dyadic-tail}

For \(s\in\{0,\ldots,q\}\), I omit the ordinary buckets below \(s\). Their total contribution is
\[
 T_s=\mathbb E[X\mathbf1_{\{a<2^s\}}],\qquad
 0\leq T_s\leq V_s=
 \min\left({2^s-1\over2^q},u_*,\overline{\sqrt v}\right).
 \tag{D4}\label{eq:dyadic-tail-bound}
\]
The first upper bound is the largest possible finite value in the prefix. The mean upper bound follows from nonnegativity, and the second-moment upper bound follows by Cauchy--Schwarz. The omitted prefix is represented by the interval \([0,V_s]\) and its midpoint \(V_s/2\). Its error is therefore at most \(V_s/2\), rather than an uncharged omission bias. For the default choice I take the largest \(s\) for which \(V_s/2\leq t/4\), where \(t\) is the aggregate finite-mean tolerance. This leaves a positive budget
\(E=t-V_s/2\geq3t/4\).

For each retained bucket choose a rational monetary allowance \(e_b\) with
\[
 0\leq e_b\leq{r_bU_b\over2},\qquad
 {V_s\over2}+\sum_{b=s}^q e_b\leq t.
 \tag{D5}\label{eq:dyadic-error-budget}
\]
If \(e_b=r_bU_b/2\), the interval \([0,U_b]\) has midpoint \(U_b/2\) with scaled error at most \(e_b\); that bucket requires no shots. Otherwise the scalar amplitude-estimation tolerance is \(\epsilon_b=e_b/r_b<U_b/2\leq1/2\). Every active bucket must satisfy the declared scalar range \(\epsilon_b\geq10^{-6}\). The implementation rejects an unsupported allocation rather than invoking an unproved extension of the scalar controller. If the whole strengthened prior already satisfies \(u_*\leq2t\), I use its midpoint directly and invoke no buckets.

I retain equal and cost-dependent allocation candidates. For positive rational weights \(w_b\), define caps \(c_b=r_bU_b/2\) and supported floors
\(f_b=\min(c_b,10^{-6}r_b)\). Provided \(\sum f_b\leq E\), I start at the floors and distribute the remaining budget proportionally to \(w_b\) among unsaturated buckets. Whenever a proposed increment reaches \(c_b-f_b\), I fix that bucket at its cap, subtract exactly the required increment, and redistribute among the remainder. A nonterminal pass fixes at least one bucket, so this exact rational procedure terminates after at most the number of retained buckets. Every resulting allowance belongs to \([f_b,c_b]\); its sum is at most \(E\), and every active probability tolerance is within the declared range. If all caps sum to at most \(E\), all buckets are zero-shot. Equal allocation uses zero floors and is checked afterward against the same scalar range. Its active allowances are at least \(E/L\), where \(L\) is the number of retained buckets, because only capped buckets can leave the free set.

The cost-dependent weights have an explicit but limited justification. In the continuous surrogate
\[
 \min_{e_b>0,\,\sum e_b=E}\ \sum_b{d_b\over e_b},\qquad d_b>0,
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \left(\sum_b\sqrt{d_b}\right)^2
 \leq\left(\sum_b{d_b\over e_b}\right)\sum_b e_b.
\]
Equality holds exactly when \(e_b=E\sqrt{d_b}/\sum_j\sqrt{d_j}\). I take
\(d_b=(c_{A,b}+c_{Q,b})r_b\overline{\sqrt{U_b}}\) and use rational upward square roots as candidate weights. The actual scalar shot ceilings, reflection costs, floors and zero-shot caps do not coincide with that continuous objective. I therefore evaluate complete discrete resource caps for every retained candidate and claim only a minimum over that explicit finite set. The retained minimum is restricted to those candidates.

\section{Joint confidence and financial error transfer}
\label{sec:dyadic-confidence}

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the active buckets. If it is nonempty, I assign
\(\alpha_b=\alpha/|\mathcal A|\). For each bucket I invoke either the separately proved three/five controller or the full-batch three/five/seven controller with \((\epsilon_b,\alpha_b,U_b)\). Their own proofs give an interval \([\ell_b,h_b]\) containing \(p_b\) with failure probability at most \(\alpha_b\), and width at most \(2\epsilon_b\) on a certified return. Zero-shot buckets use \([0,U_b]\). The allocation and prior are fixed before the measurements of that price are observed. Conditional coverage of the scalar adaptive batches also permits previous bucket histories, provided the backend supplies fresh independent measurements of each requested experiment.

By the union bound,
\[
 \Pr\left(\exists b\in\mathcal A:p_b\notin[\ell_b,h_b]\right)
 \leq\sum_{b\in\mathcal A}\alpha_b\leq\alpha.
\]
On the complementary event the exact identity \eqref{eq:dyadic-identity} gives
\[
 \mu\in I=\left[\sum_{b=s}^q r_b\ell_b,
                   V_s+\sum_{b=s}^q r_bh_b\right].
\]
Its width satisfies
\[
 |I|\leq V_s+2\sum_{b=s}^q e_b\leq2t.
\]
Intersecting \(I\) with the deterministic whole-mean prior \([0,u_*]\) cannot remove the true mean on this event, and cannot increase the interval width. Its midpoint consequently has absolute finite-mean error at most \(t\). An empty scalar or aggregate intersection is returned with an explicit confidence-failure status. No empty interval is repaired by moving an endpoint or inventing an estimate.

For the financial implementation, let \(C\) be the retained analytic control correction and let the target undiscounted residual be \(R\). The existing deterministic certificate supplies
\[
 |C+H\mu-V|\leq\beta_{\mathrm{det}},
 \qquad \beta_{\mathrm{det}}\leq0.005,
\]
for the stipulated continuous-model target \(V\), including its numerical correction error. With \(t=0.005/H\), the dyadic midpoint estimate obeys
\[
 |C+H\widehat\mu-V|
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm{det}}+H|\widehat\mu-\mu|
 \leq0.01
\]
with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). A discount factor \(D>0\) gives discounted error at most \(D(\beta_{\mathrm{det}}+Ht)\). The present positive-rate examples have \(D\leq1\); a general application with \(D>1\) must reduce the undiscounted tolerance accordingly. I use \(\alpha=0.01\) for each preselected price, or \(\alpha=0.01/27\) for one preselected implementation per contract in a simultaneous 27-contract guarantee. Selection of precision, architecture or allocation is based on deterministic cost certificates, so it introduces no data-dependent confidence selection.

\section{Complete costs and the scope of the second-moment improvement}
\label{sec:dyadic-cost}

For bucket \(b\), let its emitted preparation and Grover operation have nonnegative additive metric costs \(c_{A,b}^{(m)}\) and \(c_{Q,b}^{(m)}\). If the scalar realised history uses \(N_b\) shots and \(K_b\) Grover calls, its exact metric cost is
\[
 N_bc_{A,b}^{(m)}+K_bc_{Q,b}^{(m)}.
\]
The three/five planner provides deterministic separate caps \(\overline N_b\) and \(\overline K_b\), so their linear combination is an upper bound. The full-batch planner instead maximises the same linear expression over its proved finite superset of admissible odd-multiplier chains. Writing \(\mathcal H_b\) for that set, I charge
\[
 \overline C_b^{(m)}=
 \max_{h\in\mathcal H_b}
 \left(N_b(h)c_{A,b}^{(m)}+K_b(h)c_{Q,b}^{(m)}\right),
 \qquad \overline C^{(m)}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal A}\overline C_b^{(m)}.
 \tag{D6}\label{eq:dyadic-metric-cost}
\]
Every realised bucket history lies in its corresponding set, so the sum is a rigorous aggregate cap. Different metrics can have different maximising histories; the metric table does not assert that one execution simultaneously attains every cap. T count, T depth, measurement count, feedback count, reset count and flag observations are all charged through the supplied A/Q profiles. Sequential bucket experiments permit reuse of scratch, so peak qubits are bounded by the largest active compiled bucket profile, provided the backend resets the completed experiment and reuses the same workspace. Simultaneous parallel execution would require a separate qubit charge. Classical coefficient storage and controller arithmetic are recorded separately from logical quantum gates.

The finite second moment changes the probability priors of the separate buckets through \eqref{eq:dyadic-prior}. It does not change the canonical identity
\(\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf1_{\{J<X\}})=\mu(1-\mu)\). For the true bucket probabilities, a useful additional relation is
\[
 \sum_{b=0}^q r_b^2p_b
 =\sum_{b=0}^{q-1}\mathbb E[r_bX\mathbf1_{B_b}]
   +\mathbb E[X^2\mathbf1_{B_q}]
 \leq2\mathbb E X^2\leq2v.
 \tag{D7}\label{eq:dyadic-joint-moment}
\]
The inequality follows from \(r_b\leq2X\) on every ordinary bucket. Individual global-moment priors need not saturate this joint relation simultaneously. I do not replace unknown bucket probabilities by observed values to obtain a worst-case resource reduction.

A coarse complexity corollary explains the role of this reduction without substituting an asymptotic count for emitted resources. Consider the full-batch controller, equal monetary allocation, the default prefix, and a supported active tolerance. Its guarded derivative constant satisfies \(\gamma_b\leq3\sqrt{U_b}\): the prior guard is \(10^{-18}\), the upward square-root error is below \(2^{-100}\), and an active prior exceeds \(2\times10^{-6}\). Its scalar endpoint allowance is \(10^{-60}\), so the multiplier envelope satisfies
\[
 M_b={\pi_+\gamma_b\over4(\epsilon_b-10^{-60})}
 <{2\gamma_b\over\epsilon_b}
 \leq {6Lr_b\sqrt{U_b}\over E}
 \leq {6L\sqrt{2v}\over E}.
\]
The final inequality also holds for the top atom. Each admissible history grows by at least three, hence has at most
\(1+\lfloor\log_3 M_b\rfloor\) batches and
\(\sum_i k_i\leq3M_b/4\). The largest prescribed batch is at most
\[
 N_{\max,b}=
 \left\lceil{\overline{\log(3M_b/\alpha_b)}\over2r_0^2}\right\rceil,
 \qquad r_0=69/1000,
\]
because every positive odd multiplier is at least one and the prescribed failure allowance is \(2\alpha_bm/(3M_b)\). It follows that
\[
 K_b\leq N_{\max,b}\lfloor3M_b/4\rfloor,
 \qquad N_b\leq N_{\max,b}(1+\lfloor\log_3M_b\rfloor).
\]
Thus the quantum-query part of this conservative implementation has order
\(L^2\sqrt v\,t^{-1}\log(L^2\sqrt v/(\alpha t))\), in the supported non-zero-shot regime. This uses an uncentred second moment of a nonnegative residual, not merely its centred variance. It is an upper bound for this implementation and is not a quantum lower bound or an improvement over the best published bounded-variance query theorem.

For completeness, the default prefix makes \(L\) logarithmic in the requested mean tolerance rather than the output precision. If \(s<q\), maximality and the range-only prefix bound imply
\(2^{s+1}-1>t2^{q-1}\), unless a still tighter moment prior allows a larger omitted prefix. Hence \(q-s<\log_2(4/t)\) and
\(L=q-s+1\leq\lceil\log_2(4/t)\rceil+1\); also \(L\leq q+1\). The exactly finite top atom adds one bucket.

Equation \eqref{eq:dyadic-joint-moment} shows why sharper published variance-aware query methods remain relevant. If valid separate bucket-second-moment certificates \(v_b\) sum to at most \(v\), their priors can use \(2v_b/r_b^2\), and the continuous surrogate coefficient satisfies
\[
 \left(\sum_b(r_b\sqrt{p_b})^{1/2}\right)^2
 \leq L^{3/2}\left(\sum_b r_b^2p_b\right)^{1/2}
 \leq L^{3/2}\sqrt{2v},
\]
by H\"older's inequality with exponents four and four-thirds. The present global-certificate planner does not claim this sharper coefficient. Its recorded finite costs, including bucket comparison and reflection work, determine whether it is favourable, null or adverse in each financial case. A favourable logical resource comparison is not a physical runtime advantage, and a numerical gain in this setting does not establish theoretical originality.

\section{A sharp precision-dependent moment prior}
\label{sec:dyadic-sharp-prior}

I also derive a sharp finite-grid refinement, retained as an optional prior rather than an unverified theoretical novelty claim. It applies to any nonnegative numerical value with support contained in
\(\{0,l,l+\Delta,\ldots,h\}\), where \(\Delta=2^{-q}\), \(l=L\Delta>0\), \(h=J\Delta\) and \(L\leq J\). Define
\[
 m_{l,h}(v)=
 \begin{cases}
  v/l,&0\leq v<l^2,\\
  h,&v\geq h^2,\\
  (v+xy)/(x+y),&l^2\leq v<h^2,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{D8}\label{eq:sharp-grid-prior}
\]
where the third case uses adjacent grid points \(x=j\Delta\), \(y=(j+1)\Delta\) with \(x^2\leq v<y^2\). The integer \(j\) is the exact floor square root of \(v/\Delta^2\), computed by integer arithmetic on its rational numerator and denominator.

For every such random variable \(W\) with \(\mathbb EW^2\leq v\), I claim
\(\mathbb EW\leq m_{l,h}(v)\). In the first case, each allowed nonzero value is at least \(l\), so \(W^2\geq lW\) pointwise and \(\mathbb EW\leq v/l\). In the second case \(W\leq h\) pointwise. In the third case, every allowed support point \(z\) satisfies
\[
 z^2-(x+y)z+xy=(z-x)(z-y)\geq0.
\]
There is no allowed point strictly between the adjacent \(x,y\); zero also satisfies the inequality. Taking expectations gives
\(v\geq(x+y)\mathbb EW-xy\), which proves the stated bound.

Each case is attainable within this support class. Below \(l^2\), put mass \(v/l^2\) at \(l\) and the remaining mass at zero. At or above \(h^2\), take the constant \(h\). In the third case, put probability
\(\lambda=(m_{l,h}(v)-x)/(y-x)\) at \(y\) and the rest at \(x\). Since \(x^2\leq v<y^2\), the formula gives \(x\leq m_{l,h}(v)<y\), so \(\lambda\in[0,1)\). Its second moment is
\((x+y)m_{l,h}(v)-xy=v\), and its mean is the bound. Thus it is the exact largest possible mean compatible with this finite support and the second-moment upper bound.

An additional upper constraint \(\mathbb EW\leq u\) gives the sharp joint upper bound \(\min(u,m_{l,h}(v))\). To verify attainability when \(u<m_{l,h}(v)\), mix the above attaining distribution with a point mass at zero, with mixture weight \(u/m_{l,h}(v)\) when the denominator is positive. Its mean is \(u\) and its second moment remains at most \(v\). The zero case is immediate.

For an ordinary bucket I apply this result to \(W=X\mathbf1_{B_b}\), with
\(l=2^{b-q}\) and \(h=(2^{b+1}-1)2^{-q}\). Its second moment is at most \(v\). Hence
\[
 p_b\leq{\min(u_*,m_{l,h}(v))\over r_b}.
\]
For the whole numerical value I use \(l=\Delta,h=1\), which yields a sharp refinement of \(\mu\leq\sqrt v\). The same strengthened whole-value prior must be used by every single-flag and classical comparator. Sharpness is relative to the allowed support class and supplied moment constraints; it does not establish that the financial circuit attains every register integer or the extremising distribution.

The gain can be negligible at a fine production precision. In the adjacent-grid case write \(z=\sqrt v\). Direct subtraction gives
\[
 0\leq z-m_{l,h}(v)
 ={(z-x)(y-z)\over x+y}
 \leq{\Delta^2\over4(x+y)}.
\]
Thus a high-precision finite-grid prior can be mathematically sharper without producing a different integer shot or gate budget. I retain favourable, null and adverse resource consequences separately and do not infer a numerical contribution merely from the sharp bound's derivation.

\section{Certified support and competent range-aware comparators}
\label{sec:certified-support-comparator}

Suppose the finite numerical output obeys \(X=a2^{-q}\) with a proved integer maximum \(a\leq A_{\max}\leq2^q\). Its range bound is \(\rho=A_{\max}2^{-q}\). This is a deterministic property of the emitted finite circuit, not an observed maximum. I supply it to every competitor. In the dyadic partition, buckets with \(2^b>A_{\max}\) and the top atom when \(A_{\max}<2^q\) have probability zero and require no circuit. The last supported bucket has largest integer
\(\min(2^{b+1}-1,A_{\max})\). Its pointwise scaled maximum replaces the generic bucket maximum, and the sharp finite moment bound uses precisely that endpoint. The whole-value prior also uses \(\rho\), with the optional sharp finite-grid bound applied equally to the single-flag opponents.

A range-aware single flag can choose a dyadic \(R=2^{c-q}\geq\rho\). With \(J\) uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,2^c-1\}\), the flag \(\mathbf1_{\{J<a\}}\) has probability \(p_R=\mathbb EX/R\). Its monetary scale is \(R\), not one, so an error \(e\) in \(p_R\) gives finite-mean error \(Re\). All comparison, value computation and reflection costs use the actual \(c\)-bit preparation and its logical adjoint. If \(c=0\), the comparison register has one possible value, and the same counting identity applies. The existing finite support certificate must be checked before this compression is used.

A stronger comparator permits clipping at a dyadic \(r=2^{c-q}<\rho\). Define
\[
 p_r={\mathbb E\min(X,r)\over r},\qquad
 T_r=\mathbb E(X-r)_+,
 \qquad \mu=rp_r+T_r.
 \tag{C1}\label{eq:clip-identity}
\]
The exact finite flag succeeds for \(J<\min(a,2^c)\). Thus it has probability \(p_r\). Saturation at \(2^c\) gives probability one, so the cap endpoint is included correctly. The clipping operation and its clean uncomputation are charged in the compiled oracle. There is no free value computation shared across independent Grover invocations.

\section{A sharp continuous-support clipped-tail moment bound}
\label{sec:sharp-clipping-moment}

The bound in this section is a classical moment-option bound. \citet{bertsimaspopescu2002}, Sections 2.1--2.2 and Theorem 3, already gives the mean-second-moment call-bound branch \(m-rm^2/s\) under fixed moments. \citet{bertsimaspopescu2005}, Section 2, develops upper moment bounds using nonnegative dual coefficients. I derive the specialisation required for this project's clipping-bias certificate. The source versions actually consulted are the final author-hosted typeset papers, with the precise scope retained in the source audit.

Let \(0\leq X\leq\rho\), \(\mathbb EX\leq u\) and \(\mathbb EX^2\leq v\). Replace the possibly loose supplied moments by
\[
 \bar u=\min(u,\rho),\qquad
 \bar v=\min(v,\rho\bar u,\rho^2).
 \tag{C2}\label{eq:clip-effective-moments}
\]
Indeed \(X^2\leq\rho X\) pointwise, so the effective second-moment bound remains valid. If \(r\geq\rho\), or \(\bar u=0\), or \(\bar v=0\), then \(T_r=0\). For the last two cases, nonnegativity gives \(X=0\) almost surely. For \(r=0\), the sharp upper bound is \(\min(\bar u,\sqrt{\bar v})\), attained by a constant at that value. I henceforth assume \(0<r<\rho\) and \(\bar u,\bar v>0\).

The exact largest possible clipped tail over this continuous support class is
\[
 D(\rho,\bar u,\bar v,r)=
 \begin{cases}
 (\rho-r)\bar v/\rho^2,&\rho<2r,\\
 \bar v/(4r),&\rho\geq2r,\ \bar v\leq4r^2,
                    \ \bar u\geq\bar v/(2r),\\
 \sqrt{\bar v}-r,&\rho\geq2r,\ \bar v>4r^2,
                    \ \bar u^2\geq\bar v,\\
 \bar u-r\bar u^2/\bar v,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{C3}\label{eq:sharp-clipped-tail}
\]
I prove both the upper bound and attainability in every case. Let \(g(x)=(x-r)_+\).

When \(r<\rho<2r\), the ratio \(g(x)/x^2\) vanishes for \(0<x\leq r\), the desired inequality holds directly at zero, and the ratio has derivative \((2r-x)/x^3>0\) for \(r<x\leq\rho\). Consequently
\[
 g(x)\leq{\rho-r\over\rho^2}x^2
 \qquad(0\leq x\leq\rho).
\]
Taking expectations proves the first case. Put mass \(\bar v/\rho^2\) at \(\rho\) and the rest at zero. This is a probability law because \(\bar v\leq\rho^2\). Its mean \(\bar v/\rho\) is at most \(\bar u\), its second moment is \(\bar v\), and its clipped-tail expectation equals the upper bound. It is therefore sharp.

For \(\rho\geq2r\) and \(\bar v\leq4r^2\), the quadratic majorant
\[
 g(x)\leq{x^2\over4r}
 \qquad(x\geq0)
\]
holds because the difference for \(x\geq r\) is \((x-2r)^2/(4r)\), and for \(x\leq r\) the right-hand side is nonnegative. It gives \(T_r\leq\bar v/(4r)\). The law with mass \(\bar v/(4r^2)\) at \(2r\) and the rest at zero attains it. The mass is at most one and the support point is at most \(\rho\). Its mean is \(\bar v/(2r)\), which is at most \(\bar u\) under the stated branch condition. This proves the second case.

For \(\bar v>4r^2\), put \(h=\sqrt{\bar v}>2r\). The polynomial
\[
 Q_h(x)={x^2\over2h}+{h\over2}-r
\]
majorises \(g(x)\) for every \(x\geq0\). For \(x\geq r\), its difference from \(x-r\) is \((x-h)^2/(2h)\). For \(x\leq r\), it is nonnegative because \(h/2-r\geq0\). Hence
\[
 T_r\leq{\bar v\over2h}+{h\over2}-r=h-r.
\]
The constant law \(X=h\) is feasible because \(h\leq\rho\), and its mean is at most \(\bar u\) precisely when \(\bar u^2\geq\bar v\). It attains the third case.

In the remaining case let \(h=\bar v/\bar u\). I first check the properties needed for the final majorant. If \(\bar v\leq4r^2\), the second branch has failed, so \(\bar u<\bar v/(2r)\), giving \(h>2r\) and
\(\bar u^2<\bar v^2/(4r^2)\leq\bar v\). If \(\bar v>4r^2\), the third branch has failed, so \(\bar u^2<\bar v\); then \(h>\sqrt{\bar v}>2r\). Both cases give \(h\geq2r\), \(\bar u^2/\bar v\leq1\), and \(h\leq\rho\) by \(\bar v\leq\rho\bar u\).

Now define
\[
 Q(x)=\left(1-{2r\over h}\right)x+{r\over h^2}x^2.
\]
Both coefficients are nonnegative. For \(x\geq r\),
\[
 Q(x)-(x-r)=r\left({x\over h}-1\right)^2\geq0;
\]
for \(x\leq r\), \(Q(x)\geq0=g(x)\). The nonnegative coefficients permit use of the upper, rather than fixed, moment constraints:
\[
 T_r\leq\left(1-{2r\over h}\right)\bar u
                   +{r\over h^2}\bar v
       =\bar u-r\bar u^2/\bar v.
\]
The law with mass \(\bar u^2/\bar v\) at \(h\) and the remainder at zero is feasible by the properties just proved. Its first and second moments equal \(\bar u\) and \(\bar v\), respectively, and its clipped-tail expectation equals the final formula. This proves sharpness and completes the exhaustive cases. At a shared branch boundary the corresponding formulas agree; no strict-boundary gap is left between them.

The support class in this theorem is continuous. For a prescribed finite numerical grid, the bound remains valid but an attaining point \(h\), \(2r\) or \(\rho\) must actually be an allowed value before lattice attainability can be asserted. A rational square-root enclosure \([h_-,h_+]\) is obtained with exact integer arithmetic and width at most \(2^{-100}\); perfect rational squares are recognised exactly. In the third case I charge \(h_+-r\). The pointwise majorant with \(h_+\) gives
\(T_r\leq\bar v/(2h_+)+h_+/2-r\leq h_+-r\), so the upward numerical charge itself is proved valid. The code retains the dual polynomial and a feasible attaining law, or the symbolic constant-root law, for independent checks.

\section{Clipping-error allocation and equal comparison scope}
\label{sec:clipping-financial-error}

Write \(\overline D\) for the certified upper bound from \eqref{eq:sharp-clipped-tail}. I represent the clipped tail by \([0,\overline D]\), with midpoint \(\overline D/2\). If \(t>\overline D/2\), I set the scalar probability tolerance
\[
 e_r={t-\overline D/2\over r}.
 \tag{C4}\label{eq:clipping-probability-tolerance}
\]
A scalar interval for \(p_r\) of width at most \(2e_r\) combines with the deterministic tail interval through \eqref{eq:clip-identity} to give a mean interval of width at most
\(2re_r+\overline D=2t\). Its midpoint has error at most \(t\) on the scalar coverage event. The tail does not introduce a probabilistic failure allowance. If \(\overline D/2\geq t\), that clipping choice is not used as an active scalar comparator without a separate sharper bound or whole-prior zero-shot argument. The scalar implementation's supported tolerance range is checked explicitly.

The same analytic correction and previously proved deterministic financial bias transfer the finite-mean tolerance to the continuous-model price. With \(t=0.005/H\) and deterministic currency bias at most \(0.005\), the undiscounted absolute price error is at most \(0.01\) on the scalar confidence event. Discounting multiplies this bound by the positive discount factor; negative-rate settings require the corresponding undiscounted adjustment.

For reference, elementary alternatives satisfy
\[
 T_r\leq\rho-r,\qquad
 T_r\leq u\left(1-{r\over\rho}\right),\qquad
 T_r\leq v\begin{cases}
 (\rho-r)/\rho^2,&r<\rho\leq2r,\\
 1/(4r),&\rho>2r.
 \end{cases}
\]
The first is the pointwise range maximum. The second follows from the line joining \((0,0)\) and \((\rho,\rho-r)\), which majorises the convex call payoff on the interval. The third follows from the maximised ratio \(g(x)/x^2\) derived above. These bounds can be valid yet looser than the sharp upper-moment specialisation, so they are retained as earlier comparator levels rather than the final strongest clipping opponent.

I supply the same numerical support, first- and second-moment certificates to dyadic, range-normalised, clipped and original single-flag estimators. The finite reports must retain those cases, even if they remove an earlier apparent dyadic gain.

\section{A sharp range-aware classical variance certificate}
\label{sec:range-classical-variance}

The same finite support supplied to the quantum opponents also improves the classical confidence calculation. I use an elementary mean-dependent range variance bound. \citet{bhatiadavis} is a published predecessor identified in the source ledger; its final full text was not consulted. The direct proof below supplies the required inequality without relying on an unread theorem statement. Let \(X\) satisfy
\[
 0\leq X\leq\rho,\qquad \mathbb EX\leq u,\qquad
 \mathbb EX^2\leq v,
\]
where \(\rho,u,v\geq0\). If \(\rho=0\), then \(X=0\) almost surely and its variance is zero. Otherwise define
\[
 m=\min\left(u,{\rho\over2},{v\over\rho}\right),\qquad
 \sigma_*^2=m(\rho-m).
 \tag{RV1}\label{eq:sharp-range-variance}
\]
I claim \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\leq\sigma_*^2\), and that this is the sharp upper bound over the stated inequality class.

Write \(\mu=\mathbb EX\). The pointwise inequality \(X(\rho-X)\geq0\) gives \(\mathbb EX^2\leq\rho\mu\). Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X)
 =\mathbb EX^2-\mu^2
 \leq f(\mu):=\min(v,\rho\mu)-\mu^2,
 \qquad0\leq\mu\leq\min(u,\rho).
\]
On \(0\leq\mu\leq v/\rho\), the function is \(\rho\mu-\mu^2\), increasing up to \(\rho/2\) and decreasing thereafter. On \(\mu\geq v/\rho\), it is \(v-\mu^2\), which is nonincreasing. Its maximum on the permitted interval is consequently attained at \(m\). Since \(m\leq v/\rho\), its value is \(m(\rho-m)\), proving the upper bound.

To prove sharpness, put probability \(m/\rho\) at \(\rho\) and the remaining probability at zero. This is a probability law because \(0\leq m\leq\rho/2\). Its mean is \(m\leq u\), its second moment is \(\rho m\leq v\), and its variance is exactly \(m(\rho-m)\). Thus no uniformly smaller bound follows solely from the supplied range and upper first and second moments. Sharpness concerns the support class \([0,\rho]\); a particular financial circuit may not attain the extremising law. For a numerical grid containing its endpoints, the attaining support itself is available, although the particular input law can still prevent attainment.

Any independently proved smaller variance certificate can also be used, by taking the minimum with \(\sigma_*^2\). The bound above is never assigned to a quantum Bernoulli flag. It concerns independent observations of the fractional finite output \(X\).

\section{A complete range-aware Bernstein sample guarantee}
\label{sec:range-classical-bernstein}

For independent identically distributed observations \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) of the same finite law, write \(Y_i=X_i-\mu\). Then \(\mathbb EY_i=0\), \(|Y_i|\leq\rho\), and for every integer \(k\geq2\),
\[
 \mathbb E|Y_i|^k\leq\rho^{k-2}\mathbb EY_i^2
 \leq\rho^{k-2}\sigma_*^2.
\]
For \(0<s<3/\rho\), the exponential series is absolutely integrable because \(Y_i\) is bounded. Using \(k!\geq2\,3^{k-2}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E e^{sY_i}
 \leq1+{\sigma_*^2s^2\over2}
               \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(s\rho/3)^j
 \leq\exp\left({\sigma_*^2s^2\over2(1-s\rho/3)}\right).
\]
The factorial inequality holds at \(k=2\), and its induction step follows by multiplying by \(k+1\geq3\). The identical estimate holds with \(-sY_i\).

For tolerance \(t>0\), independence and exponential Markov give
\[
 \Pr\left({1\over N}\sum_iY_i\geq t\right)
 \leq\exp\left(-Nst+
      {N\sigma_*^2s^2\over2(1-s\rho/3)}\right).
\]
When \(\sigma_*^2>0\), choose
\(s=t/(\sigma_*^2+\rho t/3)\), which lies in \((0,3/\rho)\). Direct substitution gives
\[
 \Pr\left(\left|{1\over N}\sum_iX_i-\mu\right|\geq t\right)
 \leq2\exp\left(-{Nt^2\over2\sigma_*^2+2\rho t/3}\right).
 \tag{RV2}\label{eq:range-bernstein-confidence}
\]
If the variance certificate is zero, the observations equal their mean almost surely, and the deviation probability is zero; the formula remains a conservative positive upper bound. A fixed sample count
\[
 N_{\rm B}=\left\lceil
 {2\sigma_*^2+2\rho t/3\over t^2}
 \overline{\log(2/\alpha)}\right\rceil
 \tag{RV3}\label{eq:range-bernstein-sample}
\]
therefore guarantees coverage at least \(1-\alpha\). The logarithm is enclosed upward before the exact integer ceiling. The implementation uses one hundred Decimal digits and a \(10^{-80}\) allowance at \(\alpha=0.01\), where the exact argument is 200; the correctly rounded logarithm error is far below that allowance.

The independently derived Hoeffding alternative for an interval of width \(\rho\) is
\[
 N_{\rm H}=\left\lceil{\rho^2\over2t^2}
       \overline{\log(2/\alpha)}\right\rceil.
\]
It follows by scaling \(X_i/\rho\) into \([0,1]\) and applying the previously proved unit-interval inequality. Selecting the smaller of \(N_{\rm B}\) and \(N_{\rm H}\) before any samples are observed preserves its corresponding fixed-sample guarantee. An independent whole-mean prior of width at most \(2t\) can instead provide a deterministic midpoint without samples; that case must be labelled explicitly.

With \(t=0.005/H\), the same deterministic currency error certificate at most \(0.005\) gives undiscounted price error at most \(0.01\) on the classical confidence event. Discounting and a simultaneous price-confidence allocation are handled as in the quantum statement. The new sample budgets are prospective certificates unless the specified new independent sample stream is actually executed. They are not empirical confidence estimates or directly commensurate with logical quantum T counts.

\section{Why a finite range can remove the apparent flag-variance penalty}
\label{sec:weak-dispersion-range}

The earlier weak-dispersion limit compares the fractional observation with
its canonical unit-range flag. It is not a lower bound for all quantum
encodings. I now give a uniform support argument explaining why a competent
single flag can recover the relevant small scale on a bounded numerical
input family. This argument is a checked consequence of exponential
remainders, convexity and range normalisation, not a priority claim for those
methods.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform log-gap range under centred perturbations]
Let $w_i>0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$, $H>0$ and $0\leq\delta_\varepsilon<\infty$, and let real centred perturbations
$h_i(\varepsilon,v)$ satisfy
$\sum_iw_i h_i(\varepsilon,v)=0$ and
$|h_i(\varepsilon,v)|\leq\delta_\varepsilon$ for every point $v$ in a
specified input support. Put
\[
 J_\varepsilon(v)=\log\sum_iw_i e^{h_i(\varepsilon,v)}.
\]
Then
\[
 0\leq J_\varepsilon(v)\leq
 {e^{\delta_\varepsilon}\over2}
       \sum_iw_i h_i(\varepsilon,v)^2
 \leq{e^{\delta_\varepsilon}\over2}\delta_\varepsilon^2
\]
uniformly on that support. If an increasing scalar conditional price $P$
has a finite global Lipschitz constant $L\geq0$, then its normalised controlled residual
$X_\varepsilon=[P(m+J_\varepsilon)-P(m)]/H$ satisfies
$0\leq X_\varepsilon\leq Le^{\delta_\varepsilon}\delta_\varepsilon^2/(2H)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Jensen's inequality gives $\sum_iw_i e^{h_i}\geq
 e^{\sum_iw_i h_i}=1$, proving nonnegativity. Taylor's integral remainder
is the exact identity
\[
 e^x=1+x+x^2\int_0^1(1-t)e^{tx}\,dt.
\]
For $|x|\leq\delta_\varepsilon$, the integral is at most
$e^{\delta_\varepsilon}/2$. Summing the identity with weights $w_i$
cancels the linear term and gives
$\sum_iw_i e^{h_i}\leq1+(e^{\delta_\varepsilon}/2)\sum_iw_i h_i^2$.
The inequality $\log(1+z)\leq z$ for $z\geq0$ follows by integrating
$(1+z)^{-1}\leq1$, and proves the first upper bound. The second uses
$h_i^2\leq\delta_\varepsilon^2$. Monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity of
$P$ give $0\leq P(m+J_\varepsilon)-P(m)\leq LJ_\varepsilon$,
which proves the residual bound for every base $m$.
\end{proof}

For instance, if $h_i=\varepsilon b_i\cdot v+a_i(\varepsilon)$,
$\|v\|\leq M$, $\max_i\|b_i\|\leq B$,
$|a_i(\varepsilon)|\leq C\varepsilon^2$ and the weighted centring
holds exactly, I may take
$\delta_\varepsilon=BM|\varepsilon|+C\varepsilon^2$.
For fixed $B,M,C,L,H$ this yields a uniform $O(\varepsilon^2)$
residual range. Exact centring of the logarithms follows when they are
formed by subtracting their weighted geometric log mean. Centring errors
must otherwise receive their own allowance; they cannot be discarded.

Suppose the implemented output $\widehat X_\varepsilon$ belongs to $[0,1]$,
retained on the $2^{-q}$ grid, and has a proved pointwise normalised
arithmetic error $|\widehat X_\varepsilon(v)-X_\varepsilon(v)|\leq\eta_\varepsilon$ at each supported input. A valid numerical range is
\[
 \rho_\varepsilon=\min\left(1,
 {L e^{\delta_\varepsilon}\delta_\varepsilon^2\over2H}
                  +\eta_\varepsilon\right),
\]
rounded outward and then down to its largest possible grid integer as in
the finite-range certificate. If $\eta_\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon^2)$,
this range remains $O(\varepsilon^2)$ on a bounded support family.
For a nonzero certified integer support upper bound $a_{\max}$, which need not be attained, put
$c=\lceil\log_2 a_{\max}\rceil$ and $R=2^{c-q}$.
The least admissible dyadic range satisfies
$\widehat X_\varepsilon\leq R<2a_{\max}2^{-q}\leq2\rho_\varepsilon$,
with the weak inequality $R\leq2a_{\max}2^{-q}$ also sufficient.
The integer choice is exact: it is the least $c$ with $a_{\max}\leq2^c$.
If $a_{\max}=0$, the mean is zero and needs no measurements.

For an absolute finite-mean tolerance $t_\varepsilon$,
$\E\widehat X_\varepsilon=R p$ is estimated with probability tolerance
$t_\varepsilon/R$ using the range flag. On a sequence with
$t_\varepsilon\geq c_0\varepsilon^2$ and
$R\leq C_0\varepsilon^2$ for positive constants, this probability
tolerance is at least $c_0/C_0$. At fixed confidence within the retained logarithm domain $0<\log(\cdot)<100$, the proved scalar
controller therefore has an $\varepsilon$-independent shot/query cap,
provided this tolerance is in its supported domain; a tolerance at least
half the whole prior gives a zero-shot midpoint instead. To make the controller argument explicit, write $e_R=t_\varepsilon/R$
and let its proved Born prior be $u_R\leq1$. If $u_R\leq2e_R$, the prior
midpoint suffices. Otherwise $u_R>2e_R\geq2c_0/C_0$, so the initial
normalised inverse angle is bounded away from zero and its reciprocal
bootstrap multiplier is bounded. On the supported scalar domain
$10^{-6}\leq e_R<1/2$, the derivative enclosure satisfies $\gamma\leq1$.
The three/five controller's threshold is at most
$\lceil1/(5e_R)\rceil$; its finite shot and geometric-power caps are
therefore bounded at fixed confidence. The full-batch controller's maximum
multiplier is at most $\Pi_+/[4(e_R-10^{-60})]$, where $\Pi_+<4$ is the retained rational upper enclosure of $\pi$; its shot table and admissible
chains are also bounded. These expressions are uniformly finite since
$e_R$ has a positive lower bound in the supported domain. The project choice $\alpha=0.01$ and $e_R\geq10^{-6}$ lies comfortably within the logarithm guard. This is a statement
about the outer estimation factor. The financial evaluator cost, the
numerical register widths and the construction of the support certificate
can still depend on $\varepsilon$.

An unbounded Gaussian law does not satisfy the fixed support hypothesis.
A finite quantile grid does, but its support grows when its address width
grows. Distribution-transfer error and the changing support must then be
charged together with arithmetic precision. Thus the theorem neither
asserts a uniform bound for the continuous Gaussian residual nor proves
constant total pricing cost. It identifies a concrete reason that the
canonical flag's $O(\varepsilon^2)$ variance alone cannot establish a
quantum lower bound or a necessary gain from dyadic value estimation.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic cost selection preserves a price guarantee]
Let a finite set of implementations $\mathcal J$ share a deterministic
financial bias certificate $\beta_j\leq0.005$ and an aggregate finite-mean
estimation budget $t_j=0.005/H$. Suppose each implementation gives its stated
price-error guarantee with failure probability at most $\alpha$. Choose one
$j_*$ by a deterministic rule depending only on model parameters, proved
numerical bounds and emitted circuit/resource counts, before its price
measurements are observed. Then the selected price obeys its stated error
bound with failure probability at most $\alpha$. No union over $\mathcal J$
is required for that one selected price.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The information entering the selection rule contains no random measurements
from the ensuing price experiment. Conditional on that information, $j_*$ is
fixed, and the assumed controller bound applies to $j_*$. Thus the conditional
failure probability is at most $\alpha$; taking its expectation proves the
unconditional bound. A claim that every unselected candidate also succeeds
simultaneously would be different and would require a corresponding joint
allocation. Likewise a simultaneous guarantee for 27 selected prices requires
allocating $\alpha/27$ to each; the present cost table instead uses
$\alpha=0.01$ for each individual selected price.
\end{proof}

\section{Per-wire reinitialisation of a reusable ideal shot}
\label{sec:shot-reinitialisation}

The final flag measurement and a complete reinitialisation event are distinct resource counts. I make the event concrete. Let the retained logical register \(S\) contain the financial input wires, the uniform comparison wires and the flag, with \(n_S=L+c+1\) wires for a scalar comparison of width \(c\). The financial and predicate scratch returns to its clean state by the proved logical A/Q contract, including on inputs entangled with an external reference, so it need not be reset after every shot in the ideal model.

After measuring the flag in the computational basis, I measure each of the \(L+c\) remaining retained wires in that basis, and apply X to every retained wire whose recorded bit is one, including the already measured flag. For an outcome string \(z\in\{0,1\}^{n_S}\), the combined measurement and conditional reset has Kraus operator
\(K_z=|0\rangle\langle z|\). For any possibly entangled joint state \(\omega_{SR}\),
\[
 \sum_z(K_z\otimes I_R)\omega_{SR}(K_z^\dagger\otimes I_R)
 =|0\rangle\langle0|_S\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_S\omega_{SR}.
\]
This follows by expanding \(\omega_{SR}=\sum_{z,z'}|z\rangle\langle z'|\otimes\omega_{zz'}\): only equal measurement indices survive and their reference blocks sum to the partial trace. The channel therefore returns the retained register to zero and removes its correlation with previous quantum or classical records. Applying the same ideal preparation to this state supplies the fresh-shot premise of the confidence calculation.

For a scalar plan with shot cap \(\overline N\), the reset protocol adds at most
\[
 \overline N(L+c)\ \text{nonflag Z measurements},\qquad
 \overline N(L+c+1)\ \text{conditional Clifford X resets}.
\]
The \(\overline N\) flag observations were already counted and are not counted a second time. For dyadic buckets, the comparison width is \(b+1\) on ordinary buckets and zero on the top atom, so these expressions are summed with each bucket's separate shot cap and width. All added operations have zero T cost; their measurement and feedback costs are retained separately. These are ideal logical operations, not an assertion that device reset latency or physical noise is zero. A platform-specific reset primitive can replace the explicit protocol only with its own stated cost and channel guarantee.

\section{A deterministic comparator for the same finite pricing target}

I compare the retained sampling procedures with exhaustive summation before
interpreting a sampling-query improvement as a financial pricing contribution.
This comparison uses the existing financial precision pool. It does not introduce
a newly optimised precision family or an additional statistical premise.
The result below is an elementary finite-sum identity, not a claim of theoretical
novelty. Its purpose is to identify a constructive competitor that the preceding
confidence budgets do not exclude.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite summation and transfer to a continuous price]
Let the implemented financial output be
\(x:\mathcal D\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,2^q\}\), where
\[
 \mathcal D=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\{0,1,\ldots,2^{b_j}-1\},
 \qquad N=2^{\sum_j b_j}.
\]
Suppose a deterministic certificate establishes
\[
 \left|P-\left(g_0+H\,\mathbb E[X]\right)\right|\leq\beta,
 \qquad X=2^{-q}x(U),
\]
for uniform \(U\) on \(\mathcal D\), a retained geometric correction centre
\(g_0\), and the continuous undiscounted price \(P\). The certificate includes
the error of \(g_0\). Then exhaustive evaluation gives the exact rational number
\[
 \overline x=\frac{\sum_{u\in\mathcal D}x(u)}{N2^q}
\]
and the deterministic price enclosure
\[
 P\in[g_0+H\overline x-\beta,\ g_0+H\overline x+\beta].
\]
There is no statistical error or random-bit independence assumption. If every
input state is evaluated exactly, the enclosure has confidence one under the
stated numerical and modelling premises.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definition of a uniform random variable on a finite product gives
\(\mathbb E[X]=(N2^q)^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathcal D}x(u)\). Every summand is an
integer. Arbitrary-precision integer summation therefore computes the numerator
without rounding. Substitution into the assumed deterministic certificate proves
both endpoints. No concentration inequality is used. In the retained one-input
enumeration, \(N\leq2^{17}\) and \(q\leq30\), so
\(\sum x(u)\leq N2^q\leq2^{47}<2^{64}\). An unsigned 64-bit sum is consequently
exact as well. This overflow argument concerns the output reduction; the
intermediate fixed-point products continue to use the separately proved limb
arithmetic of the financial interpreter.
\end{proof}

For direct, interval and full-conditioning graphs, respectively, the exhaustive
input sizes are
\[
 N_{\mathrm{direct}}=2^{d b_y+b_z},\qquad
 N_{\mathrm{interval}}=2^{d b_y+b_u},\qquad
 N_{\mathrm{full}}=2^{d b_y}.
\]
Thus storage for the scalar oracle tables, which is a sum of marginal table
sizes, does not determine the number of financial input combinations. These are
different quantities. A large tensor count is also not a lower bound on every
classical quadrature algorithm. I record every input size in the 273-prefix pool
and restrict actual exhaustive evaluation to the prespecified two-asset,
fully conditioned scope with at most \(2^{17}\) states.

If the prepared financial interpreter charges \(E\) scalar-lane operations per
input and uses \(P_v\) vector domain predicates, I retain the following
conservative online count for a one-dimensional enumeration:
\[
 C_{\mathrm{enum}}=NE+N+(N-1)+P_v(2N-1)+(N-1)+1
                =N(E+3+2P_v)-P_v-1.
\]
The terms charge financial arithmetic, input assignments, input increments,
domain comparisons and Boolean reductions, exact output addition, and final
rational construction. For the selected two-asset full graphs,
\(E=73\) and \(P_v=7\), giving \(90N-8\). These are algorithm-level scalar
operations, not processor instructions. The full table manifests, packed-word
reads, unpacked-word writes, graph-node counts and separate preparation times
are retained. Special-function setup is not converted into an invented processor

\section{A certified continuous two-asset comparator}

The following calculation also avoids the finite normal grid entirely. I retain
the frozen rational parameters and the same undiscounted price target. Write
\[
 S_i=K\exp(\mu_i+r_iY+\lambda Z),\qquad
 Y,Z\stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}N(0,1),\qquad
 A=\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_iS_i,\qquad G=\prod_{i=1}^{2}S_i^{w_i},
\]
where \(w_i>0\), \(w_1+w_2=1\), \(K=100\), and \(\lambda>0\).
The retained exact rational check verifies
\(\mu_i+(r_i^2+\lambda^2)/2=(r-\delta)T\), so this is the stipulated
risk-neutral model. Define
\[
 m_A(y)=\log\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i+r_iy},\qquad
 m_G(y)=\sum_iw_i(\mu_i+r_iy),\qquad
 F(s)=\min\{(s-K)^+,H\}.
\]
The common-factor conditional scalar price is
\(C_\lambda(m)=\mathbb E[F(K e^{m+\lambda Z})]\).

\begin{lemma}[Positive strike representation and analytic scalar kernel]
For real \(m\), let \(h=H/K\), \(a=\log(1+h)\),
\(t_0=m/\lambda\) and \(t_1=(m-a)/\lambda\). Then
\begin{align*}
 C_\lambda(m)
 &=K\int_1^{1+h}\Phi\!\left(\frac{m-\log k}{\lambda}\right)\,dk\\
 &=K\left\{e^{m+\lambda^2/2}
     [\Phi(t_0+\lambda)-\Phi(t_1+\lambda)]
       -[\Phi(t_0)-\Phi(t_1)]+h\Phi(t_1)\right\}.
\end{align*}
For positive real \(\lambda\), the displayed closed expression is an entire
function of the complex variable \(m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every \(s\geq0\),
\(F(s)=\int_K^{K+H}\mathbf1_{\{s>x\}}\,dx\); the value at an endpoint is
irrelevant to this integral. Tonelli's theorem applies because the integrand is
nonnegative. Substitution \(x=Kk\) gives the first equality and the normal
probability \(\Phi((m-\log k)/\lambda)\).
For the second equality, integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_1^{1+h}\Phi\!\left(\frac{m-\log k}{\lambda}\right)\,dk
 =(1+h)\Phi(t_1)-\Phi(t_0)
 +\int_1^{1+h}\frac1\lambda
       \phi\!\left(\frac{m-\log k}{\lambda}\right)\,dk.
\]
In the last integral set \(k=e^{m-\lambda t}\). Completing the square yields
\[
 \int_{t_1}^{t_0}e^{m-\lambda t}\phi(t)\,dt
 =e^{m+\lambda^2/2}
       [\Phi(t_0+\lambda)-\Phi(t_1+\lambda)].
\]
Combining the boundary terms proves the formula. The normal CDF extension
\(\Phi(z)=(1+\operatorname{erf}(z/\sqrt2))/2\) is entire, as are the
exponential and their finite products and sums. The logarithm \(a\) is a
positive-real-parameter constant, not a logarithm of the variable \(m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Residual quadrature and a one-sided Gaussian tail enclosure]
Put \(\mu_G=\sum_iw_i\mu_i\), \(r_G=\sum_iw_ir_i\), and
\(\sigma_G=\sqrt{\lambda^2+r_G^2}\). For every \(L>0\),
\[
 P=\mathbb E[F(A)]
   =C_{\sigma_G}(\mu_G)
     +\int_{-L}^{L}
       [C_\lambda(m_A(y))-C_\lambda(m_G(y))]\phi(y)\,dy+\tau_L,
 \qquad 0\leq\tau_L\leq2H\Phi(-L).
\]
Suppose validated integration returns a real interval \([I_-,I_+]\) for the
finite integral, validated scalar evaluation returns \([g_-,g_+]\) for the
geometric correction, and \(T_+\geq2H\Phi(-L)\). Then
\[
 P\in[I_-+g_-,\ I_++g_++T_+].
\]
For the direct conditional arithmetic price, the analogous interval is obtained
by omitting the geometric correction and integrating
\(C_\lambda(m_A(y))\phi(y)\). Its omitted tail obeys the same bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Weighted arithmetic--geometric inequality gives \(A\geq G\) pointwise. Since
\(F\) is increasing and takes values in \([0,H]\),
\(0\leq F(A)-F(G)\leq H\). Conditioning on \(Y=y\) preserves both
inequalities and gives the residual kernel in the displayed integral.
Also \(\log(G/K)=\mu_G+r_GY+\lambda Z\), whose Gaussian variance is
\(r_G^2+\lambda^2\), proving the exact geometric correction.
The omitted normal probability is
\(\mathbb P(|Y|>L)=2\Phi(-L)\). Multiplication by the pointwise bound \(H\)
proves the one-sided tail enclosure. Addition of outward endpoints proves the
price interval. The direct case uses \(0\leq F(A)\leq H\) in precisely the
same tail argument.
\end{proof}

I use the existing authorised \texttt{python-flint} 0.9.0 implementation of
Arb/Acb validated ball arithmetic. The ball-enclosure foundation is established by \citet{johansson2017}; I consulted the author-hosted typeset version, Sections 2.2 and 5.1, and the current installed API contracts separately. The primary integral uses 128-bit precision,
\(L=8\), and a requested absolute integration tolerance \(10^{-16}\).
The requested tolerance is not used as an achieved error bound. The actual
returned finite ball, the separately returned correction ball and the outward
Gaussian-tail bound determine every endpoint. Each endpoint is exported as an
exact dyadic rational from its mantissa and exponent; decimal displays are
rounded outwards. The direct representation is separately computed with
176-bit precision, \(L=10\), and requested tolerance \(10^{-24}\).

The Acb quadrature routine evaluates complex neighbourhoods as well as real
points. Although the scalar cap kernel is entire, the log basket
\(m_A(y)=\log\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i+r_iy}\) need not be analytic on an arbitrary
complex neighbourhood because its argument may vanish or meet the principal
logarithm's branch cut. I therefore forward the quadrature routine's analytic
verification flag to the complex logarithm. A non-finite analytic evaluation is
returned to the integrator, which must subdivide or fail to return a finite
enclosure. I do not replace this check with the fact that the integration path
is real. No clipping, midpoint substitution or heuristic quadrature error is
used to construct the price certificate. The assurance is conditional on the
validated-library enclosure contract and the checked mathematical integrand;
it is not a claim that this project proves the software implementation itself.

\section{A finite-order deterministic certificate for Gaussian baskets}
\label{sec:taylor-continuous-certificate}

I next construct a continuous-model deterministic comparator that applies to the higher-dimensional frozen family. The construction uses a finite Taylor identity with a checked remainder; convergence of an infinite Taylor series is unnecessary. I retain orders zero through eight in advance and report every contract, including contracts for which these bounds fail to attain the target radius. This comparator removes the finite Gaussian grid and sampling error from its own price certificate. Its mathematical and arithmetic costs must therefore be reported separately from quantum logical gate counts.

\subsection{Exact geometry and the smoothed capped price}
\label{sec:taylor-geometry}

Let $Y\sim N(0,I_p)$ and $Z\sim N(0,1)$ be independent. For positive weights $w_i$ satisfying $\sum_{i=1}^n w_i=1$, positive strike and spot $K$, exact drifts $\mu_i$, exact loading vectors $R_i\in\mathbb R^p$ and a common scale $\lambda>0$, I write
\[
 S_i=K\exp(\mu_i+R_i\cdot Y+\lambda Z),\qquad
 \mu_G=\sum_i w_i\mu_i,\qquad R_G=\sum_iw_iR_i.
\]
The arithmetic and geometric baskets are $A=\sum_iw_iS_i$ and $G=\prod_i S_i^{w_i}$. Define $d_i=\mu_i-\mu_G$, $s_i=R_i-R_G$ and
\[
 m_G(y)=\mu_G+R_G\cdot y,\qquad
 T(y)=\sum_iw_i\exp(d_i+s_i\cdot y).
\]
Then $A=GT(Y)$, $\sum_iw_id_i=0$ and $\sum_iw_is_i=0$. Jensen's inequality gives $T(y)\geq\exp(\sum_iw_i(d_i+s_i\cdot y))=1$ for every real $y$. This centring is exact; it is not a numerical approximation to the frozen loadings. For cap $H>0$, put
\[
 P_{\lambda,H}(m)=\mathbb E_Z\bigl[\min\{(K\exp(m+\lambda Z)-K)_+,H\}\bigr].
\]
Writing $L_H=\log(1+H/K)$, the identity $\min\{(x-K)_+,H\}=\int_K^{K+H}{\bf1}_{\{x>u\}}\,du$ and Tonelli's theorem yield
\begin{equation}
 P_{\lambda,H}(m)=\int_0^{L_H}K e^x\Phi((m-x)/\lambda)\,dx.
 \label{eq:taylor-strike-integral}
\end{equation}
Consequently $0\leq P_{\lambda,H}\leq H$, and the undiscounted arithmetic price is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi=\mathbb E_Y\bigl[P_{\lambda,H}(m_G(Y)+\log T(Y))\bigr].
 \label{eq:taylor-price-target}
\end{equation}
The common Gaussian scale is essential to the stated derivative certificate: I do not apply the bounds below at $\lambda=0$.

\subsection{Derivative bounds and the finite Taylor identity}
\label{sec:taylor-derivative-bound}

For each non-negative integer $j$, the Fourier representation of the standard normal density implies
\[
 \phi^{(j)}(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}(iu)^j e^{-u^2/2}e^{iux}\,du,
 \qquad
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|\phi^{(j)}(x)|\leq B_j,
 \qquad
 B_j=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|u|^je^{-u^2/2}\,du.
\]
The integral is absolutely convergent, so differentiation under the integral follows by domination by $|u|^je^{-u^2/2}$. Direct integration gives $B_0=(2\pi)^{-1/2}$ and $B_1=\pi^{-1}$. Integration by parts on $(0,\infty)$ gives $B_j=(j-1)B_{j-2}$ for $j\geq2$; the boundary terms vanish because a Gaussian dominates each polynomial. Differentiation of \eqref{eq:taylor-strike-integral} is justified by its finite integration domain and the preceding uniform bounds. For each $\ell\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 P_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(m)
 =\lambda^{-\ell}\int_0^{L_H}K e^x\phi^{(\ell-1)}((m-x)/\lambda)\,dx,
 \qquad
 \sup_m|P_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(m)|\leq H\lambda^{-\ell}B_{\ell-1}.
 \label{eq:taylor-price-derivatives}
\end{equation}
Thus every required derivative is continuous and bounded.

Let $s(r,\ell)$ denote signed Stirling numbers of the first kind, defined by $s(0,0)=1$ and $s(r+1,\ell)=s(r,\ell-1)-r s(r,\ell)$, with values outside $0\leq\ell\leq r$ equal to zero. With $D=\partial/\partial m$, equivalently, $\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}(D-a)=\sum_{\ell=0}^r s(r,\ell)D^\ell$. For fixed real $m$, set $g_m(t)=P_{\lambda,H}(m+\log t)$ for $t>0$. The chain rule and induction give
\begin{equation}
 g_m^{(r)}(t)
 =t^{-r}\sum_{\ell=1}^r s(r,\ell)P_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(m+\log t),\qquad r\geq1.
 \label{eq:taylor-falling-derivatives}
\end{equation}
Indeed, differentiating $t^{-r}Q(D)P(m+\log t)$ produces $t^{-r-1}(D-r)Q(D)P(m+\log t)$; the induction begins with $g_m'(t)=t^{-1}P'(m+\log t)$. Since $t\geq1$ in the interval between $1$ and $T(y)$, \eqref{eq:taylor-price-derivatives} yields
\begin{equation}
 |g_m^{(r)}(t)|\leq H C_r(\lambda),\qquad
 C_r(\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=1}^r|s(r,\ell)|\lambda^{-\ell}B_{\ell-1}.
 \label{eq:taylor-uniform-constant}
\end{equation}

For a fixed non-negative order $k$, Taylor's theorem with integral remainder gives, for every $y$,
\[
 g_{m_G(y)}(T(y))
 =\sum_{a=0}^k\frac{(T(y)-1)^a}{a!}g_{m_G(y)}^{(a)}(1)
 +\frac1{k!}\int_1^{T(y)}(T(y)-t)^k g_{m_G(y)}^{(k+1)}(t)\,dt.
\]
The integral's absolute value is at most $H C_{k+1}(\lambda)(T(y)-1)^{k+1}/(k+1)!$. All moments of $T$ are finite, since each term is an exponential of a finite-dimensional Gaussian and there are finitely many terms. The displayed bound and the bounded derivatives justify taking expectations. With
\begin{equation}
 c_0=\mathbb E P_{\lambda,H}(m_G(Y)),\qquad
 c_a=\frac1{a!}\sum_{\ell=1}^a s(a,\ell)
       \mathbb E\bigl[(T(Y)-1)^aP_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(m_G(Y))\bigr],\quad a\geq1,
 \label{eq:taylor-coefficients}
\end{equation}
I obtain the finite-order certificate
\begin{equation}
 \left|\Pi-\sum_{a=0}^k c_a\right|
 \leq R_k,\qquad
 R_k=\frac{H C_{k+1}(\lambda)}{(k+1)!}\mathbb E[(T(Y)-1)^{k+1}].
 \label{eq:taylor-price-remainder}
\end{equation}
The direction of the remainder is not inferred from the non-negativity of $T-1$. The enclosure is two-sided. Each finite $k$ is admissible, even when increasing $k$ enlarges this bound. In particular, the finiteness of every lognormal moment does not imply that $R_k\to0$ as $k\to\infty$.

\subsection{Finite Gaussian evaluation of every coefficient and remainder moment}
\label{sec:taylor-gaussian-tilt}

For a multi-index $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\in\mathbb N_0^n$, write $|\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i$, $\alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!$, $w^\alpha=\prod_iw_i^{\alpha_i}$, $d_\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_id_i$ and $v_\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_is_i$. The multinomial theorem gives
\[
 T(y)^j=\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}w^\alpha
             \exp(d_\alpha+v_\alpha\cdot y).
\]
There are $\binom{n+j-1}{j}$ terms. For $j=0$, the unique zero multi-index has weight one, exponent zero and exposure zero. Completing the square in the Gaussian density gives, for every integrable $f$ for which the left side exists,
\[
 \mathbb E[e^{v\cdot Y}f(\mu_G+R_G\cdot Y)]
 =e^{\|v\|^2/2}\mathbb E[f(\mu_G+R_G\cdot v+R_G\cdot Y)].
\]
The identity follows from $e^{v\cdot y-\|y\|^2/2}=e^{\|v\|^2/2}e^{-\|y-v\|^2/2}$ and the change of variable $y-v$. Put $\sigma^2=\lambda^2+\|R_G\|^2$. Gaussian convolution and bounded differentiation show, for each $\ell\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[e^{v\cdot Y}P_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(\mu_G+R_G\cdot Y)]
 =e^{\|v\|^2/2}P_{\sigma,H}^{(\ell)}(\mu_G+R_G\cdot v).
 \label{eq:taylor-tilt-identity}
\end{equation}
For $\ell=0$, this follows by combining the independent Gaussian variables $R_G\cdot Y$ and $\lambda Z$. For $\ell\geq1$, differentiation with respect to $\mu_G$ passes under the expectation by the uniform bound in \eqref{eq:taylor-price-derivatives}. This reasoning remains valid when $R_G=0$.

I therefore define the explicitly finite sums
\begin{align}
 U_{j,\ell}&=\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}w^\alpha
 \exp(d_\alpha+\|v_\alpha\|^2/2)
 P_{\sigma,H}^{(\ell)}(\mu_G+R_G\cdot v_\alpha),
 \label{eq:taylor-tilted-finite-sum}\\
 M_r&=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj
 \sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}w^\alpha
          \exp(d_\alpha+\|v_\alpha\|^2/2).
 \label{eq:taylor-moment-finite-sum}
\end{align}
Then $c_0=P_{\sigma,H}(\mu_G)$, $M_r=\mathbb E[(T-1)^r]$ and, for $a\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 c_a=\frac1{a!}\sum_{\ell=1}^a s(a,\ell)
               \sum_{j=0}^a(-1)^{a-j}\binom aj U_{j,\ell}.
 \label{eq:taylor-evaluable-coefficient}
\end{equation}
These expressions retain the dependence between $T$ and $m_G$. Replacing $m_G$ by its mean before applying the Gaussian tilt would remove the shift $R_G\cdot v_\alpha$ and would generally change the coefficient.

For implementation, set $z_0=m/\sigma+\sigma$, $z_1=(m-L_H)/\sigma+\sigma$ and $Q(m)=\Phi(z_0)-\Phi(z_1)$. The original capped-price formula is
\[
 P_{\sigma,H}(m)=K[e^{m+\sigma^2/2}Q(m)-\Phi(m/\sigma)+(1+H/K)\Phi((m-L_H)/\sigma)].
\]
For $x_0=m/\sigma$, $x_1=(m-L_H)/\sigma$ and $z_i=x_i+\sigma$, direct substitution into the normal density gives $e^{m+\sigma^2/2}\phi(z_0)=\phi(x_0)$ and $e^{m+\sigma^2/2}\phi(z_1)=e^{L_H}\phi(x_1)=(1+H/K)\phi(x_1)$. Since $Q'(m)=[\phi(z_0)-\phi(z_1)]/\sigma$, these two identities cancel the boundary density terms in the derivative and give $P_{\sigma,H}'(m)=K e^{m+\sigma^2/2}Q(m)$. For $j\geq1$,
\[
 Q^{(j)}(m)=\sigma^{-j}(-1)^{j-1}
       [\operatorname{He}_{j-1}(z_0)\phi(z_0)
        -\operatorname{He}_{j-1}(z_1)\phi(z_1)],
 \qquad
 \operatorname{He}_{j+1}(z)=z\operatorname{He}_j(z)-j\operatorname{He}_{j-1}(z),
\]
with $\operatorname{He}_0=1$ and $\operatorname{He}_1=z$. The product rule therefore gives
\[
 P_{\sigma,H}^{(\ell)}(m)=K e^{m+\sigma^2/2}
                    \sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell-1}{j}Q^{(j)}(m),\qquad\ell\geq1.
\]
I check this closed-form derivative calculation against formal power-series differentiation and the strike-integral representation rather than treating its appearance as validation.

\begin{theorem}[A finite Gaussian price certificate]
\label{thm:finite-gaussian-price-certificate}
Under the positive-weight, independent-Gaussian and positive-scale hypotheses
of Section~\ref{sec:taylor-geometry}, let $k\geq0$ be an integer and define
$c_a$, $M_{k+1}$ and $C_{k+1}(\lambda)$ by
\eqref{eq:taylor-evaluable-coefficient},
\eqref{eq:taylor-moment-finite-sum} and
\eqref{eq:taylor-uniform-constant}, with $c_0=P_{\sigma,H}(\mu_G)$.
Then all these quantities are finite and
\[
 \Pi\in\left[\sum_{a=0}^k c_a-
 \frac{H C_{k+1}(\lambda)M_{k+1}}{(k+1)!},
 \sum_{a=0}^k c_a+
 \frac{H C_{k+1}(\lambda)M_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\right]\cap[0,H].
\]
For rational model coefficients, or other supplied computable coefficients
with validated enclosures and $\lambda$ bounded away from zero, the endpoints
have finite expressions in exponential and scalar Gaussian functions that
can be enclosed to any prescribed positive arithmetic accuracy. This
statement does not require increasing $k$ until a Taylor series converges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exact weighted centring gives $T\geq1$. The falling-factorial derivative
identity and Fourier bound give the uniform majorant
$|g_m^{(k+1)}(t)|\leq H C_{k+1}(\lambda)$ for $t\geq1$.
The integral Taylor remainder is therefore bounded pointwise by
$H C_{k+1}(\lambda)(T-1)^{k+1}/(k+1)!$.
Every moment of $T$ is finite, so this bound can be integrated.
The Gaussian tilt and multinomial identities evaluate each coefficient
and the non-negative remainder moment by the stated finite sums, retaining
the dependence between $T$ and the geometric log price. The exact payoff
range permits intersection with $[0,H]$.
All elementary functions in these finite sums are computable at positive
scales, and finite sums and products preserve computability. Validated
outward arithmetic, with input refinement where needed, encloses those
operations. Finiteness of the expression does not imply that its remainder
bound decreases without limit as $k$ increases.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Different initial prices and monitored averages}
\label{sec:taylor-financial-extension}

The algebra also covers unequal positive initial prices. Put
$G_0=\prod_i S_{i,0}^{w_i}$ and replace
$d_i$ by $\log(S_{i,0}/G_0)+\mu_i-\mu_G$.
Its weighted mean is still zero. The smoothed scalar kernel is then
$P_{\lambda,H}(m+\log(G_0/K))$, so every tilted evaluation uses
$\mu_G+\log(G_0/K)+R_G\cdot v_\alpha$.
The derivative constants are unchanged because their majorants are uniform
in the base log price. This proves the extension without assuming equal
initial prices or independence of the arithmetic--geometric ratio and its
geometric control.

A discretely monitored arithmetic Asian payoff can be treated by taking
its positive asset-monitor observations as basket components. If their joint

with an independent residual Gaussian factor, the same argument applies;
the earlier monitored-factor criterion specifies that hypothesis.
The number of components in the multinomial count then includes monitoring

\subsection{Outward arithmetic and the retained experimental scope}
\label{sec:taylor-outward-scope}

Every frozen decimal input is parsed as an exact rational number. I compute exact multinomial weights, exposures, dot products and shifts before enclosing the exponential, logarithm, square root, error function and all subsequent arithmetic by outward Arb balls at the predeclared precision of 224 bits. Cancellation in \eqref{eq:taylor-moment-finite-sum} and \eqref{eq:taylor-evaluable-coefficient} remains inside this arithmetic; it is not estimated by decimal display precision. If $[L_k,U_k]$ encloses $\sum_{a=0}^k c_a$ and $\overline R_k$ is an exact upper endpoint of an Arb enclosure of the non-negative expression in \eqref{eq:taylor-price-remainder}, the price enclosure is
\[
 [L_k-\overline R_k,U_k+\overline R_k]\cap[0,H].
\]
I retain its endpoints as exact dyadic rationals. Multiplication by an outward enclosure of the positive factor $e^{-rT_{\rm mat}}$ gives a separate discounted-price interval. The reported success test is that the undiscounted interval has midpoint radius at most $0.01$ in the model's unspecified price currency unit. No stochastic confidence level is needed for this deterministic bound.

The calculation retains orders zero through eight for all 27 frozen contracts. Evaluation through order eight requires $\binom{n+8}{8}$ tilted-kernel calls per cap; the ninth-order remainder requires $\binom{n+9}{9}$ geometric terms including all lower degrees. These counts are 45 and 55 for two assets, 495 and 715 for four assets, and 12,870 and 24,310 for eight assets. The reported run evaluates all retained orders before selecting the narrowest certified interval; I also retain the first order attaining the requested radius, rather than presenting its lower term count as the measured cost of the full run. The finite Taylor certificate succeeds in all eighteen four- and eight-asset contracts and in seven of the nine two-asset controls. The two failures have $\lambda=0.05$ and caps 10 and 25. They are failures of the retained uniform remainder bound, not evidence of a pricing discrepancy. The older independent continuous quadrature resolves those two controls. These results materially strengthen the classical comparator but do not, without literature comparison, establish originality of the expansion or a wall-clock relation between CPU work and quantum logical T counts.

\section{Randomised finite-grid pricing with certified stopping}
\label{sec:higherdim-rqmc}

Independent bounded randomised-quadrature replicates and non-asymptotic confidence procedures have published antecedents \citep{jainrqmc,maurerpontil,waudbysmithramdas}. I derive the particular shift-batch and checkpoint guarantees used here in full.

\subsection{Exact finite targets under independent digital shifts}
\label{sec:rqmc-finite-target}

I fix a certified financial graph before generating any random shifts. Its input
group is $\mathcal G=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\{0,\ldots,2^{b_j}-1\}$, with addition given
by coordinatewise bitwise exclusive-or, denoted by $\oplus$. The retained graph
returns an integer $a(u)$, and I write $x(u)=2^{-q}a(u)$. The finite range
certificate gives $0\leq x(u)\leq\rho$ for every $u\in\mathcal G$. I denote
its exact uniform mean by
\[
 \mu=|\mathcal G|^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathcal G}x(u).
\]
For any deterministic points $t_1,\ldots,t_N\in\mathcal G$, a shift
$\Delta$ that is exactly uniform on $\mathcal G$ defines
\[
 B(\Delta)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}x(t_i\oplus\Delta).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Uniform finite marginals and independent shift batches]
\label{prop:rqmc-finite-unbiased}
For every deterministic point set, $0\leq B(\Delta)\leq\rho$ and
$\mathbb E B(\Delta)=\mu$. If the shift vectors $\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_R$
are independent and each exactly uniform on $\mathcal G$, their batch means
$B(\Delta_1),\ldots,B(\Delta_R)$ are independent and identically distributed.
These statements do not imply independence among the points within a batch
or a variance reduction proportional to $N^{-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every fixed $t\in\mathcal G$, the map $\Delta\mapsto t\oplus\Delta$
is its own inverse and therefore a bijection of the finite group. Consequently,
for every $u\in\mathcal G$,
$\mathbb P(t\oplus\Delta=u)=|\mathcal G|^{-1}$. Thus
$\mathbb E x(t\oplus\Delta)=\mu$. Linearity of expectation gives
$\mathbb E B(\Delta)=N^{-1}\sum_i\mu=\mu$, without using pointwise
independence. The range follows by averaging values in $[0,\rho]$.
Finally, each batch mean is the same deterministic function of its own shift
vector. Applying deterministic functions separately to independent identically
distributed vectors preserves independence and their common distribution.
\end{proof}

The engine forms its digital points as integers, truncating the retained
32-bit Joe--Kuo words to the relevant $b_j$ leading bits. It then draws an
independent $b_j$-bit shift for every coordinate of each replicate. It applies
exclusive-or directly to these finite words. No conversion to a floating-point
uniform variate enters the financial graph. The confidence argument above
uses only the finite-group bijection; it does not infer accuracy from the
quality or discrepancy of the chosen direction numbers. I retain the
author-distributed direction file, algorithm and BSD licence described in
the source ledger. My actual PCG64DXSM stream is a reproducible numerical
implementation of the declared ideal-shift model, and is not a proof of
that model's randomness assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[A range and moment prior for a shifted batch]
\label{prop:rqmc-prior-variance}
Suppose that the finite target has proved bounds
$\mathbb E x(U)\leq u$ and $\mathbb E x(U)^2\leq v$, where $U$ is uniform
on $\mathcal G$, $0\leq u\leq\rho$ and $0\leq v\leq\rho^2$.
For $\rho>0$, define
\[
 m_* = \min\{u,\rho/2,v/\rho\},\qquad
 V_* = m_*(\rho-m_*).
\]
Then $\operatorname{Var}(B)\leq V_*$. For $\rho=0$, $B=0$ and its variance
is zero. This bound requires no assumption on the within-batch covariance.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of the square gives, for every shift,
\[
 B(\Delta)^2\leq\frac1N\sum_i x(t_i\oplus\Delta)^2.
\]
The preceding proposition therefore yields $\mathbb E B^2\leq v$.
The pointwise inequality $B^2\leq\rho B$ gives a second bound
$\mathbb E B^2\leq\rho\mu$. Also $0\leq\mu\leq u$. Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(B)\leq\min\{v,\rho\mu\}-\mu^2.
\]
To maximise this upper bound on $\mu\in[0,u]$, split the interval at
$v/\rho$. On $[0,v/\rho]$ the expression is
$\rho\mu-\mu^2$, whose derivative is $\rho-2\mu$. It increases until
$\rho/2$ and decreases afterwards. On $[v/\rho,u]$, when that interval is
nonempty, the expression is $v-\mu^2$ and is nonincreasing. The maximum is
therefore attained at $\mu=m_*$ and equals $V_*$. The degenerate case is
immediate from $0\leq B\leq0$.
\end{proof}

I use this deterministic prior only to select the graph and to cap the
confidence bound on its batch variance. My prospective selection count is
\[
 R_{\mathrm{plan}}=
 \max\left\{1,
 \left\lceil
 \frac{(2V_*+2\rho\epsilon/3)\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{event}})}
      {\epsilon^2}
 \right\rceil\right\},
 \qquad \epsilon=\frac{0.005}{H}.
\]
I minimise $64R_{\mathrm{plan}}$ times the retained conservative scalar-lane
operation count over both fully conditioned methods and all their inherited
prefixes. This count is a prospective graph-selection objective. It is not
the production stopping rule, a measured runtime, a globally optimal
classical cost, or a claimed $N^{-1}$ variance rate. I retain all candidates
and the deterministic tie-breaking rule before generating the production
shifts. The production confidence construction uses the prescribed
checkpoint allocation in the separate confidence proposition.

\subsection{Exact batch statistics and monetary reconstruction}
\label{sec:rqmc-integer-reconstruction}

In the retained experiment $N=64$ and $q\leq30$. For replicate $j$ I compute
\[
 T_j=\sum_{i=1}^{64}a(t_i\oplus\Delta_j),\qquad
 D=64\,2^q,\qquad B_j=T_j/D.
\]
Each summand is at most $2^q$, so $T_j\leq2^{36}$ and the 64-word unsigned
sum is exact. All cross-replicate totals and square totals are assembled
as arbitrary-precision integers. For each finite checkpoint I retain
$S=\sum_jT_j$, $S_2=\sum_jT_j^2$ and
$Q=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor R/2\rfloor}(T_{2k-1}-T_{2k})^2$. They determine
\[
 \overline B=\frac{S}{RD},\qquad
 \widehat\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}^2=
 \frac{Q}{2\lfloor R/2\rfloor D^2},\qquad
 \widehat\sigma_{\mathrm{sample}}^2=
 \frac{RS_2-S^2}{R(R-1)D^2}.
\]
The ordinary sample variance is retained as a diagnostic. It does not
substitute for the proved pair-based confidence bound.

The square reduction uses $T=\ell+2^{16}h$, where
$0\leq\ell<2^{16}$ and $0\leq h\leq2^{20}$. In a reduction containing
at most $2^{20}$ words, $\sum\ell^2<2^{52}$,
$\sum\ell h<2^{56}$ and $\sum h^2\leq2^{60}$.
Each unsigned 64-bit reduction is therefore safe. I reconstruct
\[
 \sum T^2=\sum\ell^2+2^{17}\sum\ell h+2^{32}\sum h^2
\]
using Python integers. The same argument applies to the unsigned absolute
paired differences, which are at most $2^{36}$. I retain the integer batch
sums, shift-stream hash, financial-output hash and predetermined scalar
replay points. A separate interpreter replays every retained financial
output, every batch sum and every checkpoint moment.

If the confidence construction returns $\mu\in[\ell_\mu,h_\mu]$, and the
retained geometric correction centre is $c$ with total correction,
arithmetic and distribution allowance $\beta$, the undiscounted payoff
is enclosed by
\[
 [c+H\ell_\mu-\beta,\ c+Hh_\mu+\beta]\cap[0,H].
\]
I apply the strictly positive discount factor to this interval and report
an enclosed midpoint with its rounding allowance. For these frozen contracts,
$\beta\leq0.005$ and $r=0.03$, $T=1$. If the finite-mean confidence
half-width is at most $0.005/H$, the price interval has half-width at most
$e^{-0.03}(0.005+0.005)<0.01$, apart from the explicitly retained numerical
enclosure increments, which are smaller than the strict slack. If the
confidence intersection is empty, I retain that outcome as an explicit
confidence failure and produce no reconstructed price interval. If the
prespecified maximum is reached with a wider nonempty interval, I retain
the interval and record that the requested statistical certificate was
not obtained.

\subsection{A finite-checkpoint confidence certificate for independent shift batches}
\label{sec:rqmc-confidence-certificate}

I treat an entire independently shifted digital net as one observation. I assume
that $B_1,\ldots,B_R$ are independent copies of a variable $B\in[0,\rho]$, where
$0\leq\rho\leq1$. I write $\mu=\mathbb E B$, $\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(B)$,
$m=\lfloor R/2\rfloor$, and
\[
 Z_i=\tfrac12(B_{2i-1}-B_{2i})^2,\quad
 M=\rho^2/2,\quad
 \overline Z=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m Z_i,\quad
 \overline B=R^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^R B_j.
\]
The $Z_i$ are independent, $0\leq Z_i\leq M$, and
$\mathbb E Z_i=\sigma^2$. The last identity follows by expanding the square
and using independence of the two batch means in each pair. It does not require
independence among financial evaluations within a batch.

\begin{lemma}[A pair-based upper confidence bound on the batch variance]
\label{lem:rqmc-pair-variance}
Let $0<\delta<1$, put $t=\log(1/\delta)/m$, and define
\[
 U_{\rm pair}=\overline Z+Mt+
       \sqrt{M^2t^2+2Mt\overline Z}.
\]
Then $\mathbb P\{\sigma^2>U_{\rm pair}\}\leq\delta$.
If a deterministic certificate gives $\sigma^2\leq V_*$, the same statement
holds for $U=\min\{V_*,U_{\rm pair}\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $x\geq0$, $e^{-x}\leq1-x+x^2/2$. One proof observes that
$g(x)=1-x+x^2/2-e^{-x}$ has $g(0)=g'(0)=0$ and
$g''(x)=1-e^{-x}\geq0$. Since $Z_i^2\leq MZ_i$, for every $\theta\geq0$,
\[
 \mathbb E e^{-\theta Z_i}
 \leq1-\theta\sigma^2+\theta^2M\sigma^2/2
 \leq\exp(-\theta\sigma^2+\theta^2M\sigma^2/2).
\]
Multiplication over the independent pairs and Markov's inequality give, for
$s\geq0$ and $\sigma^2>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P\{\overline Z\leq\sigma^2-s\}
 \leq\inf_{\theta\geq0}
       \exp(-m\theta s+m\theta^2M\sigma^2/2)
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{ms^2}{2M\sigma^2}\right).
\]
When $M=0$ or $\sigma^2=0$, all $Z_i$ vanish almost surely and the conclusion
is immediate. In the other cases $U_{\rm pair}$ is the larger root, as a
function of $v$, of $(v-\overline Z)^2=2Mtv$. Therefore
$\sigma^2>U_{\rm pair}$ implies
$\overline Z<\sigma^2-\sqrt{2Mt\sigma^2}$. If this threshold is negative,
the event is empty. Otherwise the preceding inequality, with
$s=\sqrt{2Mt\sigma^2}$, bounds its probability by $e^{-mt}=\delta$.
The deterministic prior can be imposed by intersection without introducing
another random event.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A mean interval with a variance estimate from the same observations]
\label{prop:rqmc-mean-confidence}
Let $0<\alpha_v,\alpha_m<1$, use the preceding lemma with
$\delta=\alpha_v$, and set
\[
 L=\log(2/\alpha_m),\qquad b=\frac{\rho L}{3R},\qquad
 w(U)=b+\sqrt{b^2+\frac{2UL}{R}}.
\]
Suppose that a deterministic mean certificate gives $0\leq\mu\leq u\leq\rho$.
The possibly empty interval
\[
 I=[\max\{0,\overline B-w(U)\},\ \min\{u,\overline B+w(U)\}]
\]
contains $\mu$ with probability at least $1-\alpha_v-\alpha_m$. An empty
intersection is an explicit confidence-failure outcome and cannot supply a
price estimate. The variance and mean observations need not be independent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For completeness, put $Y=B-\mu$. Then $\mathbb E Y=0$, $|Y|\leq\rho$ and
$\mathbb E Y^2=\sigma^2$. For $k\geq2$,
$\mathbb E|Y|^k\leq\rho^{k-2}\sigma^2$ and
$k!\geq2\,3^{k-2}$. Expanding the exponential and bounding the absolute
values of its terms show that, for $|\theta|\rho<3$,
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\theta Y}
 \leq 1+\frac{\sigma^2\theta^2}{2(1-|\theta|\rho/3)}
 \leq\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2\theta^2}{2(1-|\theta|\rho/3)}\right).
\]
This derivation applies to $Y$ and $-Y$. Independence, Markov's inequality
and the choice $\theta=e/(\sigma^2+\rho e/3)$ give, for $e>0$ and
$\sigma^2>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\overline B-\mu|\geq e\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{Re^2}{2\sigma^2+2\rho e/3}\right).
\]
If $\sigma^2=0$, $\overline B=\mu$ almost surely and the required conclusion
is immediate. Solving $Re^2=L(2\sigma^2+2\rho e/3)$ gives $e=w(\sigma^2)$.
Thus the event
$E_m=\{|\overline B-\mu|\leq w(\sigma^2)\}$ has probability at least
$1-\alpha_m$. The pair lemma gives
$E_v=\{\sigma^2\leq U\}$ with probability at least $1-\alpha_v$.
On their intersection, monotonicity of $w$ gives
$|\overline B-\mu|\leq w(U)$, and the deterministic prior contains $\mu$.
A union bound therefore proves the stated coverage. In particular, replacing
$\sigma^2$ by the random $U$ is justified on a joint event, rather than by
conditioning a Bernstein inequality on an estimated variance.
\end{proof}

I prescribe eleven checkpoints $R_j=2^{6+j}$, $j=0,\ldots,10$, before
observing any shifts. At every checkpoint I assign
$\alpha_v=\alpha_m=1/2200$. Applying the proposition at each checkpoint
and then a union bound gives simultaneous coverage at least
$1-22/2200=0.99$ for one selected price. This permits stopping at the first
of these checkpoints whose nonempty interval meets the retained tolerance.
It does not establish coverage for an unprescribed stopping time, or
99 per cent simultaneous coverage for all 27 prices. The batches at different
checkpoints share observations; independence between checkpoints is unnecessary
for this finite union bound.

The numerical implementation replaces both logarithms by upward enclosures.
For rational arguments $1<x\leq10^{40}$, I evaluate their quotient and logarithm
with correctly rounded \texttt{Decimal} arithmetic at 160 significant digits,
and add $10^{-120}$. The quotient has relative rounding error at most
$5\times10^{-160}$; $\log x<93$, and the elementary logarithm and final addition
have absolute rounding errors below $10^{-157}$. The increment therefore
strictly exceeds the accumulated rounding error. A rational square-root
argument $a\geq0$ is enclosed by
\[
 \operatorname{sqrt}_{+}(a)=
  2^{-160}\left\lceil\sqrt{\lceil 2^{320}a\rceil}\right\rceil.
\]
Both integer roundings are evaluated with arbitrary-precision integers and
$\operatorname{sqrt}_{+}(a)^2\geq a$ exactly. These upward substitutions
can only enlarge $U_{\rm pair}$ and $w(U)$, apart from the justified cap by
$V_*$. Interval endpoints and recorded statistics are rational. The software
premise of correctly rounded elementary functions remains explicit.

The probability inequalities above are established statistical mechanisms,
with published empirical-Bernstein randomised quasi-Monte Carlo antecedents.
I use the derivation to justify the particular finite-checkpoint certificate,
not to claim a new generic confidence theorem. The separate retained audit
checks every stored integer statistic and endpoint, and exactly sums
Bernoulli pair-type probabilities at $R=64$ and $R=128$. These finite
checks provide implementation evidence; the proof supplies the distribution-free
claim under its stated independence and range hypotheses.

\subsection{What the comparison establishes}

The useful mathematical object is now the explicit continuous-model
certificate in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-gaussian-price-certificate}. It
provides a sufficient test for pricing to a stated tolerance without
quantum mean-estimation calls. Failure of that test at a chosen order does
not prove that quantum computation is necessary, that higher orders succeed,
or that a different classical method fails. The all-contract combination
with quadrature is a constructive benchmark result for this frozen Gaussian
family, rather than a universal statement about the difficulty of financial
pricing.

The previous 82.1 per cent T-cap saving remains a checked comparison between
encodings. It cannot carry an advantage claim for these contracts when a
classical price enclosure already meets the required accuracy. Nor does the
presence of fixed-point and logical-gate costs constitute a physical quantum
runtime comparison. The exact mathematical contribution candidate concerns
the evaluable coefficient, remainder and method-selection argument; published
Taylor expansions, geometric controls and basket bounds remain essential
predecessors \citep{ju,kornzeytun,caldana,jiprezedate}.
The consulted sources distinguish their constructions, but do not establish
absence of an equivalent certificate from all earlier literature.
The full Ju article and compatible modern quantum algorithms remain material
comparison gaps. A positive literature claim requires completing those
specific comparisons, rather than adding model names or repeating the
resource saving as if it were an algorithmic breakthrough.
