\begin{abstract}
I derive the exact financial invariants and the basis-dependent continuous-to-finite-grid allowance used in the registered rational factor-basis comparison. The rotation is compiled into loading coefficients before the pricing circuit is built. It is not a quantum state rotation, and Gaussian invariance does not establish invariance of a truncated or rounded product grid. The proof supplies a common continuous derivative-price target and a separately valid monetary certificate for each representation. The mathematics uses established Gaussian invariance, differentiation and a cell Taylor identity; I do not claim a new general rotation or transport theorem.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} derivative pricing; Gaussian factorisation; exact orthogonal rotation; continuous-to-finite-grid certification; logical quantum resources.

\section{Financial model and the exact basis change}
Orthogonal changes of Gaussian representation have an established role in
classical derivative-pricing dimension reduction \cite{imai2006}.
The finite monetary certificate below is my project-specific application;
it does not establish priority for that general mechanism.
Let $w_i>0$, $\sum_{i=1}^n w_i=1$, $K>0$, $\lambda>0$, and
$R\in\R^{n\times p}$. The application has rational weights and loading
coefficients, $K=100$, maturity one year, cap $H=1$ and $p=n-1$.
Let $Z\sim N(0,1)$ and $Y\sim N(0,I_p)$ be independent under the specified
risk-neutral measure. The terminal asset levels are
\[
 S_i=K\exp\{\mu_i+\lambda Z+R_iY\},
 \qquad
 \mu_i=(r-q)-\tfrac12(\lambda^2+\|R_i\|_2^2).
\]
Here $R_i$ is a row vector. These are the terminal laws of the established
constant-coefficient geometric Brownian model with rate $r$ and common
dividend yield $q$. The stated drift gives
$\E S_i=K e^{r-q}$; the corresponding discounted gain processes are
martingales. Pricing below uses this specified no-arbitrage measure and
does not infer a new asset-dynamics law from a quantum circuit.

For $O\in\R^{p\times p}$ with $OO^\mathsf T=O^\mathsf TO=I_p$, set
$R^O=RO$. Write
\[
 \bar\mu=\sum_iw_i\mu_i,\quad
 r_G=\sum_iw_iR_i,\quad d_i=\mu_i-\bar\mu,\quad
 s_i=R_i-r_G.
\]
The geometric log level, arithmetic conditional level and relative gap are
\begin{align*}
 m(y)&=\bar\mu+r_Gy, &
 X(y)&=\sum_iw_i e^{\mu_i+R_i y},\\
 T(y)&=\frac{X(y)}{e^{m(y)}}=\sum_iw_i e^{d_i+s_i y},&
 U(y)&=T(y)-1.
\end{align*}
The identities $\sum_iw_i d_i=0$ and $\sum_iw_i s_i=0$ and convexity of
the exponential imply $T(y)\geq1$ for every $y$, hence $U(y)\geq0$.
The superscript $O$ denotes the same definitions with $R$ replaced by
$R^O$, with the same weights and drifts.

\begin{theorem}[Continuous financial invariants]\label{thm:invariants}
An exact orthogonal right rotation preserves the full log-asset covariance,
each risk-neutral drift and the joint continuous law of all terminal asset
levels. It also preserves the joint law of $(m,U)$, the geometric log
variance $\lambda^2+\|r_G\|_2^2$, weighted residual dispersion
$\sum_i w_i\|s_i\|_2^2$, every finite nonnegative integer moment of $U$,
and every integrable function of the terminal assets. In particular, the
arithmetic capped-call price and the exact geometric coefficients
$c_0=\E P_\lambda(m(Y))$ and
$c_1=\E[U(Y)P_\lambda'(m(Y))]$ are unchanged whenever the latter
expectation is integrable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $i,k$,
\[
 (R_iO)(R_kO)^\mathsf T=R_iOO^\mathsf T R_k^\mathsf T
 =R_iR_k^\mathsf T.
\]
Thus the covariance entries
$\lambda^2+R_iR_k^\mathsf T$, including their diagonal entries, are
unchanged. The drift formula depends only on those diagonal entries, so
each $\mu_i$ is unchanged. Linear weighting gives
$r_G^O=r_GO$ and $s_i^O=s_iO$.
Orthogonality preserves their norms and inner products, proving the
assertions about geometric variance and residual dispersion.

If $Y^O\sim N(0,I_p)$, then $OY^O\sim N(0,I_p)$: its characteristic
function at $v$ is
$\exp\{-\|O^\mathsf T v\|_2^2/2\}=\exp\{-\|v\|_2^2/2\}$.
The pair $(Z,OY^O)$ has the same law as $(Z,Y)$ because $Y^O$ and $Z$
are independent. Pointwise,
$R^O_iY^O=R_i(OY^O)$,
$m^O(Y^O)=m(OY^O)$ and $U^O(Y^O)=U(OY^O)$.
Consequently the asserted joint laws agree, and expectations of
integrable functions agree. The capped payoff is bounded, so its
expectation is integrable without an additional tail assumption.

For completeness, every stated integer moment exists. For $k\geq0$,
multinomial expansion gives
\[
 \E T(Y)^k=
 \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^n\\|\alpha|=k}}
 \frac{k!}{\prod_i\alpha_i!}\prod_iw_i^{\alpha_i}
 \exp\left\{\sum_i\alpha_i d_i+
       \tfrac12\left\|\sum_i\alpha_i s_i\right\|_2^2\right\}.
\]
This is a finite sum of finite quantities. Each exponent is unchanged
because the vector inside the norm right-multiplies by $O$. Binomial
expansion of $(T-1)^k$ proves the same conclusion for $U^k$.
More generally, all Gaussian exponential monomials are determined by the
exact centred Gram matrix $[s_i s_k^\mathsf T]$ and, for geometric
exponential tilting, the cross products $r_Gs_i^\mathsf T$ and
$\|r_G\|_2^2$. These are checked by exact rational arithmetic in the
retained certificates. Since the global bound on $P_\lambda'$ below is
finite and $\E U<\infty$, the application also has an integrable $c_1$.
\end{proof}

\section{A basis-dependent direct residual certificate}
For $H>0$ define the smoothed conditional capped-call kernel
\[
 P_\lambda(x)=\E_Z\min\{(Ke^{x+\lambda Z}-K)_+,H\}.
\]
The positivity of $\lambda$ supplies Gaussian smoothing. The application
uses independently checked global constants
\[
 \|P_\lambda'\|_\infty\leq M_1,
 \qquad \|P_\lambda''-P_\lambda'\|_\infty\leq M_2.
\]
These kernel constants depend on $K,H,\lambda$, which the basis change
preserves. Define
\[
 F_O(y)=P_\lambda(\log X^O(y))-P_\lambda(m^O(y)).
\]
Monotonicity of $P_\lambda$ and weighted arithmetic--geometric mean
give $0\leq F_O(y)\leq H$ pointwise. The full undiscounted arithmetic
price is $c_0+\E F_O(Y)$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:invariants} proves that this
target is identical for every admitted basis.

Let
\[
 A_j(O)=\max_i|R^O_{ij}|,\qquad r_j(O)=(r_GO)_j,
\]
and set
\begin{align*}
 B_1(O)&=\sum_{j=1}^p\{A_j(O)+|r_j(O)|\},\\
 B_2(O)&=\sum_{j=1}^p A_j(O)^2+\|r_G\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
The second term of $B_2$ is invariant. The coordinatewise loading maxima
and the $\ell^1$ norm in $B_1$ need not be invariant.

\begin{lemma}[Global coordinate derivatives]\label{lem:derivatives}
The residual satisfies, for every $y\in\R^p$,
\[
 |\partial_j F_O(y)|\leq M_1\{A_j(O)+|r_j(O)|\},
 \qquad
 |\partial_{jj}F_O(y)|\leq (M_1+M_2)
                  \{A_j(O)^2+r_j(O)^2\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppress the superscript $O$ and write $\ell=\log X$ and
$\pi_i=w_i e^{\mu_i+R_i y}/X$. The numbers $\pi_i$ are nonnegative and
sum to one. Differentiation of the finite exponential sum gives
\[
 \ell_j=\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij},\qquad
 \ell_{jj}=\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}^2-\ell_j^2.
\]
Hence $|\ell_j|\leq A_j$ and
$\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}^2\leq A_j^2$. Since $m_j=r_j$ and $m_{jj}=0$,
$F_j=P_\lambda'(\ell)\ell_j-P_\lambda'(m)r_j$, proving the first bound.
For the second derivative, retain the log-Hessian cancellation:
\[
 (P_\lambda\circ\ell)_{jj}
 =(P_\lambda''-P_\lambda')(\ell)\ell_j^2
   +P_\lambda'(\ell)\sum_i\pi_i R_{ij}^2.
\]
Its absolute value is at most $(M_1+M_2)A_j^2$.
Also $|P_\lambda''|\leq M_1+M_2$, so
$|(P_\lambda\circ m)_{jj}|\leq(M_1+M_2)r_j^2$.
Their difference obeys the stated bound. No sign assumption on loadings
has been used, and replacing the full $A_j$ by a centred-loading maximum
would not follow from this argument.
\end{proof}

Let $\nu(dy)=\ind_{[-L,L]}(y)\varphi(y)\,dy$, of mass
$a=\Phi(L)-\Phi(-L)$. Partition $[-L,L]$ into $N$ intervals of equal
$\nu$-mass $a/N$. For each cell $C$ choose its actually implemented
rounded midpoint-quantile node $c_C\in C$, and put
\[
 \tau=\frac aN\sum_C\delta_{c_C},\quad
 \mu_{1,C}=\int_C(y-c_C)\nu(dy),\quad
 \mu_{2,C}=\int_C(y-c_C)^2\nu(dy),
\]
\[
 b_1=\sum_C|\mu_{1,C}|,\qquad
 b_2=\frac12\sum_C\mu_{2,C}.
\]
The same one-dimensional rule is used in each coordinate of each basis,
with its cell masses and rounded nodes certified outward. In particular,
$\tau$ is unnormalised; the uniformly sampled finite label distribution
is $\tau/a$.

\begin{proposition}[Common target, separate finite-grid allowances]
\label{prop:transfer}
For every admitted orthogonal matrix,
\begin{align*}
 \left|\E F_O(Y)-\int F_O\,d\tau^{\otimes p}\right|
 &\leq \beta(O),\\
 \beta(O)&=a^{p-1}\{b_1M_1B_1(O)
                 +b_2(M_1+M_2)B_2(O)\}+H(1-a^p).
\end{align*}
Consequently each finite-grid representation targets the same continuous
derivative price, with its own deterministic monetary error allowance.
For $r\geq0$, multiplication by $e^{-r}$ preserves the undiscounted
upper error bound. For general $r$, multiplication by $e^{-r}$ must be
retained explicitly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a twice continuously differentiable one-dimensional function $f$,
the integral Taylor identity at $c_C$ is
\[
 f(y)-f(c_C)=f'(c_C)(y-c_C)
 +(y-c_C)^2\int_0^1(1-t)f''(c_C+t(y-c_C))\,dt.
\]
Integration over $C$ and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \left|\int_C f\,d\nu-\frac aN f(c_C)\right|
 \leq |\mu_{1,C}|\sup|f'|+
           \frac{\mu_{2,C}}2\sup|f''|.
\]
The first moment is generally nonzero for a rounded quantile midpoint;
omitting its contribution would be invalid. The remainder is positive
in its weight, so the second moment already accounts for the exact
rounded node displacement.

Expand the tensor difference as the telescoping sum
\[
 \nu^{\otimes p}-\tau^{\otimes p}
 =\sum_{j=1}^p
 \nu^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes(\nu-\tau)
                  \otimes\tau^{\otimes(p-j)}.
\]
For the $j$th summand, hold the other coordinates fixed and apply the
preceding cell bound to $F_O$. Lemma~\ref{lem:derivatives} is global,
so its constants remain valid at every hybrid point. Integration in
the remaining $p-1$ coordinates multiplies the bound by $a^{p-1}$,
because every remaining factor has mass $a$. Summing over $j$ yields
the first two terms of $\beta(O)$. The continuous Gaussian mass outside
$[-L,L]^p$ is $1-a^p$, and $0\leq F_O\leq H$ bounds its contribution
by $H(1-a^p)$. This proves the full continuous-to-finite-grid bound.
The continuous target agrees across bases by
Theorem~\ref{thm:invariants}. Discounting is multiplication by the stated
positive deterministic factor, giving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact registration and the limits of the comparison}
The retained pool contains the identity and every single coordinate plane
$(a,b)$, $0\leq a<b<p$, with the exact rational block
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}3/5&-s\\s&3/5\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad s\in\{-4/5,4/5\}.
\]
Since $(3/5)^2+(4/5)^2=1$, each matrix is exactly orthogonal and all
rotated loadings remain rational. The pool has $1+2\binom p2$ elements,
giving counts $1,7,7,43$ for the four declared stress cases. The
screen chooses the smallest outward certified direct allowance, breaking
ties by candidate identifier. Every candidate is retained, and no
financial-price answer or quantum-resource outcome enters this choice.
Earlier loading-only proxy diagnostics motivated the direction, so this
is registration before the fresh full certificates rather than a claim
of blind discovery.

I retain identity and the screen-selected rotation for subsequent complete
resource costing, omitting a duplicate identity. Complete logical costs
then determine deployment within that declared pair. A lower
$\beta(O)$ alone does not prove a lower circuit charge: rotation may
increase finite support extrema, normalisers, fixed-point loading error,
lookup ranges or qubits. Coefficients, actual finite moments, finite
support and arithmetic profiles must be reconstructed in the new basis.
If another independent allowance is valid for the same representation,
the deterministic minimum remains valid. This selection has no statistical
selection penalty, but it does not give global basis or precision
optimality.

The equality in continuous law is not an assertion that
$O(\tau/a)^{\otimes p}=(\tau/a)^{\otimes p}$, nor that the truncation
box is rotation invariant. In general the rotation maps a box to a
different region and does not permute the rounded product support.
Accordingly, finite $U$ moments and the finite probability prior can
change even though their continuous counterparts do not. No previously
computed finite prior or oracle normaliser may be transferred on the
basis of Gaussian invariance alone. Since every representation is
separately tied to the same continuous target by
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}, there is no need to claim equality of
the finite targets or to treat one as an exact rotation of the other.

The rotation is classical parameter preparation. No quantum rotation gate
is performed by the pricing method. Its preparation cost belongs to the
shared classical setup record, and the complete rebuilt quantum oracle
must charge the new coefficients and the actual finite input law.
Every classical competitor receives the same rotated representation and
certificates. Any previously valid classical certificate for the common
continuous price remains valid under Theorem~\ref{thm:invariants}; it is
not weakened by changing the quantum implementation basis. The finite
pool can establish a project-specific reduction relative to the retained
identity quantum implementation, but neither a new Gaussian theorem nor
practical quantum advantage follows from that result.
