# Quantitative preparation for graduate study

## 1 Purpose and assessment

I use this project to demonstrate mathematical reasoning, financial modelling and reproducible computation in preparation for quantitative finance graduate study. A defensible project-specific investigation remains valuable, but a new quantum theory, a publication or practical quantum advantage is not a condition for that purpose. Oxford explicitly states that publications are not expected, while requiring probability, statistics, ODEs/PDEs, linear algebra and analysis, particularly real analysis ([2027/28 admissions](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/graduate/courses/msc-mathematical-and-computational-finance)).

The present repository demonstrates substantial applied mathematical depth. It does not demonstrate the whole prerequisite checklist or mastery of every graduate module. My principal remaining gaps in this project are differential-equation pricing, general statistical estimation/calibration, continuous constrained optimisation and broader analysis/probability preparation. These are distinct from subjects outside the selected research question.

This assessment distinguishes entry requirements, taught core material and optional specialisms. It concerns retained work, not independently assessed personal mastery. A credible application requires the ability to explain, reproduce and modify the results, with accurate disclosure of assistance and sources. A repository does not replace required degree subjects, grades or credits.

## 2 Evidence against the mathematical foundations

The [main paper](generalisation_proofs.tex) and [supporting index](supporting_proofs_index.md) contain the detailed derivations. The following classifications concern demonstrated operations and arguments, not course exemptions.

| Foundation | Present evidence | Boundary or missing evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single/multivariable calculus | Gaussian derivative recurrences, repeated chain/product rules, gradients, second derivatives, Taylor remainders, multiple integration and exponential moments; main Sections 3–5 and Gaussian transport appendix | No systematic coverage of principal values, gamma recursion or divergence/curl/Gauss/Stokes. Those topics should not be inserted into basket pricing merely to populate a checklist. |
| Linear/matrix algebra | Positive-definite covariance, positive-semidefinite residuals, quadratic forms, square roots, rank-one decomposition and orthogonal factor changes; financial model/common-factor appendix and factor-basis comparison | This is substantive applied evidence, not comprehensive treatment of vector spaces, linear maps and spectral theory. |

| Measure theory | Finite Borel/Stieltjes measures, atoms and one-sided CDF limits, Tonelli/Fubini, dominated convergence and conditional Fatou; conditional kernel, measure-change and transport proofs | No general construction of Lebesgue measure, Carathéodory extension, general Radon–Nikodym theorem or systematic Lp theory. |
| Probability | Gaussian conditioning/tilting, moments, independent factors, conditional prices, finite CDF error and non-asymptotic confidence | Limited distributional breadth; no systematic characteristic-function inversion, LLN/CLT derivation, conditional expectation as L2 projection or careful general optional-stopping exercise. |
| Stochastic calculus and finance | GBM, Itô-based log-price solution, constant Brownian drift change under an equivalent measure, martingale pricing and admissible-wealth no-arbitrage argument; financial model and measure-change appendix | These are explicit special cases. | Statistics and computational inference | Exact-binomial confidence, concentration bounds, confidence spending, finite moments and Monte Carlo/RQMC diagnostics | Confidence for numerical estimation is not model calibration. | Differential equations | A specified SDE and its explicit lognormal solution | No substantive ODE/PDE theory or implemented pricing PDE with boundary conditions, stability and convergence analysis. This is a material Oxford-related gap. |
| Optimisation | Convexity/Jensen/Cauchy arguments, scalar extrema and finite precision/budget selection | Enumerating resource settings is not evidence of general convex optimisation, KKT/duality, quadratic programming or dynamic programming. |
| Programming | Vectorised fixed-point Python, guarded arithmetic, validated integration/root isolation, independent checks and boundary regressions | Python is the demonstrated language. ## 4 What the thesis benchmark establishes

Oxford's [sample dissertation page](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/node/34183) confirms the 2021 European quantitative-finance thesis award. The supplied thesis is dated Trinity 2020. It connects Hawkes processes and a scaling limit to limit-order-book dynamics, derives likelihoods/gradients, estimates parameters, checks residual fit and stability, simulates SDEs and compares an SPDE alternative. Its conclusion reports imperfect performance, including a more volatile sample. The relevant material is in Sections 2–4, conclusion 5.1 and Appendices A/C, not merely the award label.

I take its connected mathematical and computational argument as the benchmark. It is a completed master's dissertation, not a pre-admission requirement or proof that every foundation must appear in one project. Its combination of assumptions, derivation, implementation, comparison and financial interpretation is transferable. Its specific data entitlement is not: access to its LOBSTER observations is neither needed nor assumed here. The supplied PDF and extracted text are not redistributed in this repository; the source identity and consulted sections are recorded in the register.

## 5 Development priorities

The first addition should connect the existing financial law to a classical pricing PDE and a reproducible numerical benchmark. For a one-asset or geometric-control case, I should derive the replication/PDE and martingale prices under stated assumptions, specify terminal/boundary conditions, implement a finite-difference solver and compare with the analytic price and Monte Carlo. Error/convergence, sensitivities, numerical stability and computational cost should be reported separately. This would demonstrate presently missing foundations while strengthening the classical comparison supporting the quantum method. It is not yet implemented.

A second, compact extension should derive conditional expectation as an L2 projection and its variance decomposition, then quantify the conditional/control-variate improvement already used in basket pricing. A subsequent estimation exercise can use explicitly synthetic return observations or authorised free data and derive a likelihood and parameter uncertainty. Estimating a physical-return mean does not identify the risk-neutral drift, which remains fixed by the no-arbitrage specification. Synthetic calibration demonstrates implementation and inference, not independent empirical validation. A constrained hedging or portfolio problem with proved convexity and KKT conditions is a coherent optimisation extension if it answers a financial question; it need not become another chapter of this pricing paper.

For Oxford-specific preparation, an original small C++ pricing implementation would add direct evidence in the language used by its computing courses. I should keep the Python implementation as the general portfolio entry point. Broader analysis and probability should be evidenced through graded coursework where available and independently solved exercises, rather than turning the research paper into a textbook.

## 6 Completion and presentation

I should expose a short reproducible financial example before the specialised resource comparisons: assumptions, mathematical target, price, numerical error and interpretation. The paper should retain necessary derivations and rigorous appendices. An admissions-facing README should make the proof/code connection visible and describe concrete work, not claim passion from volume or theoretical novelty from presentation.

For this purpose, completion means a coherent explained financial problem, checked mathematics, reproducible pricing comparisons, documented limitations and honest personal preparation evidence. A new theory or a favourable quantum result is optional. The frozen research records and source-priority limits remain intact; negative comparisons continue to demonstrate judgement. The current main paper remains a working paper until the selected completion work is actually finished. No commit is authorised by this audit.

The full published menus are retained within their stated accessible boundaries. Restricted materials, unavailable dynamic catalogues, every peripheral elective syllabus and unpublished future timetables have not been represented as read. Official inconsistencies and suspended/non-counting entries remain explicit in the register.
