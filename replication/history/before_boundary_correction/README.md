# Certified finite corrections for quantum basket pricing

## 1 Research question and result

I develop quantum derivative-pricing methods under an established classical no-arbitrage model. The [main paper](generalisation_proofs.tex) presents the current method, essential derivations and a focused proof appendix. The [supporting index](supporting_proofs_index.md) maps the full proofs, exact source versions, audited data and earlier investigations. The complete pre-condensation paper is preserved verbatim in history/full_derivations_before_condensation.

Analytical second-correction preconditioning lowers complete canonical-estimation T caps against a competent positive first-correction comparator in three active cases, by approximately 5.5, 20.8 and 59.9 per cent. Direct second-correction evaluation beats the integral oracle in every active matched case. Both financial value circuits, clean inverses, signed flags and their monetary-error certificates are implemented. The independent precision search retains 97 programs and 11,872 decisions, with actual emitted costs and joint confidence allocation.

These are conditional comparisons within the retained quantum compiler and finite precision pool. The stronger classical Taylor/quadrature policy already resolves all 27 synthetic prices, with zero quantum estimation calls. The two-asset grid validation itself uses paid classical reference work; it cannot be treated as free information when arguing advantage. No practical quantum advantage or theoretical priority is established.

## 2 Financial and numerical scope

The payoff is a positive-weight capped arithmetic basket call spread under constant-coefficient risk-neutral GBM with a common independent Gaussian factor. The price is a no-arbitrage expectation; quantum mechanics enters its coherent numerical evaluation. The derivative-norm theorem covers nonnegative threshold measures, including capped spreads and cash digitals. Exact finite product-grid moments and conditional error contracts are not restricted to one asset count.

All27 frozen model/cap cases remain retained. The three methods have 81 valid fixed-width arithmetic/domain certificates. The current complete continuous-to-finite quantum pricing budgets cover nine two-asset cases. The eighteen higher-dimensional cases retain failed sufficient financial-transfer envelopes and are not ranked as complete priced algorithms. All cases are synthetic. REIT parameters can later be examined with existing LSEG access; no new private data, entitlement or subscription is used.
