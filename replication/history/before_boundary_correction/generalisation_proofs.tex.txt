\title{Certified finite corrections for quantum basket pricing\\[3mm]
\large Finite Gaussian certificates, coherent value oracles and matched resource tests}
\author{Amanjeet Singh}
\date{7 October 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
I construct and certify finite-order quantum basket-pricing methods under
an established classical no-arbitrage model. Gaussian tilts evaluate
analytic control coefficients while preserving covariance, and a sharp
combined derivative norm bounds the remaining correction. I implement both
an integral density oracle and direct correction evaluation, with exact
finite-grid moment priors, signed flags and a complete monetary-error
certificate. An independently checked precision search retains 97 programs
and 11,872 decisions. Direct second-correction pricing lowers complete
logical T caps against the competent positive first-correction comparator
in three active cases, by approximately 5.5, 20.8 and 59.9 per cent.
The integral oracle loses to direct evaluation in every active matched case.
A quadratic auxiliary certificate substantially tightens its error budget
without changing its circuit. Detailed proofs, coherent gate checks and
rejected configurations are retained. The stronger classical certificates
already resolve all 27 synthetic contracts; these quantum comparisons
therefore establish conditional resource improvements, rather than
practical quantum advantage or theoretical priority.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textit{Keywords:} financial mathematics; capped basket option;
Gaussian conditioning; finite Taylor certificate; joint derivative norm;

\section{Research question and relation to the literature}
\label{sec:question}

The research question is whether analytic second-correction preconditioning
and signed moment priors reduce complete quantum pricing cost relative to
a positive first-correction evaluator, and whether an integral or direct
second-correction oracle supplies the better implementation at the same
financial accuracy and confidence.  I keep the underlying financial law classical.  Quantum
mechanics enters through coherent state preparation, reversible arithmetic,
interference, amplitude amplification and measurement.  The priced object
remains a risk-neutral expectation with an explicit no-arbitrage argument.
This distinction fixes the target before selecting an estimator and permits
a fair comparison with numerical finance methods which already exploit
conditioning, smoothing and analytical controls.

The paper has three connected mathematical components.  First, I retain a
finite-order Gaussian basket expansion and evaluate its coefficients by
exact exponential tilts, including their covariance shifts.  Secondly, I
bound the combined price derivative before taking absolute values of its
separate terms.  The resulting norm is sharp over positive threshold
measures of fixed mass and is computably enclosed by rational root isolation
and outward Gaussian evaluation.  Thirdly, I represent the exact second
Taylor correction as a bounded signed density-polynomial observable with
two auxiliary variables.  I turn that observable into complete finite value
and success-flag circuits, then derive the monetary consequences of every
financial, auxiliary, arithmetic and statistical approximation.

The integral construction has a specific reason to exist: it can evaluate a
small correction through products of small, nonnegative factors, whereas a
literal direct implementation subtracts separately evaluated conditional
prices.  This may change the precision required by particular arithmetic
constructions.  It does not by itself imply an estimator improvement.
Auxiliary randomisation adds conditional variance, and a stable direct
implementation can preserve the same cancellation.  The empirical question
therefore concerns complete matched preparations and estimators, with
adverse cases retained.  I include a direct second-correction evaluator
with the same normaliser, and a positive first-correction evaluator with
its own competent finite mean prior.

Geometric conditioning is established in financial computation.
\citet{curran} conditions Asian and portfolio prices on their geometric
mean, and \citet{dingechormann} develops conditional Monte Carlo and control
variates for baskets and Asian options.  Polynomial approximations and
Taylor expansions have substantial antecedents
\citep{ju,olivaresalvarez2016}.  The final published text of
\citet{olivaresalvarez2016} supplies a directly relevant conditional
polynomial-pricing comparison.  The full article of \citet{ju} remains
unavailable in the retained access record; its bibliographic identification
is not treated as a completed source-level exclusion.  General basket-price
bounds provide another substantial classical comparator
\citep{caldana}.  The signed-Stirling relationship between price and
log-price derivatives is already explicit in \citet{carr2001}.
I use these mechanisms as foundations, rather than relabelling them as
new discoveries.

Quantum amplitude estimation and amplification supply the established
mean-estimation framework \citep{brassard,montanaro}.  Complete derivative
pricing costs require far more than a favourable query exponent:
state preparation, financial arithmetic and error correction assumptions
can determine whether a proposed gain survives
\citep{rebentrost,chakrabarti}.  Derivative pricing by quantum signal
processing is a published alternative \citep{stamatopoulos}, and
\citet{rallfuller2023} connects amplitude estimation directly with quantum
signal processing.  The retained source audit separates their compatible
oracle assumptions from this implementation's realised contract.  It does
not convert an unevaluated quantum alternative into a defeated baseline.

My project-specific contribution is the checked joint-norm pricing
certificate and its use in fully specified second-correction oracles, finite
arithmetic contract and matched comparison.  Operator sharpness, finite
circuit correctness and monetary accuracy are proved separately.  The
numerical findings concern frozen synthetic contracts and emitted logical
circuits.  They do not establish world priority for the underlying calculus,
a realised device advantage, or calibration to observed exotic prices.
Source versions and consulted sections are recorded in the supporting
source ledgers.  Full earlier derivations and rejected constructions are
preserved in the supporting archive; the main text follows the present
method and its decisive comparisons.

\section{Financial model and the frozen contracts}
\label{sec:model}

Let $\tau>0$, $r_f\in\R$ and dividend yields $q_i\in\R$ be constant.
Under a physical probability, suppose the ex-dividend stock system is
\[
 dS_i/S_i=b_i\,dt+\Sigma_i\,dW,
\]
where the filtration is generated by a $d_B$-dimensional Brownian motion
and the constant matrix $\Sigma\in\R^{n\times d_B}$ has full row rank.
Choose $\theta$ satisfying
$\Sigma\theta=b-r_f\mathbf1+q$.  The strictly positive process
$L_t=\exp(-\theta\cdot W_t-\|\theta\|^2t/2)$ is a martingale of mean one:
the assertion follows by conditioning on independent Gaussian increments
and evaluating their exponential moment.  It defines an equivalent
probability $Q$ on the finite horizon.  Multiplying an increment's normal
density by its exponential factor shifts its mean by
$-\theta\Delta t$, preserving covariance and independence.  Therefore
$W_t^Q=W_t+\theta t$ is Brownian under $Q$, and the stock drift becomes
$r_f-q_i$.

The discounted cumulative gain
\[
 e^{-r_ft}S_i(t)+\int_0^t e^{-r_fu}q_iS_i(u)\,du
\]
has zero drift under $Q$.  The explicit GBM solution has finite second
moments on $[0,\tau]$, so its stochastic integral is a true martingale.
For a self-financing admissible zero-cost strategy whose discounted wealth
is bounded below, localisation followed by conditional Fatou's lemma makes
the discounted wealth a supermartingale.  Nonnegative terminal wealth which
is positive with positive probability would contradict its nonpositive
expectation.  This establishes absence of arbitrage in the stated
admissibility class.  Full row rank is sufficient, rather than necessary.
If Brownian risk is unspanned, I use this stipulated $Q$; the quantum
estimator does not select a new pricing measure.

At maturity, independent standard normals $Y\in\R^p$ and $Z\in\R$ give
\begin{equation}
 S_i=K\exp(\mu_i+R_i\cdot Y+\lambda Z),\qquad
 \mu_i=(r_f-q_i)\tau-\tfrac12(\|R_i\|^2+\lambda^2),
 \label{eq:stocks}
\end{equation}
with $K>0$ and $\lambda>0$.  The loadings include the horizon scale.
It\^o's formula for $\log S_i$ proves this expression and gives
$\E_QS_i=Ke^{(r_f-q_i)\tau}$.  For positive weights summing to one, define
\begin{equation}
 A=\sum_iw_iS_i,\quad G=\prod_iS_i^{w_i},\quad
 F_H(x)=\min\{(x-K)_+,H\},\quad
 V=\E_QF_H(A),\quad \Pi=e^{-r_f\tau}V.
 \label{eq:financial-target}
\end{equation}
The time-$t$ conditional price is
$e^{-r_f(\tau-t)}\E_Q[F_H(A)\mid\mathcal F_t]$.
Its discounted value is a bounded martingale, so adding it as a traded
claim preserves the same no-arbitrage argument.  The numerical certificate
below concerns this single model price; separately rounded quotes across
strikes need not form an arbitrage-free quoted surface.

Write
\begin{equation}
 \mu_G=\sum_iw_i\mu_i,\quad R_G=\sum_iw_iR_i,\quad
 d_i=\mu_i-\mu_G,\quad s_i=R_i-R_G,
 \quad m(y)=\mu_G+R_G\cdot y.
 \label{eq:geometry}
\end{equation}
Then $\sum_iw_id_i=0$ and $\sum_iw_is_i=0$ exactly.  With
\begin{equation}
 T(y)=\sum_iw_ie^{d_i+s_i\cdot y},\qquad U(y)=T(y)-1,
 \label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
the identity $A=GT(Y)$ holds.  Jensen's inequality gives $T\geq1$;
hence the arithmetic basket exceeds its geometric control pointwise.
This deterministic ordering justifies the positive first correction.
It does not imply that the ratio and the geometric level are independent.

The numerical family has $K=100$, $\tau=1$, $r_f=0.03$, $q_i=0.02$,
$n\in\{2,4,8\}$, $\lambda\in\{0.05,0.2,0.4\}$ and
$H\in\{1,10,25\}$, giving 27 contracts from nine covariance models.
The two-asset anchor uses $w=(1/2,1/2)$ and $R=(-0.1,0.2)^{\mathsf T}$.
For four and eight assets, the residual loading matrix is a scaled Helmert
contrast matrix, with weights
$(0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1)$ and
$(0.3,0.2,0.15,0.1,0.08,0.07,0.06,0.04)$ respectively.
Its scale gives weighted contrast dispersion $0.0225$ in the ideal matrix,
matching the two-asset anchor.  The actual 70-digit decimal loadings are
frozen as exact rationals, their drifts are computed from those rationals,
and exact rational $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ factorisation checks positive
covariance pivots and full volatility row rank.  The ideal-to-frozen
dispersion discrepancy is below $1.8\times10^{-71}$.
Matching this one dispersion does not match the full payoff distribution
or isolate a causal effect of dimension.

These are reproducible synthetic experiments.  No newly purchased dataset
or inaccessible option sample is used.  LSEG Workspace may support a later
publicly reproducible calibration exercise within the existing entitlement,
but historical returns alone would not validate an observed exotic price.
The present claims depend on exact model inputs and retained arithmetic
certificates, rather than an unreported private empirical sample.

\section{Conditional smoothing and the joint derivative certificate}
\label{sec:norm}

The capped payoff has the positive layer-cake representation
$F_H(x)=\int_K^{K+H}\ind\{x>k\}\,dk$.
Tonelli's theorem therefore gives the conditional kernel
\begin{equation}
 P_{\lambda,H}(m)=\E_ZF_H(Ke^{m+\lambda Z})
 =\int_0^{\log(1+H/K)}Ke^x\Phi((m-x)/\lambda)\,dx.
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
The undiscounted price is
$V=\E_Y P_{\lambda,H}(m(Y)+\log T(Y))$.
Conditioning has removed the nonsmooth payoff threshold from the financial
integrand.  It also supplies the exact geometric control
$c_0=\E P_{\lambda,H}(m(Y))$.
The positive first correction
$R_1=P_{\lambda,H}(m+\log T)-P_{\lambda,H}(m)$ is in $[0,H]$.
Gaussian smoothing and control identities are useful classical reductions
before any quantum encoding is considered \citep{curran,bayer}.

The derivative result extends beyond a single capped call.  Let $\nu$ be a
finite nonnegative Borel measure on real log thresholds, of mass $H$,
and define
\begin{equation}
 P_\nu(m)=\int\Phi((m-x)/\lambda)\nu(dx),\qquad
 g_m(t)=P_\nu(m+\log t),\quad t>0.
 \label{eq:measure-kernel}
\end{equation}
For the capped call,
$\nu(dx)=Ke^x\ind_{[0,\log(1+H/K)]}(x)dx$.
A cash digital is a point mass; finite positive mixtures of thresholds are
also covered.  Negative threshold weights, unbounded payoff mass and
negative basket weights are not hypotheses of this theorem.

Let $s(r,\ell)$ be signed Stirling numbers of the first kind, with
$s(0,0)=1$ and
$s(r+1,\ell)=s(r,\ell-1)-rs(r,\ell)$.
The Hermite polynomials satisfy
$\operatorname{He}_0=1$, $\operatorname{He}_1=z$ and
$\operatorname{He}_{j+1}=z\operatorname{He}_j-j\operatorname{He}_{j-1}$.
Define
\begin{equation}
 A_{r,\lambda}(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^r s(r,\ell)\lambda^{-\ell}
 (-1)^{\ell-1}\operatorname{He}_{\ell-1}(z),\qquad
 D_r(\lambda)=\sup_z|A_{r,\lambda}(z)\phi(z)|.
 \label{eq:joint-norm}
\end{equation}
Every polynomial times the Gaussian density is bounded and tends to zero
at both infinities.  In particular $D_r$ is finite and strictly positive.
The separate Hermite norms
$\mathcal H_j=\sup_z|\operatorname{He}_j(z)\phi(z)|$ give the valid
triangle bound
$D_r\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^r|s(r,\ell)|\lambda^{-\ell}\mathcal H_{\ell-1}$.
Computing the combined norm preserves cancellation between derivative
terms which this triangle inequality loses.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp positive-threshold bound]
\label{thm:norm}
For $r\geq1$, $\lambda>0$ and the finite positive measure in
\eqref{eq:measure-kernel},
\begin{equation}
 g_m^{(r)}(t)=t^{-r}\int
 A_{r,\lambda}((m+\log t-x)/\lambda)
 \phi((m+\log t-x)/\lambda)\nu(dx),\qquad
 |g_m^{(r)}(t)|\leq HD_r(\lambda)t^{-r}.
 \label{eq:norm-derivative}
\end{equation}
The constant $HD_r$ is the smallest uniform constant over measures of mass
$H$, real $m$ and positive $t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The identity $\phi^{(j)}=(-1)^j\operatorname{He}_j\phi$ follows by
induction from $\phi'=-z\phi$ and the recurrence.  Its boundedness and
finite measure mass justify dominated differentiation under $\nu$.
If $D=\partial_m$, differentiating $t^{-r}Q(D)P(m+\log t)$ gives
$t^{-r-1}(D-r)Q(D)P(m+\log t)$.  Induction therefore yields
$g_m^{(r)}=t^{-r}\sum_\ell s(r,\ell)P_\nu^{(\ell)}$ and then the
integral in \eqref{eq:norm-derivative}.  Positivity of $\nu$ gives the
bound.  Since the leading term of $A_{r,\lambda}$ is
$(-1)^{r-1}\lambda^{-r}z^{r-1}$, the polynomial is nonzero.
Continuity and Gaussian decay give a maximiser $z_*$.  Take
$t=1$, $m=x_*+\lambda z_*$ and $\nu=H\delta_{x_*}$.
The absolute derivative is exactly $HD_r$, proving uniform sharpness.
\end{proof}

This sharpness concerns the stated measure class; a particular capped-call
measure or basket remainder need not attain the norm.  The proof also
identifies exactly which object must be computed.  For rational $\lambda$,
$Q_{r,\lambda}=A_{r,\lambda}'-zA_{r,\lambda}$ is a rational polynomial of
degree $r$.  Every positive absolute maximum of $A_{r,\lambda}\phi$ lies
at a real root of $Q_{r,\lambda}$.  Exact square-free reduction, a rational
root-counting sequence and finite rational isolation find every distinct
root; Gaussian evaluations over the isolating intervals give an outward
upper certificate $\overline D_r\geq D_r$.
Appendix~\ref{app:roots} supplies the termination and enclosure argument.
All finite calculations below use $\overline D_r$, rather than an
uncertified maximisation on a numerical mesh.  Their arithmetic is
implemented with Arb midpoint-radius enclosures \citep{johansson2017}.

For comparison with the earlier separated bounds, Gaussian Fourier
inversion gives
$\mathcal B_j=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_\R |u|^je^{-u^2/2}du$ and
$\mathcal H_j\leq\mathcal B_j$.
Direct integration gives $\mathcal B_0=(2\pi)^{-1/2}$,
$\mathcal B_1=\pi^{-1}$ and
integration by parts gives $\mathcal B_j=(j-1)\mathcal B_{j-2}$.
Thus the individual-norm triangle and Fourier triangle are distinct
competent comparators.  Retaining both separates a gain from cancellation
from a gain obtained by tightening each derivative separately.

\section{Exact Gaussian coefficients and finite classical screening}
\label{sec:coefficients}

Put $\sigma^2=\lambda^2+\|R_G\|^2$.
For a multi-index $\gamma\in\mathbb N_0^n$, define
$|\gamma|=\sum_i\gamma_i$, $\gamma!=\prod_i\gamma_i!$,
$w^\gamma=\prod_iw_i^{\gamma_i}$,
$d_\gamma=\sum_i\gamma_id_i$ and
$v_\gamma=\sum_i\gamma_is_i$.
The multinomial theorem and completion of the Gaussian square imply
\begin{align}
 T(y)^j&=\sum_{|\gamma|=j}\frac{j!}{\gamma!}w^\gamma
                   e^{d_\gamma+v_\gamma\cdot y},\label{eq:T-multinomial}\\
 \E[e^{v\cdot Y}P_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(\mu_G+R_G\cdot Y)]
 &=e^{\|v\|^2/2}P_{\sigma,H}^{(\ell)}(\mu_G+R_G\cdot v).
 \label{eq:gaussian-tilt}
\end{align}
For the second identity, use
$e^{v\cdot y-\|y\|^2/2}=e^{\|v\|^2/2}e^{-\|y-v\|^2/2}$ and shift the
integration variable.  Independent Gaussian convolution changes the common
scale to $\sigma$.  Bounded differentiated kernels justify passing each
finite derivative through the expectation.  This remains valid at $R_G=0$.
The shift $R_G\cdot v$ retains the covariance between the geometric level
and the ratio.  Removing it generally changes the coefficient.

Let
\begin{align}
 J_{j,\ell}&=\sum_{|\gamma|=j}\frac{j!}{\gamma!}w^\gamma
 e^{d_\gamma+\|v_\gamma\|^2/2}
 P_{\sigma,H}^{(\ell)}(\mu_G+R_G\cdot v_\gamma),\label{eq:J}\\
 M_r&=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj
 \sum_{|\gamma|=j}\frac{j!}{\gamma!}w^\gamma
 e^{d_\gamma+\|v_\gamma\|^2/2}.
 \label{eq:continuous-moments}
\end{align}
Then $M_r=\E U^r$ and all terms are finite.
Taylor's theorem about $t=1$ defines
\begin{equation}
 c_0=P_{\sigma,H}(\mu_G),\qquad
 c_a=\frac1{a!}\sum_{\ell=1}^as(a,\ell)
        \sum_{j=0}^a(-1)^{a-j}\binom ajJ_{j,\ell},\quad a\geq1.
 \label{eq:coefficients}
\end{equation}
The inner alternating sum is the exact expectation of
$U^aP_{\lambda,H}^{(\ell)}(m(Y))$.
Its cancellation is kept inside outward arithmetic, not estimated from
printed decimal precision.

\begin{theorem}[Finite Gaussian price certificate]
\label{thm:finite-price}
Under \eqref{eq:stocks}--\eqref{eq:ratio}, for every finite integer $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|V-\sum_{a=0}^kc_a\right|
 \leq\frac{H\overline D_{k+1}(\lambda)M_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}.
 \label{eq:finite-certificate}
\end{equation}
Each coefficient and moment is evaluated by the finite sums above.
For rational inputs and positive common scale, validated enclosures of all
endpoints exist at any prescribed positive arithmetic accuracy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The integral Taylor remainder is
$k!^{-1}\int_1^T(T-t)^kg_m^{(k+1)}(t)dt$.
Since $T\geq1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:norm} bounds its absolute value by
$H\overline D_{k+1}U^{k+1}/(k+1)!$.
Every Gaussian exponential has all finite moments; finite sums preserve
this property, so the remainder is integrable.  Taking expectations gives
\eqref{eq:finite-certificate}.  The signed-Stirling derivative formula,
\eqref{eq:T-multinomial} and \eqref{eq:gaussian-tilt} evaluate all its
coefficients.  The finite collection of exponential and scalar normal
functions is computable at a positive scale, and outward arithmetic
preserves their enclosures.
\end{proof}

If $[L_k,U_k]$ encloses the coefficient sum and $\overline R_k$ bounds the
right side, I use
$[L_k-\overline R_k,U_k+\overline R_k]\cap[0,H]$.
Intersections across finitely many retained degrees can only improve this
interval.  I stop when its midpoint radius meets the monetary target,
and otherwise use a separately certified method or declare failure.
The argument is finite-order: finite lognormal moments do not establish
convergence of the infinite Taylor expansion.  The earlier Fourier
triangle remainder eventually grows for any nonzero centred loading,
so taking ever more terms is not a valid convergence policy.

The kernel evaluations are explicit.  With $L_H=\log(1+H/K)$,
$x_0=m/\sigma$, $x_1=(m-L_H)/\sigma$ and $z_i=x_i+\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
 P_{\sigma,H}(m)=K\{e^{m+\sigma^2/2}[\Phi(z_0)-\Phi(z_1)]
                   -\Phi(x_0)+(1+H/K)\Phi(x_1)\}.
 \label{eq:closed-kernel}
\end{equation}
The density identities
$e^{m+\sigma^2/2}\phi(z_0)=\phi(x_0)$ and
$e^{m+\sigma^2/2}\phi(z_1)=(1+H/K)\phi(x_1)$ cancel the boundary terms
in differentiation.  They give
$P'_{\sigma,H}=Ke^{m+\sigma^2/2}[\Phi(z_0)-\Phi(z_1)]$;
higher derivatives follow by the product rule and Hermite recurrence.
The retained audit compares these evaluations with differentiated series
and the strike-integral representation.  A formal closed formula is not
used as its own verification.

The certificate extends to unequal positive spots by replacing $d_i$ with
$\log(S_{i,0}/G_0)+\mu_i-\mu_G$, where
$G_0=\prod_iS_{i,0}^{w_i}$, and shifting every scalar-kernel argument by
$\log(G_0/K)$.  Weighted centring and the covariance shift survive.
Positive asset-monitor observations can likewise be treated as basket
components when their joint Gaussian log prices admit an equal independent
common loading $\lambda>0$.  The component count then includes monitoring
times; the multinomial cost can grow substantially.  These are analytical
extensions, not additional empirical tests.

\section{Integral and direct coherent correction evaluators}
\label{sec:oracles}

I write $m(y)=m_G(y)$ and use $m=m(y)$ and $U=U(y)$ in pointwise formulas.
For $r\geq1$, define the pointwise remainder after order $r-1$ by
\[
 R_r(y)=g_{m_G(y)}(T(y))-
          \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}g_{m_G(y)}^{(j)}(1)U(y)^j/j!.
\]
Taylor's integral formula and $t=1+zU$ give
\begin{equation}
 R_r(y)=\frac{U(y)^r}{r!}\E_{Z_r}[g_{m(y)}^{(r)}(1+Z_rU(y))],
 \qquad Z_r\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,r).
 \label{eq:beta-identity}
\end{equation}
At $U=0$ this is the continuous zero extension.  For the capped-call
threshold measure, an independent uniform $S$ represents its strike
integral by $x(S)=\log(1+HS/K)$.  The resulting correction is
\begin{equation}
 \E R_r=\frac H{r!}\E_{Y,Z_r,S}
 [U^r t^{-r}A_{r,\lambda}(\xi)\phi(\xi)],\qquad
 t=1+Z_rU,\quad \xi=(m+\log t-x(S))/\lambda.
 \label{eq:integral-observable}
\end{equation}
This formula represents the small correction through a density polynomial,
without evaluating and subtracting its raw conditional-price terms.

I implement $r=2$, so
$A_{2,\lambda}(\xi)=-\lambda^{-1}-\xi\lambda^{-2}$ and $Z_2$ is the
minimum of two independent uniforms.  Let $U_*>0$ dominate $U$ on all
realised financial nodes and set
\begin{equation}
 B_2=H\overline D_2U_*^2/2,\quad
 a=(U/U_*)^2t^{-2},\quad
 h(\xi)=\{1+A_{2,\lambda}(\xi)\phi(\xi)/\overline D_2\}/2.
 \label{eq:factorised-kernel}
\end{equation}
Since $t\geq1$, $0\leq a,h\leq1$.
The exact integral evaluator probability is
\begin{equation}
 p^{\mathrm{int}}=(1-a)/2+ah(\xi),\qquad
 X^{\mathrm{int}}=2p^{\mathrm{int}}-1,
 \qquad |X^{\mathrm{int}}|\leq U^2/U_*^2.
 \label{eq:integral-probability}
\end{equation}
The monetary correction is $B_2\E X^{\mathrm{int}}$ before a financial
truncation factor.  If $U_*=0$, the correction is zero and its estimator is
omitted.  The ratio is never evaluated as an indeterminate $0/0$.

The direct second-correction competitor uses the same control
$c_0+c_1$, finite financial law and $B_2$:
\begin{equation}
 p^{\mathrm{dir}}=\tfrac12+
 \frac{P_{\lambda,H}(m+\log T)-P_{\lambda,H}(m)-UP'_{\lambda,H}(m)}{2B_2}.
 \label{eq:direct-probability}
\end{equation}
The pointwise Taylor bound gives $p^{\mathrm{dir}}\in[0,1]$ and the same
score bound as \eqref{eq:integral-probability}.
The positive first-correction competitor uses the control $c_0$ and
\begin{equation}
 B_1=\min\{H,H\overline D_1U_*\},\qquad
 p^{(1)}=\frac{P_{\lambda,H}(m+\log T)-P_{\lambda,H}(m)}{B_1}.
 \label{eq:first-probability}
\end{equation}
It belongs to $[0,1]$ because $P$ is increasing and
$g_m'(t)\leq H\overline D_1$ for $t\geq1$.
All three constructions have explicit, method-specific controls,
normalisers and finite arithmetic certificates.

\begin{proposition}[Auxiliary variance penalty]
\label{prop:variance}
With continuous auxiliaries and the same retained financial law,
$\E[B_2X^{\mathrm{int}}\mid Y]=R_2(Y)$ and
\[
 \Var(B_2X^{\mathrm{int}})=\Var(R_2(Y))+
 \E\Var(B_2X^{\mathrm{int}}\mid Y)\geq\Var(R_2(Y)).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:integral-observable} gives the conditional mean.
All integrands are bounded on the retained support, so their second
moments are finite. The algebraic identity also extends to the full
Gaussian law as a numerical-integrand statement, because its ratio moments
are finite; the bounded probability interpretation uses the retained support.
Expanding the squared centred variable into its conditional centred part
and conditional mean makes the cross term zero.  This is the displayed
variance decomposition.
\end{proof}

The integral method must therefore earn any advantage through its complete
evaluator or precision requirements, rather than through an invented
variance reduction.  It also faces a stable direct implementation as an
important limitation of the present comparison.  A shared divided difference
or conditional Taylor kernel could avoid subtraction as effectively as the
integral representation.  The retained direct circuit is a fully charged
competitor, but is not a lower bound on every possible direct algorithm.

A local weak-dispersion argument explains the intended precision mechanism.
On a fixed retained support, scale the centred loading contrasts as
$s_i=\delta\bar s_i$ and the centred drift contrasts as
$d_i=\delta^2\bar d_i$, with exact weighted centring.  Taylor expansion of
the exponential, uniform on that support, cancels the term linear in
$\delta$.  Thus $U=O(\delta^2)$.
If its quadratic coefficient is nonzero somewhere on the fixed support,
its nonnegative maximum is positive, giving
$U_*=\Theta(\delta^2)$ and $B_2=\Theta(\delta^4)$.
An $h$-kernel error is then multiplied by $O(\delta^4)$; an absolute $U$
error enters through $\partial_U(U^2Q)=2UQ+U^2\partial_UQ$, with leading
order $O(\delta^2\eta_U)$ on its guarded domain.
The individual raw-price errors in \eqref{eq:direct-probability} do not
have that suppression before subtraction.  These orders concern the
specified constructions with fixed scale and support; they do not replace
the actual finite arithmetic propagation or establish superiority over a
stable cancellation-preserving opponent.

\section{Finite preparation and complete monetary error}
\label{sec:finite}

I use independent uniform financial labels and compute midpoint
truncated-normal coordinates by a reversible integer table.  Let $L>0$,
$N=2^{b_y}$, $\alpha_1=\Phi(L)-\Phi(-L)$ and
\begin{equation}
 y_j=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\Phi(-L)+\alpha_1\frac{j+1/2}{N}\right),
 \quad 0\leq j<N,\qquad \alpha=\alpha_1^p.
 \label{eq:quantile-grid}
\end{equation}
Each actual coordinate $\widehat y_j$ is the retained rounded dyadic
integer divided by $2^q$, with its specified offset removed.
Its outward quantile enclosure, monotonicity and membership of $[-L,L]$
are checked.  Duplicate coordinates keep their label multiplicities.
Hadamards prepare the independent label registers exactly; reversible
lookup computes the coordinates and its inverse clears the workspace.
This is a realised finite law, not a free Gaussian amplitude oracle.

The finite normaliser uses the actual retained support.  If $[\ell,h]$
covers every ideal node enclosure and actual dyadic coordinate, then
\begin{equation}
 U_*\geq\max_{v\in\{\ell,h\}^p}\sum_iw_ie^{d_i+s_i\cdot v}-1
 \label{eq:finite-vertex}
\end{equation}
is sufficient.  Fixing all other coordinates leaves a convex function of
one coordinate; its maximum on the interval is at an endpoint.
Applying this observation successively proves \eqref{eq:finite-vertex}.
Outward evaluation of every vertex, including 128 for $p=7$, certifies the
bound.  The larger continuous truncation box is still used by the separate
financial transfer.  Confusing these two roles would either invalidate the
normaliser or inflate it unnecessarily.

The alternative uniform-box preparation uses exact dyadic coordinate
midpoints on $[-L,L]^p$ and multiplies the score by
$\omega(y)=e^{-\|y\|^2/2}\in[0,1]$.
Its external factor is $C_L=(2L/\sqrt{2\pi})^p$ rather than $\alpha$.
This avoids an inverse-normal lookup, but its normalisation and density
arithmetic are charged, and the volume factor can be severe at larger
$p$.  I retain it as a transfer alternative instead of assuming that
removing one table makes it competitive.

The auxiliary law is also actual and finite.  Two independent $b_Z$-bit
uniform midpoint labels are compared coherently; their integer minimum is
returned cleanly.  With $h_Z=2^{-b_Z}$ and $z_j=(j+1/2)h_Z$, its probability
mass is exactly
$\{(N_Z-j)^2-(N_Z-j-1)^2\}/N_Z^2=2h_Z(1-z_j)$.
This equals the continuous $\operatorname{Beta}(1,2)$ mass of that cell.
The strike register is an independent uniform midpoint law with
$h_S=2^{-b_S}$.  Preserving the exact Beta cell masses supports a quadratic
auxiliary certificate stronger than a generic Lipschitz coupling.

Let $M_{r,N}=\E_N[\omega U^r]$ under the
actual financial label law, with $\omega=1$ for quantiles.  I use its outward
upper endpoint whenever it enters an error or probability bound.
Let $\overline E_2$ and $\overline F_2$ bound the first and second
argument derivatives of $A_{2,\lambda}\phi$.
The rigorous auxiliary bounds before the external factor are
\begin{align}
 \beta_Z&\leq Hh_Z^2
 \left(\overline D_3M_{3,N}/12+\overline D_4M_{4,N}/48\right),
 \label{eq:beta-error}\\
 \beta_S&\leq\frac{H^3M_{2,N}h_S^2}{48K^2}
   (\overline F_2/\lambda^2+\overline E_2/\lambda).
 \label{eq:strike-error}
\end{align}
The finite support implies $M_{4,N}\leq U_*M_{3,N}$ when a fourth
moment has not been retained.
Certified Hermite norms give
$\overline E_2=\overline{\mathcal H}_1/\lambda+
\overline{\mathcal H}_2/\lambda^2$ and
$\overline F_2=\overline{\mathcal H}_2/\lambda+
\overline{\mathcal H}_3/\lambda^2$ as valid choices of upper bounds.
Appendix~\ref{app:aux} derives the constants and the permitted transfer order.
Continuous Gaussian moments cannot silently replace these finite-law moments.

All elementary functions use one-dimensional endpoint tables with certified
interpolation errors.  The integer graph computes exponentials, the positive
ratio $U$, logarithms of $1+U$ or $1+ZU$, inverse-square factors and either
the density polynomial or the capped-price kernels.
The arithmetic certificate propagates absolute errors with the actual
operand ranges; it never assumes a favourable relative error for a nearly
cancelled price difference.  Every function argument and integer operation
is checked within its domain.  The direct second correction uses a signed
final subtraction followed immediately by an explicit two's-complement
projection onto $[0,1]$.  Signed words are not subsequently multiplied as
unsigned financial values.
Appendix~\ref{app:arithmetic} gives the scalar and product error contracts.

If the final represented threshold is $k\in\{0,\ldots,2^q\}$, a uniform
$q$-bit comparator, extended by one high zero bit, has success probability
exactly $k/2^q$.  Both endpoints zero and one are represented correctly.
Copying the numerical output and uncomputing the financial workspace yields
a clean value oracle; computing the comparator flag before uncomputation
yields a clean flag oracle.  The actual logical constructions and their
controlled uses are emitted and checked, rather than assigned a free
oracle cost.  Piecewise interpolation stores an unsigned slope magnitude
and a decreasing bit, so no modular negative slope is multiplied.

\begin{theorem}[Complete price-error allocation]
\label{thm:error}
Let $\beta_Y$ bound the exact continuous correction against the exact
mass-weighted finite financial correction.  Let $e_c$ bound the midpoint
error of its continuous coefficient sum, and let $[F_-,F_+]$ enclose the
external factor, whose midpoint is used, with radius $e_F$.
Suppose the centred value oracle has probability error at most $\eta_p$,
its output lies in $[0,1]$, and the signed correction estimator contributes
monetary error $e_{\rm stat}$ before the factor at its stated confidence.
For either second correction,
\begin{equation}
 |\widehat V-V|\leq
 e_c+\beta_Y+F_+(\beta_{\rm aux}+2B_2\eta_p+e_{\rm stat})+e_FB_2,
 \label{eq:total-error}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\rm aux}=\beta_Z+\beta_S$ for the integral method and zero
for direct evaluation.  For the first correction replace the coefficient
sum by $c_0$, $2B_2\eta_p$ by $B_1\eta_p$ and $B_2$ by $B_1$ in the
factor term.  Discounting multiplies the complete bound by $e^{-r_f\tau}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert successively the exact coefficient, exact finite financial correction,
finite auxiliary average and implemented average.  Their differences have
the bounds in the hypothesis and \eqref{eq:beta-error}--\eqref{eq:strike-error}.
The signed score changes by at most $2\eta_p$, giving $2B_2\eta_p$;
the positive first-correction score changes by at most $\eta_p$.
The external-factor approximation is charged against the implemented
bounded monetary score, whose absolute value is at most its normaliser.
The triangle inequality gives \eqref{eq:total-error}; statistical confidence
is inherited from the separately justified estimator event.
\end{proof}

I use the same requested monetary accuracy, confidence and maximum
deterministic allowance across matched methods.  A graph can pass every
integer-domain check and still fail this financial allowance.
The root-to-price error budget is the criterion for admission to the
resource comparison, rather than the number of fractional bits in isolation.
Figure~\ref{fig:error-flow} identifies where each allowance enters; its
purpose is to prevent a correct finite circuit from being mistaken for a
complete continuous pricing certificate.

\section{Signed flags and competent finite moment priors}
\label{sec:priors}

The centred encoding can have probability near one half even when its
monetary correction is small.  I therefore also implement two exact signed
parts of the represented probability:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p_+=\max(0,2\widehat p-1),\qquad
 \widehat p_-=\max(0,1-2\widehat p),\qquad
 \widehat p_+-\widehat p_-=2\widehat p-1.
 \label{eq:signed-parts}
\end{equation}
The transformations use exact dyadic scaling and positive subtraction.
They preserve the difference on every represented basis input, including
$\widehat p\in\{0,1/2,1\}$.
They are separate success flags with separately charged preparation and
confidence costs; I do not estimate a fictitious flag with the variance of
a signed classical output.  Their deterministic recombination uses the
centred base certificate in Theorem~\ref{thm:error}, avoiding a double
charge for an exact integer identity.

For either ideal second-correction construction,
$p_\pm\leq|X|\leq\omega U^2/U_*^2$.
The positive-part map is one-Lipschitz, so the implemented flags satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \E_N\widehat p_\pm\leq
 u_2:=\min\{1,M_{2,N}/U_*^2+2\eta_p\}.
 \label{eq:rare-prior}
\end{equation}
The expectation is under the prepared label law.  Neither its Gaussian
box mass $\alpha$ nor its uniform-box monetary factor $C_L$ belongs in
this probability prior.  They enter monetary reconstruction after
estimation.  A prior reduction improves a canonical estimator's amplitude
error bound only if the increased flag-preparation costs and two confidence
allocations are also included.

The finite moments are exactly computable without a financial price.
Define the one-dimensional exponential sum
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_N(v)=N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{v\widehat y_j},
 \label{eq:finite-mgf}
\end{equation}
and, for uniform-box weighting, replace each summand by
$e^{vy_j-y_j^2/2}$.  Independence and the multinomial expansion give
\begin{equation}
 \E_N[\omega T^r]=
 \sum_{|\gamma|=r}\frac{r!}{\gamma!}w^\gamma e^{d_\gamma}
 \prod_{k=1}^p\mathcal L_N\!\left(\sum_i\gamma_is_{ik}\right),\qquad
 M_{r,N}=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj\E_N[\omega T^j].
 \label{eq:finite-moment}
\end{equation}
These are finite sums over actual dyadic nodes.
For the weighted law the zeroth raw moment is $\E_N\omega$, not one.
All cancellations remain enclosed outwards.
Thus a moment calculation can use products of one-dimensional sums instead
of $N^p$ financial price evaluations.  Its scalar preprocessing cost is
still retained; separability does not make it free.

The positive first-correction comparator also receives a competent prior.
The capped payoff is increasing and one-Lipschitz in the underlying price.
For fixed $Y=y$, $A=GT$ and the independent common factor therefore imply
\begin{equation}
 0\leq R_1(y)\leq Ke^{m(y)+\lambda^2/2}U(y).
 \label{eq:underlying-gap}
\end{equation}
The corresponding finite expectation retains the level-ratio correlation:
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_N=Ke^{\mu_G+\lambda^2/2}
 \left\{\sum_iw_ie^{d_i}\prod_{k=1}^p\mathcal L_N(R_{G,k}+s_{ik})
       -\prod_{k=1}^p\mathcal L_N(R_{G,k})\right\}.
 \label{eq:correlated-gap}
\end{equation}
Expanding $e^{R_G\cdot Y}(T-1)$ proves this identity.
Combining it with $R_1\leq H$ and $R_1\leq H\overline D_1U$ gives
\begin{equation}
 u_1=\min\left\{1,
 \frac{\min\{H\E_N\omega,H\overline D_1M_{1,N},\overline\Gamma_N\}}{B_1}
 +\eta_{p,1}\right\},
 \label{eq:first-prior}
\end{equation}
where $\overline\Gamma_N$ is an outward upper endpoint.
Replacing $\E(e^{R_G\cdot Y}U)$ by a product of separate expectations
would generally invalidate this bound.  The prior is derived from moments
and the Lipschitz property, not a secretly supplied option price.

\section{Estimation and complete logical resource accounting}
\label{sec:estimation}

Let $\mathcal A$ be a clean preparation with success probability $p$,
including every financial and comparator register.  The Grover iterate is
formed from $\mathcal A$, its inverse and the good-state and zero-state
reflections.  Canonical phase estimation uses controlled powers of this
iterate \citep{brassard}; I retain the controlled global signs of the
reflections, since a global sign becomes a relative phase under control.
For $M=2^m$, the standard amplitude estimate obeys, on its success event,
\begin{equation}
 |\widetilde p-p|\leq
 \frac{2\pi\sqrt{p(1-p)}}M+\frac{\pi^2}{M^2}.
 \label{eq:canonical-error}
\end{equation}
This follows from the two Grover eigenphases and the nearest phase-grid
outcomes: writing $p=\sin^2\theta$, the relevant phase error is at most
$\pi/M$, and Taylor expansion of $\sin^2$ bounds the first-order term by
$2\sqrt{p(1-p)}\pi/M$ and the second-order term by $\pi^2/M^2$.
The probability of the nearest-grid event is bounded below by the retained
canonical phase-estimation theorem.  The exact finite confidence calculation
and phase-tape verification remain in the supporting derivations.

If $p\leq u$, the variance term is bounded by $u(1-u)$ for $u\leq1/2$
and by $1/4$ otherwise.  I apply \eqref{eq:rare-prior} or
\eqref{eq:first-prior}, rather than assuming an arbitrary favourable
amplitude.  Exact finite postprocessing error and the compiled inverse
Fourier transform's implementation error are charged separately.
Fresh median repetitions amplify confidence by the exact binomial tail
for their certified single-shot success probability.  The allocation is
fixed before observing results.  Two signed-part estimates share a union
budget; all 27 prespecified prices share an overall failure allowance of
one per cent.  The retained protocol assigns $1/2700$ per selected price.
Deterministic selection among certified implementations requires no
statistical selection penalty because no measurement outcomes are used to
choose their resource plans.

The inverse Fourier transform has explicit Clifford and T sequences,
based on certified single-qubit rotation approximations
\citep{rossselinger2016}.  Scalar phases are retained until they are
physically irrelevant to the final measurement.  For phase widths through
64, distant unsupported controlled phases are replaced by identity with a
charged operator-norm error; retained approximations and omitted phases
are summed by a telescoping product bound.
The complete largest-width bound is about $0.0022013$, below the stipulated
$1/64$ implementation limit.  The 64-bit tape has 167,223 T gates; the earlier 32-bit
profile is preserved at 68,823 T gates.  The extension charges omitted
distant phases rather than silently assigning them exact rotations.
This is an implementation-error statement, not an execution on a quantum
device.

A complete count includes the initial preparation, every controlled
financial preparation and inverse, good and zero reflections, inverse
Fourier transform, confidence repetitions, observed qubits and reset of
reused shot registers.  Each method receives both coherent lookup architectures and can select
its lowest complete cap before any estimator shots.  The study retains both exact seven-T and
coherent eight-T construction profiles and their emitted chronological
tapes.  QROM mechanisms have established predecessors \citep{babbush};
using them correctly is an implementation obligation, not a novelty claim.
An exact lookup compute/erase pair costs 14 T gates, while the coherent
AND pair costs eight; ordinary arithmetic uses literal seven-T Toffolis
in both modes. Financial table generation, norm generation, finite moment sums and
continuous-reference validation are reported as classical setup.
A logical T count is not directly equated to CPU time or fault-tolerant
wall-clock time.

For a centred second-correction flag with probability tolerance $e_p$,
the monetary statistical allowance is $2B_2e_p$ before the factor.
For two signed flags with tolerances $e_+,e_-$ it is
$B_2(e_++e_-)$, at their joint confidence.
The positive first correction uses $B_1e_p$.
I select only configurations satisfying the complete monetary constraint,
with at most half the requested radius assigned to deterministic error.
The final study fixes $q=28$, $b_y=10$ and the two auxiliary widths
at eight, then tunes each scalar table independently within six to twelve
address bits. The unchanged all-twelve program remains in every method
pool, alongside three greedy searches with deterministic allowances
$0.001$, $0.0025$ and $0.005$. The search ranks certified cell savings
per marginal monetary error, with a fixed node-name tie rule; the final
choice minimises the actual complete T cap. Every proposed map, guard
result, chosen step and cost is retained. This is a reproducible finite
search, rather than a global precision optimum.  Equal financial accuracy, information, confidence,
architecture and admissibility are the matched requirements.

\section{Complete root isolation for the joint norm}
\label{app:roots}

This appendix gives the finite certificate behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}.  Write $A=A_{r,\lambda}$ and $Q=A'-zA$.
Since $(A\phi)'=Q\phi$, every nonzero absolute maximum is at a real root
of $Q$.  Zeros of $A$ and limits at infinity contribute zero.
For rational $\lambda$, all coefficients are rational and $\deg Q=r$.
Replace $Q$ by its monic square-free part $q=Q/\gcd(Q,Q')$; this keeps
every distinct root.

Start a remainder sequence with $q_0=q$, $q_1=q'$ and define
$q_{j+1}$ as minus the polynomial remainder of $q_{j-1}$ by $q_j$.
Positive rescalings do not change signs.  The sequence ends in a nonzero
constant.  Consecutive terms cannot vanish together, since the recurrence
would propagate that zero to the last constant.  At an internal zero the
neighbouring terms have opposite signs, so their adjacent comparisons
contribute one sign variation on both sides.  At a root of $q_0$, its
nonzero derivative makes the variation count decrease by one.
Thus, omitting zero values, $V(a)-V(b)$ counts roots in $(a,b)$ when
$a,b$ are nonroots.

Every root lies within
$1+\max_{j<r}|a_j/a_r|$ for the coefficients of $q$.
Outside that radius the absolute sum of its lower terms is strictly less
than the leading term, so cancellation is impossible.
Choose a larger integer radius and subdivide rationally, preserving the
exact root-count sum.  If a proposed midpoint is a root, choose a nonroot
among the $r+1$ interior fractions $j/(r+2)$ of the interval; a degree-$r$
polynomial cannot vanish at all of them.  Every such split contracts each
child by at most $(r+1)/(r+2)$.  Distinct roots have positive separation,
so repeated contraction first isolates each and then attains the required
interval width.  This proves finite termination, including rational roots.

Evaluate $|A\phi|$ with an outward Arb ball covering both exact endpoints
of each isolating interval.  The largest upper endpoint is a global upper
certificate, because all maximising roots are covered.  Evaluation at
midpoints gives lower bounds on the global supremum even when those points
are not roots.  The independent implementation reconstructs the rational
polynomials, verifies total root counts and interval endpoint signs, and
uses higher-precision Gaussian evaluations to check the retained envelopes.
No claim that interval midpoint evaluation is the exact maximum is needed.

\section{Signed interpolation and arithmetic contracts}
\label{app:arithmetic}

Let $f\in C^2([a,a+s])$ and grid spacing $h=s/2^b$, with a power-of-two
span $s$ and $b\leq q+\log_2s$.  At every endpoint $x_j$, retain an
unsigned dyadic value $n_j2^{-q}$ and an outward error $e_j$.
On a cell $[u,v]$, the exact linear interpolation error equals
$f''(\zeta)(x-u)(x-v)/2$ for some interior $\zeta$.
Subtract the linear interpolant and a multiple of $(t-u)(t-v)$ chosen to
give a third zero at $x$, then apply Rolle's theorem twice to prove this
identity.  Since $(x-u)(v-x)\leq h^2/4$, endpoint rounding and one integer
truncation give
\begin{equation}
 e_f\leq\max_je_j+\|f''\|_\infty h^2/8+2^{-q}.
 \label{eq:appendix-interpolation}
\end{equation}
An argument error $\eta_x$ adds $\|f'\|_\infty\eta_x$ if both arguments
remain in the declared interval.  Projection onto that interval does not
enlarge the distance from an admissible exact argument.

The actual table stores the base integer, the absolute difference to the
next endpoint, and its decreasing bit.  It multiplies the unsigned magnitude
by the within-cell remainder and chooses unsigned addition or subtraction.
The adjustment cannot underflow a decreasing base: it is at most the
endpoint difference, and both endpoints are nonnegative.
The final endpoint has its own address, so saturation at the right endpoint
does not wrap to the left.  The packed magnitudes, intermediate additions
and input slices are checked against their actual widths.
Outward endpoints are coarsened downwards and upwards to
$2^{-384}\mathbb Z$ before serialization; the enlarged intervals determine
all nodal errors.  Extreme Gaussian tails are not treated as exact zeros.

For $x\geq a>0$, usable first and second derivative bounds are
$a^{-1},a^{-2}$ for $\log x$, and $2a^{-3},6a^{-4}$ for $x^{-2}$.
For $e^x$ both are $e^{a+s}$.
For $h$ in \eqref{eq:factorised-kernel}, the bounds are
$(\overline{\mathcal H}_1/\lambda+
\overline{\mathcal H}_2/\lambda^2)/(2\overline D_2)$ and
$(\overline{\mathcal H}_2/\lambda+
\overline{\mathcal H}_3/\lambda^2)/(2\overline D_2)$.
They follow by differentiating
$A_2\phi=-\phi/\lambda+\phi'/\lambda^2$.
Differentiating the finite strike integral gives
$\|P^{(j)}\|_\infty\leq H\lambda^{-j}\overline{\mathcal H}_{j-1}$.
Moreover $P'\geq0$ and $P'\leq K+H$:
the pathwise log-scale derivative is the underlying price when it lies
between $K$ and $K+H$, and is zero otherwise.  The pathwise capped payoff
is $(K+H)$-Lipschitz in log-scale, justifying dominated differentiation.
These whole-domain inequalities give the required $P$ and $P'$ tables.

For products, either identity
$\widehat x\widehat y-xy=x(\widehat y-y)+\widehat y(\widehat x-x)
=y(\widehat x-x)+\widehat x(\widehat y-y)$ yields a valid absolute error.
Taking the smaller range-based upper bound and adding one truncation unit
preserves small-factor suppression.  Addition and subtraction add operand
errors.  Scaling charges its rounded coefficient and any dropped bits.
Each node independently propagates implemented ranges; intersecting them
with an ideal range expanded by its certified error retains correlations
without removing rounding.  Empty intersections or domain violations reject
the graph.  Signed final differences must fit strictly within the
unambiguous two's-complement range before projection.  Positive-subtraction
temporaries are predicate-masked before any downstream multiplication.
These checks are essential hypotheses of the monetary certificate.

\section{Quadratic auxiliary discretisation}
\label{app:aux}

For $N_Z$ cells of width $h_Z$, the minimum-register law has the exact
$\operatorname{Beta}(1,2)$ cell masses stated in Section~\ref{sec:finite}.
For $f\in C^2[0,1]$, expand in a cell centred at $z_j$ with
$u=z-z_j$.  Its density is $2(1-z_j)-2u$.
The first centred cell moment is $-h_Z^3/6$, and the second is
$2(1-z_j)h_Z^3/12$.
Taylor's formula therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 |\E f(Z)-\E f(Z_N)|\leq
 h_Z^2\{\|f'\|_\infty/6+\|f''\|_\infty/24\}.
 \label{eq:beta-midpoint-proof}
\end{equation}
For the linear terms sum $N_Zh_Z^3=h_Z^2$; for the quadratic remainder
use $\sum_j2(1-z_j)h_Z=1$ and the nonnegative cell density.
Set $f_y(z)=g_{m(y)}''(1+zU)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:norm} gives
$|f_y'|\leq H\overline D_3U$ and
$|f_y''|\leq H\overline D_4U^2$.
Multiplication by $U^2/2$ and finite financial expectation prove
\eqref{eq:beta-error}.

For a uniform strike midpoint rule, each cell's linear Taylor term
integrates to zero and its quadratic remainder integrates to at most
$\|f''\|_\infty h_S^3/24$.
At fixed $y,z$, let $\xi(s)=(m+\log t-\log(1+Hs/K))/\lambda$.
Then $|\xi'|\leq H/(K\lambda)$ and
$|\xi''|\leq H^2/(K^2\lambda)$.
The second derivative of $t^{-2}A_2(\xi(s))\phi(\xi(s))$ is at most
$(H^2/K^2)(\overline F_2/\lambda^2+\overline E_2/\lambda)$.
Multiplying by $HU^2/2$ proves \eqref{eq:strike-error}.
Replace Beta while the strike remains continuous, then replace strike
under the already finite Beta law; its uniform-in-$z$ bound permits this
order.  Both steps use the same actual financial law and therefore the
same moments.  A relative Gaussian weight is carried inside their finite
expectation, not inserted afterwards as a different moment law.

\section{Financial tails and continuous-to-finite transfer}
\label{app:financial-transfer}

Let $E_L=\{Y\notin[-L,L]^p\}$.
The positive first remainder has tail at most $H\mathbb P(E_L)$.
Since $R_2=R_1-UP'(m)$ and
$P'_*=\min\{K+H,H\overline{\mathcal H}_0/\lambda\}$,
$|R_2|\leq H+P'_*T$ is a valid global tail envelope.
Gaussian tilting evaluates its exponential tail terms:
\begin{equation}
 \E[e^{v\cdot Y}\ind_{E_L}]=e^{\|v\|^2/2}Q_v(L),\qquad
 Q_v(L)\leq\sum_{j=1}^p\{\Phi(-L-v_j)+\Phi(v_j-L)\}.
 \label{eq:tilted-tail}
\end{equation}
The equality follows by shifting the Gaussian square; the inequality is
the union bound for the coordinates of $Y+v$.
Combining it with the finite exponential sum for $T$ gives an explicit
outward continuous-tail bound without private data.

For financial grids on the retained box, a coordinate Lipschitz bound
$L_j$ on a correction gives ideal conditional-normal midpoint error
$L\sum_jL_j/N$.  Split each probability cell at its midpoint and use
monotonicity of its quantile function.  The two half-cell differences are
bounded by $1/(2N)$ times their endpoint increments; summing telescopes
to $(L-(-L))/(2N)=L/N$.  Successive coordinate coupling proves the product
bound.  Actual quantile-node rounding adds
$\sum_jL_j\max_k|\widehat y_k-y_k|$ separately.
The derivative bounds follow by differentiating the integral remainder,
using the certified $\overline D_r$ and its argument derivatives on the
full box.  Their complete expressions are retained in the supplementary
certificate derivation.  Large sufficient widths are recorded as failures.

Uniform-box quadrature has a stronger smooth grid rate but a larger
monetary factor.  For $f=\omega R$, the product rule gives
$\|\partial_{jj}f\|_\infty\leq
\|\partial_{jj}R\|_\infty+2L\|\partial_jR\|_\infty+(L^2+1)\|R\|_\infty$.
Integrating the second-order Taylor remainder on each cell gives
one-coordinate expectation error $(2L)^2\|f''\|_\infty/(24N^2)$.
A telescoping tensor product uses the sup-norm contraction of both positive
averaging operators, and yields
$C_L(2L)^2\sum_j\|\partial_{jj}f\|_\infty/(24N^2)$.
This remains a grid-only bound until the continuous tail is added.

The retained one-dimensional transfer instead compares two validated
intervals.  If $[V_-,V_+]$ encloses the continuous price,
$[c_-,c_+]$ its coefficient sum and $[J_-,J_+]$ the exact mass-weighted
finite correction, then
\begin{equation}
 \beta_Y=\max\{|J_--(V_+-c_-)|,|J_+-(V_--c_+)|\}
 \label{eq:reference-transfer}
\end{equation}
bounds $|J-(V-c)|$.  Interval subtraction proves this directly.
It includes tail, financial grid and actual node rounding, but no auxiliary,
value or statistical error.  Its classical reference and finite evaluations
are charged and hashed.  A reference which already determines $V$ cannot
serve as free preprocessing evidence of practical quantum advantage.
