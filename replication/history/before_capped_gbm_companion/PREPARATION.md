# Quantitative preparation for graduate study

## 1 Purpose and evidential standard

I use this project to demonstrate mathematical reasoning, financial modelling and reproducible computation for quantitative finance graduate study. A checked project-specific argument is valuable, but a new quantum theory, publication or practical quantum advantage is unnecessary for this purpose. Oxford explicitly says publications are not expected while requiring probability, statistics, ODEs/PDEs, linear algebra and analysis, particularly real analysis ([2027/28 admissions](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/graduate/courses/msc-mathematical-and-computational-finance)).

The repository demonstrates substantial applied calculus, covariance/linear-algebra reasoning, conditional probability, measure-theoretic arguments, validated numerical pricing and Python. It does not establish the whole undergraduate prerequisite checklist. The principal preparation gaps are broad proof-based analysis/probability, differential-equation pricing, statistical estimation/calibration and continuous constrained optimisation. A quantum calculation does not compensate for those gaps.

I distinguish entry requirements, teaching prerequisites within the degree, taught core material and optional specialisms. I also distinguish retained artefacts from independently assessed personal proficiency. I should be able to reproduce the important derivations, explain their assumptions and modify the implementation, with accurate attribution of assistance. A repository does not replace degree subjects, grades, credits or evidence of unaided understanding.

## 3 Mathematical foundations demonstrated

The [main paper](generalisation_proofs.tex), [supporting index](supporting_proofs_index.md) and [independent evidence assessment](results/preparation_review/independent_project_evidence.md) provide the proof/code pointers. The classifications below concern substantive operations and arguments, not course exemptions.

| Foundation | Evidence in this project | Boundary and strengthening needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single/multivariable calculus | Repeated chain/product rules, Gaussian derivative recurrences, gradients/second derivatives, Taylor remainders, multiple integration and moments. | Explain remainder hypotheses and dominated differentiation independently. Principal values, gamma recursion and vector calculus are not systematically demonstrated; they need preparation exercises rather than artificial pricing chapters. |
| Linear/matrix algebra | Positive-definite covariance, positive-semidefinite residuals, quadratic forms, matrix square roots, rank-one decomposition and orthogonal factor changes. | Explain vector spaces, rank and spectral reasoning, including singular covariance, without relying on a factorisation routine. |
| Real analysis/topology | Uniform envelopes, continuous global extrema, decay at infinity, compact root brackets, exact root isolation and finite termination. | Completeness, epsilon–delta/uniform continuity, sequences/series of functions, metric topology, connectedness, sequential/topological compactness and contraction mappings are not broadly demonstrated. UZH names compactness explicitly. |
| Measure theory | Positive Borel/Stieltjes measures, atoms/one-sided CDF limits, product measures, Tonelli/Fubini, dominated differentiation and conditional Fatou. | General measure construction, Carathéodory extension, Lebesgue integration, Radon–Nikodym hypotheses and Lp theory need independent preparation. An explicit Gaussian density shift is a special case. |
| Measure-theoretic probability | Gaussian conditioning/tilting, independent factors, moments, finite confidence and an already proved total-variance decomposition. | Distributional breadth, characteristic-function inversion, convergence modes, LLN/CLT hypotheses, conditional expectation as an L2 projection and appropriate optional stopping are not comparably demonstrated. |
| Stochastic calculus/financial mathematics | Itô-based GBM/log-price solution, constant Brownian measure change, integrability, admissible trading/localisation and martingale pricing under a stipulated no-arbitrage law. | General stochastic-integral/SDE foundations, stopping-time problems and the replication/PDE route. The constant-coefficient proof is not a general Girsanov or FTAP theorem. |
| Statistics/computational inference | Non-asymptotic confidence/concentration, finite moments and correct independence between RQMC replicate shifts. | Likelihood calibration, parameter uncertainty, sufficiency/efficiency, hypothesis testing, regression/time-series, diagnostics and Bayesian/MCMC inference are largely absent. Numerical confidence does not validate an estimated financial model. |
| Differential equations/numerics | A specified SDE with an explicit solution; validated integration and root isolation. | ODE/PDE breadth, pricing PDE derivation, terminal/boundary conditions, an implemented stable scheme and a convergence comparison. Quadrature is a different numerical task. |
| Optimisation | Convexity/Jensen/Cauchy arguments, scalar extrema and finite precision/resource selection. | Continuous constrained optimisation, KKT/duality, quadratic programming and a financial decision problem. Finite width enumeration is not portfolio optimisation or optimal control. |
| Programming | Substantial Python/NumPy/Flint work, exact fixed-point arithmetic, independent representations and overflow/boundary regressions. | A short reproducible financial example and independent explanations. A small original C++ engine would support Oxford-specific preparation; credited Joe–Kuo C++ is external source. |

Representative implementations are [classical_engine.py](classical_engine.py), [continuous_quadrature.py](continuous_quadrature.py), [rqmc_pricing.py](rqmc_pricing.py) and [check_sharp_gaussian_moduli.py](check_sharp_gaussian_moduli.py). Existing regressions check arithmetic and mathematical premises. This review does not rerun experiments or establish personal authorship/mastery from their presence. Kernel differentiation is also not an implemented and validated hedging/Greeks study.

## 5 Priorities and completion

My first project addition should be one classical capped-GBM validation companion. I should derive self-financing replication and the Black–Scholes PDE under the same dividend/discount conventions, prove the martingale/call-spread price, specify terminal/far-boundary conditions, implement one finite-difference method and compare prices/Greeks with the analytic expression and Monte Carlo. Stability/consistency should concern the actual scheme; observed refinement alone is not a convergence proof. Grid, finite-domain, arithmetic and sampling errors should remain distinct. This closes a genuine preparation gap and strengthens the classical comparator for quantum pricing. It is planned, not completed work.

I should then derive conditional expectation as an L2 projection and connect it to the total-variance identity already proved in the paper, comparing raw, conditional and control-variate estimators under matched information. A subsequent synthetic estimation exercise should derive a multivariate-return likelihood, estimate volatility/covariance, quantify uncertainty and check admissibility. A physical-return mean does not identify the risk-neutral drift fixed by no arbitrage. Synthetic estimation demonstrates inference/implementation, not independent market validation. A constrained hedging example with a proved convex objective and KKT conditions is a coherent later addition. These companions need not enlarge the main quantum paper.

In parallel, I should strengthen broad analysis/probability through assessed coursework where available and independently solved exercises. An original small C++ pricing implementation is useful for Oxford-specific preparation. A short executable financial example should make assumptions, price/error and proof/code connections visible before specialised gate counts. No licensed observations, paid data or new entitlement are required for these priorities.

Oxford's [award announcement](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/node/34183) confirms the supplied Hawkes-process thesis's 2021 European prize; the thesis itself is dated Trinity 2020. The consulted Sections 2–4, conclusion 5.1 and Appendices A/C connect process theory to likelihood estimation, diagnostics, SDE simulation and an SPDE comparator, including adverse results. I use that connected argument as a benchmark, not a pre-admission novelty requirement. It is a completed master's dissertation; its data entitlement does not transfer to this project. Neither its PDF nor extracted text is redistributed here.

Completion for the present purpose means a coherent financial question, checked mathematics, readable reproducible pricing comparisons, documented limitations and defensible personal preparation evidence. Every taught elective need not be reproduced. Existing adverse quantum/classical comparisons remain valuable evidence of judgement. The scientific paper remains a working paper.
