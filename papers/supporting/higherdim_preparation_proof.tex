\section{Independent product-law preparation}
\label{sec:higherdim-preparation-proof}

I retain the frozen rational financial parameters and the exact rounded
quantile law. Let $N=2^{b_y}$, let $\alpha_L=\Phi(L)-\Phi(-L)$, and let
\[
 y_k^*=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\Phi(-L)+\alpha_L\frac{k+1/2}{N}\right),
 \qquad
 y_k=2^{-q}\operatorname{round}(2^q y_k^*),\quad 0\leq k<N.
\]
Every label has probability $1/N$, including labels with repeated rounded
coordinates. Rounding is nearest with half-integer ties directed upwards.
The numerical construction certifies that the enclosure of every positive
$y_k^*$ lies strictly within one rounding bin. Consequently no tie occurs,
and the identities $y_{N-1-k}^*=-y_k^*$ and $y_{N-1-k}=-y_k$ follow from the
symmetry and strict monotonicity of $\Phi$. No amplitude-loading approximation
is introduced by the moment calculation.

\begin{proposition}[Finite empirical exponential moments]
Let $\eta=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}a_k\delta_{y_k}$ be a finite signed measure,
$M=\max_k|y_k|$ and $A=\sum_k|a_k|$. For a nonnegative integer $J$, set
$p_j=\sum_k a_k y_k^j/j!$. Then for every real $v$,
\[
 \left|\int e^{vy}\,\eta(\mathrm dy)-\sum_{j=0}^{J}p_jv^j\right|
 \leq A e^{|v|M}\frac{(|v|M)^{J+1}}{(J+1)!}.
\]
If $a_k=1/N$, the measure is symmetric and $J$ is even, the odd coefficients
vanish exactly and the omitted even-power tail is nonnegative.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any real $x$, Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\[
 e^x-\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{x^j}{j!}
 =\frac{x^{J+1}}{J!}\int_0^1(1-t)^J e^{tx}\,\mathrm dt.
\]
The integrand is bounded in absolute value by $e^{|x|}(1-t)^J$;
integration gives $e^{|x|}|x|^{J+1}/(J+1)!$. Multiply this bound by
$|a_k|$, use $|vy_k|\leq |v|M$, and sum the finite collection of terms.
For the uniform symmetric measure, cancellation of each pair $y,-y$ proves
that all odd empirical powers vanish. Its MGF is the average of
$\cosh(vy_k)$, whose convergent even-power series has nonnegative terms.
This also proves the one-sided omitted-tail statement. Certified interval
evaluation encloses each coefficient, polynomial and remainder bound.
The construction does not replace the finite law by a Gaussian law.
\end{proof}

Let $Y_1,\ldots,Y_p$ be independent with this identical rounded-node law,
and write
\[
 T(Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_i e^{d_i+s_i\cdot Y},\qquad U(Y)=T(Y)-1.
\]
The frozen centring identities are $\sum_iw_i=1$,
$\sum_iw_id_i=0$ and $\sum_iw_is_i=0$, with all $w_i>0$.
Jensen's inequality therefore gives $T(Y)\geq1$ and $U(Y)\geq0$ pointwise.
Denote the scalar finite-law MGF by $M_N(v)=N^{-1}\sum_ke^{vy_k}$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite product-law factorisation]
For every nonnegative integer $r$,
\[
 \mathbb E_N T^r
 =\sum_{\substack{a\in\mathbb N_0^n\\|a|=r}}
 \frac{r!}{\prod_i a_i!}\prod_iw_i^{a_i}
 e^{\sum_i a_i d_i}
 \prod_{j=1}^{p}M_N\!\left(\sum_i a_i s_{ij}\right),
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_N U^r
 =\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj\mathbb E_N T^j.
\]
These equalities are finite algebraic identities. They hold independently
of the quality of the quantile discretisation as an approximation to the
continuous Gaussian expectation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the multinomial theorem pointwise to the finite sum defining $T$.
Each resulting monomial has coefficient
$r!\prod_iw_i^{a_i}/\prod_i a_i!$ and exponential
$\exp(\sum_i a_id_i+\sum_j\sum_i a_is_{ij}Y_j)$.
Independence factors its expectation into the stated scalar MGFs. Every sum
is finite, so interchange of expectations and sums needs no limiting
argument. The second identity is the binomial theorem for $(T-1)^r$.
Although the latter identity has alternating coefficients, certified interval
arithmetic retains its cancellation uncertainty. A negative upper endpoint
would reject the calculation. A negative lower endpoint may be replaced by
zero because $U^r\geq0$ was proved independently.
\end{proof}

The same information supplies a strong first-correction comparator. With
$m_G=\mu_G+r_G\cdot Y$ and independent common Gaussian factor of volatility
$\lambda$, the finite-law expected underlying-price difference is
\[
 \Gamma_N=K e^{\mu_G+\lambda^2/2}
 \left\{
 \sum_i w_i e^{d_i}\prod_{j=1}^{p}M_N(r_{Gj}+s_{ij})
 -\prod_{j=1}^{p}M_N(r_{Gj})\right\}.
\]
I derive this identity by first integrating the common factor, then
expanding $e^{m_G}U$ and applying independence to each term. For the capped
and ordinary call, monotonicity and the one-Lipschitz property of the payoff
give $0\leq\mathbb E_N R_1\leq\Gamma_N$. Thus the comparator receives
the full covariance-preserving information rather than a product of marginal
upper bounds. The ordinary call is unbounded, but all quantities above are
finite on this finite law. Continuous integrability and no-arbitrage remain
properties of the original Gaussian financial model, separately established
in the main paper.

To normalise the finite quantum oracle, I compute $T$ at every vertex of
the coordinate box containing the actual nodes. I enlarge each coordinate
extremum outwards by the certified maximum displacement between an actual
node and its ideal-quantile enclosure. The enlarged box therefore contains
both the actual dyadics and every ideal-quantile enclosure used by the
existing arithmetic propagation. For fixed other coordinates,
the function $T$ is convex in each remaining coordinate, as its second
derivative is a finite sum of nonnegative exponential terms. A convex
function on an interval has its maximum at an endpoint. Applying this
argument successively to all $p$ coordinates proves that the maximum of
$T$ on the box is bounded by its largest vertex value. Outward evaluation
of the $2^p$ vertices gives $U_*$. This is a finite-law normaliser and is
not a continuous-tail or financial-grid transfer certificate.

The number of multinomial terms at order $r$ is
$\binom{n+r-1}{r}$. Evaluation through order $R$ uses
$\binom{n+R}{R}$ such terms. Caching equal rational scalar rates avoids
repeated empirical MGF evaluations. The preparation cost includes generation
of the $N$ rounded nodes, empirical-power accumulation, polynomial evaluations,
geometric coefficient evaluations, and all $2^p$ vertex evaluations.
It avoids enumeration of the $N^p$ Cartesian labels without assuming that
state preparation or classical preprocessing is free. The cached geometry is
shared only when the exact tuples $(w,d,s,r_G,\mu_G+\lambda^2/2)$ agree.
The existing derivative-norm certificates and closed Gaussian coefficient
formulas are supplied identically to all classical and quantum competitors.
This derivation records the project-specific finite-law certificate and its
cost. I do not claim originality for multinomial factorisation, exponential
Taylor bounds, Gaussian tilting, or coordinatewise convexity.
