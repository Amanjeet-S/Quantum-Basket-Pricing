I checked the corrected source and its clean control-character scan. The theorem itself correctly uses $\operatorname{Var}(H_0)>0$. No unresolved mathematical blocker remains in the reviewed argument.

This review does not establish theoretical novelty or a quantum advantage. It checks the argument and the retained parameter certificate. The new moment promise concerns the classical fractional output $X$, rather than the current Bernoulli quantum flag.

## 6. Generic moment and weak-dispersion arguments

The joint-MGF theorem expands a finite positive weighted exponential sum under an arbitrary joint latent law. Finiteness at the explicitly required linear arguments makes every expanded term integrable. Weighted centring, $\log T\leq T-1$ and the kernel's global slope prove the displayed bound without independence. The theorem's implemented-output transfer follows from the same pointwise error argument. Its formula is an algebraic moment certificate, rather than a claim that every financial model has finite exponential moments.

For the broader weak-dispersion theorem, deterministic weighted-centred intercepts bounded by $C\varepsilon^2$ cancel the entire linear Taylor term. Their individual limits after division by $\varepsilon^2$ are unnecessary. The limiting term is half the weighted quadratic loading form. The displayed dominator is

$$D(V)=\tfrac12(B\|V\|+C\varepsilon_0)^2\exp(\varepsilon_0B\|V\|+C\varepsilon_0^2).$$

Its square is bounded by a finite constant times $(1+\|V\|^4)\exp(2\varepsilon_0B\|V\|)$, exactly the integrability assumption. Almost sure convergence of the finite base and continuity of the globally bounded derivative then give the residual's pointwise limit and an integrable square dominator. Dominated convergence proves the $L^2$ limit, and the stated inequalities transfer it to first and second moments. The $Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ mean and $\Theta(\varepsilon^4)$ fractional variance require the respective leading coefficients to be strictly positive.

The Gaussian risk-neutral specialisation correctly retains the drift perturbation $\varepsilon b_i\cdot(Y-R_0)-\varepsilon^2(\|b_i\|^2-D_b)/2$. With $R_0=0$, the orthogonal quadratic-form calculation gives the stated trace and squared-trace moments. A symmetric two-point example with a constant base instead has constant leading $H_0$, so its leading variance coefficient vanishes. This is a boundary of the positive-variance claim. The canonical flag identity follows exactly from conditional expectation and conditional variance. Its weaker dispersion order does not constitute a lower bound for coherent quantum amplitude estimation or a physical speed comparison.

The relative-MSE discussion and its Bernstein qualification are appropriately restricted. A bounded-range Bernstein linear term can dominate at a relative tolerance shrinking with dispersion. The theorem does not claim a dispersion-independent count from that bound, and an absolute financial tolerance eventually permits the residual to be omitted. Retaining those qualifications is essential to avoid turning a valid limit argument into a misleading computational advantage claim.
