# Financial support and moment certificates after quantum encoding

## 1 Research question and checked outcome

I now ask: when do financial residual support and moment certificates change the cheapest fully charged quantum estimation encoding at a fixed monetary error and confidence, and where do competent deterministic classical methods remove the apparent benefit? This question applies to a numerical financial expectation with separately certified modelling, distributional and arithmetic assumptions. It does not depend on labelling its underlying assets as REITs. Positive-asset and weak-dispersion arguments in generalisation_proofs.tex state wider mathematical scope; the present complete circuit comparison remains a stipulated Gaussian basket instantiation.

The strongest checked positive result is a smaller complete logical T upper budget from a competent single flag supplied with the actual finite-output support and moment bounds. Range normalisation improves 16 of 27 selected contract budgets. Clipping improves 17 of 27. Their combined family reduces 17, leaves 10 unchanged and increases none, relative to a unit-range flag that already receives the same strengthened whole-mean prior. The largest reduction is 82.1 per cent, approximately a 5.575-fold budget reduction. These are minima of proved upper caps over an explicit finite implementation set, rather than measured quantum runtimes or a dominance theorem.

The new dyadic numerical-value estimator loses all 27 comparisons with the strongest single flag. Its selected T cap is 21.00–122.03 times the single-flag cap. I retain its construction, proofs, costs and negative results. The result rejects this implementation with these global moment bounds as the preferred method on the frozen family; it does not reject every variance-aware quantum mean algorithm, every allocation or bucket-local certificate.

## 3 Mathematical and circuit construction

The value oracle has a phase-correct clean-work XOR action on arbitrary numerical targets and entangled logical inputs. Each dyadic bucket computes its membership and a finite uniform comparison, then erases its complete arithmetic history. The top atom a=2^q is represented separately. The proof derives the Grover rotation, full preparation/inverse costs and the measured-lookup instrument conditions. Tests and emitted-gate audits establish coherent action in small complete circuits; production counts reuse retained emitted financial blocks and freshly emitted predicates. No quantum hardware was executed.

I certify finite support by maximising the convex log arithmetic/geometric ratio over every vertex of the actual rounded-quantile box. Method-specific log slopes and positive arithmetic/geometric-gap bounds give alternative ideal residual ranges. I add the pointwise arithmetic allowance, convert the outward endpoint to an exact rational and floor only at the final integer support bound. This procedure improves the range below one in 157 of 273 financial prefixes, with minimum certified lattice range approximately 0.108105. It does not bound continuous Gaussian support or remove the distributional-transfer error. The independent audit checks 13,266 row-vertices, 68 exact marginal supports, 50,342 production outputs and 1,580 independent ideal-payoff/body-error comparisons. Numerical samples supplement the uniform proof rather than supplying its maximum.

A clipped flag prepares only c uniform comparison bits, pads with known zeros and compares against all q+1 value bits. Its Born probability is E[min(X,r)]/r, r=2^(c-q). The full comparison uses the existing predicate cost; an explicit clamp circuit is unnecessary. I prove an excess-tail enclosure 0<=E[(X-r)+]<=D from support and upper moments, add its midpoint D/2, and debit D/2 from the existing estimation allowance. The finite-grid mean lemma also applies to min(X,r). I do not assign the fractional output's variance to a Bernoulli flag.

The clipping certificate is a checked specialisation of established moment-option majorisation, including Bertsimas and Popescu (2002, Sections 2.1–2.2, Theorem 3) and Bertsimas and Popescu (2005, Section 2). Dyadic mean estimation is established in Montanaro (2015, Sections 2(c)–2(d)); quantum cost-dependent allocation has An et al. (2021) as a predecessor. The current finite-grid convex-hull prior can be sharp while changing no integer budget at high precision. I make no priority claim for these generic ingredients. Exact versions and consulted sections remain in SOURCES.md and checks/clip_review.md.

## 6 A transferable explanation of the encoding result

The new support theorem is independent of the 27-contract instantiation. Centred log perturbations h_i with sum w_i h_i=0 and |h_i|<=delta satisfy 0<=log(sum w_i exp(h_i))<=exp(delta) sum w_i h_i^2/2. A bounded log-price slope then gives an O(epsilon^2) residual range for uniformly O(epsilon) centred perturbations on bounded support. With pointwise arithmetic error of the same order, the implemented range retains that scale. A dyadic single-flag range R is less than twice the certified finite maximum. At a mean tolerance of order epsilon^2, its probability tolerance is bounded below, so the outer estimator's fixed-confidence query cap is bounded within the proved implementation domain. Oracle cost and changing numerical widths remain separate.

The proof explicitly handles zero output, endpoints c=0 and c=q, the nonzero-shot prior bootstrap, the rational upper enclosure of pi and the controllers' logarithm guards. An independent review checks 6,113 dyadic endpoint cases and 20 nonconstant bounded-support examples. Those examples are mathematical demonstrations, not calibrated financial evidence. An unbounded Gaussian model does not meet a fixed support hypothesis; a refining quantile grid has changing support and distributional error. The theorem therefore explains why the canonical unit-range flag's variance penalty is insufficient to prove a query separation. It does not establish constant total pricing cost.

## 7 Contribution assessment and next discriminating work

The 17 improved complete quantum budgets are a positive project-specific computational consequence of checked support, moment and numerical-transfer certificates. The constructive range/clipping opponents and deterministic prices make the research claim more credible by identifying which apparent improvements fail. That evidence is valuable, but it does not yet establish an original theorem, a literature gap or practical quantum advantage. Moment-option bounds, finite-grid convexity, range normalisation, geometric controls, conditional smoothing and dyadic quantum means all have established antecedents. The Liu–Li bounded-support comparison remains incomplete at its equation/proof level, and compatible QSP and stronger variance-aware algorithms remain unimplemented.

I retain the research question at the financial certificate/encoding/cost interface. The next discriminating work is a competent higher-dimensional deterministic and randomised quasi-Monte Carlo benchmark, with valid error criteria and charged setup, followed by a focused prior-art comparison of the precise support/moment-to-circuit consequence. A new conditional or bucket-local certificate is worth developing only if it changes a complete budget beyond these opponents and supplies a substantive transferable argument. The present global-moment dyadic implementation has not earned further development through its results. Changing the asset label or adding models alone would not resolve originality.

All numerical contracts remain synthetic and all data acquisition used free sources or existing authorised access. No new purchase, subscription, OptionMetrics entitlement or private empirical dataset was used. REIT observations from existing LSEG access could later support a labelled illustration after a field, dividend and adjustment audit; they would not validate an unobserved exotic-option price.
