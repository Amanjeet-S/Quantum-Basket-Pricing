## 4. Classical attack

For the continuous auxiliary law, the old classical score F(Y) is the conditional mean of H times the new event. The exact variance identity is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H\mathbf1_{event})
=\operatorname{Var}(F(Y))+\mathbb E[F(Y)(H-F(Y))].
\]

The additional term is nonnegative and is strictly positive for a nontrivial finite positive interval under a Gaussian with lambda>0. Therefore the new binary classical score has no variance advantage over the existing analytically conditioned score. This comparison concerns classical Monte Carlo scores, rather than the conditional variance of either quantum algorithm's already Bernoulli objective. The distinction is stated explicitly in the supporting proof.

A fair classical competitor may analytically integrate both new auxiliary variables, restoring the exact existing conditional kernel. It can also choose any permitted rotated basis and use quadrature or QMC in that representation. Existing continuous financial prices remain valid under the proved no-arbitrage covariance identity. The new oracle does not demonstrate a previously unavailable complete-price capability.
