# 1 Scope and evidence

I reviewed the current main paper, complete four-case quantum plans, bounded Chebyshev proof and controller interpretation, existing source-consultation and novelty ledgers, and the new seven-basis complete resource study. I did not acquire data, read a new external source, change the repository, stage files or make commits. This review distinguishes mathematical correctness, a constructive logical implementation result and a literature contribution. The portable staged shared-threshold producer and its frozen protocol, scalar archive and full resource results provide a new concrete response to the attacks below. The separate independent production audit passed its mathematical, table, predicate and resource checks; this review does not replace it.

# 2 Quantum algorithms and preparation adversary

Severity P1: neither the previous bounded Chebyshev estimator nor exact rational factor rotations establish a stronger quantum pricing algorithm in a competitive sense. The Chebyshev pilot certifies 111 of 400 declared diagnostic histories, while every diagnostic median exceeds the earlier QPE cap. Its worst-history allowance is greater than that earlier cap. The diagnostic mesh is not the distribution of a financial flag and cannot identify expected financial execution cost. Full-alpha published ChebAE, the original 36-contract panel and a compatible nontrivial upstream payoff QSP baseline remain unclosed comparisons. The final sources actually consulted are described in qsp_source_ledger.md and SOURCES.md. An empirical fit or a named QSP theorem supplies neither the controller's confidence coverage nor the finite financial price certificate.

Severity P1: the rotations improve selected complete T caps by only 0, 0.04751, 0.15641 and 2.07119 per cent. Every winning method remains positive R1, with the same QPE phase size, confidence repetition count, normaliser, word width and peak allocation. The measured gain comes from constant-loading arithmetic in the chosen compiler. A screen that improves an analytic loading proxy by tens of per cent and a full algorithm that saves only fractions of a per cent demonstrate why a proxy is not an algorithmic contribution. This result is worth retaining as a falsification of an attractive surrogate objective. It is insufficient to motivate a new quantum-finance model on its own.

Severity P1: the logical arithmetic uses conservative Bennett-style out-of-place operations. Its counts are implementation caps, not lower bounds or universal hardware prices. The actual invariant reflection core and scalar-table inverse must be charged, and a physical adjoint must swap T with T-dagger. Optimised adders, constant multipliers, per-node widths, lookup grouping and depth-aware schedules can alter the ranking. An oracle that wins inside this graph family has not defeated all coherent arithmetic. The current two coherent lookup architectures and published QROM ancestry are useful safeguards, but do not close this stronger-compiler comparison.

Severity P1: low dimensionality is a severe practical weakness. All original 36 prices and all four stressed cap-one prices have complete classical certificates. Logical T caps of tens or hundreds of billions do not translate into a physical advantage over those classical methods. State preparation is correctly explicit here, using Hadamards and reversible one-dimensional quantile tables, but software construction time, magic-state factories, code distance, connectivity, classical feedback and fault-tolerant wall-clock time remain separate. No private data or claimed market validation repairs this computational mismatch.

Severity P2: no-arbitrage belongs to the continuous financial target and its admissible pricing measure. A finite Gaussian grid need not itself preserve every continuous discounted martingale identity. The financial error certificate relates its output to the no-arbitrage target. Independent noisy prices at multiple strikes are not automatically an arbitrage-free surface; a surface claim would require simultaneous intervals and structural constraints. Current individual-contract conclusions do not require that additional claim.

# 3 Positive integral R1 design adversary

The identity R1 = Delta times the integral of P-prime at m+t Delta is correct. It is not new calculus. Its immediate sampled score is bounded by B = Delta-max times M1, which exceeds H=1 in six of the seven declared bases. A normaliser of one cannot be assigned merely because the integral averages to a capped correction. Clipping individual integrand scores at one introduces bias. The separately frozen screen rebuilds all financial-box vertices using 512-bit outward arithmetic, rather than using sampled maxima or a price answer.

The normalisers are approximately 0.89964 for the two-asset case; 2.34714 and 2.63009 for the two four-asset low-lambda bases; 2.02378 and 2.32859 for the four-asset lambda-0.2 bases; and 2.65313 and 2.44317 for the eight-asset bases. A single midpoint fails the deterministic budget in both four-asset families. For eight assets, the favourable zero-new-arithmetic QPE screen already doubles phase size from 512 to 1024 at four or eight auxiliary bits; a single midpoint forces 8192. This is a decisive rejection of an automatic benefit from the raw integral score, rather than a proof that every possible integral oracle must lose.

Conditional randomisation cannot intrinsically improve classical variance. The exact identity Var(score) = Var(R1(Y)) + E Var(score given Y) retains an auxiliary variance penalty. The new positive integral still needs a complete evaluator, a representation error, its true range, both the prior and statistical budget, and all clean-work charges. The cheapest decisive next experiment is therefore a representation that obtains its bounded score structurally while eliminating expensive conditional-price tables.

# 4 Constructive stronger candidate tested in this phase

The selected candidate uses the existing positive threshold-measure representation. For a cap, draw a shared independent uniform S and Gaussian N and encode the event mG < log(1+H S/K) minus lambda N <= mA. Its expected score is exactly R1 and its pointwise score is H times a Boolean flag. This retains normaliser H, evaluates the log-ratio once and replaces two conditional-price tables by one small-range logarithm and two clean comparisons. The general identity extends to every positive finite threshold measure of finite mass H. Negative measures and an infinite-mass uncapped vanilla representation are excluded.

The proof derives a strike midpoint bound, a rounded truncated-normal Kolmogorov bound including left limits, an arithmetic event sandwich and the complete financial monetary bound. The inner Gaussian label law is normalised, so its truncated mass is not multiplied into the price decoder. Its missing mass and rounded nodes enter the CDF allowance. The represented upper endpoint is built from the same represented lower endpoint plus a nonnegative represented log-ratio, and guard certificates exclude overflow. The exact Boolean predicate retains strict lower and non-strict upper endpoints, including ties. Numeric output zero is only a body-certifier interface; it is never identified as the success probability.

Before fresh production results, the policy fixed the four previously selected complete-resource winning bases, outer scalar addresses at twelve bits, ten inner normal bits, four uniform strike bits, six strike-log table bits, q=28, the two coherent lookup architectures, unchanged alpha=1/3600 and monetary radius 0.01, and no repairs. All four complete certificates pass. Selected complete T caps are 20,855,422,300; 83,560,988,725; 89,247,538,825; and 203,322,773,325. Relative to the paired selected direct positive-R1 cap these reduce T by approximately 17.5883, 7.55594, 8.76694 and 3.90281 per cent. QPE phase sizes and 25 repetitions remain unchanged. The respective full undiscounted price error caps are approximately 0.00902104, 0.00730807, 0.00679662 and 0.00800736. These results are constructive logical resource improvements. The independent audit reconstructed 411 DAG nodes, 146 support vertices at 768 bits, 108,826 ordinates, eight profiles and eight complete QPE plans, and checked small exact dirty-flag predicates and their coherent phases. Large financial profiles are checked by composition, without exhaustive full-state simulation. The results return no quantum derivative-price outcome and prove no physical advantage.

The declared conditional probability errors are about 0.000978 to 0.001038. They are appreciably greater than the direct numerical table errors, but do not cross a QPE phase boundary in these four cases. That finite fact, rather than an assertion that auxiliary encoding has no approximation cost, explains the observed gain. The common threshold is essential. Drawing separate threshold samples at the two endpoints would destroy the nonnegative interval identity and create a signed estimator requiring different charging and certificates.

Severity P1, resolved within a finite pool: the original two numerical price tables at twelve address bits were a potentially overprecise rival. After identifying this flaw, I registered an independent PA/PG address pool with each width in {6,8,10,12}, retaining all other outer precision, word widths and complete financial confidence. All 64 outcomes are retained, including twelve deterministic rejects. The complete numerical winner uses ten bits for each price table in every case, with T caps 23,789,339,125; 88,203,283,550; 95,203,161,050; and 209,495,066,950. The untuned frozen threshold implementation still improves these stronger caps by approximately 12.3329, 5.26318, 6.25570 and 2.94627 per cent. These are the appropriate main comparison percentages. The larger earlier reductions concern the historical twelve-bit rival. Exact historical full T caps and deterministic errors reproduce at the pool's twelve/twelve point. This strengthens the evidence without turning a finite pool into global precision optimality.

The classical conditional-variance observation does not imply a new quantum measurement-variance penalty. The original numeric direct-R1 oracle also encodes a Bernoulli flag through a uniform probability-comparison register. In the continuous ideal limit both preparations have global good probability E R1/H and the same two-dimensional Grover angle. The new score neither reduces that good-flag variance nor changes the generic ideal amplitude-estimation query law. Its measured benefit is a cheaper coherent preparation, subject to separately certified finite-error effects on the required resolution.

# 5 Literature and contribution claim adversary

The source ledgers already establish that threshold smoothing, comparator flags, Gaussian preparation, conditional pricing, QROM and amplitude estimation have published antecedents. The shared-threshold identity follows immediately from the already stated positive threshold kernel in the main paper. It should be presented as a development of that architecture, not a discovery of a new probability identity or quantum principle. The new project-specific argument is that this explicitly emitted binary encoding, its complete CDF/arithmetic/financial certificate and its matched logical resource cap improve the declared direct-price-table competitor. That is concrete value beyond a plan to build an oracle. Literature priority and superiority over compatible published digital or QSP pricing encodings still require source-level comparison.

The result is more persuasive than changing a latent basis, but its absolute scale remains prohibitive in these examples. The classical opponent receives the same model and can integrate both auxiliaries analytically, recovering the existing conditional kernel and lower or equal variance. The claim cannot be a classical variance improvement or a practical quantum win. A broader useful conclusion is a sufficient implementation criterion for positive finite threshold payoffs: replacing two smooth kernel evaluations by a shared interval predicate can save coherent evaluator work while preserving H normalisation, provided its CDF and endpoint-arithmetic allowances leave the statistical resolution unchanged. Its actual usefulness outside these four contracts is still an empirical and implementation question.

# 6 Ranked development and rejection policy

The first priority is independent mathematical and literal predicate/resource replay of the newly emitted candidate. Any understated endpoint error, uncharged atom, inappropriate extra mass factor, non-clean workspace, incorrect inverse phase, overflowing integer path or mismatched confidence allowance rejects the current result. Preserve the frozen result and record a declared repair rather than silently changing the source behind its protocol.

The second priority is give the same shared-threshold and direct preparations to modern mean estimators, with matched probability tolerances and total confidence. This isolates oracle savings from estimator-policy choices. The existing bounded controller is a transparent comparison but is not an unchanged implementation of published ChebAE. Reject algorithm-level superiority claims if its improvements require an unspecified financial-flag distribution, omit failed pilots or borrow the old financial allowance unchanged.

The third priority is implement efficient constant multiplication and per-node integer widths in both opponents. In the eight-asset graph, rational-loading scale operations dominate much of the cost, while removing conditional-price tables alone has a limited ceiling. The relevant objective is total preparation plus estimation and certificate cost, not a loading norm surrogate. Apply the same optimisation to all methods; reject a claimed representation gain if it vanishes when a shared compiler improvement is granted to the competitor.

The fourth priority is test a predeclared family of positive finite cash-digital mixtures or other bounded increasing payoff measures. For finite threshold mixtures the shared label preparation and exact measure weights need their own preparation and rounding charge. This is the most direct extension of the proved architecture. Reject universal-payoff claims when the measure has negative weights, infinite mass, costly unproved amplitude loading or no valid derivative/threshold discretisation certificate.

The fifth priority is a nontrivial QSP payoff-kernel comparison with continuum error and explicit phase synthesis. It could produce a more substantial quantum-algorithm contribution, but requires a compatible upstream scalar signal, polynomial parity and magnitude proofs, all phase tapes and adjoints, full monetary error and a matched emitted cost. A finite-node fit, an unspecified block encoding or a renamed identity signal is not an acceptable substitute.
