# Architecture and estimator source audit

## 1. Scope and keywords

I conducted this audit on 5 October 2026 to identify published implementation and estimation comparators for the capped arithmetic basket residual. I distinguish a published source's assumptions from my proposed compiler choices. I made no changes to the research repository and used no purchased access. Keywords: quantum finance, coherent conditioning, QROM, temporary logical AND, amplitude estimation, finite precision, geometric control variate.

## 2. Unary iteration and the meaning of the four-T count

I consulted the final publisher PDF of Babbush et al., *Encoding Electronic Spectra in Quantum Circuits with Linear T Complexity*, Physical Review X 8, 041015 (2018), in Safari. The relevant material is sections III.A and III.C, pages 9–13, figures 4, 6 and 10, and section VI, figure 22. The [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.8.041015) constructs unary iteration using logarithmically many selector ancillas, with the promise that the binary index is smaller than the table length. Adjacent selector erasure and computation can be merged. Its optimised dense traversal has L−1 logical AND computations and a reported T count of 4L−4. Section III.C applies this traversal to word lookup, with the classical word represented by the presence or absence of CNOTs. The T count is independent of word length in this model, although the CNOTs, storage and output qubits remain relevant resources. Figure 22 explicitly uses a Hadamard, measurement and a conditional phase correction for AND erasure.

I therefore treat published unary iteration as a required stronger comparator. I do not identify its four-T logical AND with an exact seven-T Toffoli followed by that gate's unitary inverse. The paper's surface-code depth estimates are also architecture-specific. A sparse table still has its original index domain; skipping a zero row does not establish a smaller promised domain. I require an emitted, audited traversal before assigning savings from sparse pruning.

## 3. Temporary logical AND and coherent use

I consulted the final publisher PDF of Gidney, *Halving the cost of quantum addition*, Quantum 2, 74 (2018), in Safari. I read the introduction, opportunity-cost discussion, figure 3 and its caption, the results, and the discussion accompanying figure 5. The [published paper](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2018-06-18-74) describes a four-T AND computation, including its initial magic state, and zero-T erasure by an X-basis measurement followed by conditional CZ correction. Figure 5 supplies sufficient conditions for replacing a compute/uncompute Toffoli pair: the later operation erases the earlier result, and intervening operations tolerate the ancillary entanglement and relevant phase behaviour. The discussion explicitly includes reversibly constructed Grover predicates, while warning about ancillary requirements and limitations. The alternative inverse construction's net resource saving includes recovering a magic state; it is not a claim about six literal T gates in an exact Toffoli decomposition.

I read the diagram captions and surrounding text, but did not visually transcribe every wire-level gate in figure 3. An implementation must use the verified diagram or an independently checked equivalent. Measurement does not by itself exclude this construction from amplitude estimation. Corrected branches may realise an exact logical unitary channel on retained registers. I require a branchwise or channel proof, ancillary reset, and verified controlled operations or inverses where the estimator needs them. I retain measurements, feedforward and ancillary lifetime in the resource record. I do not assign these counts to the present exact-Toffoli tape by substitution.

## 4. A fair constructive architecture comparison

I would first compare the existing selector traversal with a stronger emitted exact unitary traversal at identical table entries, finite inputs and monetary tolerance. Because an X, CNOT and exact Toffoli tape is a phase-free permutation, its complete Boolean-map verification establishes the appropriate coherent action once the exact gate decompositions are accounted for. I would retain selector operations, word fanout, work qubits, live ancillary peaks, compute and uncompute, interpolation arithmetic, the probability comparator, and the Grover reflections. I would separately report a measurement-assisted construction only after the preceding logical-channel requirements have been checked. This separation prevents an apparent saving that comes from changing the gate model while leaving the old implementation in place.

I would retune all competing financial graphs under each implemented architecture. A tighter kernel certificate can alter table size and precision, while a better selector changes their relative costs. I would retain classical endpoint generation and circuit-description size. A one-dimensional function table is a genuine known-function implementation, but it is still precomputed data with a cost. It does not justify a general claim about an optimal quantum evaluator.

## 5. Known-function competitors

I consulted O'Brien and Sünderhauf, *Quantum state preparation via piecewise QSVT*, Quantum 9, 1786 (2025), in its final publisher PDF through retrieval, sections 2.3–2.4, 3.3 and 4.1. The [published paper](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-07-03-1786) analyses filling fractions, continuous polynomial bounds, precision and amplification costs. Its segmentation optimisation is restricted to dyadic segments and specified approximation requirements. Section 4.1 uses an exact comparator to improve its inner linear block encoding. These are stronger known-function approaches than an unoptimised lookup assumption. Its state-preparation task is not identical to a scalar fixed-point payoff evaluator. Applying it here requires a verified domain encoding for the computed log-basket, an appropriate polynomial normalisation, conversion between amplitude and flag probability, and the arithmetic prefix and inverse. I cannot transfer a function-only reported advantage to the complete pricing graph.

The final publication of McArdle, Gilyén and Berta, *Quantum State Preparation without Coherent Arithmetic*, Physical Review Letters 136, 240603 (2026), is verified at [the publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/ntvs-c48s). I consulted its final metadata and abstract through retrieval, not its full final theorem text. It is the published successor of arXiv:2210.14892. Its arithmetic-free function preparation is a relevant unimplemented competitor; the abstract is insufficient for assigning detailed complete-oracle costs.

An attempted Safari reading of the QSVT source was interrupted and is not recorded as a reading. The successful Babbush and Gidney readings are distinguished from the retrieval readings above.

## 6. Published confidence guarantees and financial priors

I consulted section 2 and section 4, including Algorithm Est_Amp and Theorem 12, of Brassard, Høyer, Mosca and Tapp, *Quantum Amplitude Amplification and Estimation*, Contemporary Mathematics 305, 53–74 (2002), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/305/05215). The text actually consulted was an AMS-copyright 2002 typeset chapter PDF hosted by the volume editor, [Samuel Lomonaco](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/lomonaco/ams/specialpapers/brassard/Brassard.pdf), through retrieval. For implemented probability a, Theorem 12 gives error at most 2πk√(a(1−a))/M+k²π²/M², with success at least 8/π² for k=1 and greater than 1−1/[2(k−1)] for k≥2. Its query count is not a complete gate count. Its amplitude-estimation construction uses a measurement-free unitary A and its inverse.

I consulted the final publisher HTML of Grinko et al., *Iterative quantum amplitude estimation*, npj Quantum Information 7, 52 (2021), through primary-source search retrieval, especially Theorem 1 and Algorithms 1–2. I did not read its supplementary proof or final PDF in Safari during this audit. The [published theorem](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-021-00379-1) provides confidence 1−α and interval width at most 2ε, with fewer than (50/ε)log((2/α)log₂(π/(4ε))) Grover applications under its stated schedule. Algorithm 1 starts from the full angular interval. FINDNEXTK avoids ambiguity by keeping the amplified interval in an invertible cosine half-plane. A different prior-based schedule needs its own proof; this theorem does not certify an arbitrary change of initial interval or power rule.

For an independently certified upper bound a≤u, the factor √(a(1−a)) in Theorem 12 is at most √(u(1−u)) when u≤1/2, and at most 1/2 otherwise. This is an established theorem's elementary consequence. I must first enlarge a continuous-price prior by the retained finite-grid and arithmetic bounds, normalised by H, so that it bounds the actual implemented flag probability. Confidence amplification, state preparations, controlled Grover applications, Fourier operations where present, classical processing and reflections must all remain in the cost. Intersecting an estimator's valid confidence interval with a deterministic prior preserves coverage, but does not on its own prove a smaller query count.

I verified Aaronson and Rall, *Quantum Approximate Counting, Simplified*, SOSA 2020, 24–32, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611976014.5), from SIAM's published record and abstract. I did not consult its full proof. It provides a published QFT-free estimation antecedent. I do not describe it as an ACM Transactions on Algorithms paper. A proposed three/five power policy requires comparison with these established ambiguity-management methods before claiming estimator novelty.

## 7. The positive residual and the strongest defensible research question

The existing source ledger records a final Safari reading of Rebentrost, Gupt and Bromley, Physical Review A 98, 022321 (2018). In this audit I additionally checked its [author version v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.00109v2), dated 23 August 2018, section VI. The discussion following equation 56 invokes arithmetic–geometric ordering and an upper bound involving the stock maximum to support a variance bound. It establishes variance-aware quantum Asian pricing. I do not attribute a constructed geometric-payoff control residual or the present Jensen logarithmic-gap prior to that discussion. The [final publication](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.98.022321) remains the preferred citation for its established method.

For this project's positive-weight common-loading model, I can derive the residual bound from capped-payoff monotonicity, its one-Lipschitz property and the lognormal first moments. The additional bound on E[m_A−m_G] follows by applying Jensen's inequality to log(Σw_i exp(D_i)), then evaluating Gaussian exponential moments. With matched drifts it is J₀=D/2+log(Σw_i exp(−(R_i−R_G)·R_G)). Consequently the currency residual mean is bounded by min(H, E[A]−E[G], HL_cap J₀), where L_cap is a valid global Lipschitz bound for the conditioned kernel. This bound supplies a deterministic estimator prior and, because a residual in [0,H] has second moment at most H times its mean, a variance upper bound. These are checked elementary derivations. They can improve classical confidence or sampling budgets too.

I verified Kemna and Vorst, *A pricing method for options based on average asset values*, Journal of Banking & Finance 14(1), 113–129 (1990), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-4266(90)90039-5), from the author institution's published record and the publisher abstract. I did not consult its full final text in this audit. Its average-option Monte Carlo variance reduction is an established financial antecedent; this record does not by itself settle whether the exact present inequality appears in that paper. I cannot infer originality from unsuccessful keyword searches.

My bounded research question is whether contract-specific kernel and residual certificates reduce a complete, explicitly implemented quantum cost at fixed monetary error and confidence after all financial baselines are retuned under stronger lookup architectures. I would retain a negative result if the improvement disappears. A new elementary inequality, a compiler implementation, or a separately proved estimator variant can contribute a project-specific argument, but none establishes a general quantum optimum, an end-to-end speedup over the strongest classical method, or theoretical novelty without a closer mathematical comparison. At the start of this audit the immediate blockers were measurement-assisted QROM, estimator-policy proofs, an unimplemented QSVT competitor and a matched complete classical cost comparison. The subsequent study implements and checks the lookup channel and estimator policy. General arithmetic improvements, QSVT and a matched complete classical runtime comparison remain open.
