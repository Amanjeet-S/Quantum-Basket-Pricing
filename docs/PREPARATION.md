# Quantitative preparation for graduate study

## 1 Purpose and evidential standard

I use this project to demonstrate mathematical reasoning, financial modelling and reproducible computation for quantitative finance graduate study. A checked project-specific argument is valuable, but a new quantum theory, publication or practical quantum advantage is unnecessary for this purpose. Oxford explicitly says publications are not expected while requiring probability, statistics, ODEs/PDEs, linear algebra and analysis, particularly real analysis ([2027/28 admissions](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/graduate/courses/msc-mathematical-and-computational-finance)).

The repository demonstrates substantial applied calculus, covariance/linear-algebra reasoning, conditional probability, measure-theoretic arguments, validated numerical pricing and Python. It does not establish the whole undergraduate prerequisite checklist. The principal remaining preparation gaps are broad proof-based analysis/probability, empirical statistics/model diagnostics, optimisation beyond static quadratic hedging and differential-equation breadth beyond the new capped-GBM companion. A quantum calculation does not compensate for those gaps.

I distinguish entry requirements, teaching prerequisites within the degree, taught core material and optional specialisms. I also distinguish retained artefacts from independently assessed personal proficiency. I should be able to reproduce the important derivations, explain their assumptions and modify the implementation, with accurate attribution of assistance. A repository does not replace degree subjects, grades, credits or evidence of unaided understanding.

## 5 Priorities and completion

I completed the [capped-GBM companion](../paper/capped_gbm_companion.tex), with a [compiled paper](../paper/capped_gbm_companion.pdf), [reproducible study](../scripts/run_capped_gbm_study.py) and [synthetic evidence](../results/capped_gbm/study_manifest.json). It derives replication, the martingale/call-spread price, financial far-field conditions, a backward-Euler maximum principle and convergence for the actual kinked payoff. Seven contracts compare prices and five Greeks, retaining domain/interpolation allowances, analytic bump bias, adverse refinement and matched-budget Monte Carlo rankings. A bounded mixed pathwise/likelihood-ratio gamma estimator supplies a stronger comparator and finite-sample raw uncertainty. The same companion now proves conditional expectation as an L² projection and compares jointly projected controls with independent pilot coefficients; [the conditioning study](../results/capped_gbm/conditioning/study_manifest.json) retains non-commuting coefficient choices, failed coarse quadrature and adverse pilot rankings. The methods and measured gains are attributed carefully; no theoretical priority or formally certified floating-point coverage is claimed. This closes the selected pricing/PDE implementation gap while leaving broader differential-equation preparation necessary.

The projection/control step is complete as a prices-only mathematical and numerical study. It connects the existing total-variance identity to Hilbert-space geometry; its numerical variance gains do not establish equal-cost superiority or new quantum theory. The [Gaussian-estimation step](../results/capped_gbm/calibration/study_manifest.json) is complete under independent, equally spaced, noise-free synthetic returns. It derives the likelihood, covariance-rank failure, parameter uncertainty and its capped-price image, while keeping physical drift distinct from pricing drift. The [constrained-hedging companion](../paper/constrained_hedging_companion.tex) now closes the selected finite-dimensional optimisation gap. Its funded physical mean-square objective includes bias; risk-neutral prices determine costs. Full convexity, KKT and shadow-price proofs connect to constrained/unrestricted/cash-only comparisons, independent solver checks and adverse numerical cases. It demonstrates a financial decision problem under a known synthetic law, without claiming whole-module mastery or realised market hedging performance. The separate nine-page paper leaves the 29-page pricing/calibration companion unchanged.

In parallel, I should strengthen broad analysis/probability through assessed coursework where available and independently solved exercises. The next selected programming priority is an original small C++ pricing implementation for Oxford-specific preparation. A short executable financial example should make assumptions, price/error and proof/code connections visible before specialised gate counts. No licensed observations, paid data or new entitlement are required for these priorities.
