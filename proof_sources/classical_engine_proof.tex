\section{Exact classical evaluation of the finite quantum target}
\label{sec:exactclassical}

I construct an exact classical comparator for the retained finite graph. I use
unsigned 64-bit NumPy arrays to execute integer operations and retain Python
integers for sample moments. This comparator uses the same financial graph
and the same one-dimensional tables as the coherent evaluator. It does not
establish that these tables are the strongest classical discretisation of the
continuous financial model. I make no novelty claim for the following
elementary integer identities.

\begin{lemma}[Safe product after the financial truncation]
Let $18\leq q\leq30$, $w\leq q+7$, and $0\leq a,b<2^w$. Define
$a_H=\lfloor a/2^q\rfloor$, $a_L=a\bmod2^q$ and similarly $b_H,b_L$.
Then
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{ab}{2^q}\right\rfloor
 =2^q a_Hb_H+a_Hb_L+a_Lb_H
   +\left\lfloor\frac{a_Lb_L}{2^q}\right\rfloor .
\]
Every product, shift and addition on the right-hand side fits in an unsigned
64-bit word. Its final reduction modulo $2^w$ is the retained unsigned
fixed-point multiplication.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $(2^q a_H+a_L)(2^q b_H+b_L)$ gives the identity, since the first
three terms after division by $2^q$ are integers. The high limbs are smaller
than $2^7$ and the low limbs are smaller than $2^q$. Hence the unshifted
low-limb product is smaller than $2^{60}$, each cross product is smaller than
$2^{37}$ and the shifted high-limb product is smaller than $2^{44}$.
The sum is exactly $\lfloor ab/2^q\rfloor<2^{2w-q}\leq2^{44}$.
Every partial sum is nonnegative and bounded by this sum. All operations
therefore fit in 64 bits. The output bit mask is exactly reduction modulo
$2^w$. For a known $k<2^q$, the high limb of $k$ vanishes and the formula
reduces to $a_Hk+\lfloor a_Lk/2^q\rfloor$. A binary-power multiplier reduces
to a shift. A native unsigned product is used for a positive drop only when
$(2^w-1)k<2^{64}$ has been checked. With zero drop, native wrap followed by
the $w$-bit mask remains exact because $2^w$ divides $2^{64}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact unsigned product windows]
Put $B=2^{32}$ and write unsigned 64-bit inputs as $a=a_0+Ba_1$ and
$b=b_0+Bb_1$, with all limbs smaller than $B$. Let $p_{ij}=a_i b_j$ and
\[
 m=\lfloor p_{00}/B\rfloor+(p_{01}\bmod B)+(p_{10}\bmod B),
\]
\[
 L=(p_{00}\bmod B)+B(m\bmod B),\qquad
 U=p_{11}+\lfloor p_{01}/B\rfloor+\lfloor p_{10}/B\rfloor
       +\lfloor m/B\rfloor .
\]
Then $ab=L+2^{64}U$. For $0\leq d<128$ and $1\leq v\leq64$, the
implementation in \texttt{multiply\_shift\_u64} returns
$\lfloor ab/2^d\rfloor\bmod2^v$ exactly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $ab=p_{00}+B(p_{01}+p_{10})+B^2p_{11}$ and carry its central limb.
This gives the asserted decomposition. Each $p_{ij}<2^{64}$ and $m<3B$.
Moreover $U<2^{64}$ because $ab<2^{128}$, so every nonnegative partial sum
forming $U$ fits in 64 bits. If $0<d<64$, the low 64-bit window is the bitwise
OR of $L\gg d$ and $U\ll(64-d)$, where the latter is reduced modulo
$2^{64}$. The first term occupies only the low $64-d$ bits and the second
only the remaining bits. Their OR is therefore their sum in that window.
If $64\leq d<128$, the low window is $U\gg(d-64)$. If $d=0$, it is the
native low product word. Masking the final $v$ bits gives the required result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Equality of the finite classical and quantum targets]
For a retained graph with valid function addresses and the same stored
tables, \texttt{FiniteEvaluator.evaluate} equals the scalar integer graph
output for every valid input tuple. Under independent uniform finite input
addresses, let $a$ be that output and $X=a/2^q$. Then the coherent comparator
flag has success probability $\E X$, and an independent classical sample
mean of $X$ estimates the same finite target without bias.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Addition and subtraction followed by the register mask give their specified
modular results, since $2^w$ divides $2^{64}$. The preceding lemmas prove the
unsigned multiplication and constant-product branches. A quantile operation
gathers its stored integer word. A midpoint uniform operation returns
$(2i+1)2^{q-b-1}$, as specified. For an interpolation fractional width $f$,
the gathered cell is $j=\lfloor x/2^f\rfloor$, the remainder is
$r=x\bmod2^f$, and the returned value is
\[
 \left(b_j+\left\lfloor\delta_jr/2^f\right\rfloor\right)\bmod2^w.
\]
The native product branch is used only after checking
$\max_j\delta_j(2^f-1)<2^{64}$; otherwise the product-window lemma applies.
If a register's sign bit is set, its signed interpretation is $a-2^w<0$
and the lower clipping branch returns zero. Otherwise its signed value is
$a\geq0$ and the unsigned minimum with the cap is exactly the upper clip.
Every node consequently agrees with its scalar rule. Induction in the
topological graph order proves equality of outputs. Releasing a last-used
array changes no value consumed later.

Conditioned on an address tuple, there are exactly $a$ good comparison
integers among the $2^q$ equally likely comparison integers. The flag's
conditional success probability is therefore $a/2^q$. Averaging over the
uniform address law gives $\E X$. Linearity of expectation gives the same
expectation for an independent classical sample mean. Existing distribution,
arithmetic and geometric-correction bounds are still required to transfer
this finite target to the continuous financial price. A seeded NumPy
pseudorandom run is an implementation diagnostic; its seed is not a proof of
the independent random-bit premise of a confidence theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact accumulated sample moments]
Let $0\leq a_i\leq2^q$ with $q\leq30$. The class
\texttt{ExactMoments} retains $N$, $S=\sum_i a_i$ and $Q=\sum_i a_i^2$
exactly, including when $Q>2^{64}$. For $N>1$, its normalised sample mean
and unbiased sample variance are the exact fractions
\[
 \overline X=\frac{S}{N2^q},\qquad
 s_X^2=\frac{NQ-S^2}{N(N-1)2^{2q}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $a_i=\ell_i+2^{16}h_i$, with $\ell_i<2^{16}$ and
$h_i\leq2^{14}$. Each reduction chunk contains at most $2^{20}$ outputs.
The low-square, high-square and cross-product sums are respectively smaller
than $2^{52}$, at most $2^{48}$ and smaller than $2^{50}$. The first-moment
sum is at most $2^{50}$. They therefore fit in unsigned 64-bit words. Their
assembly is performed with arbitrary-precision Python integers using
\[
 Q=\sum_i\ell_i^2+2^{17}\sum_i\ell_i h_i+2^{32}\sum_i h_i^2.
\]
Summing chunks and merging accumulators preserve these integer identities.
The mean formula follows directly. Expanding
$\sum_i(a_i-S/N)^2=Q-S^2/N$ and dividing by $(N-1)2^{2q}$ gives the variance
formula. A floating rendering of either exact fraction is a diagnostic. The
sample variance is not substituted for a proved variance upper bound in a
fixed confidence certificate.
\end{proof}

The operation profile reports conservative scalar-lane counts for this
particular NumPy implementation, table bytes and separate setup timing. It
also charges scalar broadcasts to every lane. A complete classical workflow
must additionally charge table generation, random generation, input validation,
allocation and exact-moment reduction. These counts do not describe CPU
instructions or an optimised classical lower bound. Comparing them directly
with logical T gates cannot establish physical quantum speed-up.
