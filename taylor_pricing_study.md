# 1. Continuous deterministic certification of the frozen Gaussian baskets

I evaluate a finite Taylor expansion of the conditional capped arithmetic price in the arithmetic-to-geometric ratio, retaining the dependence between that ratio and the geometric log basket. The coefficients and remainder moments reduce to finite Gaussian exponential tilts. I compute them with exact rational model parameters and outward Arb balls. This is a continuous-model calculation: its price certificate does not require a finite Gaussian grid or stochastic estimation. I predeclare the maximum order as eight and the arithmetic precision as 224 bits, retain every order from zero to eight, and retain every contract in the original 27-contract protocol.

The construction is a checked finite-order derivation. I do not claim convergence of its infinite series, new generic Taylor theory, a new Gaussian-tilt identity, market calibration or theoretical originality. A literature comparison is required before deciding whether the project-specific combination or error certificate adds anything beyond published basket-expansion methods. The principal immediate use is to strengthen the classical comparator.

# 2. Certificate and numerical outcomes

For every real factor realisation, exact weighted centring implies that the arithmetic-to-geometric ratio T is at least one. The rth derivative with respect to T is the falling-factorial operator D(D−1)…(D−r+1) applied to the smooth common-Gaussian conditional price, multiplied by T raised to −r. Fourier bounds on derivatives of the normal density give the uniform constant C_r(λ). Taylor's theorem therefore encloses the remainder at order k by H C_(k+1)(λ) E[(T−1)^(k+1)]/(k+1)!. Each expectation is a finite sum of exact multinomial weights and outward-enclosed Gaussian exponentials. The correlation between T and the geometric log price is retained through the Gaussian tilt shift R_G·v_alpha.

The retained calculation certifies 25 of the 27 prices within midpoint radius 0.01 in the model's unspecified price currency unit. All eighteen four- and eight-asset contracts succeed. The two unresolved controls have two assets, λ=0.05 and caps 10 and 25. Their narrowest retained radii are 0.07916196365 and 0.19790490912 at order five. The older continuous two-asset quadrature certifies those two controls, so the combined deterministic comparator now covers all 27 contracts. Failure of the uniform Taylor bound is not a pricing discrepancy.

The largest selected radius among four-asset contracts is 0.001765790218, for λ=0.05 and cap 25. The corresponding largest eight-asset radius is 0.000057037645. The best retained intervals for these higher-dimensional contracts occur at order eight. The first order meeting radius 0.01 is generally smaller: the difficult four-asset λ=0.05, cap-25 case first succeeds at order five, requiring 126 tilted-kernel terms for that cap; the corresponding eight-asset case first succeeds at order four, requiring 495 terms. Those first-success counts are retained diagnostics. They are not presented as the measured cost of the full experiment, which evaluates all retained orders.

# 3. Work and reproducibility

The full retained run takes 15.3647 seconds on the current already-authorised python-flint runtime. Geometry and outward moment setup account for 6.6747 seconds and tilted-kernel work accounts for 8.6827 seconds. These are machine-specific diagnostics, not a hardware-independent benchmark or a quantum runtime comparison. The calculation evaluates 25,080 distinct geometric exponentials and 120,690 tilted scalar kernels. Geometry is cached across the three common-Gaussian scales because the exact centred drift, centred loadings and geometric exposure are identical within each asset family. Each scalar-kernel call computes and reuses every derivative through order eight.

For n assets, calculation through order eight uses binomial(n+8,8) scalar-kernel terms per cap, and the ninth-order remainder uses binomial(n+9,9) geometric terms including all lower degrees. These counts are 45 and 55 for two assets, 495 and 715 for four assets, and 12,870 and 24,310 for eight assets. This finite count can become expensive outside the retained asset/order regime. The unbounded lognormal ratio also means that sufficiently high-order moments can enlarge the uniform remainder bound. Neither small asset count nor the success of this matched frozen family establishes a general pricing complexity result.

The implementation depends on the existing `continuous_quadrature.py` interval utilities and the unchanged frozen `results/generalisation_protocol.json`. Run `python taylor_pricing.py` with the already-installed python-flint 0.9.0 environment, then `python checks/audit_taylor_pricing.py` and `python checks/test_taylor_pricing.py`. Reproduction needs the retained `results/continuous_quadrature.json` for the independent two-asset comparison. No new package purchase, data entitlement or installation is required. All retained endpoints are exact dyadic rational fractions; decimal strings are display values only.

# 4. Independent checks and interpretation

The independent audit checks 540 scalar derivatives against formal power-series differentiation, 60 derivative values against direct strike integrals, and 81 coefficient values against a separately integrated one-dimensional Gaussian representation. The strike-integral checks include the positive price and first-derivative integrands; higher derivative integrands are signed. All 81 retained two-asset/order price intervals contain the older continuous-quadrature enclosures. A separate repeated-polynomial-multiplication construction reproduces every one of the 25,080 multinomial weights through degree nine. Eighty-three selected exposure/exponent identities are checked using pair-covariance expansions instead of the production exposure norm. Six unit contracts also pass.

The higher-dimensional certificate follows from the mathematical derivation together with outward arithmetic and exact input checks. The one-dimensional quadrature checks validate implementation identities but do not constitute an independent higher-dimensional integration experiment. This distinction prevents the checks from carrying more evidential weight than they support.

The result removes a major weakness in the previous classical comparison: every frozen contract now has a deterministic continuous-model price certificate, rather than a sampling-only baseline in higher dimensions. Logical T reductions remain meaningful comparisons among complete quantum implementations, but these synthetic contracts no longer support a claim of quantum advantage based on the absence of a competent deterministic classical solver. The revised contribution question must consequently address general applicability, literature overlap and regimes beyond the unusually favourable retained Gaussian geometry.
