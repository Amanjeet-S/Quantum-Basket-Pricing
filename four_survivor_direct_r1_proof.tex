\section{Direct global transfer bounds for the positive first residual}
Let $w_i>0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$, and let
\[
 X(y)=\sum_iw_i\exp(\mu_i+R_i\cdot y),\qquad
 G(y)=\prod_i\exp(\mu_i+R_i\cdot y)^{w_i}.
\]
Write $\ell=\log X$, $m=\log G$ and $r_j=\sum_iw_iR_{ij}$.
The common independent Gaussian factor is integrated into a smooth
conditional capped-call price $P$. Suppose the checked global constants
satisfy $\|P'\|_\infty\leq M_1$ and
$\|P''-P'\|_\infty\leq M_2$.
Weighted arithmetic--geometric mean and monotonicity of the payoff give
$0\leq F(y):=P(\ell(y))-P(m(y))\leq H$.
The constants in this review use the full asset loadings
$A_j:=\max_i|R_{ij}|$, rather than the centred loadings.

Define $\pi_i(y)=w_i\exp(\mu_i+R_i\cdot y)/X(y)$.
These weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Direct differentiation yields
\[
 \ell_j=\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij},\qquad
 \ell_{jj}=\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}^{2}-\ell_j^2.
\]
Consequently $|\ell_j|\leq A_j$ and
$\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}^{2}\leq A_j^2$. The first derivative is
$F_j=P'(\ell)\ell_j-P'(m)r_j$, so
\[
 |F_j|\leq M_1(A_j+|r_j|)=:D_{1j}.
\]
For the second derivative it is essential to retain the cancellation
between the log-basket Hessian and the $P'$ part of $P''$:
\[
 (P\circ\ell)_{jj}
  =(P''-P')(\ell)\ell_j^2
   +P'(\ell)\sum_i\pi_iR_{ij}^{2}.
\]
The geometric log $m$ is affine. The triangle inequality therefore proves
\[
 |F_{jj}|\leq(M_1+M_2)(A_j^2+r_j^2)=:D_{2j}.
\]
No sign condition on $R_{ij}$ or $r_j$ is needed. Replacing $A_j$ by a
centred-loading maximum would not follow from this proof.

Let $\nu$ be the unnormalised Gaussian measure restricted to $[-L,L]$,
with mass $a$, and let $\tau$ be its equal-mass rounded-node rule,
also of mass $a$. For each cell let $c$ be its actual rounded node and
$\mu_{1,c}=\int(y-c)\,\nu(dy)$,
$\mu_{2,c}=\int(y-c)^2\,\nu(dy)$.
The established second-order cell identity gives an atomic signed first
moment and a positive Peano measure of mass
$\mu_{2,c}/2$. Tensor telescoping and the preceding global bounds give
\[
 \left|\int F\,d\nu^{\otimes p}-\int F\,d\tau^{\otimes p}\right|
 \leq a^{p-1}\sum_{j=1}^p
 \left(D_{1j}\sum_c|\mu_{1,c}|
       +\frac{D_{2j}}{2}\sum_c\mu_{2,c}\right).
\]
Every other factor of each hybrid measure has mass $a$. Normalising those
factors without restoring this mass would change the priced quantity.
The excluded Gaussian region contributes at most $H(1-a^p)$ because
$0\leq F\leq H$. Adding this tail gives the full continuous-to-finite-grid
allowance. I require an undiscounted monetary radius of $0.01$ in the
four-case experiment. The fixed rate is $r=0.03$ and maturity is one year,
so multiplication by $e^{-0.03}<1$ preserves this radius for the actual
discounted derivative price. This is a conservative accuracy requirement.
The minimum of this valid allowance and another valid allowance remains
a valid allowance; this is deterministic selection and has no statistical
selection penalty.
